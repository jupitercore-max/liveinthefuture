# Research: US Drug Spending Crosses $1 Trillion — GLP-1 Class Drives a Third of Growth

## Slug: us-drug-spending-trillion-glp1-third-of-growth

## Journalist: Priya Anand (Health/Longevity beat)

## Core Thesis
US prescription drug spending is about to cross $1 trillion for the first time in history, and a single drug class — GLP-1 receptor agonists — accounts for nearly one-third of all spending growth. At 11% of American adults now using these drugs (up from 3% two years ago), the economics of healthcare are being reshaped faster than any insurance model, federal budget, or employer benefits plan was designed to handle. Nobody has calculated what happens at 20% or 30% adoption.

## Original Calculation
**The GLP-1 cost-effectiveness paradox:**
- GLP-1s are simultaneously the most cost-effective drug class in a generation (per ICER: they reduce cardiovascular death by 20%, kidney disease progression by 24%) AND the most budget-destroying
- $132B in GLP-1 spending in 2025 (ASHP report)
- Obesity rate dropped from 39.9% (2022) to 36.4% (2026) = 3.5 percentage points ≈ 9.2M fewer obese adults
- CDC estimates excess annual medical costs of obesity: ~$2,505 per obese adult (2019 dollars, ~$2,850 adjusted to 2026)
- 9.2M × $2,850 = ~$26.2B in avoided medical costs from reduced obesity
- Net cost to the healthcare system: $132B - $26.2B = ~$105.8B net new spending
- At current trajectory (usage roughly doubling annually), 2028 spending could exceed $300B — 30% of total US drug spending
- The breakeven: GLP-1s would need to reduce obesity by 15+ percentage points (not 3.5) to offset their own cost through reduced downstream care. That implies 25-30% adult adoption would be needed just to make the math neutral.

## Key Data Points

### Adoption (Gallup, May-June 2026, n=5,065)
- 11% of US adults currently using GLP-1s for weight loss (up from 3% in 2024)
- 15% have used GLP-1s at some point (up from 6% in 2024)
- 1/3 of compounded GLP-1 users switched FROM brand-name (cost-driven)

### Drug Spending (ASHP Annual Report, April 2026)
- Total US Rx spending: $915B in 2025 (up 12.7% YoY — fastest in two decades)
- Projected to exceed $1 trillion in 2026
- Tirzepatide: ~$60B in 2025 spending
- Semaglutide: ~$60B in 2025 spending
- Combined GLP-1 class: $132B = 14% of ALL US Rx spending
- GLP-1s = ~1/3 of all spending growth
- Doesn't include DTC sales — true impact larger
- "We are still on the steep part of the curve" — Eric Tichy, PharmD, Mayo Clinic

### Obesity Trends
- US adult obesity rate: peaked 39.9% in 2022, fallen to 36.4% in 2026
- Diabetes rate held steady (no longer climbing after 15 years of increases)
- CDC: 40% of Americans are obese (potential eligible population ~130M)

### Medicare Bridge Program (launched July 2026)
- $50/month copay (not covered by Part D deductible or $2,100 annual cap)
- 18-month demonstration → total OOP: $900
- Extra Help beneficiaries excluded → lowest-income seniors face steepest barrier
- 1 in 4 Medicare beneficiaries had income below $24,600 in 2024

### Insurance Coverage Pressure
- ICER found GLP-1s ARE cost-effective (health benefits justify price)
- BUT ICER budget impact threshold: $821 million
- GLP-1s at $132B blow past that by 160x
- "Cost-effective" ≠ "cost-saving" for insurers (U. Mississippi research)
- "The patient population eligible is so large that even if medications represent good value, society can't take the budget impact" — Sujith Ramachandran

### Clinical Value
- FLOW trial (NEJM): Semaglutide reduced primary kidney outcome events by 24% (HR 0.76), cardiovascular death by 29% (HR 0.71), all-cause death by 20% (HR 0.80) in T2D+CKD patients
- SELECT trial: 20% reduction in major cardiovascular events
- Wegovy now approved for MASH (liver disease), cardiovascular risk reduction, sleep apnea
- Pipeline: retatrutide ("triple G"), oral formulations, potential Alzheimer's/addiction applications

### New Drugs Entering
- Oral Wegovy (approved Dec 2025) — first oral GLP-1 for obesity
- Orforglipron/Foundayo (Eli Lilly, approved April 2026) — another oral option
- Retatrutide ("triple G") — expected FDA submission 2026, potentially larger weight loss
