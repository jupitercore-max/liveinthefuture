# Research: New York's Data Center Moratorium — The Cost of Saying No to AI Infrastructure

## Thesis
New York just became the first US state to impose a moratorium on hyperscale data center construction (July 14, 2026). Governor Hochul's executive order pauses permits for 50+ MW facilities for one year. The original analysis: by cross-referencing pipeline data from Virginia, Texas, and Georgia against New York's moratorium threshold, we can calculate the exact dollar value of AI infrastructure investment New York is declining — and what happens if the moratorium pattern spreads to the 5-10 most likely follower states.

## Category
⚡ Energy / 🏙️ Urban

## Primary Sources

### NY Moratorium (July 14, 2026)
- Governor Hochul executive order: 1-year moratorium on hyperscale data centers using 50+ MW
- First statewide moratorium in US history
- Covers facilities whose state environmental permits haven't been deemed complete
- Dept. of Public Service to create environmental impact statement: energy demand, water use, air pollution, noise, disproportionate effects on disadvantaged communities
- Hochul also pursuing repeal of sales tax exemptions for large data centers
- NY currently has ~130 data centers vs 600+ in Virginia, ~500 in Texas
- Source: Scientific American, Reuters, NYPost, Governor's office press release

### Data Center Investment Context
- 50 MW ≈ enough electricity for ~50,000 US homes (EIA data)
- Only 1 in 3 Americans approve fast pace of data center construction (Reuters/Ipsos poll)
- Most Americans oppose building one in their community
- DSA chapters in Seattle, Portland organizing against data centers
- Global: now 69+ restrictions on data centers from Ireland to Australia

### Grid Stress
- PJM (67M people): set all-time demand record 168.2 GW on July 2, 2026
- PJM capacity auction short by 7 GW of reserve margin target
- PJM warned "data center load growth outpacing new electricity supply, degrading reliability"
- Spot prices swung from $30 to $300/MWh in hours during July heatwave
- UK grid breached safety limits during June heatwave — first summer grid emergency since 2008

### US Emissions Paradox
- US accounted for 47% of global increase in carbon emissions in 2025 (Energy Institute 2026 Statistical Review)
- Despite 28% surge in domestic solar capacity
- US coal-related emissions jumped 13%
- US = 40% of global data center electricity consumption
- Primary driver: AI data center expansion

### The Political Economy
- Empire AI: Hochul announced in 2024 a "state-of-the-art AI computing center in Upstate NY" to make state "national leader in AI"
- Two years later, moratorium
- Partnership for the City of NY (Steve Fulop, CEO): "sends a signal about our competitiveness"
- Manhattan Institute analyst: "NY is really shooting itself in the foot"
- DSA organizing declared "one of the biggest political opportunities I've ever seen" (Astra Taylor, June 21 DSA Ecosocialism event)

## Original Analysis: The Moratorium Math

### What NY is declining (calculation)
- Average hyperscale data center: 100-300 MW capacity
- Typical hyperscale CapEx: $10-15M per MW (McKinsey/JLL estimates)
- At 200 MW average: $2B-$3B per facility
- NY had ~5-8 hyperscale facilities in active permitting/planning pipeline (based on industry tracker data)
- Total frozen investment: ~$10B-$24B
- Construction jobs per facility: ~2,000-3,000 temporary
- Permanent operations jobs per facility: ~50-100
- Tax revenue per facility: ~$5-10M/year in property taxes + $20-50M in construction taxes

### Where the investment goes instead
- Virginia (Data Center Alley): has 600+ centers, adding 15-20 hyperscale/year
- Texas: ~500 centers, no moratorium, ERCOT adding capacity
- Georgia: aggressive incentives, already landing TeraWulf-style deals
- Ohio: nuclear power restart for data centers
- Former industrial sites (Kentucky, Tennessee): brownfield pivots

### The Domino Risk (Novel)
States most likely to follow NY:
1. Oregon (Portland DSA active, water concerns)
2. Washington (Seattle DSA, Quincy water table)
3. Connecticut (grid stress, residential ratepayer politics)
4. Massachusetts (Cape Wind precedent, progressive governance)
5. California (water, CEQA, ratepayer advocacy)

If top 5 moratorium-risk states freeze 50+ MW permits: 
- Estimated 30-50 planned hyperscale facilities affected
- $60B-$150B in total frozen investment
- Concentration of AI infrastructure in 3-4 "permissive" states creates supply chain, grid, and national security risks

### The Paradox: Saying "No" Doesn't Reduce Demand
- AI compute demand doubles every 6-9 months
- A moratorium doesn't reduce demand — it displaces it
- Result: MORE concentrated power draw in fewer locations (Virginia, Texas)
- Those locations hit grid constraints FASTER
- Net effect on national grid: worse, not better
- Analogy: housing NIMBYism didn't reduce housing demand; it created homelessness

### The Strongest Counterargument
NY's moratorium might actually be smart policy:
- Forces the industry to develop efficiency standards BEFORE building
- Creates a model framework that other states can adopt to PERMIT data centers with conditions
- Without standards, the default is unregulated growth (see: xAI's unpermitted turbines in Mississippi)
- The 1-year pause costs far less than decades of unplanned grid burden
- EU precedent: Ireland paused data center approvals in 2022, then resumed with stronger rules
