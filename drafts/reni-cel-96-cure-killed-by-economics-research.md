# Research: The 96% Cure That Died in a Boardroom

## Slug: reni-cel-96-cure-killed-by-economics
## Journalist: Dr. Iris Blackwell
## Category: 🧬 Genomics & Clinical Research
## Date: 2026-07-17

## Thesis
Editas Medicine's reni-cel—a CRISPR-Cas12a gene therapy for sickle cell disease—achieved a 96% functional cure rate in its RUBY trial (27/28 patients had zero vaso-occlusive events), then the company killed the program in December 2024 because it couldn't find a commercial partner. The NEJM published the results in April 2026: a scientific obituary for a therapy that worked. Meanwhile, the competing Cas9-based therapy Casgevy (exa-cel) by Vertex/CRISPR Therapeutics achieved nearly identical efficacy (97% VOC-free in Phase 3) and already has FDA approval. The paradox: a $500M investment in reni-cel development could have unlocked billions in societal savings for a disease that costs the U.S. $2.98 billion per year. But Editas had $270M in cash and no partner willing to share the risk.

## Primary Sources

### 1. NEJM: RUBY Trial (Reni-cel, SCD)
- **Citation:** Hanna R, et al. "CRISPR-Cas12a Gene Editing of HBG1 and HBG2 Promoters to Treat Sickle Cell Disease." NEJM, April 1, 2026. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2415550
- Phase 1-2, multicenter, open-label, single-group study
- 28 patients, ages 12-50, with severe SCD (≥2 severe VOCs/year for 2 years)
- Single infusion of reni-cel after myeloablative busulfan conditioning
- Median follow-up: 9.5 months (range 0.7-25.2)
- **Results:**
  - HbF baseline: 2.5±2.5% → 48.1±3.2% at month 6 (maintained or higher thereafter)
  - Total Hb: 9.8±1.7 → 13.8±1.9 g/dL at month 6 (normalization)
  - 27/28 patients (96.4%) had ZERO vaso-occlusive events after infusion
  - 1 patient had 2 severe VOCs after infusion
  - Neutrophil engraftment: median 23 days (range 14-29)
  - Platelet engraftment: median 25 days (range 17-51)
  - No serious AEs related to reni-cel
  - **Study terminated early by sponsor ("reassessment of clinical development priorities")**
  - "Results of an analysis that was not prespecified are reported"

### 2. NEJM: EdiTHAL Trial (Reni-cel, beta-thalassemia)
- **Citation:** Frangoul H, et al. "CRISPR-Cas12a Gene Editing of HBG1 and HBG2 Promoters to Treat β-Thalassemia." NEJM, April 2026. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2501277
- Patients with transfusion-dependent beta-thalassemia
- Durable engraftment, high HbF levels, elimination of transfusion need
- No serious AEs related to reni-cel, no deaths
- Study also terminated early by sponsor

### 3. NEJM: CLIMB SCD-121 Phase 3 (Exa-cel/Casgevy, SCD)
- **Citation:** NEJM, 2024. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2501277 (different article)
- 44 patients, median follow-up 19.3 months
- 29/30 evaluable (97%) free from VOC for ≥12 consecutive months
- 30/30 (100%) free from hospitalization for VOC
- CRISPR-Cas9, targets BCL11A enhancer (different mechanism from reni-cel)
- FDA approved as Casgevy: December 2023 for ages 12+; July 2025 for children ages 2-11

### 4. Editas Medicine Corporate Actions
- December 2024: Terminated reni-cel clinical development
- 65% workforce reduction (~180 positions)
- Restructuring charges: $26.1M (Q2 2025) + $53.1M total estimated
- Cash at end of 2024: ~$270M
- Couldn't find commercial partner for reni-cel
- CEO Gilmore O'Neill: "Recent scientific breakthroughs...convinced us that the timelines around the near-term viability of in vivo CRISPR-edited medicines have accelerated meaningfully"
- Pivoted to in vivo gene editing platform (LNP delivery to HSCs)
- Cash runway: into Q3 2027
- Stock price: ~$2.20 (as of March 2026 analysis)
- Lead remaining candidate: EDIT-401 (in vivo), clinical proof-of-concept targeted late 2026

### 5. SCD Economic Burden (U.S.)
- **Source:** Udeze C, et al. Advances in Therapy, 2023. 40(8):3543-3558
- ~100,000 Americans with SCD
- Annual healthcare costs for patients with recurrent VOCs: $67,282 (vs $4,134 for matched controls)
- Lifetime costs (to age 50): $3.8M vs $229K for controls
- Average cost per VOC: $9,868
- Mean 5.0 VOCs per year for patients with recurrent VOCs
- 71% of costs are VOC-related ($47,663/year)
- Total U.S. annual burden: ~$2.98 billion
- 2.7 inpatient admissions/year, 5.0 ED visits/year

### 6. Casgevy Pricing
- List price: ~$2.2 million per one-time treatment
- FDA approved for SCD and transfusion-dependent beta-thalassemia

## Original Calculation: The Break-Even Math on an Orphaned Cure

### The question: How many patients would Editas have needed to treat to justify completing reni-cel development?

**Assumptions:**
- Remaining development cost (Phase 3 + manufacturing scale-up + FDA submission): $300-500M
  - Phase 3 SCD trial: ~$150-200M (based on similar gene therapy Phase 3 costs)
  - Manufacturing facility/scale-up: ~$100-150M
  - Regulatory submission + post-market: ~$50-100M
  - Conservative estimate: $400M
- Treatment price (matching Casgevy): $2.2M per patient
- Manufacturing COGS for ex vivo cell therapy: ~$500K-800K per patient
  - Apheresis, CD34+ cell isolation, CRISPR editing, quality testing, cryopreservation, shipping
  - Estimate: $650K (midpoint)
- Gross margin per patient: $2.2M - $650K = $1.55M

**Break-even calculation:**
- $400M development cost ÷ $1.55M gross margin/patient = **258 patients to break even**
- U.S. eligible patient population (severe SCD, ≥2 VOCs/year, ages 12-50): estimated 16,000-25,000
- Even at 258 patients, that's just 1-1.6% of the eligible population
- At peak annual treatment capacity of 200-500 patients/year:
  - Year 1-2: ~200 patients → $310M gross → nearly pays off development
  - Year 3+: pure profit at $1.55M/patient

**Societal math:**
- Cost of NOT curing: $67,282/year × 50 remaining years = $3.36M per patient in healthcare costs
- Cost of curing: $2.2M one-time treatment
- Net savings per patient: $3.36M - $2.2M = **$1.16M**
- If 10,000 patients treated over the therapy's lifetime: **$11.6 billion in net societal savings**
- The $400M development cost represents 3.4% of the societal savings it would unlock

**The absurdity:**
- Editas's market cap when it killed reni-cel: ~$185M (below the cost of a single Phase 3 trial)
- The therapy's societal value exceeds the company's entire valuation by 60x
- A $400M investment with a potential $11.6B societal return = 29:1 benefit-cost ratio
- But the return accrues to the healthcare system, not to Editas's balance sheet (at least not all of it)

### The Cas12a vs Cas9 Comparison

| Parameter | Reni-cel (Cas12a) | Exa-cel/Casgevy (Cas9) |
|-----------|-------------------|------------------------|
| Enzyme | AsCas12a (Acidaminococcus) | SpCas9 (Streptococcus pyogenes) |
| Target | HBG1 + HBG2 promoters (BCL11A binding sites) | BCL11A erythroid enhancer |
| Mechanism | Disrupts repressor binding → HbF reactivation | Disrupts enhancer → reduces BCL11A → HbF reactivation |
| PAM sequence | TTTV (T-rich, 5') | NGG (G-rich, 3') |
| Guide RNA | Single crRNA (self-processing) | Dual (crRNA + tracrRNA) or sgRNA |
| Edit sites | 2 (dual-promoter editing) | 1 |
| HbF at 6 months | 48.1% (SCD trial) | ~40-49.5% (varies by trial) |
| VOC-free rate | 27/28 (96.4%) | 29/30 (96.7%) evaluable |
| Trial phase | Phase 1-2 | Phase 3 |
| FDA status | Terminated | Approved (Dec 2023) |
| Patients treated | 28 (SCD) | 44 (SCD Phase 3) + 11 children |
| Follow-up | 9.5 months median | 19.3 months median |

**Key finding:** Clinically equivalent outcomes, but reni-cel was orphaned at Phase 1-2 while exa-cel completed Phase 3 and reached market. The molecular tools are different; the results are interchangeable.

## Limitations
- Reni-cel data are Phase 1-2 with shorter follow-up (9.5 vs 19.3 months); long-term durability unknown
- Break-even calculation assumes Casgevy-equivalent pricing, which itself faces access challenges
- COGS estimate for ex vivo cell therapy is approximate; actual manufacturing costs vary
- Eligible patient population estimate is rough—precise epidemiological data on severe SCD with recurrent VOCs is limited
- Societal savings calculation uses healthcare costs only; does not include lost productivity, caregiver burden, or quality-of-life improvements
- Editas may have had additional strategic reasons for termination beyond the stated ones (e.g., IP concerns, manufacturing feasibility)
