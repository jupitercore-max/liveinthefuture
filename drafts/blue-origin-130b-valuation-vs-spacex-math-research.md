# Research Notes: Blue Origin $130B Valuation Analysis

## Story Angle
Blue Origin seeks first outside funding at $130B pre-money valuation. Original calculation: cost per orbital launch achieved ($28B/3 = $9.3B each) vs SpaceX's investment efficiency. Analysis of what the market is actually pricing.

## Key Data Points

### Blue Origin
- Founded: September 2000 by Jeff Bezos
- Seeking: $10B at $130B pre-money valuation (July 8, 2026)
- Lead investor: Coatue Management ($4B), Bezos contributing $2B more
- First outside funding in 26-year history
- Employees: ~11,000 (PitchBook 2026); had 14,000 pre-Feb 2025 layoffs (10%)
- Estimated revenue: ~$2.8B (RocketReach 2026 estimate)
- Total Bezos investment: ~$28B since founding (FT estimates; Bezos started at $1B/yr Amazon stock sales in 2017)
- Annual burn rate: ~$5B (FT)
- New Glenn stats:
  - 3 total orbital launches (first: Jan 16, 2025)
  - 2 successes, 1 upper-stage failure (April 2026, lost AST SpaceMobile satellite)
  - 1 pad explosion during static fire test (May 2026)
  - Cost per launch: $68-110M
  - LEO capacity: 45,000 kg
  - Only operational pad (LC-36) destroyed in May explosion
  - Expects to fly again "before end of year"
- No recurring revenue product (no Starlink equivalent)
- Key contracts:
  - NASA Artemis HLS (Blue Moon): $3.4B + Blue Origin investing "well north" of contract value (~$7B total)
  - Amazon Leo/Kuiper: 12 launches + option for 15 more
  - NSSL Phase 3 Lane 2: 7 launches out of 54 total (60% SpaceX, 40% ULA, BO gets 7)
  - NASA rover delivery: $188M
  - NASA commercial station: $130M

### SpaceX
- Founded: 2002
- IPO: June 12, 2026 at $1.75T valuation (now ~$2T+)
- Revenue: $18.67B in 2025 (+33% YoY)
  - Starlink: $11.4B (61%), $4.4B operating profit
  - Space: $4.1B
- EBITDA: $6.6B (net loss $4.94B GAAP)
- Pre-IPO total funding raised: ~$12B
- Launches: 170 in 2025 (96 in 2023, 134 in 2024)
- Starlink: 10.3M subscribers, ~9,600 satellites
- Current stock: ~$149 (down from $225 peak)
- 100,000 Gen3 Starlink satellites applied to FCC

### Rocket Lab
- Market cap: ~$50B
- Revenue: $601.8M FY 2025 (+38% YoY)
- Net loss: $198.2M
- Acquiring Iridium for ~$8B
- ~50+ Electron launches

## Original Calculations

1. Investment per orbital launch:
   - Blue Origin: $28B / 3 launches = $9.33B per launch
   - SpaceX: $12B raised / ~600 cumulative launches (2010-2025) = $20M per launch
   - Ratio: Blue Origin spent 467x more per launch than SpaceX

2. Valuation/revenue multiple:
   - SpaceX: $2T / $18.7B = 107x trailing
   - Blue Origin: $130B / $2.8B = 46x trailing
   - Rocket Lab: $50B / $602M = 83x trailing
   - Blue Origin's multiple looks reasonable on surface — but its revenue is mostly one-time contracts, not recurring

3. Bezos ROI vs S&P 500:
   - $28B invested 2000-2026 → $130B = 4.64x total return
   - Annualized over 26 years: ~6.2% CAGR
   - S&P 500 Jan 2000 to Jul 2026: roughly 4.3x total return (with dividends ~6x)
   - Blue Origin has underperformed a passive index fund

4. Revenue needed to justify:
   - At SpaceX's EV/Revenue of 107x: Blue Origin needs $1.2B revenue (has it)
   - But SpaceX's premium is Starlink (recurring, growing); Blue Origin has no equivalent
   - For Blue Origin to match SpaceX's revenue: needs to grow 6.7x to $18.7B
   - Amazon Leo: 3,236 satellites ÷ 48/launch = 67 launches minimum
   - At $68-110M/launch = $4.6-7.4B in total launch revenue (spread over multiple years)

5. Valuation per kg to orbit:
   - SpaceX: $2T / (170 launches × ~16,000 kg avg) = $735K per annual kg
   - Blue Origin: $130B / (target 12 launches × 45,000 kg) = $241K per annual kg
   - Seems favorable to BO — but BO's target launch rate is aspirational, not proven
   - At actual historical rate (2/year): $130B / (2 × 45,000) = $1.44M per annual kg
