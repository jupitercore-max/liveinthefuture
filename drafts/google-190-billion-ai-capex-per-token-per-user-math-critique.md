# 7-Critic Evaluation: Google's $190 Billion AI Capex Article (#348)

## 1. 🔍 General Editor — 9.0/10
**Strengths:** Strong structure with clear section headers. Opens with a concrete number (6x multiplier). Data-dense throughout with every paragraph earning its place. The narrative arc (overview → per-user → per-token → per-agent → energy → comparison → counterargument → limitations → actionable) is logical and builds effectively. The "nobody divided one by the other" deck is excellent editorial instinct.
**Weaknesses:** At ~2,550 words, slightly long. The Big Picture section could be tighter — the NASA comparison is vivid but the US R&D comparison is less impactful since it makes a similar point. Could trim 100 words there.
**Score: 9.0**

## 2. 🗣️ Voice Coach — 9.5/10

Voice is distinctive: analytical but not dry, authoritative without being preachy. **Score: 9.5**

## 3. ⚖️ Ethics Reviewer — 9.0/10
**Strengths:** No self-congratulation. No moral lecturing. The energy section presents facts without preaching about climate (a trap most publications fall into). The "strongest case for it" section is genuinely fair to Google. Limitations are honest and specific.
**Weaknesses:** The "lock-in strategy" language in the Spark section edges toward editorial opinion rather than analysis. Could be more precise about what "lock-in" means in consumer choice terms.
**Score: 9.0**

## 4. 📱 Social/Shareability — 9.5/10
**Strengths:** Headline is immediately shareable — contains a promise (math nobody ran), a concrete number ($190B), and a personal hook (per-user). The deck is excellent pull-quote material. "Six times. That's how quickly..." is a strong opening for social snippets. The $211/user/year figure will be widely cited. The Spark loss-leader analysis is the kind of original take that gets quoted in newsletters.
**Weaknesses:** No visual data (chart/infographic) beyond the table. A token growth chart would increase shareability.
**Score: 9.5**

## 5. ⚖️ Legal Accuracy — 8.5/10
**Strengths:** All financial figures sourced to Google's I/O keynote or TrendForce. Energy estimates properly cited to academic research. API pricing linked to official Google pricing page. NASA budget linked to Wikipedia (acceptable as a cross-reference for widely-known data).
**Weaknesses:** The $350B Google revenue figure for 2025 is an estimate ("approximately") — should note this is based on reported Q1-Q3 plus analyst estimates for Q4 since full-year 2025 would have been reported by now. The per-user revenue calculation ($65) could be challenged on methodology — "people who touch at least one Google product" is vague. The Gemini Flash API pricing uses "approximately" which is appropriate hedging.
**Score: 8.5**

## 6. 🔬 Research Rigor — 9.5/10
**Novel contribution:** Multiple. (1) The $211/user/year capex calculation. (2) The $5/million-token infrastructure floor. (3) The Spark VM loss-leader analysis with specific GCP pricing comparison. (4) The 55x efficiency-per-dollar calculation. (5) The energy footprint range estimate with country comparisons. (6) The "2x efficiency buys 4 months of headroom" observation.
**Limitations:** Explicitly stated and thorough — addresses all major analytical weaknesses.
**Strongest counterargument:** Present and stated at full strength (contracted revenue, capacity-constrained growth). Not strawmanned.
**Verifiability:** Most claims are traceable. I/O figures come from official transcript. Academic paper cited with link. API pricing linked.
**Score: 9.5**

## 7. 📊 Data Presentation — 8.5/10
**Table:** The capex comparison table is clean and useful. Column alignment good.
**Inline numbers:** Well-formatted. Use of "38.4 quadrillion" rather than scientific notation is appropriate for the audience.
**Missing:** A token growth chart (9.7T → 480T → 3.2Q) would be powerful. The energy range (38-115 TWh) could benefit from a visual comparison bar.
**So what test:** Every number has a human-scale anchor (Netflix plan, Philippines electricity, NASA budget). Strong.
**Score: 8.5**
