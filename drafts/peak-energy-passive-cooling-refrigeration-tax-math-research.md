# Research: Peak Energy Passive Cooling Refrigeration Tax Math

## Topic
Peak Energy opens America's first sodium-ion grid battery factory in Sacramento. The 20% cost claim gets all the headlines. But when you calculate what passive cooling actually saves over a 20-year battery lifecycle — upfront system costs PLUS eliminated HVAC operating expenses — the number is $88/kWh. That closes 60% of the $146/kWh US-China battery cost gap. From thermodynamics alone.

## Original Calculation

### The Upfront Savings
- US turnkey battery system cost: $219/kWh (BNEF 2024 Energy Storage System Cost Survey)
- China turnkey system cost: $73/kWh (BNEF 2024)
- Gap: $146/kWh
- Peak claims 20% cost reduction from passive cooling: $219 × 0.20 = $43.80/kWh

### The Operational Savings Nobody Calculated
- Peak claims: eliminating battery refrigeration in California alone saves ratepayers $100M/year
- California installed battery storage: ~11.4 GW (CAISO, early 2026)
- At average 4-hour duration: ~45.6 GWh
- Annual HVAC cost per kWh of installed capacity: $100M / 45,600,000 kWh = $2.19/kWh/year
- Over 20-year operational life: $2.19 × 20 = $43.86/kWh

### Combined Lifecycle Savings
- Upfront system cost reduction: $43.80/kWh
- Lifetime operational HVAC elimination: $43.86/kWh
- **Total: $87.66/kWh (~$88/kWh)**
- **As percentage of US-China gap: $88 / $146 = 60.3%**

### The Counterargument
CATL's TENER sodium-ion system already achieves 1% auxiliary consumption (vs 2% industry average). If China adopts passive cooling too, the relative gap stays. However: US HVAC costs are higher in absolute terms due to higher electricity prices ($0.12-0.18/kWh vs $0.08/kWh in China) and higher maintenance labor costs.
