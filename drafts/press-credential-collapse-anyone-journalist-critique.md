**Score: 9/10**

**Strengths:**
- The article avoids moralizing about the Mangionistas' speech content — it focuses on the structural credential question, not whether their views are abhorrent (though it quotes their words directly and lets readers draw their own conclusions)
- The strongest counterargument section engages honestly with the gatekeeping critique: "The gates were never protecting journalism. They were protecting incumbents."
- The self-implication of LITF's own Fourth Estate AI pitch is ethically rigorous — the article doesn't exempt itself from the trend it's diagnosing
- Ron Kuby's constitutional position is presented at full strength without being dismissed

**Concerns:**
- The article arguably under-engages with the possibility that credential dilution is net-positive for democracy — the counterargument section raises it but the article's overall posture treats dilution as loss. A stronger steelman would note that the pre-digital credentialing regime actively excluded marginalized voices and that the current mess, however chaotic, represents broader participation
- The CVS Health executive detail about Weissbrot's mother is included for its ironic value but could be read as ad hominem (daughter of healthcare exec cheers murder of healthcare CEO)

**No self-congratulation. No preachy tone. The article diagnoses without prescribing.**
