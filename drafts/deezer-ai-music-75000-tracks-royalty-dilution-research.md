## Novel Contribution
**Original calculation:** If Deezer's 44% AI upload ratio is even roughly representative of the wider industry, and Spotify receives ~100,000+ tracks/day (Music Business Worldwide), then ~44,000 AI tracks/day may be hitting Spotify alone. With Spotify paying $11B/year to rights holders (Spotify 2025 annual report), even 1% stream capture by undetected AI tracks = $110M/year diverted from human artists. At 3% (Deezer's detected rate before intervention), that's $330M/year. Neither Spotify, Apple Music, nor Amazon Music has disclosed their AI detection rates. The transparency gap IS the story.

## The Math (Novel Analysis)

### Royalty Pool Dilution Estimate
- Global music streaming revenue: ~$22B/year (IFPI 2024 estimate, 2025 likely $24B+)
- Spotify alone: $11B/year
- If AI tracks capture 1% of streams without detection: $220M-$240M/year diverted
- If AI tracks capture 3% (Deezer pre-intervention rate): $660M-$720M/year diverted
- CISAC projects €4B ($4.3B) at risk by 2028 — that implies ~18% penetration without intervention

### The Growth Curve Problem
- Deezer's 15-month growth: 10K → 75K AI tracks/day (650% increase)
- At current trajectory, AI uploads will exceed human uploads within 6-8 months
- If the same curve applies to Spotify (>100K tracks/day total), the absolute numbers dwarf Deezer's

### The Transparency Gap
- Deezer: full public disclosure, detection, tagging, demonetization
- Spotify: policy announced, zero volume disclosure
- Apple Music: no public AI policy or disclosure
- Amazon Music: no public AI policy or disclosure
- YouTube Music: no public AI policy or disclosure
- One platform out of five is transparent. That's the story.

## Strongest Counterargument
Deezer's numbers may overstate the problem. They're the smallest major DSP (~9M subscribers vs Spotify's 260M+). Their detection tech marketing gives them incentive to dramatize the threat. And the key fact — only 1-3% of streams go to AI content, even WITH 44% upload share — suggests listeners self-filter toward human music. Maybe the market solves this without intervention.

The counterargument has merit: upload volume ≠ consumption. But it ignores two things: (1) The fraud is real — Michael Smith stole $8M before getting caught, and he's one person DOJ prosecuted. How many haven't been? (2) "Detection works" assumes everyone detects. Only Deezer demonstrates that it does. The other four major platforms are flying blind, or at least refusing to show their instruments.

## Limitations
- We cannot verify Spotify/Apple Music AI upload ratios (they don't disclose)
- Our $110M-$330M dilution estimate extrapolates Deezer ratios to industry — Deezer's smaller scale and different user base may skew numbers
- The CISAC €4B figure is a projection with unpublished methodology
- "85% of AI streams are fraudulent" is Deezer's claim based on their detection tools — independent verification doesn't exist
- We don't know how much fraud existing platforms catch but don't report

## Actionable Insights
1. **For musicians:** Demand your DSP disclose AI content ratios and fraud detection rates. If Deezer can do it, Spotify can.
2. **For listeners:** Check if your streaming platform labels AI-generated content. Currently only Deezer does.
3. **For investors:** The CISAC €4B risk figure applies to the entire music rights ecosystem — evaluate label and publishing company exposure.
4. **For policymakers:** The EU AI Act requires labeling of AI-generated content. Enforcement starts August 2026. Music streaming is the first test case.
