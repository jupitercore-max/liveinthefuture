### Regulatory Comparison
- **FAA (US):** 5-stage type certification process. Joby has been in process ~9 years. Stage 4 completed March 2026. Final stage (Stage 5, Type Certificate issuance) pending. Treats eVTOLs under Part 21 with special conditions.
- **CAAC (China):** Issued EHang Type Certificate. Allowed commercial passenger service in April 2026. Faster regulatory path, fewer intermediate gates.
- **EASA (EU):** SC-VTOL (Special Condition for VTOL) framework. Generally tracking behind FAA.

### The Unit Economics Gap (Original Analysis)
- Joby lost $930M in 2025 with essentially zero revenue
- At 200 mph top speed, ~100 mile range per charge → ~30 min flight
- 4 passengers per flight
- If Joby achieves 10 flights/day/aircraft (optimistic): 40 passenger-trips per aircraft per day
- With 100 aircraft fleet: 4,000 passenger-trips per day → 1.46M per year
- $930M loss / 1.46M hypothetical trips = $637 subsidy per trip
- Even pricing rides at UberBlack levels (~$100 for a 30-mile trip), break-even requires ~$700/ride
- Helicopter equivalent (Blade NYC): ~$195 per seat for ~60 miles
- Standard Uber/Lyft: ~$2-4/passenger-mile
- eVTOL target: $3-6/passenger-mile at scale (company projections)
- Reality: At current burn rate, implicit cost is ~$15-25/passenger-mile

### Strongest Counterargument
The best case for the FAA's slow approach: autonomous aircraft carrying passengers are fundamentally different from ground vehicles. One failure kills everyone on board AND potentially people on the ground. The FAA's Part 23 certification process has kept US commercial aviation at near-zero fatal accident rates. China's faster path may come at a cost we haven't measured yet — EHang's autonomous 2-seater is not the same risk profile as Joby's 5-seat piloted aircraft. Speed of certification may be inversely correlated with long-term safety record.
