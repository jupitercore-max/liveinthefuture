# Research: CCA Attrition Economics — The $25M Fighter Designed to Die

## Angle
The US Air Force is building its first fleet of combat aircraft explicitly designed for affordable attrition — jets the Pentagon expects to lose in battle. The numbers reveal this might be the most consequential force structure decision in a generation.

**Original calculation**: The "Attrition Exchange Ratio" — a cost-benefit framework comparing CCA losses vs manned aircraft losses in a peer conflict, using CSIS wargame data and real cost figures.

## Original Calculation: Attrition Exchange Ratio

### Hardware Exchange Ratio
- F-35A unit cost: $82.5M (Lot 17) to ~$101M (latest lots with engine)
- CCA unit cost: <$25M (beating one-third target)
- **Pure hardware ratio: 3.3:1 to 4:1** — you can lose 3-4 CCAs for every F-35

### Total Cost Including Pilot Training
- F-35 pilot training: ~$11M (GAO estimate for fighter pilot development through operational readiness)
- CCA pilot training: $0 (no onboard pilot; ground operators manage multiple CCAs)
- **Adjusted ratio: ($82.5M + $11M) / $25M = 3.74:1**
- And that doesn't count the pilot's life — which is literally invaluable

### Weapon Station Economics  
- F-35 internal weapons load (air superiority): 4 weapons (2× AIM-120 AMRAAM + 2× AIM-9X or SDB)
- CCA estimated weapons load: 2 weapons (confirmed AIM-120 captive carry tests by Anduril)
- **Cost per weapon station in contested air**:
  - F-35: $82.5M / 4 = $20.6M per weapon
  - CCA: $25M / 2 = $12.5M per weapon (39% cheaper)
  - CCA Increment 2 ($15M): $15M / 2 = $7.5M per weapon (64% cheaper)

### CSIS Wargame Applied
Using base scenario midpoint: US loses ~270 aircraft
- **Without CCAs**: 270 × $82.5M = $22.3B hardware + 270 × $11M pilot training = $3.0B = **$25.3B + ~270 pilot casualties**
- **With 30% CCA mix** (CCAs absorb disproportionate 50% of losses due to being sent on highest-risk missions):
  - 135 manned aircraft lost × $82.5M = $11.1B + 135 × $11M = $1.5B = $12.6B
  - 135 CCAs lost × $25M = $3.4B
  - **Total: $16.0B + ~135 pilot casualties**
  - **Savings: $9.3B in hardware/training + ~135 pilot lives preserved**

### Force Generation: CCAs vs Pilot Pipeline
- Current fighter pilot shortage: 1,142 pilots
- Time to train one fighter pilot through operational readiness: 3-4 years
- Time to build one CCA: months (production line, no pilot training needed)
- 150 CCAs = 300 weapon stations added to the force without training a single new pilot
- Equivalent F-35 force (300 weapon stations = 75 F-35s) would require 75 fighter pilots the USAF doesn't have
