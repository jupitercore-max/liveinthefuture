Sodium-Ion Costs $0.007 Per Stored kWh-Cycle. LFP Costs $0.015. Iron-Air Costs $0.003. The Grid Storage Market Just Split Into Three.

Three battery chemistry milestones landed in the same week of June 2026. A cost-per-cycle analysis with current pricing reveals the grid storage market is fragmenting into three duration bands, each served by a different chemistry. The $/kWh headline number has been hiding the real economics.

By Anya Volkov · Energy Systems · June 23, 2026 · ☕ 11 min read

The number 59 keeps showing up in energy storage pitch decks, slide after slide, quarter after quarter, as though it settles an argument that has barely begun. That is the current average cell cost of sodium-ion batteries in dollars per kilowatt-hour, according to market data compiled across multiple industry sources this month. Lithium iron phosphate sits at $52. Standard interpretation: LFP is still cheaper, sodium needs more scale to compete. Wrong metric.

Between June 20 and June 23, three announcements landed in quick succession, each from a different chemistry, each signaling that the grid storage market has quietly fractured into separate races that most analysts are still covering as one. CATL unveiled the TENER Sodium, the world's first field-validated sodium-ion battery energy storage system, at an event in Munich. Dutch startup Ore Energy signed a 1 GWh iron-air battery deal with energy supplier Budget Thuis, the largest iron-air storage agreement in continental Europe. And Form Energy's CEO stood on stage with the Prince of Wales to announce plans for "dozens" of iron-air facilities in the UK.

The Metric That Actually Matters

Cell cost per kilowatt-hour tells you what a battery costs when it is new, which is useful for procurement but useless for predicting what a utility will actually pay to store and retrieve energy over the two decades a grid asset is expected to operate. For that, you need cost per stored kWh-cycle: the cell cost divided by the number of charge-discharge cycles before the battery degrades past usefulness.

Here is what that calculation looks like with current June 2026 data:

Chemistry Cell Cost ($/kWh) Cycle Life Cost Per kWh-Cycle ($)
Sodium-ion $59 8,000 $0.00738
LFP $52 3,500 $0.01486
Iron-air $20 7,300 $0.00274

The sodium-ion cycle life comes from Zhongke Haina's testing with FAW Jiefang on heavy-duty trucks, where their 339 kWh sodium-ion pack demonstrated over 8,000 cycles under fast-charging conditions, with each full charge taking only 20 to 25 minutes. BYD claims its sodium chemistry exceeds 10,000 cycles, though that figure has not been independently verified. The LFP figure uses the midpoint of real-world grid-storage cycle life, which ranges from 2,000 to 4,000 cycles depending on depth of discharge, temperature management, and C-rate, per a peer-reviewed MDPI assessment of retired CALB cells and a Eureka Patsnap comparison of lithium phosphate longevity data; lab-optimized LFP cells have reached 8,000 full equivalent cycles, but typical commercial deployment falls well short of that ceiling because field conditions impose stresses that controlled lab environments do not reproduce. Iron-air's 7,300 cycles assumes one cycle per day across Form Energy's projected 20-plus-year lifespan, with less than 10% capacity degradation.

Sodium-ion is 50% cheaper per cycle than LFP. Not 13% more expensive, as the cell-cost headline suggests; the ranking inverts completely.

Efficiency Tax

Cost per cycle is half the story, because round-trip efficiency determines how much energy you actually recover for every kilowatt-hour you charge. Sodium-ion and LFP both operate around 90% round-trip efficiency. Iron-air sits between 45% and 60%, with most real-world estimates clustering around 52%, meaning nearly half the energy dissipates as heat during the reversible rusting process that is the chemistry's central trick and its central limitation.

Adjusting for efficiency by dividing cost-per-cycle by round-trip efficiency:

Chemistry Raw $/kWh-cycle Round-Trip Efficiency Efficiency-Adjusted $/kWh-cycle
Sodium-ion $0.00738 ~90% $0.0082
LFP $0.01486 ~90% $0.0165
Iron-air $0.00274 ~52% $0.0053

Even after the efficiency penalty, iron-air remains the cheapest option per cycle, and sodium-ion holds a 2x cost advantage over LFP that the efficiency tax cannot close because both chemistries share effectively identical round-trip performance; the efficiency gap reshuffles the magnitudes without changing the ranking.

CATL's Sodium System by the Numbers

CATL's TENER Sodium is not just a battery chemistry but a full station-level platform engineered specifically around the electrochemical characteristics that make sodium-ion cells behave differently from lithium-ion across their state-of-charge curve. Each system delivers over 30 MWh of rated capacity per module, and 34 modules fill a 1 GWh site. Two engineering decisions in the platform design illustrate where sodium's economics gain an additional edge that cell-level comparisons miss.

Sodium-ion cells have a wider voltage range than lithium-ion cells during discharge, which means the power conversion system must handle a broader operating window or sacrifice efficiency at the state-of-charge extremes where most conventional designs lose performance. CATL built a dedicated bidirectional DC voltage regulation system that automatically boosts output in the low-voltage range. This keeps the PCS operating at optimal efficiency across the entire discharge curve, improving round-trip efficiency by roughly 2 percentage points. For a 1 GWh site cycling once daily, that 2% translates to 20 MWh per day, or 7,300 MWh per year. At European wholesale electricity prices around €60 per megawatt-hour, that is approximately €438,000 per year. Free money from better power electronics.

The second gain comes from thermal engineering, where CATL's top-discharge airflow architecture eliminates thermal islands at the source, reducing heat generation by nearly 30% compared to conventional battery enclosure designs and cutting auxiliary power consumption from an industry average of 2% to just 1%, which over a year at the gigawatt-hour scale saves roughly 3,650 MWh, another €219,000 at the same wholesale rate. Together, these system-level improvements deliver approximately €657,000 annually in value that does not show up in any cell-cost comparison, and they compound over a 20-year asset life into the tens of millions.

The TENER Sodium also operates at 65 decibels, 10 decibels below conventional battery systems. Siting a battery storage facility closer to load centers saves transmission and distribution costs. CATL says the platform is compatible with LFP systems and shares the same physical footprint, meaning operators can switch between sodium and lithium chemistries without redesigning projects or repeating certification. Cumulative sodium-ion shipments are expected to reach 1 GWh by end of 2026, with global deliveries starting June 2027.

Lithium's Volatility Premium

Cost-per-cycle calculations assume stable input costs, but lithium's have been anything but. Battery-grade lithium carbonate surged 95% in less than two months at the start of this year, jumping from approximately $13,433 per metric ton in early December 2025 to $26,278 by late January 2026, according to Nasdaq's Q1 2026 market update. That surge was driven by production delays at CATL's own Jianxiawo lepidolite mine, maintenance outages at competing operations, and speculative buying on thin spot liquidity.

As of early June, lithium carbonate has stabilized around $24,840 to $25,200 per ton, up 14.2% year over year. A 2025 surplus of 141,000 tonnes of lithium carbonate equivalent is projected to narrow to roughly 109,000 tonnes by end of 2026. UBS and Morgan Stanley have warned of a potential 80,000-tonne deficit if commissioning timelines for African and Australian mining projects slip further.

Sodium does not have this problem, given that it is over 1,000 times more abundant than lithium in the Earth's crust and is widely distributed across every continent. Raw material for sodium-ion cathodes is effectively soda ash, the same substance used to make glass and laundry detergent, with no single-country supply chain chokepoint. For grid operators signing 20-year power purchase agreements backed by battery storage, the implicit volatility premium on lithium-based chemistry is a cost that cell-price comparisons pretend does not exist.

Where Each Chemistry Wins

The market is not converging on one winner. It is stratifying into three duration bands, each served by the chemistry whose cost-per-cycle economics are strongest for that use case.

0 to 4 hours: sodium-ion. Daily solar arbitrage, frequency regulation, peak shaving on fast cycles. Sodium-ion's combination of lowest cost-per-cycle among high-efficiency chemistries, extreme cold tolerance (Zhongke Haina's FAW test retained over 90% capacity at minus 40 degrees Celsius), and supply chain security make it the natural fit for applications that cycle every day and need to operate in any climate. CATL's Naxtra sodium-ion cells are already shipping in passenger EVs, including the Changan Nevo A06. A German teardown of a Chinese sodium-ion cell published this week found manufacturing quality on par with Tesla's best lithium cells, with only 5.3% resistance spread across 120 tested cells.

4 to 8 hours: LFP, but threatened. Peak shaving at the multi-hour scale, solar-plus-storage co-location, evening demand coverage. LFP remains dominant here because of massive installed manufacturing capacity and a decades-long reliability track record, but its position is eroding as sodium-ion energy density has reached 175 Wh/kg (CATL's Naxtra targeting this level), closing the gap with LFP's 160 to 180 Wh/kg range. If sodium-ion manufacturing scales to match LFP's supply chain depth, LFP's advantage in this band reduces to inertia.

24 to 100 hours: iron-air. Multi-day renewable bridging, seasonal smoothing, grid resilience during extended weather events. No other chemistry can economically store electricity for four days. Form Energy's iron-air batteries use iron, water, and air. The discharge process is literally rusting: iron reacts with oxygen, releasing electrons. Charging reverses the reaction, converting iron oxide back to iron. Iron-air delivers stored energy at roughly $20 per kilowatt-hour, compared to about $125 per kWh for grid-scale lithium systems, according to Form Energy's data shared with The Times. Ore Energy's 1 GWh Netherlands deal starts with a committed 400 MWh first phase delivering in 2028, scaling to the full gigawatt-hour over subsequent phases.

Limitations

This analysis uses cell-level costs. System-level costs (battery management systems, cooling infrastructure, housing, inverters, and grid interconnection) add 30% to 60% on top and vary by chemistry. Sodium-ion's lower energy density means larger physical footprints, adding land and enclosure costs that our cell-level comparison does not capture. Iron-air's lower power density (50 to 100 W/L versus 250 to 500 W/L for lithium-ion) limits its use in applications requiring rapid grid response. That 8,000-cycle figure for sodium-ion comes from manufacturer testing, not independent grid-storage deployments over multiple years. Form Energy's $20/kWh target has not been demonstrated at commercial scale. And round-trip efficiency for iron-air has a wide range (45% to 60%) that can shift the economics meaningfully depending on which end of the band a given installation lands on.

Strongest Case Against

LFP manufacturers are iterating rapidly. CATL, BYD, and EVE Energy are all pursuing next-generation LFP formulations that target 10,000-plus cycles with improved energy density. If LFP reaches $40 per kilowatt-hour at 6,000 cycles, its cost-per-cycle drops to $0.0067, undercutting current sodium-ion pricing. LFP has the deepest manufacturing ecosystem on Earth, the longest operational track record in grid storage, and decades of incremental optimization ahead of it. It also benefits from established recycling pathways that sodium-ion lacks entirely. The chemistry with the most manufacturing scale and the most sunk capital may simply iterate past its challengers before they can build equivalent supply chains. Samsung SDI, for instance, is pursuing lithium manganese rich cells that promise LFP-like costs at higher energy density. The three-chemistry future may be a transitional phase, not an equilibrium.

Bottom Line

The grid storage market is no longer one race with one finish line. It is three races on three tracks. For daily cycling, sodium-ion delivers the cheapest stored energy among high-efficiency chemistries, at half the cost-per-cycle of the lithium chemistry it is replacing. For multi-day storage, iron-air batteries built from iron, water, and air offer costs so low that even 48% energy losses cannot overcome their economic advantage. LFP occupies the middle band and dominates today on installed scale, but faces pressure from both sides.

If you manage grid-scale storage procurement: stop comparing cell costs. Start comparing cost-per-stored-kWh-cycle, adjusted for efficiency and lithium supply chain volatility. If you run a utility: plan for a mixed-chemistry fleet. A sodium-ion system for daily arbitrage, an LFP bank for multi-hour peak shaving, and an iron-air installation for weather-gap resilience is a portfolio, not a compromise. The single-chemistry era of grid storage ended the same week three companies proved that three different ways of storing electrons are all reaching commercial readiness at once. The question is no longer which chemistry wins. It is which chemistry wins for what.
