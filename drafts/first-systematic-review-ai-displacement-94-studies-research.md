## Novel Contribution
1. **Absolute worker impact calculation**: Frontiers study reports 14-41% (median 23%) decline in entry/mid-level software dev postings. BLS reports 1,897,100 software dev/QA/tester jobs (2023). If ~50% are entry/mid-level (~949K), a 23% decline = ~218,000 fewer job openings. At the high end (41%), that's ~389,000 positions.
2. **Dollar value of the AI augmentation premium**: 15-22% wage premium on median $133,080 salary = $19,962-$29,278/year per worker. For those who adapt, the reward is significant. For those who don't, the floor drops.
3. **Prediction vs. observation gap**: Frey & Osborne (2017) predicted 47% of US jobs "at high risk." Nine years later, this systematic review finds actual measurable displacement concentrated in a much narrower band — specifically entry-level cognitive tasks. The displacement is real but more surgical than the apocalyptic predictions suggested.
4. **Platform evidence as leading indicator**: Upwork Q1 2026 — 24% workforce layoff, 1% revenue growth, AI-related contracts actually declining. Fiverr stock down 35% YTD. These are the canaries in the coal mine because freelance platforms are where displacement shows up first (no employment contracts to buffer the transition).

## Primary Sources (3+)

### Source 1: Frontiers in Human Dynamics (Primary)
- **Paper**: Dehouche, N. (2026). "Creation, validation, obsolescence: observed evidence of AI-driven labor market displacement, 2020–2025." *Frontiers in Human Dynamics*, 8.
- **DOI**: 10.3389/fhumd.2026.1815037
- **Published**: May 7, 2026
- **Method**: PRISMA 2020 systematic review. Searched 6 databases (Scopus, Web of Science, EconLit, SSRN, IEEE Xplore, Google Scholar). 1,847 initial records → 94 included in qualitative synthesis, 42 in quantitative extraction.
- **Key findings**:
  - 14-41% reduction in entry/mid-level software dev and content creation job postings in high-income economies (2022-2024). Median: -23%.
  - 15-22% wage premium for workers demonstrating AI-augmentation capabilities
  - 2-21% reduction in posting volumes for automatable creative tasks on freelance platforms post-ChatGPT
  - Infrastructure, security, and QA roles EXPANDING alongside developer role contraction
  - Developing economies face disproportionate disruption via direct exposure AND indirect demand erosion
  - Wage polarization, credential erosion, geographic unevenness

## Strongest Counterargument
Egemen Kolemen (Princeton Plasma Physics Lab, quoted in an MIT Tech Review piece but the same methodological critique applies here): "In 2000, many analysts predicted that solar power would remain expensive — but then production exploded." The parallel: in 2024, analysts measured job posting declines and assumed permanent displacement. But what if we're measuring a transition dip — the same kind of temporary displacement that accompanied spreadsheets (accounting clerks), ATMs (bank tellers who shifted to sales roles), and containerization (dock workers)?

The strongest form of this counterargument: The 14-41% posting decline measures JOB POSTINGS, not actual employment. Companies may have stopped posting entry-level roles not because they eliminated them but because they changed how they source talent (internal training, bootcamp partnerships, AI-assisted screening that reduces posting visibility). The study's own limitations section notes "short observation windows" and "identification challenges" in distinguishing cyclical/pandemic effects from genuine AI displacement.

## Limitations to Acknowledge
- The systematic review covers 2020-2025 — the GPT-3 to early GPT-4o era. The landscape is changing fast.
- Most studies are from high-income Anglophone countries. Developing economy data is thin.
- Job posting volumes ≠ actual employment levels. Postings are a leading indicator but not a census.
- The 14-41% range is wide — a 14% decline could be cyclical; 41% is structural. The median (23%) is informative but masks this variance.
- No worker-level longitudinal data — we don't know what happened to the specific humans who would have filled those roles.
