# Research: Tesla Robotaxi Announcement-Reality Gap

## Journalist: Kai Nakamura (Transport beat)

## Thesis
Tesla has developed a pattern of making splashy robotaxi expansion announcements days before earnings calls. The quantifiable operational data — fleet size, availability, geofence coverage, crash rates — tells a radically different story than the headlines.

## Kill Test
**Pass.** Nobody has aggregated the announcement-to-reality ratio across all three pre-earnings robotaxi events with hard data. Electrek covers individual events; nobody has quantified the pattern systematically.

## 10-Star Test
**Pass.** The 35 cars vs. 3,000 cars comparison and 178x PE ratio framing are highly shareable.

## Novel Contribution
Calculate the "Announcement Theater Index" — a quantitative framework comparing:
1. Timing of announcements vs. earnings dates
2. Announced service area vs. actual availability (from RobotaxiTracker.com)
3. Fleet size vs. competitor fleet
4. Crash transparency vs. competitors

## Primary Sources (3+)

### Source 1: NHTSA Standing General Order Crash Reports
- Tesla has filed 15 crash reports since Austin launch (June 2025)
- ~500,000 cumulative miles through Nov 2025, extrapolated ~800,000 by mid-Jan 2026
- Crash rate: ~1 per 57,000 miles vs. 1 per 500,000 for average US driver (NHTSA data)
- Tesla redacts ALL crash narratives (unique among AV companies — Waymo, Zoox, Aurora all provide full descriptions)
- Source: Electrek reporting on NHTSA filings, March 16, 2026

### Source 2: RobotaxiTracker.com Independent Monitoring
- Dallas and Houston launch (April 19, 2026): 0-2% availability over first 24 hours
- Brief morning spikes to ~50% then back to zero
- Only single vehicle reported by riders in each new market
- Austin: ~35 vehicles, <20% availability after 9 months
- Source: Electrek citing RobotaxiTracker data, April 19, 2026

### Source 3: Waymo Operational Data (for comparison)
- 10 US cities with commercial service (2026)
- ~3,000 robotaxis deployed
- 127+ million autonomous miles logged
- 500,000+ paid rides per week
- Crash rate: 85% lower than human drivers for injury-causing incidents (peer-reviewed)
- Fully driverless (zero safety monitors in any vehicle)
- 10+ additional cities planned for 2026
- Source: humAI.blog compilation, Waymo disclosures, April 2026

### Source 4: Tesla Financial Data
- Q1 2026 deliveries: 358,023 (missed consensus, down from Q4 2025's 418,227)
- Stock: ~$400, 178x forward earnings
- Analyst EPS estimates: $0.24-$0.40 (wide uncertainty)
- Q1 2026 earnings: April 22
- Source: Electrek, financial data

### Source 5: Pre-Earnings Announcement Pattern
- **Event 1 (Jan 2026):** "Unsupervised" rides announcement days before Q4 2025 earnings call. Stock jumped 4%. Robotaxis vanished a week later. (Electrek, Jan 28, 2026)
- **Event 2 (Mar 2026):** Expanded Austin geofence to 245 sq mi announced. Still only ~12 unsupervised vehicles. (Electrek, Mar 31, 2026)
- **Event 3 (Apr 19, 2026):** Dallas + Houston "launch" 3 days before Q1 2026 earnings (April 22). Single vehicle per city, 0-2% availability.

### Source 6: Geofence Area Analysis
- Houston zone: Jersey Village/Willowbrook area, ~12-15 sq mi (Houston metro: 10,000+ sq mi = 0.15% coverage)
- Dallas zone: ~30-35 sq mi
- Austin total geofence: 245 sq mi
- Tesla operates with safety monitors in nearly all rides; only 1 truly unsupervised vehicle at a time

## Strongest Counterargument
Tesla bulls argue the company's camera-only approach, combined with billions of miles of fleet data from customer cars, will eventually leapfrog Waymo's more expensive sensor suite. The learning rate argument: Tesla collects orders of magnitude more driving data daily than Waymo, and neural networks trained on this data could achieve superhuman performance. The valuation reflects this optionality — investors are betting on the endgame, not current operations.

## Limitations
- RobotaxiTracker.com is crowdsourced/reverse-engineered, not official Tesla data
- Tesla does not disclose exact mileage, making crash rate calculations approximate
- We cannot verify whether safety monitors prevented additional crashes (making the true autonomous crash rate potentially worse than reported)
- Stock price movements have many confounding factors beyond robotaxi announcements
- Tesla's camera-only approach may prove more scalable long-term even if current operations are limited
