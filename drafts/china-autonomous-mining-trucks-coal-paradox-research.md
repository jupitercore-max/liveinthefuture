# Research Notes: China's Autonomous Mining Truck Surge

## Story Angle
China deployed 1,528 autonomous mining trucks in a single year (July 2024 - July 2025), capturing 87% of all new global deployments. The global fleet jumped from 2,080 to 3,832 (84% increase), with China going from 562 to 2,090 — a 272% surge that vaulted it past Australia (1,024) as the world's autonomous mining leader. But the paradox: China's autonomous trucks are overwhelmingly digging coal while the West automates iron ore and gold. The most advanced mining technology on Earth is accelerating fossil fuel extraction.

## Original Calculation
The global fleet's economic footprint, calculated from publicly available cost benchmarks:

**Fleet distribution (GlobalData, July 2025):**
- China: 2,090 trucks (54.5%)
- Australia: 1,024 trucks (26.7%)
- Canada: 344 trucks (9.0%)
- Chile: 208 trucks (5.4%)
- Rest of world: 166 trucks (4.3%)

**Operator displacement:**
Each autonomous truck eliminates ~4.2 operator positions (3 shifts × 1.4 coverage for leave/training). 3,832 trucks × 4.2 = ~16,100 operators displaced globally.

**Cost-per-tonne savings (industry benchmarks, 2026):**
Manual fleet: $2.65/tonne total variable cost
Autonomous fleet: $1.79/tonne → $0.86/tonne savings (32% reduction)
Source: Skillings Mining Intelligence, compiled from OEM aggregate data

**Utilization improvement:**
Autonomous: 20.5-22.0 hrs/day vs Manual: 16.5-18.5 hrs/day → +20% capacity
Each autonomous truck moves 4.2-4.8M tonnes/year vs 3.5-4.0M tonnes/year manual

**Safety:**
High Potential Incidents: 0.6 per 1M hours (autonomous) vs 4.2 per 1M hours (manual) = 85% reduction

## The Paradox
China's autonomous mining fleet is predominantly in coal operations. The Baishihu Coal Mine (420 trucks) and South open-pit coal mine (200+ trucks) are coal operations. CHN ENERGY is a state-owned coal/power conglomerate. Guanghui Energy is a coal/LNG company.

Meanwhile, Australia/Canada/Chile deploy autonomous trucks primarily in iron ore, gold, and copper mines.

This means the world's most advanced mining automation is accelerating the extraction of the one fossil fuel the world is supposed to be phasing out. The cost savings from autonomy make marginal coal deposits profitable.
