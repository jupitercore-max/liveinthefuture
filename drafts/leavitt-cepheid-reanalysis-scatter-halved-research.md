## Paper Details
- **Title:** "The Legacy of Henrietta Leavitt: A Re-analysis of the First Cepheid Period-Luminosity Relation"
- **Authors:** Louise Breuval (ESA/STScI), Caroline D. Huang (Harvard CfA), Adam G. Riess (STScI/Johns Hopkins)
- **Journal:** Publications of the Astronomical Society of the Pacific, Volume 137, Number 4, 2025 (p. 044001)
- **arXiv:** 2502.17438v2 (submitted Feb 24, 2025; accepted April 2025)
- **Pages:** 12 pages, 8 figures
- **DOI:** 10.1088/1538-3873/adc5a1

## Key Findings
1. Re-analyzed Leavitt's original 25 variable stars in the Small Magellanic Cloud
2. Used Leavitt's original notebook data alongside modern observations of the SAME stars
3. **Modern data reduces P-L scatter by a factor of 2** (key quantitative finding)
4. Found a **bias brightward at the short-period end** due to:
   - Non-linearity of photographic plates
   - Environmental crowding in the SMC
5. Overall: Leavitt's results are in **"excellent agreement"** with contemporary measurements
6. Reinforces the value of Cepheids in cosmology today

## Historical Context
- Henrietta Swan Leavitt (1868-1921): Harvard "computer"
- 1908: Preliminary announcement of period-luminosity correlation
- 1912: Published definitive P-L relation with 25 Cepheids in SMC (Harvard College Observatory Circular no. 173)
- Paper published under Edward Pickering's name (Observatory Director)
- Used visual inspection of photographic plates (silver halide)
- Led to Edwin Hubble's 1929 discovery of cosmic expansion
- Often cited as deserving but never receiving a Nobel Prize (died 1921 of cancer, Prize nomination came 1926)

## Hubble Tension Context
- **SH0ES** (Riess et al. 2022): H0 = 73.17 ± 0.86 km/s/Mpc (using Cepheids + Type Ia Supernovae)
- **Planck CMB** (2020): H0 = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc
- Discrepancy: >5σ — the "Hubble tension"
- **Högås (Jan 2026):** Physically-motivated priors reduce H0 from 73.0 to 70.6 km/s/Mpc, cutting tension from 5σ to 2σ
- **Casertano et al. (A&A, 2026):** "One bad measurement" ruled out — excluding any single method produces no meaningful shift
- Adam G. Riess won 2011 Nobel Prize for accelerating expansion discovery — he's co-author on the Breuval paper
- Breuval is also lead author on July 2025 paper defending Cepheid metallicity dependence against MF25 proposal

## Novel Contribution for Article
1. Original calculation: What does halving PL scatter mean in practical distance measurement? If scatter drops from ~0.4 mag to ~0.2 mag, distance uncertainty drops proportionally (magnitudes → distances via 10^(m/5)). For an SMC-distance galaxy (~60 kpc), this means distance precision improves from ±12% to ±6%.
2. Connecting the 1912→2025 pipeline: Same 25 stars observed for 113 years. Leavitt used silver halide plates with visual inspection; Breuval used Gaia parallaxes, CCD photometry, and automated light curve fitting.
3. The "meta-argument" for Cepheids in the Hubble tension debate: If Leavitt's hand-measured data agrees with modern measurements, the systematic errors critics worry about (crowding, metallicity) are bounded.
