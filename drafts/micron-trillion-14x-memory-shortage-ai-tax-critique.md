## 1. 🔍 General Editor — Overall quality, structure, engagement

**Score: 8.5/10**

**Strengths:**
- The hook is devastating: 14x in 12 months, contextualized immediately against Nvidia's 8.5x to establish the anomaly
- The structural argument (memory now costs more than compute) is the article's killer insight and is placed exactly right, after the setup and before the consumer impact
- The comparison table is well-designed and provides instant visual context
- The bear case section is one of the strongest counterargument treatments in recent LITF articles — it names specific fabs, timelines, and the exact mechanism of reversion
- Clean narrative arc: anomaly → explanation → novel calculation → consumer impact → historical context → counterargument → limitations → action

**Weaknesses:**
- At ~1,400 words of body, it exceeds the 800-1200 guideline, though the density of original calculations justifies the length
- The "What This Means for Per-Inference Cost" section could be tightened — the $0.001 figure is less impactful than the aggregate number
- Could use one more specific company example in the consumer tax section (e.g., a specific Samsung Galaxy S26 price increase attributed to memory)

**Verdict:** Strong data-driven analysis with a genuinely novel insight (memory > compute cost inversion). Publishable.
