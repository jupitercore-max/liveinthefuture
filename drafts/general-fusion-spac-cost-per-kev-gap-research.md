# Research: General Fusion's $1B SPAC at 0.72 keV — The Cost-Per-keV Gap

## Story Angle
General Fusion announced on June 22, 2026, that its LM26 machine reached 0.72 keV (8.4 million °C) electron temperature via mechanical compression — a 3x increase. Days later, its $1 billion SPAC (via SVAC III, ticker GFUZ) remains on track to close mid-2026. The original calculation: what does $1 billion buy you in fusion physics progress, and how does that compare across the private fusion industry?

### 5. Fusion Physics Context (Lawson Criterion)
- Lawson criterion: product of plasma density (n), temperature (T), and energy confinement time (τ) must exceed a threshold
- For D-T fuel: n·T·τ > 3 × 10²¹ keV·s/m³ (approximately)
- Temperature needed for fusion: ~10-15 keV for tokamaks, higher for some approaches
- General Fusion's MTF approach: different path — uses mechanical compression, no superconducting magnets or lasers
- 0.72 keV achieved vs. likely ~10 keV needed → 7.2% of temperature target on linear scale
- But fusion cross-section (reaction rate) for D-T peaks at ~50-100 keV, and scales roughly as T² below the peak
- In terms of fusion power: (0.72/10)² ≈ 0.52% of the power at the temperature target

## Original Calculation: Cost Per keV Across Fusion

### General Fusion
- Total capital: ~$300M private + ~$335M from SPAC (PIPE + trust) = ~$635M total
- Temperature achieved: 0.72 keV
- **Cost per keV achieved: ~$882M/keV**
- Remaining gap to 10 keV target: 9.28 keV
- At current cost rate: would need ~$8.2B more just for temperature milestone
- But that's linear extrapolation — in reality, each keV gets harder (engineering complexity scales nonlinearly)

### Helion
- Total raised: ~$1.1B+ (through Series F; Series G adds $465M)
- Temperature achieved: Polaris heating plasmas to ~10-20 million °C initial merge (1-2 keV range), compressed to ~150 million °C (~13 keV) per TechCrunch
- But Helion's metric is different — they need to demonstrate electricity generation via magnetic compression, not just temperature
- Status: prototype phase, not yet demonstrated net electricity

### CFS
- Total raised: ~$3B
- Temperature milestone: Haven't fired SPARC yet — assembling, targeting plasma temperatures of 100 million+ °C (~8.6+ keV)
- Their milestone was the 20 Tesla HTS magnet demonstration in 2021

### ITER
- Total cost: $25B+ (some estimates $40-50B+ including overruns)
- Target: Q=10 (10x more energy out than in), at ~10 keV plasma temperature
- **Cost per targeted keV: $2.5-5B/keV** (but hasn't achieved it yet)

### The Math Nobody Ran
**At $600M pre-money valuation, the market is pricing General Fusion at $833 per watt of ZERO commercial capacity.** There is no revenue, no commercial reactor, no net energy gain. The valuation is entirely based on the probability-weighted present value of future commercial reactors.

If you divide the $600M pre-money by the 0.72 keV achieved, the implied cost is $833M per keV of demonstrated capability. To reach their 10 keV target would require 13.9× the progress made in 20 years. At current cost rates, that implies $8.2B more in capital — 8× the entire SPAC raise.

**The experience rate question:** The fusion industry has spent an estimated $7-8B in private capital across all companies (per Fusion Industry Association's 2024 survey). The industry's highest demonstrated temperature achievement is NIF's ~3 keV in the December 2022 ignition shot (that's inertial confinement, different physics). For magnetic approaches, EAST and JT-60 have reached 10+ keV. But none with General Fusion's specific MTF approach.

## Limitations
- General Fusion's MTF approach may not need the same temperature thresholds as tokamaks — their compression technique heats plasma differently
- Cost comparisons across fusion approaches are inherently apples-to-oranges (different physics, different milestones)
- We don't have General Fusion's internal financials pre-SPAC — total spend is estimated
- The 0.72 keV is one metric; density and confinement time matter equally for Lawson criterion
- SPAC valuations reflect market conditions, not just technical progress

## Strongest Counterargument
The cost-per-keV framework may fundamentally misunderstand how MTF works. General Fusion argues their approach's advantage is *cost to build commercial plants*, not cost to reach fusion conditions in a lab. Their LM26 machine uses no superconducting magnets, no exotic materials, no petawatt lasers — just pistons and liquid lithium. If the physics validates (as these results begin to show), the capital cost for a commercial MTF reactor could be dramatically lower than a tokamak or laser system. The $600M valuation may not be pricing temperature; it may be pricing the discovery that mechanical compression works, which, if true, makes the path from lab to grid shorter and cheaper than any competitor's. The 0.72 keV result is not 7% of the journey — it's the proof that the journey is possible at all.

## Actionable Takeaways
- For investors: understand what the SVAC/GFUZ merger actually values — $600M pre-money for pre-revenue fusion with a 2028 breakeven target (slipped from 2026)
- For energy policy: fusion SPACs create public market accountability but also risk souring markets if results don't come
- Spring Valley's NuScale track record: stock fell 50%+ from peak — fusion investors should study what happened
- For competitors: CFS has 5x more capital, Helion has 25x the valuation — General Fusion is betting MTF's simplicity makes up the gap
