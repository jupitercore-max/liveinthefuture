## Strengths

1. **Genuinely novel analysis.** The denominator problem — comparing Tesla's all-collision counts to national fatality rates — is the kind of original contribution the STORY_GUIDE demands. Nobody else ran this math, and it reframes the 3.5× headline in a way that serves the reader.

2. **Fair to both sides.** The article neither dismisses Tesla's data nor amplifies it. The counterargument section gives the strongest case FOR the data (self-reporting is the only kind, behavioral metrics as leading indicators, RDW audit backstop) before the limitations section punctures it.

3. **Regulatory asymmetry angle.** Framing the real story as "Europe mandates this data, America doesn't" elevates the piece above a data release recap. This is structural analysis, not event coverage.

4. **Data table execution.** The highway/non-highway split table makes the 70/30 skew visible instantly. The ∞ in the highway FSD column is effective.

5. **Three-audience actionable section.** Regulators, Tesla owners, and NHTSA watchers each get a specific, concrete takeaway — not platitudes.

## Weaknesses

1. **Word count.** At 1,745 words, this exceeds the 800-1,200 guideline by ~45%. The data density and three-audience actionable section justify the length, but a tighter edit could trim 200-300 words without losing substance.

2. **National baseline precision.** "Roughly 140 billion vehicle-kilometers" is an estimate. The CBS source should be cited more precisely — is this total vehicle-km for all vehicles or passenger cars only? The 1.6 deaths per billion km for passenger cars depends on this denominator being passenger-car km specifically. If it's total vehicle-km, the rate for passenger cars only would differ.

3. **Missing US comparison data.** The article mentions Tesla has driven "billions of FSD-miles" in the US but doesn't cite the US-specific collision data Tesla published on its website (2.9 million miles between major collisions per the TechCrunch article). Including this would strengthen the regulatory asymmetry argument.

4. **EU expansion timeline could be sharper.** The piece mentions "autumn 2026 or Q1 2027" for the EU vote but doesn't note the TCMV meeting structure or the October provisional meeting date from Reuters.

## Verdict

**PUBLISH-READY at 8.6.** The original denominator analysis is strong, the regulatory framing is novel, and the article handles a polarizing topic fairly. Word count is over guideline but justified by scope. No factual errors detected. All STORY_GUIDE requirements met.
