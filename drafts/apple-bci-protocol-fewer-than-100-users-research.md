## Kill Test
✅ Would we notice if this article didn't exist? YES. It quantifies the vast gap between BCI investment/platform-building and actual patient adoption. Nobody has mapped the investment-per-patient ratio or the platform trojan horse dynamic in one place.

## 10-Star Test
✅ Surprising headline (Apple built a protocol that serves <100 humans), data-heavy (funding totals, patient counts, market projections), provocative comparison (more money invested per BCI patient than per organ transplant), actionable (what this means for disability tech and consumer electronics roadmaps).

## Novel Contribution
Original calculation: investment-per-implanted-BCI-patient ratio. Total private BCI investment (~$1B+: Neuralink $650M, Synchron $200M, others) plus Beijing's $2.1B government fund, divided by ~50-80 total humans with implanted BCIs worldwide = roughly $40-60 million invested per person who currently has a brain implant. No one has run this number.

Also: mapping the "trojan horse" pipeline — how Apple building a BCI HID protocol, combined with government investment and clinical trial expansion, creates the infrastructure for a transition from medical to consumer BCI adoption.

## Primary Sources

### 1. Apple BCI HID Protocol
- Apple created a BCI Human Interface Device protocol enabling thought-controlled access to iPhones, iPads, and Apple Vision Pro
- Synchron was the first company to integrate via this protocol
- Works through Switch Control accessibility framework
- No physical or voice input required
- Source: Medical Economics, Digital Health News, Electronic Specifier

### 2. Synchron (Stentrode)
- $200M Series D funding (led by Double Point Ventures)
- FDA-cleared endovascular BCI (no craniotomy — implanted via blood vessels)
- 10 patients implanted as of Series D announcement
- FOCUS-AU trial: 10 MND patients starting April 2026 in Melbourne (next-gen Stentrode, 16 command outputs)
- SWITCH trial (NCT03834857): 4 patients, 85.2% movement decoding accuracy, 12 months, zero serious adverse events
- CEO Tom Oxley: called Apple integration "next-generation interface layer"
- Acquired equity stake in Acquandas to strengthen supply chain
- Source: Digital Health News, Inside BCI, NeurologyLive, PharmExec

### 3. Neuralink (N1 Implant)
- PRIME clinical trial expanded to 21 participants globally
- GB-PRIME at UCLH (UK): 7 participants with severe paralysis
- $650M funding raised (June 2025)
- 3rd human implant completed early 2025; targeted 20-30 procedures in 2025
- 1,024+ electrodes per implant
- Originally targeted 1,000 implants by 2026 (not achieved)
- Noland Arbaugh: first patient (2024), gaming and daily tasks via thought
- Source: UCLH NHS, Digital Health News, ainvest.com

### 4. Beijing BCI Investment Fund
- ¥15 billion ($2.1B) government BCI fund
- 60% allocated to hardware development, 40% to clinical trials
- Targets commercialization by 2030
- Aims to address China's clinical translation gap
- Focus: implantable systems for paralysis, speech, psychiatric conditions
- Establishing regulatory pathways and semiconductor manufacturing
- Source: bciintel.com

### 5. China's BCI Ecosystem
- NEO implant: first clinical BCI device approved in China, enables brain-controlled grasping
- 24+ BCI funding events in 2025, 17+ rounds in 2026
- Government roadmap to establish BCI industrial system by 2030
- Target: 2-3 BCI industrial zones
- Domestic BCI market: 3.2B yuan (2024) → 3.8B yuan (2025)
- Source: en.dahe.cn, newschinamag.com

### 6. CorTec Brain Interchange

## Strongest Counterargument
The strongest case against the "billions chasing 100 patients" framing: every transformative medical technology had this phase. Cochlear implants (1984 FDA approval) served ~200 patients in their first five years; today 1.2 million people have them. Heart pacemakers went from 8 implants in 1958 to 1 million per year today. The investment-per-patient ratio during early clinical trials is always astronomical because you're funding the platform, not the patients. The question isn't whether the ratio is high — it's whether BCIs will follow the cochlear trajectory (mass adoption) or the artificial heart trajectory (perpetual niche).

## Limitations
- Exact count of implanted BCI patients is approximate — companies don't consistently disclose
- Beijing's $2.1B fund allocation details may shift during implementation
- Apple's BCI HID protocol scope and roadmap details are limited
- Investment figures don't capture government research grants (NIH, DARPA) which add billions more
- "Consumer BCI" timeline remains speculative
