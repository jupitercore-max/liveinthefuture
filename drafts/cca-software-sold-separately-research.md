# Research: CCA "Software Sold Separately" — The Pentagon's App Store for Combat Aircraft

## Story Angle
The Air Force just awarded production contracts for its first fleet of semi-autonomous combat drones (Collaborative Combat Aircraft). But the real revolution isn't the drones themselves — it's that the Pentagon deliberately decoupled the autonomy software from the airframe, creating a competitive market for combat AI. Six companies are now competing to write the code that flies beside F-35s. This is the first time in Pentagon procurement history that combat aircraft software and hardware are purchased separately.

## Original Calculation: The Force Multiplication Economics
Nobody has run the full cost comparison: closing the fighter gap with CCAs vs. F-35s, factoring in pilot lifecycle costs, production bottlenecks, and time-to-capability. The math shows CCAs generate more combat mass per dollar by a factor of roughly 2x.

## Novel Calculations

### Calculation 1: Closing the Fighter Gap — CCAs vs. F-35s
- Fighter gap: 1,558 required - 1,145 actual = 413 gap
- Closing with F-35s: 413 × $110.3M (with support) = $45.5B + 413 pilots × $15.7M training = $6.5B = **$52.0B total**
- Annual O&S addition: 413 × $6.6M = $2.7B/year
- Time: at 48 F-35As/year max = 8.6 years

- Closing with CCAs at 3:1 equivalence: need ~1,239 CCAs for equivalent force effect
- At $25M average (blended Inc 1 and Inc 2): 1,239 × $25M = **$31.0B total**
- No pilot training: $0
- Annual O&S (estimated at 40% of F-35A): 1,239 × $2.6M = $3.2B/year
- Time to deliver: production can ramp faster (simpler aircraft, parallel production lines, no pilot bottleneck)

**Savings: $21B in procurement, $6.5B in pilot training, years faster delivery**

### Calculation 2: Software Market Size
- If 1,000 CCAs deployed with "software sold separately" model
- Autonomy software lifecycle updates at ~$500K-$1M per aircraft per year (estimated from F-35 software sustainment costs scaled down)
- Annual software market: $500M-$1B/year
- Over 30-year lifecycle: $15B-$30B addressable market for combat AI software
- Six vendors competing → no single-vendor lock-in (vs. F-35's Lockheed monopoly on ALIS/ODIN)

### Calculation 3: Force Ratio Impact
- Current: ~1,145 US fighters vs. 2,410 PLAAF combat aircraft = 0.47:1 ratio
- With 1,000 CCAs at 1:1 equivalence: 2,145 vs. 2,410 = 0.89:1
- With 1,000 CCAs at 2:1 (CCAs are attritable, not 1:1): 1,645 equivalent vs. 2,410 = 0.68:1
- But CCAs are expendable — they change the calculus from "preserving expensive platforms" to "generating mass"
- China adding ~100-120 5th-gen fighters/year. US adding ~48 F-35As/year.
- With CCAs at scale (100+/year by 2029): US closes the production gap

### Calculation 4: Per-Sortie Cost
- F-35A: $42,000/flight hour × 1.5 hrs avg sortie = $63,000/sortie (O&S only)
- CCA at $25M, 2,000-hr design life: $12,500/hr amortization
- CCA O&S at F-16 rates (no pilot, simpler): ~$6,000-$8,000/hr
- CCA total per sortie: ~$27,750-$30,750/hr × 1.5 hrs = **$41,625-$46,125/sortie**
- But CCA is attritable. The sortie it flies instead of an F-35A saves one F-35A from risk.
- If a CCA is shot down: $25M lost. If an F-35A is shot down: $110.3M + $15.7M trained pilot + potential POW political cost
