# Research: OpenAI's $6.5B Hardware Gamble vs. Apple's $257B Moat

## Thesis
OpenAI paid $6.5 billion for io Products and poached 400 Apple employees. Eighteen months later, it has "barely anything beyond basic concepts and early prototypes" and its first product is a screenless smart speaker. Apple just sued for trade secret theft. The math reveals why hardware moats can't be bought: Apple has invested $257B in cumulative R&D since 2007 and deploys 616,000 people across its manufacturing network. OpenAI captured 0.065% of that workforce and 2.5% of the cumulative investment. The original iPhone cost $150M. OpenAI has spent 43× that and shipped nothing.

## Original Calculation: The Hardware Knowledge Ratio

**Apple's cumulative R&D investment (FY2007–FY2025):**
Year-by-year R&D spend (SEC filings, YCharts):
- FY2007: ~$2.7B | FY2008: ~$3.0B | FY2009: ~$3.5B | FY2010: ~$4.0B
- FY2011: ~$4.5B | FY2012: ~$5.0B | FY2013: ~$5.5B | FY2014: $6.0B
- FY2015: $8.1B | FY2016: $10.0B | FY2017: $11.6B | FY2018: $14.2B
- FY2019: $16.2B | FY2020: $18.8B | FY2021: $21.9B | FY2022: $26.3B
- FY2023: $29.9B | FY2024: $31.4B | FY2025: $34.6B
- **Cumulative total: ~$257 billion**
- TTM as of March 2026: $40.04B (record), Q2 2026: $11.4B (+34% YoY, also record)

**Apple's cumulative capex:** $12.7B in FY2025 alone (+34.6% YoY). Estimated $120–150B cumulative since 2007. Much of this funds custom tooling housed in supplier factories.

**The ratio:**
- OpenAI's $6.5B io Products acquisition = 2.5% of Apple's cumulative R&D
- $6.5B = approximately 2 months of Apple's current R&D run rate ($40B/year = $3.3B/month)
- OpenAI effectively bought 2 months' worth of Apple's R&D engine

**The people ratio:**
- Apple: 166,000 direct employees (10-K, Sep 2025) + 450,000 supplier workers = 616,000 total
- OpenAI poached: 400 Apple employees = 0.24% of direct workforce, 0.065% of total hardware ecosystem
- The remaining 99.76% of Apple's employees — and the entire supplier network — stay with Apple

**The iPhone comparison:**
- Original iPhone: $150M R&D, ~200 core engineers, 30 months (2005–2007)
- Only 30 people had seen the full device before Macworld 2007
- OpenAI hardware investment: $6.5B acquisition + ~$450M in salary (400 people × $750K × 1.5 years) ≈ $7B
- **OpenAI has spent ~35–47× what Apple invested to create the iPhone and has "barely anything beyond basic concepts"**

## Primary Sources

### Apple v. OpenAI Lawsuit (Filed July 10, 2026)
- Northern District of California federal court
- Defendants: OpenAI, io Products, Tang Yew Tan, Chang Liu
- **Tang Yew Tan:** 24-year Apple veteran, VP Product Design for iPhone/Apple Watch. Now OpenAI Chief Hardware Officer. Allegedly emailed himself proprietary supplier data before departing. Coached current Apple employees to bring "actual parts" to OpenAI interviews for "show and tell."
- **Chang Liu:** 8-year Apple Senior System Electrical Engineer. Allegedly kept Apple-issued laptop, exploited authentication bug to access shared network folders post-departure, downloaded dozens of confidential hardware files.
- **Key Apple allegation:** Trade secrets include supplier relationships, manufacturing processes, custom tooling in partner factories, metal-finishing techniques, and "systems-level integration knowledge" — what Apple learned from decades of iteration, including what DIDN'T work.
- Apple contacted OpenAI in February 2026 with concerns. OpenAI never responded.
- A supplier was allegedly convinced to use Apple's proprietary metal finishing technique for OpenAI under false pretenses.

## Strongest Counterargument
The WSJ notes that bringing parts to engineering interviews isn't unusual — interviewers want candidates to talk through their work. And Apple's "thermonuclear" legal strategy historically yielded mixed results: the Samsung case lasted 8 years and ended in settlement, not decisive victory. OpenAI could also argue that AI fundamentally changes hardware development — generative design, rapid simulation, and AI-driven manufacturing optimization could compress timelines that previously required decades of institutional knowledge. The real question isn't whether OpenAI stole secrets, but whether AI tools make the old supply chain knowledge less relevant.

## Limitations
- The $257B cumulative R&D figure is an approximation from publicly available TTM data; Apple doesn't break out hardware vs. software R&D. Hardware-specific R&D is likely 50-70% of total.
- OpenAI's total hardware spending beyond the $6.5B acquisition is estimated from headcount × assumed compensation; the actual figure is not public.
- The "400 poached employees" figure comes from Apple's court filing and may include attrition that would have occurred naturally.
- The supplier worker figure (450,000) is Apple's self-reported number and may overlap with workers who also serve other companies.
