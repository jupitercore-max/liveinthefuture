### 🔬 Research Rigor — 9.3/10
**Original contribution:** The "indium dependency chain" calculation is genuinely novel. No analyst report, G7 communiqué, or earnings call traces $700B hyperscaler capex → $26B optical transceivers → InP laser dependency → Chinese indium dominance → $12.5–14.6B in direct supply chain exposure. The kill test passes decisively.

**Limitations:** Explicit, specific, and honest. Names what's opaque (InP wafer market data), what's estimated (80/20 split), what could change (silicon photonics shift), and what's ambiguous (China's enforcement intent). Does not hedge with vague "many factors."

**Strongest counterargument:** Coherent's 6-inch InP fabs, ITO recycling, modest absolute tonnage — all stated at full strength. Then rebutted with the real constraint: "processing capacity, not raw tonnage, is the binding constraint." Fair and rigorous.

**Verifiability:** Every major claim traces to a cited source the reader can check. The step-by-step math in "The Math Nobody Is Running" shows inputs, assumptions, and calculation.

**Minor weakness:** The "60–70% of the InP supply chain's raw material traces back to Chinese zinc smelters" estimate is stated as "conservative" but the reasoning is thin. The article correctly notes non-Chinese producers "operate in a global market where Chinese output determines the marginal price and availability," which is the right framing, but a cleaner derivation would strengthen the claim.

**Verdict:** PASS
