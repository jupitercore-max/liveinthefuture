# Research Notes: The AI Rehiring Boomerang

## Thesis
55% of companies that made AI-driven layoffs now regret the decision. We calculated the total economic cost of premature AI displacement in the US: approximately $1 billion in wasted turnover costs in 2025 alone, before accounting for lost institutional knowledge and morale damage.

💼 Labor & AI

## Primary Sources

### 1. Goldman Sachs — Updated AI displacement forecast (June 25, 2026)
- Increased estimate from 6-7% to >9% of US jobs displaced by AI over 10 years
- That's ~15 million workers (up from ~10M)
- Based on "flow" methodology (workers exiting jobs) vs. prior "stock" (unemployed pool)
- Each 1% increase in tech-driven productivity → 0.5-0.6% increase in job destruction rate over following 2 years
- BUT: AI also expected to contribute to the 25-35M new jobs created annually
- Peak unemployment impact: <1% if losses distributed over 10 years
- Source: PYMNTS, June 25, 2026; Goldman Sachs note by economist Joseph Briggs

### 2. Actual AI-driven layoffs in 2025 — Challenger, Gray & Christmas
- AI directly cited in ~55,000 US job cuts in 2025
- That's only 4.5% of total announced layoffs (1.1M+)
- Represents ~0.03% of total US employment
- In March 2026 alone: 15,341 AI-cited layoffs = 25% of that month's total (leading single reason)
- Source: JP Morgan analysis; Challenger, Gray & Christmas data

### 3. Forrester — Employer regret rate
- 55% of employers who made AI-driven cuts now regret the decision
- Source: LinkedIn post by Mukul Singhal citing Forrester research

### 4. Gartner (February 2026) — Customer service headcount data
- Only 20% of customer-service leaders actually reduced headcount due to AI
- Most kept staffing steady or grew volume
- Gartner predicts 50% of companies that did cut CS roles citing AI will rehire for similar functions by 2027 (often under new titles)
- Source: Gartner Feb 2026 report

### 5. WSJ (July 27, 2026) — "Big Companies Are Starting to Hire Again"
- Companies defying predictions of AI wipeout
- Lattice CEO Sarah Franklin: companies stopped hiring entry-level employees thinking AI agents could pick up slack — realized humans are necessary to work alongside AI
- "Just because you have coding agents doesn't mean you're not hiring engineers"
- Many companies now back in hiring mode for junior positions
- Robert Half CEO M. Keith Waddell: AI's impacts "more benign than some have feared"
- Initial jobless claims near lowest since 1969
- Source: WSJ, July 27, 2026

### 6. Klarna — The poster child for AI overcorrection
- CEO claimed AI chatbot could replace ~700 customer service agents
- Paused hiring
- Quality and customer satisfaction dropped
- By 2025: CEO admitted they had "gone too far"
- Began rehiring humans in hybrid roles
- Australian Commonwealth Bank had similar reversal
- Source: Multiple (LinkedIn analysis, WSJ)

### 7. MIT Research — Economic viability of AI replacement
- For computer-vision tasks, AI was economically viable for only 23% of exposed wages
- Humans remained cheaper in the large majority of cases
- Source: MIT study (cited in LinkedIn analysis)

### 8. Young tech worker displacement — Goldman Sachs
- Unemployment rate for 20-30 year olds in tech rose by nearly 3% since early 2024
- More than 4x greater than overall rate
- Tech sector's share of US employment declining since ChatGPT launch (Nov 2022)
- Source: Goldman Sachs note, per Entrepreneur.com

## Original Calculation: The Cost of Premature AI Layoffs

### Inputs
- Total AI-cited US layoffs in 2025: ~55,000 (Challenger)
- Employer regret rate: 55% (Forrester)
- Therefore, ~30,250 workers were laid off prematurely (will need to be replaced/rehired)
- Average salary of displaced white-collar workers: ~$72,000 (BLS median for office/admin + professional occupations)

### SHRM Turnover Cost Data
- SHRM 2024: average cost-per-hire = $4,700
- But total turnover cost (separation costs + vacancy costs + hiring costs + onboarding/training + lost productivity) = 50-200% of annual salary depending on role
- For professional/white-collar roles: approximately 100-150% of annual salary
- Conservative estimate: 75% of annual salary = $54,000 per displaced worker

### The Bill
- 30,250 workers × $54,000 per turnover cycle = $1.634 billion
- Using more conservative 50% of salary: 30,250 × $36,000 = $1.089 billion
- Using SHRM cost-per-hire only (understates): 30,250 × $4,700 = $142 million

### Range: $1.1 billion to $1.6 billion in wasted turnover costs from premature AI-driven layoffs in 2025

### Additional uncounted costs:
- Institutional knowledge loss (hard to quantify but real)
- Morale impact on remaining workers (Gallup: disengagement costs ~$8.9 trillion globally)
- Severance packages (typically 1-6 months salary)
- Legal/compliance costs
- Brand/reputation damage
- The "AI washing" premium: companies announcing AI-driven cuts to appear innovative, then quietly rehiring

## Kill Test: Original Calculation
✅ Nobody has calculated the dollar cost of premature AI-driven layoffs using the Forrester regret rate × Challenger layoff numbers × SHRM turnover cost data. This is a novel combination of three independently published datasets.

## Strongest Counterargument
The regret rate may not mean the same thing as "will rehire those same roles." Regret could mean the company lost more than expected in quality or output, but still won't rehire because the cost savings remain. Additionally, some of those 55,000 layoffs may have been labeled "AI-driven" for narrative convenience (to impress investors/board) while the actual driver was cost-cutting during an economic slowdown.

## Limitations
- Forrester's 55% regret figure likely comes from a survey with limited sample size (methodology not fully public)
- Challenger data captures only announced/disclosed layoffs; actual AI displacement may be higher (shadow layoffs, hiring freezes, contract non-renewals)
- SHRM turnover costs are averages across industries — actual costs vary enormously by role and seniority
- The Goldman Sachs 15M jobs figure is a 10-year projection; comparing it to one year of actual data is apples-to-oranges (though illuminating)
- Not all "regretted" cuts will result in rehiring
