# Critique: Alphabet $85B Equity AI Capex

## 🔍 General Editor — 82/100

**Strengths:** Strong structural flow from opening number ($84.75B) through the "why" to the original analysis. The revenue justification timeline calculation is genuinely novel and well-constructed. The transition from company-specific to industry-wide analysis works smoothly. Related articles are well-chosen.

**Weaknesses:** The opening paragraph is overloaded — $84.75B, $80B upsizing, $18B common, $16.75B preferred, 6.25%, $10B Berkshire, $40B ATM — that's seven numbers before the reader has context for why they matter. The deck also front-loads too many figures. Consider moving the structural breakdown to a dedicated section and opening with the thesis more sharply.

**Suggestions:**
- Open with the self-funding gap thesis ("even the most cash-rich companies can't self-fund AI"), THEN introduce the offering structure
- The "Why?" single-word paragraph is effective
- "Because the math doesn't close" is a strong callback to the implied question

## 🔬 Research Rigor — 80/100

**Original Contribution:** ✅ Revenue justification timeline (7-10 years) and self-funding gap ($200-250B/year) are novel calculations not found in the cited sources. The dot-com comparison with inflation adjustment is well-constructed.

**Limitations:** ✅ Present and thorough. GPU depreciation cycle vs telecom infrastructure lifespan is a genuine insight that strengthens the limitations section. However, the limitations paragraph is a single 180+ word block — break it up for readability.

**Strongest Counterargument:** ✅ AWS analogy is the right comparison, presented at full strength. Berkshire's $10B as a credibility signal is well-deployed.

**Verifiability:** ⚠️ Most claims cite specific sources, but some figures lack inline links:
- "$307 billion in revenue last year" — no citation
- "CoreWeave ~$23B capex, $4B annualized revenue" — no source
- "$142 billion" 2021 IPO proceeds — no source
- Individual hyperscaler debt figures in the table need sourcing

**Methodology:** ⚠️ The 7-10 year calculation uses "30% operating margin" and "40% year-over-year growth rate" but doesn't show the year-by-year math. Consider adding a simple cumulative table or at least stating the formula more explicitly.

**"So what?" test:** Numbers are well-anchored to human context ($142B IPO year comparison, housing 2006 comparison).
