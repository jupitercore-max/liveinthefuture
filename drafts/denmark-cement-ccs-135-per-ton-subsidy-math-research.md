# Research Notes: Denmark Cement CCS $135/Ton Subsidy

## Article #406 | Category: 🌍 Climate | Journalist: Viktor Holm

## Core Story
Denmark signed a $2.55B (16.5B DKK / ~€2.2B) CCS subsidy contract with Aalborg Portland (subsidiary of Cementir Holding) on June 9, 2026. The ACCSION project will capture up to 1.25M tons CO2/year starting 2030, for 15 years. The subsidy rate is 875 DKK/ton (~€117/ton, ~$135/ton). Air Liquide provides Cryocap capture tech (95% capture rate). Harbour Energy provides transport and storage infrastructure.

### The "Green Premium" Math (original calculation)
- Denmark pays €117/ton to capture
- EU ETS penalty today: ~€74/ton to emit
- Delta: €43/ton (~$50) = the "green premium" Denmark is paying above the pollute-freely alternative
- BUT: EU ETS is rising. At €117/ton (projected within years), the subsidy becomes cheaper than polluting
- At projected €130/ton: capturing is CHEAPER than paying penalties
- Breakeven: when ETS = €117/ton, CCS becomes free money

### Global Scale Cost Estimate (original)
- Global cement process emissions: ~1.4 Gt/yr (49% of 2.9 Gt)
- At Denmark's €117/ton: €164B/year to CCS all process emissions
- Global cement market revenue: ~$420B/year
- Raw CCS-all cost: ~39% of global cement revenue
- BUT Stanford says only 12% cement cost increase at €141/ton (combo of strategies, not CCS alone)
- Even 12% = ~$50B/year additional cost globally
- Global construction spending: ~$14 trillion/year
- Cement = ~3% of construction cost → CCS adds 0.36% to construction cost

### The Storage Bottleneck
- Northern Lights Phase 1: 1.5 Mt/yr (fully booked)
- Northern Lights Phase 2: 5 Mt/yr by 2028
- Viking CCS: 15 Mt/yr target by 2035
- ACCSION alone needs 1.25 Mt/yr
- Global cement needs: ~1,400 Mt/yr CCS
- Current operational CCS storage: <10 Mt/yr globally
- Gap: ~140x between operational capacity and theoretical need

## Strongest Counterargument
CCS extends fossil-fuel era thinking: rather than rethinking construction materials entirely (mass timber, geopolymer cement, earth construction), we're spending $2.55B to keep limestone-burning alive for 15 more years. The $50/ton green premium could instead fund development of alternative binders that eliminate process emissions entirely.
