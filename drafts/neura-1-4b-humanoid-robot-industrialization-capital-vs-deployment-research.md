This is not an isolated event. It sits inside a massive capital wave into humanoid robotics.

## Novel Contribution: The Capital-to-Deployment Ratio

This is the number nobody is publishing:

- **$65B+ in collective humanoid company valuations** vs **<500 humanoid robots doing real industrial work**
- That's **~$130M in market cap per deployed humanoid robot**
- Compare to industrial robots: ~4.3 million installed globally (IFR 2024), industry market size ~$55B → **~$12,800 per installed unit**
- The humanoid premium: **~10,000x** the capital intensity of proven industrial robotics
- Even generous counting (include Unitree's 5,500 mostly-quadruped units): ~$65B / 6,000 = **~$10.8M per unit**

**The bet:** Investors aren't buying today's robots. They're buying the belief that unit economics will flip at scale. NEURA targets "several million robots by 2030." If they ship 1 million units at $50K each, that's $50B in revenue against a $7B valuation — reasonable. If they ship 10,000 units, the math collapses.

6. **NEURA's $1B+ order backlog** is the largest disclosed pipeline
7. **China's 450,000+ registered robotics companies** = intense competition driving costs down fast

## The Danger Signal: China's Price War Has Started

Reuters/Unitree reporting (June 11, 2026):
- Unitree Q1 2026: Revenue +68% YoY but profit **-53% YoY** (40M yuan, ~$5.9M)
- Cause: R&D costs (10% of revenue) + **price cuts as new rivals crowd in**
- 46 robotics companies in HK IPO pipeline alone
- 450,000 registered robotics companies in China
- Pattern identical to EVs and AI: overcapacity → price war → consolidation

This is the strongest counterargument to the capital wave thesis: the market may be enormous, but so many players are entering that margins could be structurally zero for years.
