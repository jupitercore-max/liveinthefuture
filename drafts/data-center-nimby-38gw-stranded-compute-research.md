## Original Analysis: The Stranded Compute Calculation

### Finding 1: NIMBYs Have Frozen More Compute Than Currently Exists
- Stranded capacity from NIMBY opposition: **38 GW** (Bernstein)
- Total current U.S. operational capacity: **~15 GW** (Reuters/Cleanview)
- **Ratio: 2.5×** — NIMBYs have frozen 2.5× the entire installed American compute fleet
- This is the key stat nobody is running. Every article focuses on the moratorium as a policy story. The infrastructure math tells a different story.

### Finding 2: The Acceleration Rate
- NIMBY-stranded share of pipeline: **7-9%** (summer 2025) → **11%** (June 2026)
- In absolute terms: ~26 GW (at 8% of ~325 GW pipeline a year ago) → 38 GW today
- Growth: **~46% increase** in stranded capacity in one year
- With NY's moratorium + 7+ states with pending legislation, expect this to accelerate further

### Finding 3: The Dollar Cost of Stranded MW
- Industry standard data center CapEx: **$8-12M per MW** (all-in, including IT equipment)
- Using midpoint of $10M/MW: 38 GW × $10M/MW = **$380 billion** in stranded investment
- For context: that's **54%** of the $700B annual AI infrastructure budget
- Even at a conservative $5M/MW (construction-only, no IT): $190 billion frozen
- Annual revenue per MW for hyperscale colocation: **~$1-2M/year**
- Revenue delayed: 38 GW × $1.5M/year = **$57 billion/year** in foregone data center revenue

### Finding 4: The Hochul Paradox
- **2024:** Hochul announces Empire AI, calls it path to making NY "the national leader in AI"
- **2026:** Same governor signs first state moratorium banning hyperscale data centers
- NYISO queue: 12 GW of large loads waiting to connect in NY
- At 50 MW threshold, the moratorium potentially affects every hyperscale project in the queue
- The reversal is not unique to NY — it mirrors a national pattern of state leaders first courting, then restricting, AI infrastructure

### Finding 5: The Regulatory Balkanization Scorecard
| Regulatory Regime | Example | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Full moratorium** | New York (state), Amsterdam | Freeze all new permits |
| **Conditional moratorium** | Maine (vetoed) | Freeze with project exceptions |
| **Tax + build standards** | Virginia ($0.011/kWh) | Allow but impose costs |
| **Rural ban only** | Texas (county-level) | Protect rural areas |
| **Ballot box ban** | Monterey Park, CA | Permanent voter prohibition |
| **Local NIMBY fights** | 1,200+ actions nationally | Case-by-case resistance |
| **Federal encouragement** | Trump exec order + Ratepayer Pledge | Push acceleration |

This patchwork means a data center developer now faces a **different regulatory regime in every jurisdiction**. No national framework exists. The Ratepayer Protection Pledge is voluntary.

## Counterargument (strongest case against the thesis)
The strongest case for the moratorium is that ratepayer protection has *already failed.* The March 2026 Ratepayer Protection Pledge is voluntary and unenforceable. Virginia's $0.011/kWh tax is negligible against data center revenue of $150-200/MWh. Residential electricity bills in data-center-heavy regions have risen 30% since 2020 (JLL). Without binding regulations, the externality costs (grid upgrades, water depletion, noise) are socialized onto communities that receive minimal economic benefit — only 2% of a typical hyperscale data center's operating budget goes to local labor. New York's moratorium may be the economically rational response to a market failure.

## Limitations
- The 38 GW "stranded" figure from Bernstein doesn't specify exactly which projects are permanently blocked vs. temporarily delayed. Some may relocate rather than disappear.
- The $10M/MW CapEx multiplier is a blended industry average. Hyperscale self-builds can be higher ($12-15M/MW), while retrofits can be lower ($5-8M/MW). The $380B figure is an order-of-magnitude estimate.
- The "current U.S. capacity = 15 GW" is from early 2026 Cleanview data. Bernstein reports 50.7 GW available in North America, which includes capacity under construction and Canadian facilities. The 15 GW is a conservative operational floor.
- China's data center buildout is NOT free of local opposition — but its political system means opposition doesn't produce moratoriums. Comparing political systems on this dimension is imprecise.
