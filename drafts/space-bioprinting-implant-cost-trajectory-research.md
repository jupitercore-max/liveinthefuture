# Research Notes: Space Bioprinting Cost Trajectory

## News Hook
Reuters (Jul 10, 2026): Auxilium Biotechnologies successfully printed liver and kidney tissues aboard the ISS — first time these tissues have been bioprinted in space. Tissues returned to Earth ~2 weeks ago for analysis. Earlier mission (Feb 2025): AMP-1 printer produced 8 nerve repair implants in 2 hours.

## Primary Sources

### 1. Auxilium Biotechnologies
- AMP-1 (Auxilium Microfabrication Platform): first to print 8 implantable medical devices simultaneously in 2 hours on ISS
- <1 minute astronaut time per print session
- Astronaut time valued at $130,000/hour (Auxilium's own figure)
- System uses lightweight preloaded cartridges sent via commercial resupply
- Latest mission: liver and kidney tissues using cells from Wake Forest Institute for Regenerative Medicine (Dr. Anthony Atala)
- Initial applications: peripheral nerve repair implants
- NOT functioning organs — tissue patches first
- Years from clinical use; FDA workshop on space biomanufacturing in 2026
- Signed agreements with commercial space station developers (post-ISS)
- CEO: Jacob Koffler, PhD MBA, UCSD

## Original Calculation: Cost Per Implant

### Scenario A: ISS Today (Falcon 9 + NASA overhead)
1. Cartridge launch cost: $4,500/kg × 2kg = $9,000
2. Astronaut time: $130,000/hr × (5 min / 60) = $10,833
3. ISS rack/overhead (7-day incubation): $20,000/day × 7 = $140,000
4. Return to Earth (Dragon downmass): $4,500/kg × 2kg = $9,000
5. Printer amortization: ~$5–10M over 100 batches = $75,000
Per batch of 8: ~$244,000
**Per implant: ~$30,500**

### Scenario B: Commercial Station + Starship (~2030)
1. Cartridge launch (Starship): $200/kg × 2kg = $400
2. Crew time (commercial rate ~$10K/hr): 5 min = $833
3. Station rack time: $5,000/day × 7 = $35,000
4. Return to Earth: $200/kg × 2kg = $400
5. Printer amortization (at scale): $5,000/batch
Per batch of 8: ~$41,633
**Per implant: ~$5,200**

### Cost reduction: ~83% decline from ISS to commercial station

### Value comparison:
- Nerve repair implant (current market): $20,000–$50,000 per procedure
- Kidney transplant surgery: $160,000
- Annual dialysis avoided: $85,179/year
- Lifetime Medicare savings per transplant: $500,000+
- Current per-implant cost in orbit: ~$30,500 (competitive TODAY for nerve repair)
- Future per-implant cost: ~$5,200 (potentially viable for broader tissue repair)

## Key Tension
The bioprinting isn't the bottleneck — it's the biology. Auxilium can print implants in 2 hours. But a functional kidney is still 10+ years away. The near-term market is tissue patches: nerve conduits, cartilage repair, liver tissue patches. These are high-value medical devices ($20K–$100K each) where the orbital manufacturing premium ($30K) is already justifiable.
