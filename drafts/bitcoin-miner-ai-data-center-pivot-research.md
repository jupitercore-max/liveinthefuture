# Research: Bitcoin Miners → AI Data Centers — The $19B Pivot Economics

## News Hook (July 6, 2026)
TeraWulf (WULF) signed a 20-year lease with Anthropic for a purpose-built AI infrastructure campus at its Justified Data site in Hawesville, Kentucky. The deal is expected to generate ~$19 billion in contracted revenue. 401 MW of critical IT load. Initial capacity online H2 2027, full capacity early 2028. Stock jumped 16%+ in premarket. This is the single largest bitcoin miner-to-AI hosting deal ever announced.

## Thesis
Bitcoin mining's $8.8 GW of US power infrastructure — 19.6% of the projected 45 GW AI data center gap — is being repriced from ~$800K/MW/yr in mining revenue to ~$2.4M/MW/yr in AI hosting revenue. The pivot is not a few companies experimenting — it's a systemic migration that could become the single fastest path to closing America's AI power gap, arriving years before new generation or nuclear can break ground.

## Original Calculations

### Calculation 1: Revenue Per MW — AI Hosting vs Bitcoin Mining

**AI Hosting (from TeraWulf-Anthropic deal):**
- $19B contracted over 20 years for 401 MW
- Annual revenue: $19B / 20 = $950M/year
- Revenue per MW per year: $950M / 401 = **$2.37M/MW/year**

**Bitcoin Mining (from current hashprice):**
- S21-class miners: 200 TH/s at 3,500W → 57,143 TH/s per MW (57.14 PH/s per MW)
- Hashprice: $38.2/PH/s/day
- Daily revenue per MW: 57.14 × $38.2 = $2,183/day
- Annual revenue per MW: $2,183 × 365 = **$796,700/MW/year** (~$797K)

**Revenue multiple: $2.37M / $0.797M = 2.97x** — AI hosting generates ~3x the revenue of mining.

This is BEFORE costs. Mining has:
- Electricity: at $0.04/kWh favorable rate, 1 MW × 8,760 hrs × $0.04 = $350K/year
- Hardware depreciation: S21 ~$5,000-7,000, 200 TH/s → 5M TH/s per MW → 25,000 miners → $125-175M capex per MW
- Mining net revenue per MW: ~$797K - $350K electricity = ~$447K before hardware/overhead

AI hosting has:
- Electricity (pass-through to tenant in most deals)
- Lower capex per MW (facility retrofit ~$2-5M/MW vs. new data center ~$10-15M/MW)
- 80-90% operating margins on hosting contracts

### Calculation 2: Bitcoin Price Needed for Revenue Parity

For mining revenue to match AI hosting revenue per MW:
- Need $2.37M/MW/year from mining
- Currently getting $797K/MW/year at BTC ~$62K
- Linear scaling (ignoring difficulty adjustment): BTC would need to reach $62K × (2.37 / 0.797) = **~$184K**

BUT: Higher BTC price → more miners come online → higher difficulty → lower revenue per TH/s. This means the real breakeven price is even higher — likely **$200K+** — because difficulty adjusts upward with price.

### Calculation 3: Total Addressable Power Conversion

- US Bitcoin mining capacity: 6.3 GW operational + 2.5 GW under construction = 8.8 GW
- Projected US AI data center power gap: 45 GW (2025-2028)
- Mining capacity as % of gap: 8.8 / 45 = **19.6%**
- If Bernstein is right (20% shift): 8.8 GW × 20% = 1.76 GW converted
- If full conversion: 8.8 GW → covers 19.6% of gap

### Calculation 4: Revenue Uplift from Full Conversion

- At mining revenue: 6.3 GW operational × $797K/MW/yr = **$5.02B/year**
- At AI hosting revenue: 6.3 GW × $2.37M/MW/yr = **$14.93B/year**
- Delta: **$9.91B/year** in additional revenue potential
- Total contract value potential: At TeraWulf deal terms, 6.3 GW × $2.37M × 20 years = **$298.6B** in potential contracted value

### Calculation 5: Speed Advantage — Mining Sites vs New Build

- New data center from scratch: 3-5 years (permitting, construction, grid connection)
- Grid interconnection queue: 18-36 months just for power access
- Mining site conversion: 80% infrastructure in place, remaining 20% is capital upgrade
- Estimated conversion timeline: 12-18 months (based on TeraWulf timeline: deal in July 2026, first capacity H2 2027)
- **Speed advantage: 2-3 years faster than greenfield**

## Counterarguments

### 1. Network Security Risk (Strong)
Hash rate has fallen 14.5% from October peak. If too many miners pivot, Bitcoin's security model weakens. A 51% attack becomes theoretically cheaper.

Counter-counter: Adam Back (Bitcoin pioneer) argues difficulty adjustment is designed for this. Remaining miners become more profitable. The network self-heals. This has happened in every bear cycle.

Honest assessment: The hash rate decline IS real and IS partially structural (not just cyclical). But Bitcoin has survived larger hashrate drops before. The risk is non-zero but probably manageable unless >50% of hash power exits simultaneously.

### 2. Infrastructure Is NOT Plug-and-Play (Medium)
ASICs ≠ GPUs. Mining rigs can't run AI workloads. The conversion requires new hardware, new cooling systems, and new networking — roughly 20% rebuild.

The $2-5M/MW conversion cost is not trivial. For 6.3 GW, that's $12.6-31.5B in retrofit capex.

### 3. Contract Duration Risk (Medium)
20-year leases sound great, but AI demand could plateau or shift. If inference costs drop 10x (per scaling laws), tenants might not need the same MW. These are not take-or-pay power contracts in most cases.

### 4. Location Mismatch (Weak-Medium)
Mining sites were chosen for cheap power, not for proximity to fiber backbones or population centers. AI data centers need low-latency networking. Some mining sites may be too remote for AI hosting.

Counter: Anthropic chose TeraWulf's Kentucky site despite its rural location. For training workloads (not latency-sensitive), location matters less. The power matters more.

## Limitations
- Revenue per MW calculations use the TeraWulf-Anthropic deal as the benchmark. This is one deal and may not be representative of the entire market. Smaller miners may get less favorable terms.
- Hashprice is highly volatile. The $38.2/PH/s/day figure can move ±30% in a month.
- The "80% infrastructure transferable" claim comes from a conference LinkedIn post, not a rigorous engineering assessment.
- We don't have detailed capex breakdowns for mining-to-AI conversion projects.
- The 45 GW power gap figure is a projection; actual demand depends on AI adoption pace.
