# Research: Police Cruise Lights and Crime Deterrence

## Core Question
Do steady-burn blue "cruise lights" on police patrol vehicles reduce crime? What does the evidence actually show, and which crime types respond?

## Primary Sources

### NIJ / Vallejo PD / BetaGov Study (Anchor)
- **URL:** https://nij.ojp.gov/topics/articles/how-do-we-know-it-works-conducting-rapid-research-police-experiment-test
- **Design:** Quasi-experiment in Vallejo, CA. Patrol cars alternated lights-on/lights-off shifts. Flashing code-2 (non-emergency) lights used.
- **Results:**
  - Lights-on: 6 total auto crimes. Lights-off: 12 total auto crimes → 50% reduction (p=0.07)
  - Zero auto thefts during lights-on vs. 4 in control (p=0.03)
  - 68% increase in vehicle registration checks during lights-on condition
- **Limitations:** Small sample. Quasi-experiment, not a full RCT. p=0.07 on total auto crime does not reach conventional 0.05 threshold.

### Charlotte-Mecklenburg PD "Operation Heartbeat"
- **Sources:** charlottenc.gov 2023 End-of-Year Public Safety Report; CMPD 2024 Public Safety Report
- **Timeline:** Launched 2022-2023 in Uptown (Spectrum Center/Transit Center area)
- **Results:**
  - 36% decrease in violent crime in target areas
  - 43% reduction in robberies
  - Expanded to "Operation TRIO" in 2024 → additional 13% violent crime reduction in Uptown
  - December 2024: expanded citywide to all 13 divisions as 24/7 steady blue lights
  - 2024 full-year: overall property crime down 4% citywide
- **Confounders:** Heartbeat bundled blue lights with proactive patrols and increased manpower. Cannot isolate light effect.

### Wilmington NC PD (since 2021)
- **Source:** WSOC TV; masonmasonlaw.com crime analysis
- **Quote:** Lt. Greg Willett: "Public feedback has been real good. People like to know we're around... With the blue lights on, you might not think it makes that big a difference, but it really does."
- **Data:** Wilmington crime hit 13-year low; overall crime down 44% since 2009
- **Note:** Long-term trend, many confounders

### Kansas City Resolution 250435
- **URL:** https://clerk.kcmo.gov/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=7407075
- **Adopted:** June 5, 2025. Sponsor: Mayor Quinton Lucas
- **Context:** KCPD started steady blue lights September 2024 for high-crime/entertainment areas
- **Text:** Resolution names Baltimore, Charlotte, Chicago, DC, Wilmington as success examples
- **Also:** KCPD source: https://kcpolice.org/media/news-releases/kcpd-implements-blue-lights-on-patrol-vehicles/

### Honolulu PD — Long-Running Natural Experiment
- **Policy:** HPD uses steady-burn blue rooftop lights on all patrol vehicles, including subsidized personal vehicles (Policy 4.14, since July 2015)
- **Sources:** Honolulu Star-Bulletin (confirmed "blue, nonflashing, rooftop lights"); Jalopnik; honolulupd.org
- **Crime Data (UCR, city-data.com, ag.hawaii.gov), 2012→2024:**
  - Burglary: 4,713 → 1,646 = −65%
  - Larceny-Theft: 21,978 → 13,344 = −39%
  - Robbery: 914 → 578 = −37%
  - Assault: 1,246 → 1,165 = −6.5%
  - Auto Theft: 2,754 → 3,877 = +41% ⚠️
- **Key insight:** Property crimes requiring lingering dropped sharply. Auto theft — requiring only seconds — increased. Suggests deterrence works through perceived detection risk for longer-exposure crimes.

## Academic / Meta-Analysis Support

### Welsh (2022) — Street Lighting Meta-Analysis
- **Publication:** Criminology & Public Policy
- **Scope:** 21 studies, 4 countries, 1974-2021
- **Finding:** Street lighting → 14% reduction in total crime. Strongest effect on property crime.

### Weisburd et al. (2023) — KC Patrol Experiment Reanalysis
- **Finding:** Overturned 50-year conventional wisdom from original 1972-73 study. Found "modest impacts of police patrol on crime in police beats." Original study was underpowered.

### Caplan et al. (2021) — KCPD Risk-Based Policing
- **Publication:** Police Quarterly
- **Finding:** 24% reduction in violent crime, 165 fewer victims in focus areas in one year

## Original Contribution
Cross-referencing Honolulu's decade-long natural experiment data with new cruise light adoptions to identify crime-type-specific deterrence effects. The auto theft anomaly is the key: crimes requiring extended exposure (burglary, larceny) respond strongly to visible police presence; crimes requiring only seconds (auto theft) do not. This suggests a specific deterrence mechanism: blue lights increase perceived detection risk proportionally to time-on-scene.

## Limitations
1. No RCTs for cruise lights — Charlotte bundled multiple tactics
2. Nationwide crime declined 2023-2025, confounding city-level claims
3. All data from police vehicles, not residential deployment
4. Auto theft is the weak spot
5. Honolulu has many confounders (tourism, population, housing, meth epidemic 2016-2020)

## Strongest Counterargument
The original KC Preventive Patrol Experiment (1972-73) — most famous policing study in history — concluded routine patrol had "no significant impact on crime." Weisburd reanalysis found only "modest" effects. Cruise lights are a step further removed: they increase visibility without adding patrol time.
