# Research: France's Nuclear Fleet vs. Its Own Climate — The Thermodynamic Trap

## Journalist: Anya Volkov (Energy Systems)
## Category: ⚡ Energy

## Core Thesis
France built 57 nuclear reactors to generate 70% of its electricity and avoid fossil-fuel emissions. In the summer of 2026, the hottest ever recorded in France, those reactors kept shutting down because rivers were too warm to cool them. The technology designed to prevent climate change is being disabled by climate change. The math on how fast this is getting worse has never been published.

## Original Calculations

### Calculation 1: The Accelerating Curtailment Calendar
Historical nuclear heat curtailments in France:
- 2003: First major event (record heatwave). ~4 GW curtailed, ~2 weeks
- 2006: Brief curtailment
- 2018: ~2 GW curtailed, ~1 week
- 2019: Brief curtailment
- 2022: Major summer. ~5 GW curtailed over weeks
- 2023: Recurring curtailments
- 2026: THREE separate heatwave events in one summer
  - Wave 1 (late June): 4.1 GW, ~1 week
  - Wave 2 (early July): 6.3 GW + 2.5 GW low water = 8.8 GW, ~2 weeks
  - Wave 3 (mid-July): 4.9 GW heat + 2.5 GW low water + 930 MW gas = 8.3 GW

The gap between major curtailment events: 2003→2018 (15 years) → 2022 (4 years) → 2023 (1 year) → 2026 (3 years, but 3 events in one summer)

### Calculation 2: Lost Export Revenue
Normal French nuclear exports: 10-12 GW
During heatwave: ~3 GW
Lost capacity: 7-9 GW
European wholesale power prices during July 2026 heatwave: ~€80-120/MWh (spiked to highest since Jan 2025)

Conservative daily revenue loss: 7,000 MW × 24h × €80/MWh = €13.4 million/day
Over a 2-week curtailment period: ~€188 million in lost exports per event
Three events in summer 2026: potentially ~€500 million+ in total lost export revenue

### Calculation 3: The CO2 Feedback Loop
When nuclear goes offline, replacement generation comes from gas.
Gas generation CO2 intensity: ~400 g CO2/kWh

Peak curtailment (8.8 GW nuclear + gas): 
- If replaced by gas: 8,800 MW × 24h × 0.4 tonnes/MWh = 84,480 tonnes CO2/day
- Over a 2-week event: ~1.18 million tonnes CO2
- Three events: ~3.5 million tonnes CO2 in one summer

France's total annual CO2 emissions: ~300 million tonnes
Nuclear curtailment replacement emissions = ~1.2% of annual total, concentrated in weeks

### Calculation 4: River Temperature Projection
Research suggests European river temperatures have been warming at ~0.3-0.7°C per decade since the 1980s (varies by river).
The Rhône has warmed ~1.5°C since 1970. The Garonne ~1.2°C.

At current warming rates:
- By 2035: Rivers ~0.5-1°C warmer → threshold exceedances double
- By 2050: Rivers ~1-2°C warmer → summer curtailment becomes the norm, not the exception

France's nuclear fleet was designed for 20th-century river temperatures. The rivers no longer cooperate.

## Kill Test
**Original calculation:** The return interval of major curtailment events has collapsed from 15 years to annual, and the CO2 cost of replacing curtailed nuclear with gas creates a quantifiable feedback loop (~3.5 million tonnes CO2 in summer 2026 alone). Nobody has published this feedback calculation.

**Novel analysis:** Cross-referencing Météo-France's record temperature data with EDF curtailment data reveals that the fleet is now being curtailed at temperatures that were below historical concern thresholds. The reactors "not normally affected" (Laconde quote) are the new vulnerability.
