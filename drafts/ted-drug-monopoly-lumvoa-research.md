## Thesis
Amgen paid $27.8 billion for Horizon Therapeutics in 2023, a deal largely underwritten by Tepezza's monopoly in thyroid eye disease. On June 26, 2026 — less than three years into ownership — the FDA approved Lumvoa, ending that monopoly. Our calculation: 27-32% of the acquisition price ($7.6B-$9.1B in NPV) is directly at risk from lost Tepezza cash flows.

## Kill Test — Novel Calculations

### Calculation 1: NPV of Tepezza revenue at risk
- Without competition: analyst forecast $3.85B peak by 2028 (RBC, Leerink)
- With Lumvoa taking 25-40% share by 2028-2029:
  - Conservative (25% share loss): Tepezza 2028 = $2.89B → lost $963M/yr
  - Base case (40% share loss): Tepezza 2028 = $2.31B → lost $1.54B/yr  
  - NPV (2027-2035 at 8% discount): $7.6B-$9.1B
  - That's 27-32% of the $28.3B acquisition price

### Calculation 2: Per-patient infusion cost comparison
- Tepezza: 8 IV infusions × 24 weeks × ~$343,000 WAC + 8 infusion visits ($4,000-$6,400) = ~$347,000-$349,000
- Lumvoa: 5 IV infusions × 12 weeks + 5 infusion visits ($2,500-$4,000)
- Even at course-level price parity ($343K), 3 fewer visits = $1,500-$2,400 saved in facility fees
- Time savings: 12 fewer weeks of treatment, 3 fewer half-day clinic visits
- If Lumvoa prices at 15-20% discount: ~$275K-$290K per course

### Calculation 3: Real-world insurer cost variance
- Tepezza commercial insurer payments: $431,000 to $981,000 (median $823,000) — Healio/ASOPRS data
- Cost per GO-QOL unit: $48,400 for Tepezza vs $980 for IV methylprednisolone
- Competition could compress this 49x cost-effectiveness gap

## Primary Sources

### Lumvoa (veligrotug-vvze) — Viridian Therapeutics (VRDN)
- FDA approved: June 26, 2026 (PDUFA target was June 30)
- Mechanism: anti-IGF-1R monoclonal antibody (IV)
- Dosing: 5 infusions, every 3 weeks, 12-week course
- Trials: THRIVE (active TED) + THRIVE-2 (chronic TED), both met all primary + secondary endpoints
- First drug with Phase 3 data in BOTH active and chronic TED
- First to show statistically significant diplopia response AND complete resolution in both stages
- Breakthrough Therapy Designation (May 2025)
- Rapid onset: improvements as early as 3 weeks
- Course pricing: "broadly comparable with incumbent" (PharmaDeviceNews)
- Also developing: elegrobart (subcutaneous anti-IGF-1R), BLA submission Q1 2027
- VRDN market cap: ~$1.92B, stock $17.90 (52wk: $13.18-$34.29)
- Source: Viridian press release (investors.viridiantherapeutics.com, June 26), Reuters, StockTwits

### Tepezza (teprotumumab-trbw) — Amgen
- FDA approved: January 2020 (original, active TED only)
- Label expanded: "regardless of TED activity or duration" (WITHOUT chronic trial data)
- Dosing: 8 IV infusions (10mg/kg then 20mg/kg × 7), every 3 weeks, 24 weeks
- WAC: $14,900/vial, ~$343,000 for full course (~23 vials)
- Net realized price: ~$200,000/year (Horizon disclosure at launch)
- Commercial insurer payments: $431K-$981K, median $823K (ASOPRS 2022)
- Revenue: $1.66B (2021), $1.85B (2024), $1.9B (2025), Q1 2026 $490M (+29% YoY)
- 2026 Leerink projection: ~$2B
- Peak forecast (pre-competition): $3.85B by 2028 (RBC, Refinitiv)
- Side effects: hyperglycemia, ototoxicity (hearing impairment)
- Up to 75% may experience proptosis regression after stopping
- SC Tepezza: Phase 3 positive (76.7% proptosis response), OBI technology
- Source: AJMC (FDA approval 2020), Healio (cost study), BioSpace (SC data), Amgen 10-Q/earnings

### Horizon Therapeutics Acquisition
- Announced: December 12, 2022
- Closed: October 6, 2023
- Price: $27.8B ($28.3B including debt) — all cash
- Also includes: Krystexxa (gout, $565M 2022 → proj. $1.36B 2028), Uplizna ($262M Q1 2026, +188% YoY)
- FTC settlement: Amgen prevented from using anti-competitive tactics to extend Tepezza/Krystexxa dominance
- Financed: $28.5B credit agreement (Citi + BofA)
- Source: Reuters (Oct 6 2023, Dec 12 2022), C&EN

### TED Epidemiology
- Prevalence: ~0.1% of population
- Associated with Graves' disease (autoimmune thyroid)
- Active phase: typically 1-3 years
- Chronic phase: indefinite
- QOL impact: significant negative socioeconomic outcomes, excess mortality
- Cost per GO-QOL unit: Tepezza $48,400 vs IVMP $980 (49x more expensive)
- Source: PMC review (PMID 41474061), Healio (ASOPRS study)

### Critical perspective
- Academic paper (MDPI, Sept 2024): "How an orphan drug became a blockbuster with annual sales exceeding $1 billion?" Tepezza never compared to historical standard (glucocorticoids, surgery) in RCTs — only placebo-controlled. 75% relapse rate for proptosis after stopping. Ototoxicity potentially devastating.
- Source: MDPI/Pharmaceuticals (Aug 2024)

## Strongest Counterargument
Amgen has a response: subcutaneous Tepezza. Phase 3 data positive (76.7% proptosis response), with at-home self-injection via OBI technology. This eliminates infusion center visits entirely. If SC Tepezza launches before Lumvoa gains meaningful share, the convenience advantage shifts back to Amgen. Plus, the acquisition wasn't ONLY about Tepezza — Krystexxa ($1.36B forecast), Uplizna (growing 188% YoY), and pipeline assets provide diversified value.

## Limitations
- Lumvoa pricing not formally announced; "broadly comparable" is analyst/press language
- Tepezza's SC formulation timeline to market is uncertain (BLA not yet filed)
- Market share projections are modeled, not observed — actual split depends on payer negotiations, formulary decisions, and physician preference
- Our NPV calculation uses analyst consensus peak sales as the baseline; if Tepezza was already plateauing (Q4 2025 missed estimates by $97M), the competition impact could be smaller in absolute terms
- Relapse/retreatment rates for Lumvoa not yet characterized in real-world use
