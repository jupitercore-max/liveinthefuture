# Research: North America's Robot Orders — Auto Collapse, Pharma/Cobot Surge (Q1 2026)

## Story Angle
The Q1 2026 A3 robot order data reveals the most dramatic customer shift in the North American robotics industry's history. Automotive OEMs — the sector that BUILT the industrial robot industry — collapsed by 35.1% in units and 48.2% in revenue in a single quarter. Meanwhile, pharma labs, chip fabs, and food processing plants surged. Cobots grew 55.6% in units and 78.2% in revenue, now representing 18.1% of total orders.

The headline isn't "robots are slowing." The headline is "the robot industry's founding customer is leaving, and entirely different industries are taking over."

## Novel Contribution: Revenue-Per-Robot Bifurcation Analysis

The data reveals a hidden divergence in the market:
- Total market: units -0.1%, revenue -6.4% → average revenue per robot FELL
  - $543M ÷ 9,055 = ~$59,945/robot in Q1 2026
  - If Q1 2025 was ~9,064 units at ~$580M (6.4% more revenue) → ~$63,994/robot
  - Average price dropped ~6.3% ($4,049 per robot)
- But cobots: units +55.6%, revenue +78.2% → average cobot price ROSE ~14.5%
  - The cobot segment is getting MORE expensive per unit
  - The traditional robot segment is getting CHEAPER per unit

This means the market is BIFURCATING: cheaper commodity traditional robots (auto declining, prices falling) vs. premium cobots (pharma/electronics surging, prices rising). Nobody has calculated this divergence.

## Novel Contribution 2: Auto Share Trajectory

If auto OEM orders declined 35.1% YoY and non-auto grew 24.5%:
- Q1 2025 baseline (working backward from Q1 2026 data)
- Q1 2026: auto collapsed while non-auto grew → auto's share of the market has never been lower
- In 2022 (record year), automotive was roughly 50%+ of orders
- Now auto OEMs are plummeting — at -35.1%/year, auto becomes <15% of market within 2 years
- Non-auto is now the MAJORITY of robot orders AND the growth engine
