# Research: China Rules AI-Driven Layoffs Illegal — Global Regulatory Comparison

## Story Summary
A Hangzhou Intermediate People's Court ruled that companies cannot fire workers simply because AI can now perform their roles. The court established that AI adoption is a "voluntary business decision, not an uncontrollable external event," meaning companies cannot transfer the financial burden of automation onto individual employees. Meanwhile, 61,000+ workers globally lost jobs to AI-related cuts in just the first 4 months of 2026, and the US has virtually zero federal protections.

## Novel Contribution
**Cross-jurisdiction legal comparison of AI layoff protections** — mapping the regulatory landscape across 4 jurisdictions:
1. China: Court rulings establishing AI layoffs as illegal (Hangzhou + Beijing cases)
2. EU: AI Act classifies employment AI as "high-risk" but doesn't ban AI layoffs per se
3. US Federal: Zero protections. WARN Act requires 60-day notice for mass layoffs but has no AI-specific provisions
4. US States: Emerging patchwork — California SB 951 (90-day notice), Minnesota HF4369 (90-day notice + transitional employment), New York WARN revision (disclosure checkbox), Connecticut SB 5 (AI disclosure)

**Original calculation:** At-will employment covers ~74% of US private sector workers. None have any protection against AI replacement. Compare: China's labor contract law covers 100% of formal employees.

## Strongest Counterargument
China's labor protections could backfire: if companies can't fire workers replaced by AI, they may simply not hire in the first place, accelerating the shift to AI-first workforces. China's youth unemployment hit 21.3% in June 2023 before they stopped publishing the data. Protecting existing workers while making new hiring riskier could create a two-tier labor market where incumbents are safe and newcomers can't get in.

## Limitations
- China's court rulings are city-level, not Supreme People's Court precedent
- We don't have data on how many Chinese companies are quietly restructuring around these rulings
- US state bills (CA, MN, CT) are proposed/pending — not yet law
- The 47.9% AI-attribution figure relies on self-reporting by companies
- EU enforcement timeline keeps shifting (Digital Omnibus delays)
