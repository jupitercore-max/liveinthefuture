# Research: Pig Kidney + Tegoprubart Convergence — Waitlist Economics

## Story #507 | Category: 🧪 Genomics | Journalist: Dr. Kenji Watanabe

## Thesis
Two parallel breakthroughs — eGenesis's 69-edit CRISPR pig kidney (TNFAIP3/HMOX1 transgenes) and Eledon's tegoprubart (anti-CD40L antibody) — are already combined in the same transplant protocol. Phase 2 BESTOW data (ATC June 22, 2026): eGFR 74 vs 61 at 18 months (p<0.05), zero BPAR after 6 months. Original calculation: the $8.9B/year waiting list math.

## Primary Sources

### 1. Nature Communications (Jun 27, 2026)
"Multiple human transgenes prolong survival of triple-carbohydrate knockout porcine kidney xenografts in nonhuman primates"
- Authors: Karadagi, Hirose, Lassiter et al. (MGH/Harvard + eGenesis)
- Key finding: TNFAIP3 + HMOX1 transgenes specifically associated with improved graft survival, significantly reduced T-cell and CD11b+ myeloid cell infiltration
- 3KO (GGTA1, B4GALNT2, CMAH knockout) + human transgenes
- 4 different HTG combinations tested; TNFAIP3/HMOX1 combo was best

### 2. Nature Medicine (Dec 2025)
"Immune profiling in a living human recipient of a gene-edited pig kidney"
- EGEN-2784: 69 genomic modifications (3KO + PERV inactivation + 7 HTGs: TNFAIP3, HMOX1, CD47, CD46, CD55, THBD, EPCR)
- Tegoprubart already used in protocol: 20 mg/kg on days -3, -2, -1, 1, 3, 7, weekly thereafter
- Also: ATG, rituximab, ravulizumab + standard tacrolimus/MPA/steroids
- Day 8 biopsy: Banff grade 2A TCMR (treated with methylprednisolone + tocilizumab + ATG)

### 3. GlobeNewsWire (Jun 22, 2026) — Eledon Phase 2 BESTOW Extension Results at ATC
- eGFR at 18 months: 74 vs 61 mL/min/1.73 m² (tegoprubart vs tacrolimus, p<0.05)
- Zero BPAR after 6 months in tegoprubart arm; 64% of tacrolimus BPAR events occurred after 6 months
- Side effects: headache 2% vs 12%, extremity pain 0% vs 10%, falls 0% vs 6%, AKI 2% vs 6%
- No graft loss, no PML, no PTLD, no BK/CMV nephropathy, no new malignancies
- Phase 3 initiation: late 2026
- Longest patient on tegoprubart: ~3.5 years (Phase 1b)
- Phase 1b eGFR INCREASED from 67.0 to 74.2 mL/min/1.73 m² (12 to 24 months)

## Original Calculation: The $8.9 Billion Waitlist

**Inputs:**
- 90,000 patients on US kidney waitlist
- Annual dialysis cost: $99,369/patient
- Post-transplant maintenance: ~$15,000/year (US estimate)
- ~27,000 kidney transplants/year in US
- New listings: ~40,000/year → net list growth ~13,000/year

**Calculation:**
- Annual cost of keeping waitlisted patients on dialysis: 90,000 × $99,369 = **$8.94 billion/year**
- If xenotransplant could eliminate the waiting list:
  - Transplant year 1 cost (surgery + care): ~$150,000 (high estimate)
  - Annual maintenance thereafter: ~$15,000
  - Break-even vs dialysis ($99K/year): < 2 years
  - 5-year savings per patient: $99K×5 - ($150K + $15K×4) = $495K - $210K = **$285,000 per patient**
  - At scale for 90,000 backlog: potential savings of $25.7B over 5 years

**The eGFR calculation:**
- Tegoprubart eGFR at 18 months: 74 mL/min/1.73 m²
- Tacrolimus eGFR at 18 months: 61 mL/min/1.73 m²
- Difference: 13 mL/min = 21% better kidney function
- eGFR of 74 = CKD Stage 2 (near-normal function)
- eGFR of 61 = borderline CKD Stage 2/3a
- Published data: every 10 mL/min increase in eGFR → ~2.6x lower odds of graft loss
- Implication: tegoprubart could meaningfully extend graft half-life beyond the 12-15 year average

## Strongest Counterargument
- Pig kidneys have maxed out at 271 days. That's 9 months. Human donor kidneys last 12-15 years. The gap is enormous.
- Tegoprubart Phase 2 BESTOW had only ~100 patients total. Phase 3 hasn't started.
- No one has demonstrated a pig kidney lasting >1 year in a living human.
- eGenesis uses tegoprubart PLUS tacrolimus — it's additive, not a replacement. Still unclear if tegoprubart alone could work for xenotransplant.
- Pig kidney production at scale (GMP breeding, 69-edit QC, surgical logistics) is completely unproven.

## Limitations
- BESTOW trial: Phase 2, ~100 patients, allotransplant (human-to-human), not xenotransplant
- eGenesis clinical outcomes: 4 living human recipients total, n=4
- Cost estimates extrapolated from US and Swedish datasets, not from pig kidney-specific procedures
- Pig kidney economics (breeding, editing, quality control) completely unknown at scale
