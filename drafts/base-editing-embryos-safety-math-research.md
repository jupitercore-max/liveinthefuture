# Research: Base Editing in Human Embryos — The Safety Math Has Changed

## Topic
Two independent studies (one in Nature, one bioRxiv preprint) demonstrate base editing in human embryos for the first time, showing zero chromosomal abnormalities — solving the biggest safety problem that plagued CRISPR/Cas9 embryo editing. But mosaicism persists, and the ethical debate is escalating.

## Primary Sources

### 1. Bower et al. (2026) — Nature
- **Title:** "Base editing reveals an essential role for NANOG in human embryogenesis"
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41586-026-10792-1
- **Published:** June 25, 2026
- **Institution:** University of Cambridge Loke Centre for Trophoblast Research
- **Lead author:** Prof. Kathy Niakan
- **Key findings:**
  - First use of base editing to STUDY gene function in human embryos
  - Blocked NANOG gene — discovered it's critical for epiblast (body-forming) cell development
  - NANOG functions differently in humans vs mice (in mice, loss disrupts yolk sac too; in humans, yolk sac still forms)
  - Reduced likelihood of unintended chromosomal abnormalities vs CRISPR/Cas9
  - Done under HFEA license (UK regulatory oversight)
  - Embryos cultured max 6.5 days, then allowed to perish (no implantation)
  - Collaboration: Monash Univ, Newcastle, Broad Institute, Francis Crick, MRC Lab

### 2. Jerabek, Egli et al. (2026) — bioRxiv preprint
- **Title:** "Efficient base editing and development in human embryos without chromosomal alterations"
- **DOI:** 10.64898/2026.05.30.728989
- **Published:** June 1, 2026 (preprint)
- **Institution:** Columbia University (Dieter Egli lab)
- **Key findings:**
  - Edited PCSK9 (cholesterol gene) and HBG (fetal hemoglobin) in human zygotes
  - ZERO aneuploidy (vs frequent aneuploidy with CRISPR/Cas9)
  - ZERO large deletions
  - Small indels after base editing were rare
  - Off-target activity: guide RNA dependent
  - Protein delivery at fertilization/pronuclear stage: normal blastocyst development + stem cell derivation
  - RNA delivery: EMBRYO ARREST (striking contrast)
  - "Though this may be a step towards heritable editing, translation to a clinical context remains premature"
  - Mosaicism persists — editing needs to occur 5-12 hrs after fertilization to prevent
  - Funded by: IOCB Prague, NY Stem Cell Foundation, Genomic Prediction, Korean Fund for Regenerative Medicine
  - First reported by NYT

### 3. Background: Egli's 2020 Cell paper
- DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2020.10.025
- Showed CRISPR/Cas9 causes frequent aneuploidy in human embryos
- This was the study that effectively killed the hope of using standard CRISPR for embryo editing

### 4. He Jiankui (2018)
- Used CRISPR/Cas9 to edit CCR5 gene (HIV resistance) in embryos
- Embryos carried to term — 3 babies born (twins + 1 later)
- Convicted, 3 years prison, 3 million yuan fine
- Mosaic phenotype in the children
- Widely condemned — editing was unnecessary (proven alternatives for HIV protection existed)

## Moratorium/Ethics

### ASGCT/ISCT/ARM Moratorium
- Published May 2025 in Cytotherapy (DOI: 10.1016/j.jcyt.2025.05.007)
- Proposed 10-YEAR BAN on heritable human genome editing
- Covers editing DNA in embryos, eggs, and sperm
- David Barrett (ASGCT CEO): Egli's work "unfortunate, flies in the face of the moratorium"

### Key Ethical Voices
- **Krishanu Saha** (UW-Madison biomedical engineer): "I would not call it a breakthrough, and it does not establish genome-wide safety or clinical readiness. I find it hard to think about a scenario where this is medicine."
- **R. Alta Charo** (UW-Madison bioethics emerita): "Exciting development on moving forward on the safety side. But mosaicism continues to be a significant problem... morally defensible to use heritable genome editing if proven safe and effective"
- **Alexis Komor** (UCSD): "The cat's out of the bag... opens the floodgates... gateway to embryo editing to do enhancements"
- **Bruce Levine** (ISCT ethics chair): "Worrisome... What is concerning to me is the spin that I see in the preprint, and how this could be used by some actors"
- **Megan Allyse** (Case Western bioethicist): "There are lots of guardrails on here, and it's not clear how this particular research study threaded its way through those guardrails"

### Congressional Ban
- For ~30 years, Congress has barred federal funds for research creating/destroying human embryos
- No legal requirement for IRB/SCRO review of privately funded embryo research

## PGT-M Statistics (for original analysis)

### Systematic review (5,305 cycles)
- Clinical pregnancy rate: 34.0% per cycle
- Live birth rate: 29.7% per cycle
- With concurrent aneuploidy screening: LBR 37.6% per cycle

### Single center (572 cycles, 2,344 embryos)
- 849/2,344 (36.2%) suitable for transfer
- 1,219/2,344 (52.0%) not suitable
- 276/2,344 (11.8%) need additional consultation
- Overall LBR per embryo transfer: 44.8%
- Autosomal dominant: only 30.0% suitable, 60.3% unsuitable
- X-linked dominant: LBR 30.4% per stimulated cycle (lowest)

### Neurological disorders (43 families)
- Final transferable embryo rate: 12.45%
- Clinical pregnancy rate (of transfers): 74.19%
- Live birth rate (of pregnancies): 89.47%

## Original Analysis: The Transferable Embryo Pool Problem

For autosomal dominant conditions (1,259 embryos tested):
- Expected Mendelian ratio: ~50% carry mutation, ~50% don't
- ~630 embryos carry the dominant mutation
- Only 378 (30.0%) are both unaffected AND euploid → suitable for transfer
- If 50% of embryos are unaffected, that means ~252 of the unaffected are aneuploid (can't be helped by editing)
- Of the ~630 mutation carriers, some are also euploid but carry the pathogenic variant
- If base editing could correct the mutation in euploid carriers: these embryos join the transferable pool
- Conservative estimate: ~380 additional embryos could become transferable → nearly DOUBLING the pool from 378 to ~758
- This matters most when couples have FEW embryos — those with <4 embryos available face high cycle cancellation risk

## Key Narrative Elements

1. **The safety inflection:** 2020 = CRISPR/Cas9 proven dangerous in embryos → 2026 = base editing fixes the biggest problem → BUT mosaicism still unsolved
2. **The ethical chasm:** One study under UK HFEA oversight (highly regulated), one under private US funding (guardrails unclear)
3. **The gap that embryo editing could fill:** PGT-M works for many couples but fails for those with few embryos or all-affected embryos. For neurological disorders, only 12.45% of embryos are transferable.
4. **The timeline question:** Even if safe, regulatory framework doesn't exist. Congress bans federal funding. 10-year moratorium proposed. Realistic clinical timeline: 2035+.
5. **The delivery paradox:** Protein delivery works, RNA fails. Why? RNA toxicity causes embryo arrest. This is a fundamental biology finding.
