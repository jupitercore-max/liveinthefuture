# Research Notes: Psilocybin/Alzheimer's Case Report — Dose Economics

## Primary Source
- **Paper:** "Transient multidomain functional improvement in advanced Alzheimer's disease following high-dose psilocybin-containing mushroom administration: a case report"
- **Authors:** Marcos Lago, Mariana Cerveira, Joe Xavier Simonet
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Neuroscience, Vol 20, published May 28, 2026
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fnins.2026.1813281
- **Section:** Neuropharmacology

## Case Details
- **Patient:** Octogenarian Japanese-American woman
- **AD history:** 10 years total, 5 years of marked hypofunction and predominantly monosyllabic speech
- **Baseline:** Chronic urinary incontinence, executive dysfunction, dysphagia, dependent mobility, flat affect, severe reduction in spontaneous communication
- **Intervention:** 5g orally administered psilocybin-containing mushrooms (Enigma strain)
- **Acute phase:** Autonomic activation, clinically suspected hyperthermia, profuse sweating, prolonged deep sleep-like state
- **Result (~19h post-administration):** Spontaneous autobiographical speech lasting several hours
- **Subsequent improvements:** Restoration of urinary continence, improved ambulation, autonomous dressing, increased emotional responsiveness, sustained social interaction, contextual memory retrieval, preserved working memory for social context, spontaneous conversational engagement
- **Duration:** Effects persisted for weeks
- **Second dose:** 3g at one month; some effects continued
- **Conclusion (from paper):** "Does not imply disease reversal but suggests that residual functional capacity may persist in late-stage neurodegeneration and may become transiently accessible under specific neuromodulatory conditions."

## Kill Test Calculations

### 1. The $409 Billion Blind Spot
- 7.4 million Americans age 65+ with clinical Alzheimer's dementia (2026 Alzheimer's Association Facts & Figures)
- $409 billion in health and long-term care costs projected for 2026
- ~13 million unpaid caregivers, 19 billion+ hours valued at $446 billion
- Severe AD ≈ 30% of population = ~2.2 million Americans
- Long-term care facility cost: $104,025/person/year
- 2.2M × $104,025 = ~$228.9 billion/year for severe-stage patients
- AD drug pipeline (2025): 123 disease-modifying candidates in 175 late-stage trials
- Pipeline eligibility criteria overwhelmingly favor "patients 60-80 with mild cognitive impairment"
- Lecanemab (Leqembi): $26,500/year, early AD only
- Donanemab (Kisunla): $32,000/year, early AD only
- **Neither drug targets severe AD. No approved drug attempts functional restoration in late-stage AD.**

### 2. Dose Economics (Novel Calculation)
- 5g psilocybin mushrooms: ~$35 retail in legal markets (Oregon, Colorado retail programs)
- Clinical-grade psilocybin: estimated $200-500/dose (compounding + clinical setting overhead)
- Lecanemab total cost: $26,500 drug + $1,683 admin + $1,350 monitoring = ~$29,533/year
- Donanemab total cost: $32,000 drug + $841 admin + $1,350 monitoring = ~$34,191/year
- Cost ratio: Even clinical-grade psilocybin ($500 high estimate) vs Leqembi ($29,533) = 59× cheaper per dose
- But: Leqembi works (modestly) in early AD. Psilocybin has n=1 data in late AD. Apples-to-oranges.

### 3. AD Drug Development Failure Economics
- $42.5 billion private expenditure on clinical-stage AD R&D since 1995 (Cummings et al., Alz & Dem 2021)
- 1,099 clinical trials, 235 agents analyzed
- 117 had negative outcomes = 95% failure rate (or 99.6% using 2002-2012 data)
- Only 6 reached commercialization (5 symptomatic + aducanumab)
- Phase 3 consumed 57% of costs ($24.065 billion)
- Cost per approved AD drug: $42.5B / 6 = ~$7.1 billion

### 4. Historical Precedent: Awakenings
- Oliver Sacks documented L-dopa administration to post-encephalitic parkinsonism patients (1969)
- Patients catatonic for decades temporarily "awakened" — returned to speech, movement, personality
- L-dopa effects were dramatic but transient in these patients
- L-dopa became the gold standard for Parkinson's disease (different etiology)
- Parallel: Psilocybin might reveal something about late-stage AD neurobiology even if it's not itself a viable treatment

## Limitations (Required Section)
- n=1 case report: weakest evidence level in medicine
- No control group, no blinding, no placebo comparison
- No biomarker confirmation (no CSF, no PET, no MRI)
- Outcomes reported by caregivers (observer bias)
- Psilocybin is Schedule I (DEA) — enormous regulatory barriers
- The patient was in Brazil, where legal framework differs
- Enigma strain dosing is imprecise (variable psilocybin content in mushroom tissue)
- "Transient" improvement = not a treatment, not a cure
- Acute phase included suspected hyperthermia — safety concern in elderly patients
- No replication in any other patient

## Counterargument (Full Strength)
The strongest case against reading too much into this: the history of Alzheimer's research is littered with dramatic early findings that dissolved on replication. Aducanumab showed beautiful amyloid clearance on brain scans — the FDA approved it over advisory committee objections — and it was pulled from the market within two years. Semagacestat, bapineuzumab, solanezumab — all cleared early hurdles only to fail at scale. A single case report, with no controls and caregiver-reported outcomes, doesn't even clear the first hurdle. The base rate for an n=1 observation in AD leading to an approved therapy is, functionally, zero.
