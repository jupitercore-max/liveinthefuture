# Research: Samsung Strike + Worst Memory Shortage in 15 Years

## Headline
The Worst Memory Chip Shortage in 15 Years Just Got a Strike Deadline. Samsung's 45,000 Workers Walk in Three Days.

## Journalist
Viktor Holm (Industrial / Manufacturing / Supply Chains)

## Category

## Core Thesis
Samsung's semiconductor division posted a 48x profit surge driven by AI demand. Workers want 15% of that profit as bonuses. Samsung offered 10%. Now 45,000 workers are set to strike for 18 days starting May 21, 2026 — into a memory market that's already experiencing its worst shortage in 15 years. DDR4 spot prices are already up 20%. DRAM contract prices surged 58% in Q2. The strike hasn't even started yet.

## Novel Contribution
**The "AI ate its own tail" calculation:**
Samsung's DS division made ~KRW 54.3T ($36B) operating profit in Q1 2026. At the union's 15% demand, the quarterly bonus pool would be ~$5.4B. At Samsung's 10% offer: ~$3.6B. The $1.8B quarterly gap — $7.2B annually — is what 45,000 workers are striking over.

For context: $7.2B is roughly what it costs to build a new semiconductor fab. Samsung is essentially being asked to choose between one extra fab per year or keeping its workers. And both sides know Samsung can afford either — the question is whether workers get to share AI-era profits on the same terms as capital.

The deeper calculation: Samsung holds 36.6% of global DRAM. An 18-day production halt removes ~1.8% of annual global DRAM supply (36.6% × 18/365). But DRAM supply is already meeting only 60% of demand. Removing 1.8% from a market that's 40% undersupplied doesn't just raise prices linearly — it creates panic buying, which is exactly what the 20% DDR4 spot price jump shows even before the strike starts.

## Kill Test
**Does this story matter in 5 years?** Yes — this is the first major labor action at a semiconductor giant during the AI boom, and it establishes precedent for how AI-era profits get distributed. If Samsung's workers win 15%, every chip fab workforce in the world will take note.
