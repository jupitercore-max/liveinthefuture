# Research: Telomeric RNA Silencing Restores Immune Function

## Primary Source
- **Paper:** "Therapeutic inhibition of telomeric DNA damage response rescues hematopoietic dysfunction driven by telomere shortening and aging"
- **Journal:** Nature Aging, published June 24, 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s43587-026-01136-9
- **Lead authors:** Rossiello et al., IFOM (Italy)
- **Model:** G3 Terc-/- mice (telomerase-deficient, 3rd generation) and aged wild-type C57BL/6J mice
- **Treatment:** Telomeric antisense oligonucleotides (tASO), 15 mg/kg IP, twice weekly for 4 weeks

## Key Findings
1. tASO targets telomeric noncoding RNAs (tncRNAs) that sustain DNA damage response at short telomeres
2. Selectively suppresses telomeric DDR (tDDR) without affecting genome-wide DDR
3. Does NOT elongate telomeres — decouples telomere structure from damage signaling
4. In Terc-/- mice:
   - Reduced senescence markers (p16/Cdkn2A) in BM and spleen
   - Reduced inflammatory cytokines (IL-17A significantly, trends in IL-6, TNFα, IFNγ)
   - Restored B cell levels, normalized myeloid expansion
   - Improved HSPC colony-forming ability to WT levels
   - Competitive BM transplant: anti-TeloG tASO restored reconstitution to WT levels
5. In aged WT mice (18 months):
   - Significant reduction in p16 senescence expression
   - Improved HSPC colony formation
   - Improved COVID vaccine IgG response
6. In human HSPCs (2 donors, ages 60 and 75):
   - Increased clonogenic potential ex vivo (erythroid + myeloid colonies)
7. Safety: no tumors, no toxicity, improved weight over 9 months, no splenomegaly

## Treatment Protocol
- 4 weeks of twice-weekly IP injections
- Benefits measured at 2 months and 9 months post-treatment
- 9 months = longest measured timepoint, benefits still present

## DC/TBD Context for Original Analysis
- Dyskeratosis congenita: 80% develop BM failure by age 30
- Only curative treatment: allogeneic HSCT
- HSCT outcomes in DC: 10-year survival 30% (n=34 study), 3-year OS 66% (n=94 study)
- 57% mortality at median 10 years post-HSCT (Tokai study, n=7)
- Major HSCT complications: pulmonary fibrosis, graft failure, infections
- HSCT cost: $350K-$500K+ in US
- HSCT cannot fix non-hematopoietic telomere disease (lungs, skin, liver)

## ASO Market Context
- FDA-approved ASOs (Ionis platform): nusinersen (Spinraza) $750K first year, eplontersen (Wainua) ~$200K/year
- Most are chronic therapy; tASO is a 4-week course
- ASO manufacturing cost at scale: estimated $1-5K per course
- Simple LNA-modified phosphorothioate backbone (well-established chemistry)

## Original Contribution: Treatment Efficiency Analysis
- 4 weeks treatment → 9+ months sustained benefit = 9:1 benefit ratio
- HSCT comparison: $350K-500K, 30% 10-year survival, only fixes marrow
- tASO potential: 4-week course, well-tolerated, addresses tDDR in all tissues
- Cost per quality-adjusted benefit month (original calculation in article)

## Limitations (from paper)
1. Only 2 human donors (ages 60 and 75, both male) — no statistical power
2. Single mechanism study — can't fully separate direct tDDR effects from indirect inflammation reduction
3. Anti-TeloG showed better reconstitution than anti-TeloC in competitive transplant — mechanism unclear
4. Mouse model only; no human clinical trial
5. Long-term effects beyond 9 months unmeasured
