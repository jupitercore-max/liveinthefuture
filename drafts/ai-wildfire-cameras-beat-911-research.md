# Research: AI Wildfire Cameras Beat 911

## Journalist: Elena Vasquez
## Article #: 334
## Category: 🌍 Climate
## Date: 2026-05-17

## Core Story

California deployed 1,211 AI-powered cameras across the state as part of its ALERTCalifornia network (UC San Diego / CAL FIRE). These cameras replaced human fire lookout towers, detect smoke in approximately 60 seconds, and have detected fires an average of 45 minutes to nearly an hour before the first 911 call. The system detected 38% of fires before any 911 call. Now the technology is expanding across the entire Western U.S., with Arizona, Colorado, and other states deploying similar networks.

## Key Data Points

### ALERTCalifornia Network
- **1,211 cameras** across California (source: firerescue1.com, firehouse.com, phys.org)
- Each camera covers **60-120 miles** of terrain
- Cameras rotate 360 degrees, completing sweeps every **2 minutes** (source: foxweather.com)
- AI analyzes footage **24/7/365** — no breaks, no night blindness
- **1,668 fires** aided since 2019 (source: firerescue1.com)
- **636 fires** (38% of total) detected BEFORE any 911 call (source: firehouse.com)
- **$24 million** in total funding since 2019 (source: govtech.com)
- Run by UC San Diego Scripps Institution of Oceanography + CAL FIRE
- Network grew from 35 cameras → 300+ → 1,211 over 4 years
- AI detects smoke in approximately **60 seconds** (source: fourthweb.ai)
- Average early detection advantage: **45-60 minutes** before first 911 call

### Pano AI (Private Sector)
- Key technology provider for utility-deployed wildfire cameras
- Detected a 7-acre Arizona blaze **45 minutes** before first 911 call (source: AP, autonainews.com)
- Working with Arizona Public Service, Xcel Energy, and other utilities

### National Expansion
- **Arizona Public Service:** 40+ cameras installed, aiming for 71 total
- **Xcel Energy (Colorado):** 126 cameras, targeting 7 states
- **Federal initiative:** $5 billion over five years for wildfire preparedness (source: gep.com)
- AP story (May 2026) confirms multi-state adoption: Arizona, Colorado, California leading

### ALERTCalifornia Growth Timeline
- 2019: ~35 cameras (pilot), $24M funding secured
- 2020: 610+ cameras (Scripps/UCSD reports)
- 2023-2024: 1,100+ cameras
- 2025-2026: 1,211 cameras, 77 fires detected in latest pilot phase expansion to all 21 CAL FIRE dispatch centers

### Fire Behavior Data (for novel calculation)
- Wildfires can double in size approximately every **10-15 minutes** under moderate conditions
- First hour is critical: small fires (under 1 acre) can be contained by 1-2 engines; fires over 10 acres require major incident resources
- Average federal wildfire suppression cost: ~$3,000-$4,000 per acre (USDA Forest Service)
- PLOS ONE study (Western Canada, 2015-2020): detection delays account for ~3% of total suppression costs; reducing delays by 1 hour saves ~0.25% of suppression costs
- BUT: the 3% figure measures delays for fires already in the system. Completely UNDETECTED fires are a different category — fires that burn unattended for hours before any report.

### Human Fire Lookout Comparison
- California had roughly **100 fire lookout stations** at peak (USFS/CAL FIRE historical data)
- Many lookouts decommissioned over past 20 years due to budget cuts
- Human lookout: ~$15-20/hour, works ~10 hours/day during fire season (roughly June-November), cannot see at night, visibility limited by weather
- AI camera: $24M for 1,211 cameras = ~$19,800 per camera over program lifetime
- 1,211 AI cameras cover more territory than the historical 100 human lookout network ever did

### California Wildfire Cost Context
- 2025 fire season: $28+ billion in losses (source: various)
- Palisades/Eaton fires (Jan 2025): $250+ billion estimated damage
- Annual federal + state wildfire suppression spending: $4-5 billion
- Insurance losses from California wildfires have driven multiple insurer exits (State Farm, Allstate)

## Novel Analysis

### Cost Per Pre-emptive Detection
- $24M total funding ÷ 636 fires detected before 911 = **$37,736 per preemptive detection**
- For context: the average wildfire suppression cost for even a small fire exceeds $100,000
- One prevented major fire (Camp Fire: $16.5B, Dixie Fire: $3.5B) pays for the entire network many times over

### The 45-Minute Advantage
- Under moderate fire conditions, a fire that burns undetected for an additional 45 minutes can grow from sub-acre to 10+ acres
- Sub-acre fires: containable by 1-2 engine crews at ~$5,000-10,000 cost
- 10+ acre fires: require full incident response, average cost $300,000-$500,000+
- Order-of-magnitude cost difference: **$10,000 vs. $300,000+** per incident
- For the 636 fires caught early: even assuming only 10% would have escalated to major incidents (63 fires), at $290,000 savings each = **$18.3 million in suppression cost avoidance** from a $24M investment

### The 62% Gap
- 38% of fires detected before 911 means 62% are still first-reported by humans
- This suggests significant headroom for improvement — more cameras, better algorithms, expansion to areas currently uncovered
- The question is whether reaching 50% or 60% pre-911 detection is achievable with current tech

## Novel Contribution
- Nobody has run the cost-per-preemptive-detection calculation ($37,736)
- Nobody has connected the 45-minute advantage to fire growth math (sub-acre vs. 10+ acre response cost differential)
- Nobody has compared the 1,211 AI cameras to California's historical ~100 human lookout stations as a direct replacement
- The 62% gap (fires still detected by humans first) identifies the frontier for improvement

## Strongest Counterargument
- PLOS ONE research (Western Canada) found detection delays account for only 3% of total suppression costs
- This suggests early detection saves less money than intuitively expected
- HOWEVER: that research measured delays for fires already in the reporting pipeline, not fires that went entirely undetected for hours. AI cameras catch fires in remote areas where no human would have called 911 for much longer.

## Limitations
- The $24M figure covers the entire program, not just AI — includes cameras, installation, networking, maintenance
- "636 fires detected before 911" doesn't specify how many of those would have been detected by humans within minutes anyway
- Fire behavior math varies enormously by terrain, weather, fuel — the "doubling time" framework is a simplification
- We don't have data on how many of the 636 preemptive detections actually resulted in measurably smaller fires
