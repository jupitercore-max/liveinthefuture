# Research Notes: THAAD $35B Contract — The Missile Defense Production Gap

## Core Story
The Pentagon just awarded Lockheed Martin a $35.35 billion seven-year contract to quadruple THAAD interceptor production. At the same time, the Army launched a crash program (MOSAIC-26-03) to develop interceptors costing less than $1 million — potentially 15x cheaper. The Pentagon is simultaneously signing the biggest missile defense contract in history AND admitting the current economics are unsustainable.

## Original Analysis — The Kill Calculation

### The Depletion Ratio
- Pre-war THAAD inventory: ~500 interceptors (estimated from 40% = 198 during Epic Fury first phase)
- Total consumed: >400 interceptors (80%+ of ~500)
- Pre-war production rate: 96/year
- Years of production consumed: ~4.2 years of production burned in <4 months of combat
- **Depletion ratio: 12.5:1** (months of combat consumption vs years of production)

### The Cost Escalation
- THAAD unit cost in 2021: $9.5M
- THAAD unit cost in 2026: $15.5M
- 5-year escalation: 63%
- Annual escalation: ~10.3%/year (far exceeding defense inflation of ~3-4%)
- At this rate, THAAD will cost $25M/unit by 2031

### The Contract Math
- $35.35B ÷ $15.5M per interceptor = ~2,281 interceptors over 7 years
- Average: ~326/year
- But "quadruple" from 96 = ~384/year at full ramp
- This implies unit cost may decrease with volume, OR the contract includes non-interceptor costs (launchers, support equipment, R&D)

### The Cost Asymmetry
- THAAD interceptor: $15.5M
- MOSAIC target interceptor: <$1M
- Ratio: 15.5:1
- $35B buys ~2,281 THAADs OR ~35,350 MOSAIC interceptors (if $1M each)
- $35B buys ~141,400 interceptors at $250K each
- The asymmetry reveals the Pentagon's own admission that the economics are broken

### The Burn Rate vs Production Rate
- Epic Fury burn rate: 198 THAADs in 16 days = 12.4/day
- Current production rate: 96/year = 0.26/day
- Burn-to-production ratio: 47.5:1
- At quadrupled rate (384/year = 1.05/day): ratio drops to 11.8:1
- **Even at 4x production, combat burns interceptors 12x faster than factories make them**

### The Taiwan Problem
- CSIS estimates China scenario would require 2,000+ interceptors in first week
- Current depleted inventory + new production through 2028: ~700-800 THAADs
- Gap: ~1,200+ interceptors
- At $15.5M each, closing the gap costs $18.6B — on top of the $35B contract
