# Research: AWE 2026 — Three Tiers, One Chipmaker

## Core Story
In a single day at AWE 2026 (June 16), the AR glasses market crystallized into three visible price tiers — and one company supplies silicon to five of six major players.

## Key Announcements (June 16, 2026)

### Snap Specs Consumer AR Glasses
- Price: $2,195 ($200 refundable deposit)
- FOV: 51 degrees (stereo waveguide, LCoS projectors)
- Weight: 132g (47mm) / 136g (52mm) — Swiss TR90 polymer
- Battery: 4 hours mixed use + charging case (4 charges = 20hr total)
- Latency: 7ms motion-to-photon (verified by robotic measurement)
- Chips: Dual Qualcomm Snapdragon (one for CV, one for Lenses)
- Display: 16 million colors, electrochromic tinting (Boeing 787 tech), nanostructured waveguide
- Input: Hand tracking, voice, gestures
- Cameras: 2 full-color high-res + 2 IR computer vision + 6-axis IMU
- Prescription lenses: yes, swappable
- Ships: Fall 2026, US/UK/France
- Spiegel: memory chip cost surge "has been quite impactful"
- Stock reaction: initially -4%, then +3%
- Previous gen (Spectacles 2024): 226g, 46° FOV, 45min battery, dev-only
- Improvement: 40% lighter, 5° wider FOV, 5.3x battery life

### Qualcomm Snapdragon Reality Elite
- New premium XR chip (successor to XR2+ Gen 2)
- 60% GPU improvement (same power)
- 160% NPU improvement (48 TOPS)
- 30% CPU improvement
- Resolution: 4.4K per eye at 90fps
- Battery: 20% better, 12°C cooler under load
- On-device: 3B param model at 45 tokens/sec (10 tokens/sec faster than Vision Pro)
- First devices: XREAL Aura, Play for Dream
- Compatible with standalone and tethered form factors

### Qualcomm START Platform
- "Scalable Turnkey AI-Ready Toolkit" for white-label smart glasses
- AR1+ chip module + software + companion iOS/Android apps
- First partner: Inspecs (UK - O'Neill, Barbour, Superdry brands)
- Supports audio-only and in-lens display frames
- Any brand can now make AI smart glasses off-the-shelf

### Qualcomm CEO statement
- Working on 40+ AI wearable devices (jewelry, camera earbuds, pins, watches)
- Believes next major computing platform won't be a phone

### XREAL Aura (Android XR)
- First wired XR glasses on Android XR
- Powered by Snapdragon Reality Elite
- Reservations now open at xreal.com/aura
- Ships fall 2026
- 70° FOV (from Project Aura specs)
- Tethered design (separate compute puck)

### Kiwear Smart Ring
- Companion input for AR/XR glasses

## Strongest Counterargument
The strongest case against the "AR glasses are ready" thesis is that Snap just priced them at $2,195 and the stock dropped. Google Glass launched at $1,500 in 2013 and became a punchline. Magic Leap raised $3.6B and peaked at 6,000 units sold. The history of consumer AR is a graveyard of well-funded failures. Battery life, social acceptability, and the "killer app" problem remain unsolved. Four hours is better than 45 minutes, but it's still not all-day — and the 132-gram weight means these are still noticeably heavier than normal glasses. The START platform may democratize AI smart glasses, but that's Tier 1 (audio + AI, no display), which is a fundamentally different product from AR glasses. The question isn't whether Qualcomm can put AI into eyewear. It's whether anyone will wear it.

## Limitations
- Snap hasn't disclosed display resolution or max brightness
- No real-world consumer reviews yet (media hands-on only)
- XREAL Aura pricing unknown
- Meta Orion specs are leaked/rumored, not confirmed
- "40+ AI wearable devices" claim is from Qualcomm CEO without specifics
- Our $/FOV-degree-hour metric is novel and unvalidated — it treats all FOV degrees equally, but peripheral degrees contribute less to usability
- Platform share by OEM count doesn't weight by unit volume; Meta likely ships 10-20× more units than Snap
- Smart glasses market projections vary wildly (IDC, Counterpoint, Canalys often disagree by 2-3×)
