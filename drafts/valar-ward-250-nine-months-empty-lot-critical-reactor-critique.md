## 5. ⚖️ Legal Accuracy — 8.5/10 ✅

**Assessment:** Core legal claims are verified and accurate. EO 14301 (May 23, 2025) correctly identified and linked. DOE authority under the Atomic Energy Act (42 U.S.C. 2140) for reactors "under contract with and for the account of" the Department is the correct legal basis. NRC Construction Permit Application process for KRONOS accurately described (submitted March 2026, accepted April 2026, ~12-month review). The DOE pathway's bypass of NRC jurisdiction for pilot-stage demonstrations is accurately characterized — Reuters and DOE sources confirm participants "are exempted from securing permits from the NRC."

**Issues (must fix):**
- **"Later adding an eleventh" is factually wrong.** The DOE accepted 11 **projects** from 10 **companies** — Oklo was selected for two projects (Aurora fast reactor + a second project). No eleventh company was added later. Multiple authoritative sources confirm this: POWER Magazine ("accepted 11 projects from 10 companies"), ANS Newswire ("ten companies are 'initial selections'"), DOE Energy Fuel Line Pilot page ("the 11 reactors initially selected"). The article should say "selected ten companies for eleven projects" or simply "selected ten companies" and drop the eleventh reference.
- **"Radiant Energy" should be "Radiant Industries."** The company's official name in DOE filings, POWER Magazine, and ANS Newswire is "Radiant Industries Inc." (product name: Kaleidos). Reuters also uses "Radiant Energy" in one article, but this appears to be Reuters's error repeated here.
- **"Costs spiraled past $9.3 billion"** is imprecise. The Carbon Free Power Project was terminated in November 2023 when cost estimates reached approximately $9.3 billion (up from ~$5.3B original). Costs spiraled **to** $9.3B, not **past** it — $9.3B was the estimate at termination, not a floor. Should read "costs spiraled to $9.3 billion" or "costs reached $9.3 billion."

## 6. 🔬 Research Rigor — 9.0/10 ✅

**Original Contribution:** Strong. Three pieces of original analysis that go beyond synthesis:
1. The 4:1 speed ratio table comparing DOE vs. NRC pathway milestones month-by-month — nobody else has laid out this comparison with this granularity
2. The "16 design-build-test cycles in 12 years" calculation reframing the value of speed as iteration capacity rather than megawatt output
3. The ten-company scorecard aggregating status across all Reactor Pilot Program participants into a single table

**Limitations:** Present in a dedicated section. Covers all major blind spots: Ward 250 is 100 kWt test (not power plant), zero-power criticality ≠ energy production, NRC timeline estimates may vary, 16-iteration calculation is theoretical, HALEU fuel constraints, EO authority is revocable. Meets the guide's requirement for honest accounting vs. inline hedging.

**Counterargument:** Present and stated at reasonable strength (see Ethics Reviewer above).

**Verifiability:** Sources linked inline throughout. DOE press releases, NRC pages, Reuters, BusinessWire all cited with hyperlinks. One link to LITF's own prior Antares reporting is appropriate cross-referencing.

**Methodology Transparency:** The speed ratio table shows inputs clearly (months per milestone for each pathway). The 16-iteration calculation is transparent: 12 years ÷ 9 months/cycle ≈ 16. The 60,000 microreactor calculation (300 GW ÷ 5 MWe) is shown explicitly. The 325 NuScale plant calculation (300 GW ÷ 924 MWe ≈ 325) is shown.

**Issues:**
- The "NuScale Power spent 23 years developing" claim is defensible (DOE itself traces development to OSU research in 2000, and NRC design certification came in 2023), but could be more transparent. NuScale as a company was founded in 2007 — saying "23 years from initial research to NRC certification" or "over two decades from university lab to licensed design" would be more precise without losing the point.
- The NRC timeline estimates for KRONOS in the speed ratio table (16 months application-to-permit, 18–24 months permit-to-criticality) are marked "(est.)" which is appropriate, but the article doesn't cite the source for the 18–24 month permit-to-criticality estimate. Nano Nuclear's own 12-month review estimate is cited; the construction timeline should be sourced or noted as the author's estimate.
