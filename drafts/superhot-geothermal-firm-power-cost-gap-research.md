# Research Notes: Superhot Geothermal — The Firm Power Cost Gap Nobody Calculated

## Journalist: Anya Volkov (⚡ Energy)

## Thesis
Two next-generation geothermal startups (Quaise Energy and Fervo Energy) are racing to make the Earth's deep heat a viable baseload power source. But the real story isn't the drilling tech — it's the math nobody ran: when you compare energy sources on a **firm power** basis (24/7/365 delivery), geothermal's cost advantage over solar and wind is not incremental — it's an order of magnitude. The "levelized cost" numbers that dominate energy debates compare apples to oranges. Geothermal's true economic position is far stronger than headline LCOE suggests.

## Kill Test (Original Calculation)
**Nobody has calculated the firm-power-adjusted cost gap between superhot geothermal and renewables.** Using Bank of America's LFSCOE methodology and Quaise/Fervo's projected costs, I will show:
1. Solar's real firm-power cost is 43× its headline LCOE (Germany) or 11.5× (Texas)
2. At the DOE's $45/MWh EGS target, geothermal would be the cheapest firm clean power in history
3. Superhot rock's 5-10× energy density per well creates a specific break-even drilling premium
4. Land use comparison: superhot geothermal is 75-750× more land-efficient per GW than solar/wind

## Original Calculations

### Calculation 1: Firm Power Cost Multiplier
Using BofA methodology:
| Source | LCOE ($/MWh) | LFSCOE-95 Texas | LFSCOE-100 Texas | Firm Multiplier |
|--------|-------------|-----------------|------------------|----------------|
| Solar PV | 36 | 177 | 413 | 11.5× |
| Wind | 40 | 131 | 291 | 7.3× |
| Nuclear | 82 | 96 | 122 | 1.5× |
| Gas CC | 38 | 32 | 40 | 1.1× |
| Geothermal (conventional) | 87 (mid) | ~87 | ~87 | 1.0× |
| EGS target (2035) | 45 | ~45 | ~45 | 1.0× |

Geothermal's multiplier is 1.0× because it's inherently firm (90%+ capacity factor, 24/7).

### Calculation 2: Land Use per GW (Firm Power)
- Solar: 1 GW firm = 4 GW nameplate (25% CF) × 7.5 acres/MW = 30,000 acres
- Wind: 1 GW firm = 2.85 GW nameplate (35% CF) × 80 acres/MW = 228,000 acres
- Nuclear: ~1,600 acres (Vogtle: 3,169 acres for 2.2 GW = 1,440 acres/GW)
- Superhot geothermal: 20 acres/50 MW = 400 acres/GW

Result: Superhot geothermal is 75× more land-efficient than solar and 570× more than wind.

### Calculation 3: Supercritical Energy Density Break-Even
- Conventional geothermal well at 200°C: ~5 MW thermal → ~1.5 MW electrical (30% efficiency)
- Superhot well at 400°C (supercritical): ~25-50 MW thermal → ~10-20 MW electrical
- Energy per well: 5-10× increase for superhot vs conventional
- If superhot drilling costs 2× conventional: net cost per MW = 2/(5 to 10) = 0.2-0.4× conventional
- Break-even: superhot drilling can cost up to 5-10× more per well and still match conventional cost/MW

### Calculation 4: The 63 TW Math
- Current global electricity generation capacity: ~8 TW
- Current global electricity from geothermal: ~16 GW (0.016 TW) = 0.2% of total
- 1% of SHR = 63 TW = 8× all current electricity
- To replace all fossil fuel electricity (~5 TW): would need 5/63 = 7.9% of the 1% tapped, or 0.079% of total SHR
- Even with massive inefficiencies and losses, the resource is 3-4 orders of magnitude larger than needed

## Strongest Counterargument
Quaise has drilled 100m of granite and is approaching 1km. Project Obsidian needs 5km. That's a 5-50× gap between current achievement and commercial deployment. Millimeter-wave drilling has never been done directionally (only straight down). The physics works in a quarry; whether it works at 400°C with supercritical fluid pressure at 5km depth is unproven. Fervo's approach (adapt existing oil/gas tech) has a much shorter path to commercialization. The history of "revolutionary drilling tech" is littered with failures: American Magma's hot dry rock tests in the 1980s, AltaRock's abandoned Basel project (triggered earthquakes), and multiple DOE-funded projects that never reached commercial scale.

## Limitations
- Quaise's cost projections are pre-commercial. No superhot well has produced commercial electricity yet.
- The LFSCOE calculation uses BofA's 2022 methodology; battery costs have fallen ~30% since then, which would reduce solar/wind firm power costs.
- Project Obsidian Phase 1 will use conventional drilling; millimeter-wave drilling won't be deployed until the 365°C wells.
- The 63 TW figure comes from OSU/Quaise; independent geological verification at this scale is limited.
- Induced seismicity risk for EGS is acknowledged but not modeled in cost projections.
