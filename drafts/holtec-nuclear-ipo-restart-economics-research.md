# Research: Holtec Nuclear IPO & The Restart Cost Advantage

## Topic
Holtec Nuclear Corporation (HNUC) filed for IPO on Nasdaq, July 10, 2026. First nuclear company going public with actual revenue and profits. The story centers on nuclear restart economics vs. new-build costs, and what the IPO reveals about the cheapest path to new nuclear capacity.

## Why This Story (Kill Test)
Original calculation: **cost per megawatt** across nuclear pathways, revealing that reactor restart is 6–8× cheaper than new-build per MW and produces the best return on invested capital of any firm power source — but the restartable universe is vanishingly small.

- IPO proceeds: SMR-300 program, manufacturing expansion, growth initiatives
- Source: https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/holtec-nuclear-corporation-files-us-ipo-2026-07-10/

### 2. Palisades Restart Financials
- 800 MW reactor, closed May 2022 after 50+ years (Entergy)
- Holtec acquired for decommissioning, pivoted to restart ~late 2023
- DOE loan guarantee: $1.52B (disbursed ~$491M through 6 tranches as of Sep 2025)
- USDA: >$1.3B (including >$650M grant to Wolverine Power Cooperative)
- Michigan state grants: $300M
- Total federal/state support: ~$3.1B+ for 800 MW
- 1,000+ steam generator tubes showing corrosion cracking — "sleeving" repair underway
- Operating license expires 2031 (only ~5 years after potential restart)
- First-ever US commercial reactor restart
- 900 jobs, ~45% union labor
- Sources: Michigan.gov, Reuters, Power Magazine

### 3. Vogtle 3 & 4 (New-Build Benchmark)
- 2 × 1,117 MW = 2,234 MW (AP1000 reactors, Westinghouse)
- Original budget: $14B, begun 2009
- Final cost: ~$35B (including $3.7B Westinghouse settlement)
- Cost per MW: $35B / 2,234 MW = **$15.67M/MW**
- Timeline: 14–15 years (2009–2024), 7 years late
- 150% cost overrun
- Sources: CleanTechnica, Wikipedia, AP

### 4. NuScale UAMPS (Cancelled SMR Benchmark)
- 6 × 77 MW = 462 MW (scaled down from original 12 × 50 MW = 600 MW)
- Cost escalation: $3B (2015) → $4.2B (2018) → $6.1B (2020) → $9.3B (2023)
- Cost per MW at cancellation: $9.3B / 462 MW = **$20.1M/MW** (worse than Vogtle!)
- Target power price: $55/MWh → $89/MWh (53% increase)
- DOE cost-share: $1.35B approved
- Cancelled November 2023 — failed to attract enough subscriptions
- Sources: Utility Dive, Reuters, cooperative.com

### 5. Three Mile Island / Crane Clean Energy Center Restart (Constellation)
- 835 MW (Unit 1 — Unit 2 had 1979 meltdown, will not restart)
- Closed 2019, economically uncompetitive vs. cheap gas
- Total restart cost: $1.6B (Constellation's estimate)
- DOE loan: $1B
- Microsoft 20-year PPA worth $16B total
- Cost per MW: $1.6B / 835 MW = **$1.9M/MW**
- Target restart: mid-2027 (accelerated by ~1 year after PJM fast-tracked grid connection)
- Constellation NOT seeking federal subsidies (Microsoft paying premium)
- Sources: Reuters, Bloomberg Law, OilPrice

## The Original Calculation — Cost per MW Across Nuclear Pathways

| Pathway | Total Cost | Capacity | $/MW | Timeline | Status |
|---------|-----------|----------|------|----------|--------|
| New-build (Vogtle 3&4) | $35B | 2,234 MW | $15.67M | 14 years | Complete, 150% overrun |
| SMR new-build (NuScale) | $9.3B | 462 MW | $20.13M | Never | Cancelled |
| Restart (TMI/Crane) | $1.6B | 835 MW | $1.92M | ~4 years | In progress |
| Restart (Palisades) | ~$2B est | 800 MW | ~$2.5M | ~3 years | In progress |

**Restart is 6–10× cheaper per MW than new-build.** That's the headline math.

### Capacity-Factor Adjusted Return Analysis
Nuclear operates at ~93% capacity factor vs solar ~25%.

**Nuclear restart ($2M/MW):**
- Annual output: 1 MW × 0.93 × 8,760 hrs = 8,147 MWh
- At $50/MWh wholesale: $407K/year/MW
- 25-year revenue: $10.2M per MW invested
- Return on capital: 5.1×

**New-build nuclear ($15.67M/MW):**
- Same output: 8,147 MWh/year
- $407K/year → payback in 38.5 years
- 60-year design life return: 1.56×

**Solar + storage ($1.5M/MW nameplate):**
- Annual output: 1 MW × 0.25 × 8,760 = 2,190 MWh
- At $50/MWh: $109K/year/MW
- 25-year revenue: $2.7M per MW invested
- Return on capital: 1.8×

Nuclear restart produces the best return profile per invested dollar for firm power. But the restartable universe is perhaps 8–12 reactors nationally.

## Limitations
1. **Restart universe is tiny.** Only reactors closed within the last ~decade with intact containment and salvageable components qualify. Maybe 8–12 in the US total.
2. **Hidden costs.** Palisades 1,000+ corroded steam generator tubes could escalate the $2B estimate. Tube sleeving is expensive and not guaranteed permanent.
3. **License clock.** Palisades license expires 2031 — only ~5 years of operation unless renewed. TMI's original license expired but can seek renewal.
4. **Holtec revenue declining.** Q1 2026 revenue -7% YoY, net income -30% YoY. Core storage/decom business shrinking even as IPO pitches growth.
5. **SMR track record.** The one SMR project that reached advanced stages (NuScale/UAMPS) ended up MORE expensive per MW than conventional nuclear.

## Strongest Counterargument
The nuclear restart thesis only works because taxpayers are footing much of the bill. Palisades has absorbed ~$3.1B+ in federal/state support for 800 MW — that's $3,875/kW in subsidies alone for a plant that may only operate 5 more years under its current license. Per year of guaranteed operation, the subsidy intensity is staggering: ~$775/kW/year, compared to wind's $26/kW production tax credit. The "cheap restart" isn't cheap — the cost is just distributed differently.

## Actionable Insight
For investors watching HNUC's eventual pricing: Holtec is the rare nuclear IPO with actual cash flow. If it prices below X-Energy's ~10× revenue multiple ($6.6B implied), it may represent value relative to pre-revenue nuclear peers. But watch the core business trajectory — a company whose existing revenue is declining while taking on its most capital-intensive project ever is a risk that won't appear in the IPO roadshow.
