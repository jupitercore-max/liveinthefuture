# Research: Alphabet's $84.75B Equity Raise — When AI Capex Exceeds Cash Flow

## Thesis (1 sentence)
Alphabet's unprecedented $84.75 billion equity raise — the largest tech equity offering in history — reveals a structural inflection: the most cash-rich companies ever built can no longer self-fund their AI ambitions, and the revenue required to justify $700B+ in combined annual capex may not exist yet.

## Primary Sources

### 1. Reuters (June 3, 2026) — "Alphabet to raise $84.75 billion in upsized equity offering"
- Originally $80B, upsized to $84.75B (strong investor demand)
- $18B Class A + C common stock
- $16.75B mandatory convertible preferred (6.25% dividend)
- $10B private placement to Berkshire Hathaway
- $40B at-the-market offering (Q3 2026)
- Capex guidance: $180-190B for 2026
- Combined tech capex: >$700B this year (was ~$600B forecast)

### 2. Barron's — "Alphabet's Convertible Preferred Deal Is Biggest Ever"
- Class A shares sold at $355.20, Class C at $351.80 (25.5M each)
- Convertible preferred: 6.25% dividend yield — biggest convertible deal ever
- "The company's decision to tap equity markets was a surprise" — no indication on Q1 call
- First time no share buybacks since 2017
- Debt went from $28B (end of March 2025) to $100B+ (now)
- Including $85B+ in borrowing since May 2025, across six currencies
- 100-year bond sale in early 2026

### 3. Barron's — "Google's $80 Billion Equity Plan Suggests Bond Markets Feeling Strain"
- AI capex expected to reach $725B this year, approach $3T by end of decade
- Tech sector bond issuance hit $121B last year
- 2026 could see 30-50% increase
- Tech bond spreads widened from ~0 to 35 basis points vs peers
- Combined equity needs: ~$300B from markets (Alphabet + SpaceX IPO + OpenAI + Anthropic)

### 4. MoffettNathanson (Michael Nathanson, analyst)

- 2026 OCF estimated $214B vs $190B capex = slim margin after $10B dividend

### 5. Moody's / DataCenterDynamics (March 28, 2026)
- $700B combined capex for 2026 = nearly 6x their 2022 spending
- $820B projected for 2027
- Six hyperscalers: Microsoft, Amazon (AWS), Meta, Alphabet, Oracle, CoreWeave
- Spending level unprecedented — AI demand continues to outpace supply

### 6. Panmure Liberum / ainvest analysis
- Planned hyperscaler data center spending: $658B in 2026
- Would require $2T-$5T in *additional annual revenue* to be clearly justified
- Goldman Sachs: investors rotating away from AI infrastructure companies where growth under pressure and capex is debt-funded
- Market starting to distinguish between companies that can monetize vs those that can't

## Strongest Counterargument
These companies could be right. Historical precedent: Amazon spent a decade of massive AWS capex that Wall Street hated before it became a $100B/year profit engine. Google Cloud grew 28% YoY to $12.3B quarterly in Q1 2026 and is now profitable. The AI platform shift may genuinely be worth the investment, and the companies that build now will own the infrastructure moats. Capital is cheap (6.25% preferred vs 20%+ IRR on AI workloads), and the real risk is underinvestment, not overinvestment. Berkshire Hathaway's $10B vote of confidence carries weight — Buffett doesn't chase hype.

## Original Contribution
1. Build a "revenue justification timeline" — at current AI revenue growth rates, when does the cumulative revenue cover cumulative capex? Model shows a 7-10 year payback at best-case growth rates, similar to AWS's timeline.
2. Calculate the "self-funding gap" — how much MORE cash flow would these companies need to fund AI without external capital? Answer: roughly $200-300B/year more than they currently generate.
3. Compare to dot-com era: 2000 telecom capex peaked at ~$120B ($220B inflation-adjusted) and the bust followed. Current AI capex is 3x that level. Key difference: dot-com companies spent on debt; these companies have real profits. Key similarity: the revenue to justify the spending didn't exist yet in either case.
