# Research: Fusion's Learning Curve Is Far Worse Than Anyone Assumed

## Core Thesis
A Nature Energy study from ETH Zurich estimates fusion power's experience rate at 2-8% — meaning costs drop 2-8% per doubling of deployed capacity. Current cost projections assume 8-20%. Solar's experience rate is 23%. At 2%, fusion needs 34 capacity doublings to halve its cost. Solar needed three. $15 billion in private fusion investment is priced to a learning curve that doesn't exist.

## Primary Sources

### 1. Tang et al. (2026) — Nature Energy
- **Title:** "Fusion power experience rates are overestimated"
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41560-026-02023-8
- **Authors:** Lingxi Tang, Bessie Noll, Tobias S. Schmidt (ETH Zurich)
- **Method:** Linked three technological characteristics (unit size, design complexity, customization needs) to empirical experience rates using innovation literature, supplemented by expert interviews
- **Key findings:**
  - Fusion experience rate: **2-8%** (magnetic confinement + laser inertial confinement)
  - Current literature assumes: **8-20%** (arbitrary or repurposed from other technologies)
  - Unit size: Large (similar to coal/fission plants — large units correlate with lower ERs)
  - Complexity: Extraordinarily high — experts said "literally off the scale"
  - Customization: Intermediate (less than fission due to simpler regulations, but more than modular tech like solar)
  - Conclusion: "raises questions about the viability of FPP in future net-zero energy systems"
  - Recommendation: Use lower ERs in cost projections, energy system modeling, and investor valuations
- **Source:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41560-026-02023-8

## Original Analysis (Novel Contribution)

### Capacity Doublings to Halve Cost
Formula: n = ln(0.5) / ln(1 - ER)

| Technology | Experience Rate | Doublings to Halve Cost |
|-----------|----------------|------------------------|
| Nuclear fission | 2% | 34.3 |
| Fusion (low end) | 2% | 34.3 |
| Fusion (high end) | 8% | 8.3 |
| Onshore wind | 12% | 5.4 |
| Li-ion batteries | 20% | 3.1 |
| Solar PV | 23% | 2.6 |

### What 10 doublings mean in real terms for fusion
- If first commercial plant is ~500 MW
- 10 doublings = 500 × 2^10 = 512,000 MW = 512 GW
- Global electricity capacity: ~8,500 GW
- So 10 doublings ≈ fusion providing 6% of global electricity
- At 2% ER after 10 doublings: cost drops to 81.7% of initial (18.3% reduction)
- At 8% ER after 10 doublings: cost drops to 43.4% of initial (56.6% reduction)
- At 23% ER (solar pace): cost drops to 7.3% of initial (92.7% reduction)

### Starting cost sensitivity
- CFS targets ~$50-80/MWh LCOE for its ARC plant
- Most estimates put first-of-a-kind fusion CAPEX at $8,000-15,000/kW
- Coal: ~$2,000-4,000/kW; Fission: ~$6,000-12,000/kW; Solar: ~$500-800/kW
- Even at 8% ER, fusion would need to deploy at massive scale before approaching today's solar costs

### The "China goes all-in" counterargument
- Kolemen's solar analogy is the strongest objection
- But solar had two structural advantages fusion lacks:
  1. Modularity — you can build a panel in a factory, not a reactor on a site
  2. Unit size — a solar panel is kW-scale; a fusion plant is GW-scale
  3. Manufacturing learning — solar benefits from semiconductor-style learning curves; fusion is closer to construction-project learning
- The ETH Zurich team specifically accounted for this: modularity drives high ERs, large bespoke construction drives low ERs
- No amount of Chinese investment changes the physics of building a tokamak
