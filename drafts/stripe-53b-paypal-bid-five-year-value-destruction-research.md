# Research Notes: Stripe + Advent $53B PayPal Bid

## Topic
Stripe and Advent International's joint $53B offer for PayPal — the financial mechanics of the deal, the value destruction math, and what the revenue multiple arbitrage reveals about where value actually lives in payments infrastructure.

## Original Calculation: Three-Part Analysis

### 1. The Value Destruction Clock
- PayPal market cap peaked at ~$360B (July 2021, ~$308/share)
- Current offer: $53B ($60.50/share)
- Value destroyed: $307B over ~1,826 days (Jul 2021 → Jul 2026)
- Rate: $168M per day / $7M per hour / $117K per minute
- 85% decline from peak (S&P 500 up 74% in the same 5 years)

### 2. Revenue Multiple Arbitrage
- Stripe revenue multiple: 31.1× (Feb 2026 valuation)
- PayPal revenue multiple: 1.3× (current market)
- Gap: 23.9× — largest revenue multiple gap in payments sector history
- PayPal FY2025 revenue: $33.2B
- At Stripe's multiple: $33.2B × 31.1× = $1.03T theoretical value
- At even 5× revenue (modest improvement): $33.2B × 5 = $166B — 3.1× the acquisition price
- The bet: Stripe isn't buying a $53B company. It's buying $33.2B in revenue at a 85% discount to its own implied pricing power.

### 3. LBO Mechanics
- Acquisition price: $53B
- Bank financing: $50B committed
- Implied equity: ~$3B (Stripe + Advent 50/50 → $1.5B each)
- Leverage: ~94% debt (extremely aggressive)
- Actually more likely: $50B is a credit facility, total debt may be $35-40B with $13-18B equity
- PayPal FCF: $6.4B (FY2025)
- At $40B debt, 6.5% blended rate: $2.6B annual interest
- Interest coverage: 6.4/2.6 = 2.46× (adequate)
- Residual FCF after debt service: $3.8B
- PayPal planned savings: $1.5B over 2-3 years
- With savings captured: $7.9B FCF, debt paydown timeline: ~5 years

## Strongest Counterargument
PayPal's 1.3× multiple isn't a market error — it's the market correctly pricing terminal decline. Branded checkout is being eaten by Apple Pay and Google Pay (Apple Pay's US transaction volume surpassed PayPal's in 2025). Braintree's massive 44% volume share generates only 8% of gross profit, meaning it's essentially a zero-margin loss leader. Venmo has never been durably monetized at scale. And the $6.4B in FCF is declining: Q1 operating margins contracted 229 basis points year-over-year. Stripe may be buying a business that generates less FCF each year, making the debt service math progressively worse.

## Limitations
- Stripe is private; all financial data are estimates or self-reported metrics without audit verification
- The $50B financing structure is unreported in detail — actual debt vs. equity split unknown
- PayPal has not responded to the offer; deal may never materialize
- Polymarket's 18% probability reflects market skepticism
- Combined TPV comparison to Visa/MC is imprecise — Visa/MC are networks, Stripe/PayPal are processors
