# Research: DOE Weapons-Grade Plutonium → Five Startups, Zero Operating Reactors

## Topic
The U.S. Department of Energy selected five nuclear startups (Oklo, SHINE, Standard Nuclear, Flibe Energy, Exodys Energy) for "advanced negotiations" to receive ~20 metric tons of Cold War-era weapons-grade plutonium as fuel for advanced reactors. The catch: none of these companies has a single operating power reactor. The material is enough for 2,500 nuclear weapons.

### 8. Energy Yield Calculation
- Pu-239 complete fission: ~23 GWh thermal per kg (Compound Chemistry / physics reference)
- At 33% thermal efficiency: ~7.6 GWh electrical per kg
- 20,000 kg × 7.6 GWh = 152 TWh theoretical maximum
- Real-world burn fraction varies: fast reactors 50-90%, thermal reactors 5-7% per pass
- Conservative estimate (fast reactor, 40% of theoretical): ~60 TWh
- MOX comparison: Savannah River project would have converted 34 MT into fuel for "15 million homes annually" → 20 MT proportional → ~9 million homes = ~95 TWh/yr
- US data center demand projection: ~500 TWh by 2028
- So 20 MT Pu = roughly 12-19% of one year's data center electricity demand
- Or: ~100-170 data centers at 100MW for one year

## Strongest Counterargument
Proponents argue that permanent disposal is the real waste of a resource. Pu-239's 24,000-year half-life means it won't decay on human timescales. Burning it in reactors reduces the total radioactive inventory and generates carbon-free electricity. The material is already a liability requiring perpetual guarding; converting it to fuel extracts value while reducing the stockpile. France has demonstrated that commercial MOX reprocessing works at scale (MELOX plant since 1995). And the alternative — burying it at WIPP for $18.2B — merely defers the problem underground without extracting any benefit.
