# GLP-1 Breast Cancer Prevention Research Notes

## Core Story
Two independent studies presented at ASCO 2026 found GLP-1 receptor agonists associated with reduced breast cancer incidence:

### Study 1: UPenn (McDonald et al.)
- Source: Oral abstract, ASCO 2026 Annual Meeting
- Published in ASCO abstracts (ascopubs.org)
- Population: 111,646 women aged 45-80, BMI ≥ 25, who had undergone breast cancer screening
- GLP-1 users: 15,264 (14%)
- Cancer rate GLP-1 users: 1.62%
- Cancer rate non-users: 2.31%
- Absolute Risk Reduction: 0.69 percentage points
- After propensity score matching (age, race, ethnicity, breast density, BMI, diabetes): 30% lower odds (P < .0001)
- Period: Jan 2022 to June 2025 (~3.5 years)
- Key strength: Restricted to screened women, eliminating detection bias
- Limitation: No family history adjustment; observational

### Study 2: UT Health San Antonio (Shaikh et al.)
- Source: Poster, ASCO 2026 Annual Meeting
- Population: 80,480 women at HIGH RISK for breast cancer (genetic predisposition, family history, dense breasts, high-risk lesions) with obesity
- GLP-1 users had 16% lower incidence (HR 0.84; 95% CI 0.78-0.92; P < .001)
- Used TriNetX global database

## Original Analysis: NNT/Cost Comparison

### GLP-1 as Cancer Preventive
- ARR = 2.31% - 1.62% = 0.69%
- NNT = 1/0.0069 ≈ 145 (over ~3.5 years)
- GLP-1 costs (branded/commercial): ~$12,000-$16,200/year (Wegovy list: ~$16,200; Ozempic: ~$12,000)
- Wegovy list: $1,350/month = $16,200/year (Reuters data: reducing to $675/month in 2027)
- Self-pay discount: $149/month = $1,788/year
- Medicare negotiated: ~$7,324/year (PMC study estimate)
- Medicare bridge: $50/month = $600/year (starting July 1, 2026)
- Cost per prevented case (at list): 145 × $16,200 × 3.5 = $8.22 million
- Cost per prevented case (at Medicare negotiated): 145 × $7,324 × 3.5 = $3.72 million
- Cost per prevented case (at self-pay): 145 × $1,788 × 3.5 = $907,110
- Cost per prevented case (at Medicare bridge): 145 × $600 × 3.5 = $304,500

### Tamoxifen as Cancer Preventive (NSABP P-1)
- 13,388 high-risk women, 5-year trial
- 49% reduction in invasive breast cancer
- NNT = 47 over 5 years
- Tamoxifen generic cost: ~$30-60/month = $360-720/year
- Cost per prevented case: 47 × $540/year × 5 years = $126,900
- BUT: significant side effects (endometrial cancer RR 2.5×, thromboembolism, cataracts)

### Breast Cancer Treatment Costs (comparison)

- Stage III: $129,387-$159,442
- Stage IV: $134,682-$182,655
- Weighted average all stages: ~$103,735 (24-month)

### The Paradox (the real insight)
GLP-1s will NEVER be cost-effective as a standalone cancer prevention strategy at commercial prices. But that's the wrong frame entirely.

These drugs are already being taken by ~12% of US adults (KFF) for weight management and diabetes. The cancer reduction is a FREE RIDER benefit — it costs nothing extra because the drug is already prescribed.

The real question isn't "Should we prescribe GLP-1s for cancer prevention?" It's "Should existing GLP-1 users get more aggressive cancer screening, and should the observed 30% risk reduction change how we model population cancer burden?"

At $85B+ in projected 2026 GLP-1 sales, ~42 million Americans could be on GLP-1s within a decade. If the 30% risk reduction holds, that's ~15,700 fewer breast cancer diagnoses per year (based on ~286,000 annual US breast cancer diagnoses, ~42M women potentially on GLP-1s out of ~166M US women, 30% reduction).

## Expert Quotes
- Neil Iyengar, MD (Winship Cancer/Emory): cancer prevention via GLP-1 is "an exciting new frontier" but needs RCTs
- Jennifer Ligibel, MD (Dana-Farber): weight loss probably "a big piece of it" but direct anti-inflammatory effects possible

## Mechanisms (for the article)
Three proposed pathways:
1. Weight loss → reduced fat mass → lower estrogen (postmenopausal) + lower inflammation
2. Direct GLP-1R activation → reduces inflammation (some autoimmune improvements independent of weight loss)
3. Adiponectin increase → dampens obesity-associated inflammation
Bariatric surgery precedent: dose-dependent cancer risk reduction (more weight loss = more protection)

## Strongest Counterargument
The observational confounding problem is real. Women who take GLP-1s are:
- More health-engaged (more likely to get screened, caught earlier)
- Wealthier (can afford $1,000+/month drugs, better overall healthcare)
- Under regular medical supervision (diabetes/obesity management)
Even with propensity matching, the "healthy user effect" is extremely difficult to eliminate. The UPenn team restricted to screened women to help, but it doesn't solve everything.

Also: 3.5 years of follow-up is SHORT for cancer. What if GLP-1s delay detection rather than prevent? Unlikely given the mechanism, but not ruled out.
