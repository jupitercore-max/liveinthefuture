# Research Notes: BCI Convergence — Five Implants, Three Weeks

## Story Angle
In a 21-day window (April 18 – May 7, 2026), five separate brain-computer interface platforms crossed clinical milestones simultaneously, spanning five different medical conditions. This is not a single breakthrough story; it is a convergence story. The medical trojan horse thesis — that BCIs will enter through clinical necessity and then expand to augmentation — is playing out in real time.

## Novel Contribution
Nobody has mapped these 5 milestones together or calculated the total addressable patient population shift. When BCI indications expand from ALS/paralysis (~27M globally for spinal cord injury, ~350K for ALS) to include stroke (~101M with lasting disability), treatment-resistant depression (~100M), and blindness (~43M), the addressable population jumps from ~27 million to ~270 million. That is the quantitative heart of this article.

## Primary Sources (5)

### 1. BrainGate iBCI Typing (April 18, 2026)
- **Source:** Nature Neuroscience (doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02218-y)
- **Coverage:** Technology.org (April 18, 2026)
- **Key data:** 22 wpm typing, 1.6% word error rate, 2 patients (one ALS, one cervical SCI)
- **Method:** Microelectrode sensors in motor cortex, QWERTY keyboard mapped to finger movements, 30-sentence calibration, predictive language model
- **Lead:** Dr. Daniel Rubin (Mass General Brigham), Dr. Leigh Hochberg (Brown University, BrainGate trial leader since 2004)
- **First author:** Justin Jude
- **Notable:** At-home use demonstrated, on par with able-bodied typing accuracy
- **Condition:** ALS, spinal cord injury

### 2. CorTec Brain Interchange (April 29, 2026)
- **Source:** GlobeNewsWire press release, CorTec GmbH
- **Key data:** First fully implanted wireless BCI with BOTH therapeutic stimulation AND thought-based computer control in same patient. Stroke patient played Pong within 2 hours of introduction. 500+ days continuous stable operation. FDA Breakthrough Device Designation.
- **Method:** AirRay electrodes on brain surface (no penetration), wireless, bidirectional. Same hardware for therapy and decoding.
- **Lead:** Dr. Frank Desiere (CEO), Dr. Martin Schuettler (CTO/Co-Founder), Prof. Jeffrey Herron (UW)
- **Trial:** NCT06506279, Early Feasibility Study, 9 months post-implant
- **Four clinical pillars:** stroke rehab (UW), epilepsy (Mayo Clinic), BCI for paralysis, depression (planned, Freiburg)
- **Condition:** Stroke

### 3. Motif Neurotech DOT Stimulator (May 6, 2026)
- **Source:** InnovationMap Houston, Rice University press release
- **Key data:** FDA approval for first clinical trial. Blueberry-sized device. Sits in skull above dura, no brain contact. Wirelessly powered. $18.75M Series A (oversubscribed). Selected for ARPA-H EVIDENT initiative ($139.4M total program).
- **Method:** Digitally-programmable Over-brain Therapeutic (DOT) stimulator, electrical stimulation to brain circuits linked to depression
- **Lead:** Jacob Robinson (CEO, Rice professor)
- **Partners:** Baylor College of Medicine, Mass General Brigham, Emory, UT Health Houston, UIowa, UUtah, NYU
- **Condition:** Treatment-resistant depression

### 4. ICVP Visual Cortex Prosthesis (May 7, 2026)
- **Source:** Interesting Engineering (May 7, 2026), Illinois Institute of Technology
- **Key data:** Third blind patient implanted. 34 stimulators, 544 electrodes. Wireless. Bypasses eyes entirely, directly stimulates visual cortex. Rush University Medical Center.
- **Lead:** Philip R. Troyk (PI, Pritzker Institute, Illinois Tech), Dr. Sepehr Sani (surgeon)
- **Partners:** Johns Hopkins Wilmer Eye Institute, University of Chicago
- **Follow-up:** 1-3 year tracking, training at Chicago Lighthouse's Hilton Center
- **Condition:** Blindness

### 5. Synchron Stentrode (ongoing, multiple milestones)
- **Source:** SWITCH trial 12-month data, NeurologyLive; Apple BCI HID integration (2025)
- **Key data:** Endovascular BCI through blood vessels (no craniotomy), 12-month safety with zero serious device-related adverse events. First BCI to integrate with Apple devices. ALS patient "Mark" controlled iPad via thought.
- **Method:** Stentrode implanted via jugular vein, self-expanding stent-electrode array
- **Lead:** Synchron Inc., Bruce Campbell (SWITCH trial)
- **Condition:** ALS, paralysis
