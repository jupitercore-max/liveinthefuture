# Research: Data Centers Are Driving the Biggest Electricity Price Spike in American History

## Slug

## Topic
PJM capacity auction prices increased 11.4x in two years ($28.92 → $329.17/MW-day), driven primarily by AI data center demand. Data centers account for 63% of the price increase. 67 million ratepayers across 13 states are subsidizing data center infrastructure through higher electricity bills — $9.3 billion in costs attributed to data center load forecasts alone.

## Original Calculations

### Per-household AI tax calculation
- PJM region: 67 million people ≈ 26.8 million households (2.5 people/household avg)
- Capacity price increase: $28.92 → $329.17/MW-day = $300.25/MW-day increase
- Total capacity cost increase from 2024/25 to 2026/27: $16.1B - $4.1B (estimated at old price) ≈ $12B/year
- Per household: $12B / 26.8M = ~$448/year
- Data center share (63%): $448 × 0.63 = ~$282/year per household attributable to data center demand
- Monthly: ~$23.50/month (aligns with reported $16-$21/month range)

### Cost comparison
- Average US household electricity bill: ~$145/month = $1,740/year
- Data center capacity surcharge: ~$282/year = 16.2% of annual electricity spend
- For comparison: average US household pays ~$500/year in property taxes to local schools
- The implicit data center electricity subsidy is 56% of what households pay for schools

### Virginia extreme case
- Virginia electricity prices up 267% in 5 years
- Northern Virginia = "Data Center Alley" — world's largest data center market
- Virginia household paying $200+/month in electricity that would have been ~$75/month five years ago

## Strongest Counterargument
Data centers create jobs, tax revenue, and drive innovation. Virginia data centers generated $1.2 billion in local tax revenue in 2024. Without capacity market signals, needed generation won't be built. The price spike is temporary — investment signals will attract new generation that brings prices down. PJM's own analysis shows 2,669 MW of new generation already responding. Markets working as designed.

## Limitations
- PJM capacity costs are only one component of electricity bills (also includes energy, transmission, distribution)
- 63% attribution to data centers is from Monitoring Analytics model, which uses counterfactual analysis (what would prices have been without data center load forecasts)
- Per-household cost varies enormously by state, utility, and rate structure
- Correlation between data center growth and price increases doesn't prove 1:1 causation — other factors include coal plant retirements, post-COVID demand recovery, and market rule changes
- PJM data center load forecasts may be inflated (IEEFA's own point), meaning actual costs could be lower
