# Research: CFS Becomes First Fusion Company to Apply to PJM Grid

## Story Hook
Commonwealth Fusion Systems filed a Generation Interconnection Request with PJM Interconnection on April 28, 2026, becoming the first fusion company to apply to a major grid operator. CFS's 400 MW ARC plant ("Fall Line Fusion Power Station") in Chesterfield County, Virginia aims to generate grid-connected electricity by the early 2030s. But between the application and the first electron lies a critical path with at least 7 sequential hurdles, most of which have never been attempted for fusion.

## Novel Contribution: The Seven-Gate Critical Path
Nobody has mapped the sequential gates between "PJM application" and "first fusion electron on grid." Each gate has a minimum timeline and failure modes:

| Gate | Milestone | Min Timeline | Status |
|------|-----------|-------------|--------|
| 1 | SPARC magnets installed (18/18) | Summer 2026 | 1/18 done (Jan 2026) |
| 2 | SPARC first plasma + Q>2 demo | 2027 | Not started |
| 3 | NRC fusion regulatory framework finalized | Late 2026-2027 | Proposed rule Feb 2026, comments May 2026 |
| 4 | ARC construction start | Late 2027-2028 | Zoning approved, site design ongoing |
| 5 | PJM interconnection studies (Feasibility, System Impact, Facilities) | 2-4 years from application | Applied Apr 2026 |
| 6 | ARC construction + commissioning | 3-5 years | ~$2.5B+ cost |
| 7 | Grid synchronization + commercial operation | After all above | Target: early 2030s |

**Original calculation:** Even on CFS's best-case timeline, the critical path to grid-connected fusion is ~6 years (2026 → ~2032). If SPARC underperforms (Q<1 instead of designed Q>2), the ARC design needs rework, adding 2-3 years. The PJM queue alone is a 4-6 year process even with reforms. CFS's strategy of applying now (before proving net energy gain) is deliberate: by the time SPARC demonstrates Q>2, the PJM studies will already be partially complete, running both tracks in parallel.

**Cost comparison:** At $2.5B+ for 400 MW, ARC's implied cost is ~$6,250/kW. For context:
- New nuclear fission (Vogtle): ~$17,000/kW (as built)
- Solar + storage: ~$1,500-2,000/kW
- Natural gas combined cycle: ~$1,000-1,200/kW
- ARC's $6,250/kW is competitive with fission but 3-4× more expensive than solar+storage

But ARC is a first-of-a-kind (FOAK) plant. CFS projects subsequent plants at significantly lower cost due to learning curve. The real bet: whether CFS can get from Plant #1 ($6,250/kW) to Plant #10 (<$2,000/kW).
