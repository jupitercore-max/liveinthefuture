3. Multiplied across Kalshi's launch contracts to produce a total "MNPI surface" number (3,260)
4. Applied this analysis specifically to evaluate the viability of Kalshi's employment verification safeguard

### Limitations Acknowledgment — ✅ PRESENT, GOOD QUALITY
- Acknowledges estimated ranges, not verified headcounts
- Notes MNPI counts could be higher or lower
- Acknowledges inability to verify Kalshi's actual verification methodology
- Flags that base-rate data is from 2006–2015 and that more recent data suggests higher approval rates
- This is a genuine limitations section, not a "of course, many factors are at play" dodge.

It makes a genuinely compelling case: drug-level price discovery is valuable, equity markets can't isolate single-drug events for diversified pharma companies, and real use cases exist (formulary hedging, generic timing, insurer cost modeling). The article then explicitly leaves the question open rather than dismissing it.

### Verifiability — ✅ STRONG
11 hyperlinked citations. Primary sources used for clinical trial data (company PRs, BIO study). Only weakness is the "40 to 60" insider trading figure lacking a specific citation.

### Methodology Transparency — ✅ GOOD
- MNPI table shows the full breakdown: role, count range, access level
- Base-rate table shows inputs (approval rates), adjustments (clinical data), and outputs (estimated fair prices)
- The "272 at conservative midpoint" claim is derivable from the table
