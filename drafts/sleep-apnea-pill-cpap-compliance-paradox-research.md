# Research Notes: Sleep Apnea Pill vs CPAP Compliance Paradox

## Topic
The first non-weight-loss pill for obstructive sleep apnea (AD109) has filed for FDA approval. It's half as effective as CPAP at eliminating breathing events — but the compliance math says it might reduce more disease burden at the population level because people will actually take it.

## Key Data Points

### Prevalence
- **936 million** adults globally with OSA (AHI ≥ 5) — Benjafield et al., Lancet Respiratory Medicine, 2019
- **425 million** with moderate-to-severe OSA (AHI ≥ 15) globally
- **80.6 million** in the US (2024 est.) — SLEEP 2025 abstract
- **80%** of OSA cases remain undiagnosed
- US prevalence: 32.2% of adults (39% men, 25.8% women)
- Severe OSA associated with **3.8x increased mortality risk** — Young et al., Wisconsin Sleep Cohort, 2008

### CPAP Compliance (the problem)
- Medicare "good" compliance: ≥4 hours on 70% of nights — CMS LCD L33718
- VA study (n=925): Only **30%** meet "good" compliance, **20%** excellent, **6%** outstanding
- Average CPAP use: ~4.3-4.6 hrs/night on 34-44% of nights
- **~8 million** Americans sleep with CPAP each night (manufacturer estimate)
- One-third of CPAP owners quit within months
- CPAP is 80% effective at eliminating apneic events WHEN USED

### Economic Burden
- Annual societal cost of untreated OSA in US: **$149.6 billion** (AASM/Frost & Sullivan 2016)
  - $86.9B lost productivity
  - $26.2B motor vehicle accidents
  - $6.5B workplace accidents
  - $30B healthcare utilization
- US workplace productivity losses: **$180.2 billion/year** (Thorax 2026)
- Per-patient healthcare excess: **$1,950-$3,899/year** more than non-OSA
- Untreated OSA = **2.5x** higher healthcare costs
- Crash-rate ratio: **1.21-4.89x** for OSA individuals — Tregear 2009

### CPAP Market
- CPAP devices market: ~$5.22 billion (2026) → $7.94B by 2031
- Broader sleep apnea devices: ~$8.79-9.43 billion
- Top 3 (ResMed, Philips, Fisher & Paykel): >70% market share
- ResMed revenue: ~$3.4B (5yr), market cap ~$40B+
- ResMed just sold MatrixCare for $490M to focus on sleep/breathing (July 2026)

### AD109 (Apnimed)
- **Mechanism:** aroxybutynin (antimuscarinic) + atomoxetine (NRI). Strengthens upper airway muscles. First-in-class.
- **Phase 3 SynAIRgy trial:** 646 adults, 69 sites US/Canada
  - AHI reduction: **44%** vs 18% placebo (p < .001)
  - 6-month duration
- **Phase 3 LunAIRo trial:**
  - AHI reduction: **46.8%** vs 6.8% placebo (p < .001)
  - Sustained through week 51
  - **22.9%** achieved complete disease control (AHI < 5)
- **Side effects:** dry mouth, nausea, insomnia, difficulty urinating
- **Discontinuation:** 21% (79% retention)
- **Regulatory:** FDA Fast Track designation; NDA submitted Q2 2026; PDUFA target Q1 2027
- **Financing:** $150M credit facility (HealthCare Royalty Partners), $100M + $50M milestone from Shionogi JV sale
- No pricing announced

### Zepbound/Tirzepatide (Eli Lilly) — already approved
- FDA approved **December 20, 2024** for moderate-to-severe OSA in adults with obesity
- First-ever drug approved for OSA
- Works via weight loss (GLP-1/GIP receptor agonist)
- SURMOUNT-OSA: 5x more effective than placebo; ~25-29 fewer breathing disruptions/hour
- Nearly **50% saw symptoms resolve** (AHI normalized)
- Cost: ~$1,060/month ($12,720/year)
- Only for obese patients (BMI ≥ 30), ~60-70% of OSA population
- Weekly injection

### Inspire Medical (nerve stimulator) — for context
- Hypoglossal nerve stimulator, surgically implanted
- FDA approved 2014
- ~$30,000-$40,000 procedure
- 68% AHI reduction (STAR trial)
- For moderate-to-severe OSA who can't tolerate CPAP

## THE ORIGINAL CALCULATION: The Compliance Paradox

### Formula
**Population-Level Disease Burden Reduction = Efficacy × Coverage Rate × Patients Reached**

### CPAP (current)
- Patients reached: 8M
- Coverage rate: 40% of nights × (4.5h / 7.5h sleep) = 24% of total sleep time
- When in use: ~100% AHI elimination
- Per-enrolled-patient burden reduction: 100% × 24% = 24%
- BUT only 30% meet "good" compliance. For the 70% non-compliant:
  - Estimated 10% of nights × 50% of sleep hours = 5% coverage
  - Burden reduction: 100% × 5% = 5%
- Weighted average per enrolled patient: (30% × 24%) + (70% × 5%) = 7.2% + 3.5% = **10.7%** 
  (being generous — many of the 70% quit entirely)
- Population ETEs: 10.7% × 8M = 856,000 effectively treated equivalents
- Share of 80.6M: **1.06%**

### AD109 (projected)
- 79% of patients continue taking it
- When taken: 45% AHI reduction across entire night (100% temporal coverage)
- Per-enrolled-patient burden reduction: 45% × 79% = **35.6%**
- This is **3.3x CPAP's per-enrolled-patient effectiveness**

### The crossover calculation
For AD109 to match CPAP's current total population benefit (856K ETEs):
856,000 = 35.6% × X patients
X = **2.4 million patients**

AD109 needs only **2.4 million users** to match the entire US CPAP installed base of 8 million.

### If AD109 captures the low-hanging fruit
- Diagnosed-but-untreated: ~8M (16M diagnosed minus 8M on CPAP)
- CPAP dropouts who'd try a pill: ~2-3M
- Conservative reach: 5M patients
- Burden reduction: 35.6% × 5M = 1.78M ETEs
- That's **2.1x the current CPAP effect**

## STRONGEST COUNTERARGUMENT (at full strength)

The SAVE trial (McEvoy et al., 2016, NEJM, n=2717): CPAP did not prevent cardiovascular events in patients with moderate-to-severe OSA. If CPAP's near-complete AHI elimination doesn't prevent heart attacks and strokes, then AD109's partial AHI reduction is even less likely to. The $150B economic burden is driven largely by cardiovascular complications and motor vehicle accidents. If improving AHI doesn't translate to reducing those endpoints, the entire economic case for AD109 collapses — you're selling symptom relief (less snoring, less daytime sleepiness) but not preventing the complications that generate the cost.

Counter to the counter: The SAVE trial had notoriously poor CPAP compliance (average 3.3 hours/night), which is the very problem AD109 addresses. And AD109's continuous overnight coverage may matter more than CPAP's partial coverage for cardiovascular protection.

## LIMITATIONS
- AD109 pricing unknown — if priced like specialty drugs ($500-1,000/month), cost-effectiveness changes dramatically
- Phase 3 followed patients for 51 weeks; long-term efficacy and safety data beyond 1 year doesn't exist
- 22.9% complete disease control means 77.1% still have residual OSA
- The 80.6M US prevalence estimate uses AHI ≥ 5, which includes many with mild OSA that may not need treatment
- GLP-1 drugs already have OSA approval — AD109's advantage over Zepbound for obese patients is unclear
- No head-to-head trial between AD109 and CPAP exists
