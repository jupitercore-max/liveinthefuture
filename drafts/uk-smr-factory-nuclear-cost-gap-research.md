## Core Thesis
The UK signed its first SMR contract on April 15, 2026 — 3 Rolls-Royce SMR units at Wylfa, North Wales. The "factory-built" pitch promises £3.3B/GW vs Hinkley Point C's £10.9B/GW. But the only country that tried to commercially deploy SMRs (the US with NuScale) watched the project collapse. The question isn't whether SMRs work in theory — it's whether factory economics can survive first-of-a-kind reality.

## Key Data Points
- **Rolls-Royce SMR Wylfa deal:**
  - 3 units, 1.4 GWe total (470 MWe each)
  - £2.6B allocated in 2025 Spending Review
  - Up to £599M from National Wealth Fund
  - Cost per unit: £2-3B (2024 estimate), up from £1.8B in 2019
  - 3,000 jobs at peak construction + 5,000 supply chain
  - Powers ~3M homes for 60+ years
  - 90% factory-built (claim)
  - Final investment decision: 2029
  - First power: mid-2030s
  - GDA started April 2022, expected completion ~2026
  - Also planning up to 6 units in Czech Republic

- **Hinkley Point C comparison:**
  - 3,200 MWe (2x EPR units)
  - £35B (Feb 2026 estimate), up from original £18B
  - = £10.9B/GW
  - Construction started 2017, Unit 1 target: 2030 (was 2023)
  - Strike price: £92.50/MWh (2012 prices)

- **NuScale CFPP (cancelled Nov 2023):**
  - 462 MWe (6x 77 MWe modules)
  - Cost escalated from ~$5.3B to $9.3B before cancellation
  - = $20.1B/GW (highest of any approach)
  - Subscribers dropped out due to cost
  - Only NRC-certified SMR design in the US

- **Cost per GW comparison:**
  - Hinkley Point C: £10.9B/GW (delivered/building)
  - RR SMR at £2B/unit: £4.3B/GW (low target)
  - RR SMR at £3B/unit: £6.4B/GW (high target)
  - NuScale (cancelled): ~£16B/GW equivalent
  - UK solar + battery: ~£1.0-1.5B/GW (LCOE basis, but intermittent)

- **RR SMR cost escalation already visible:**
  - 2019: £1.8B/unit
  - 2024: £2-3B/unit
  - That's a 39-67% increase before ground is broken

## Strongest Counterargument
The RR SMR is explicitly designed to avoid NuScale's mistakes. NuScale was building a first-of-a-kind on a greenfield site with no supply chain. Rolls-Royce has decades of nuclear engineering (submarine reactors), an established UK supply chain (80% domestic content), and a proven PWR design. The "factory-built" model becomes cheaper with each unit, and the Wylfa contract covers 3 units — providing learning curve benefits NuScale never reached.

## Limitations
- We don't know the actual contracted price per unit — the £2-3B range is from 2024 public estimates, not the April 2026 contract terms
- The Hinkley Point C comparison isn't apples-to-apples (EPR is a different, much larger reactor design)
- Solar+battery comparison ignores baseload reliability and grid integration costs
- Czech Republic deployment plans are MOUs, not binding contracts
