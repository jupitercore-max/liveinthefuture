## Original Calculation: Force Structure Multiplier

### Setup
- F-35A unit cost: $80M (recent lots)
- CCA Increment 1 unit cost: ~$25M (beating the 1/3 goal)
- AIM-120 AMRAAM: ~$1.37M per missile (DoD unit cost)
- F-35A internal missile stations: 4 AIM-120s
- CCA external missile stations: ~2 AIM-120s (YFQ-44A wing-mounted, conservative estimate)

### For a fixed $3B air combat procurement budget:

**Scenario A: Pure F-35**
- 37 F-35s × $80M = $2.96B
- Missile stations: 37 × 4 = 148
- Pilots at risk: 37

**Scenario B: CCA-Augmented (1:2 F-35:CCA ratio)**
- X F-35s + 2X CCAs, where 80X + 25(2X) ≤ 3000
- 130X ≤ 3000 → X = 23
- 23 F-35s ($1.84B) + 46 CCAs ($1.15B) = $2.99B
- Missile stations: 23×4 + 46×2 = 92 + 92 = 184
- Pilots at risk: 23

**Result:**
- 24% more missile stations (184 vs. 148)
- 87% more platforms (69 vs. 37)  
- 38% fewer pilots at risk (23 vs. 37)

### Attrition Math (10% force loss over one-week contested operation):

**Pure F-35:** Lose 3.7 aircraft = $296M + 3.7 pilots
**CCA-augmented (CCAs absorb disproportionate risk, 3:1 vs F-35):**
- Of 6.9 platforms lost: ~5.2 CCAs ($130M) + ~1.7 F-35s ($136M) = $266M total
- Pilots at risk: 1.7 vs 3.7 = 54% fewer

### Cost Per Missile Shot
- From F-35: $80M platform amortized over ~8,000 flight hours ÷ ~200 sorties/year × 20 years = per-sortie cost ~$20K airframe only + $1.37M missile = ~$1.39M per shot
- From CCA: $25M ÷ equivalent lifecycle = ~$6.25K per sortie + $1.37M missile = ~$1.376M per shot
- The missile costs the same either way — but you can afford to lose the launcher

### The Attritable Logic
The key insight: CCAs are designed to be *lost*. An F-35 is a $80M platform with an irreplaceable pilot. A CCA is a $25M expendable. In a contested Pacific scenario with layered air defenses (S-400, PL-15), the CCA absorbs the first salvo of SAMs — each SAM that destroys a CCA instead of an F-35 is a $55M savings and a pilot's life saved.

If the adversary fires a $3M S-400 missile at a $25M CCA instead of a $80M F-35, the exchange ratio still favors the CCA side by $52M.

## Counterargument
The strongest case against: CCAs are still unproven in contested electronic warfare environments. The autonomous software stack — sensor fusion, target identification, weapons employment — has never been tested against a peer adversary's electronic warfare. Ukraine shows that cheap drones work against an unprepared opponent, but a near-peer with sophisticated EW could render CCAs useless. An $80M F-35 with a trained pilot who can adapt in real-time may still outperform a $25M drone that can be jammed.

## Limitations
- CCA missile load is estimated (exact weapons capacity not officially disclosed)
- Unit cost "much better than $25M" per Helfrich but exact figure classified
- Attrition rates are illustrative, not from classified wargames
- CCA operational concepts (1:2 ratio, mission types) are still in development
- Sustainment costs unknown — could narrow the gap
