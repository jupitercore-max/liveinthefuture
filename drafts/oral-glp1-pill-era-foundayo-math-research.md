# Research: The Oral GLP-1 Era — Foundayo's Math

## Story Thesis
Eli Lilly's Foundayo (orforglipron) — the first oral, non-peptide, small-molecule GLP-1 agonist — was FDA-approved in April 2026 and drew 1,390 prescriptions in its first two days. It delivers less weight loss than injectable Wegovy (12.4% vs ~17%) but removes the two biggest barriers to GLP-1 adoption: needles and refrigeration. With 110+ million obese American adults and only ~6-7 million currently on GLP-1s, the oral form factor could expand the addressable market by 3-5x. The novel contribution: a patient expansion model and manufacturing cost analysis that nobody has run.

- 59.6% lost ≥10%, 39.6% lost ≥15% at highest dose

## Original Calculations

### Calculation 1: The Needle Aversion Gap
- Published research on injection aversion: ~20-30% of patients express significant needle anxiety (trypanophobia affects est. 25% of adults per Hamilton 1995, though clinical needle aversion in medication context is lower)
- Conservative estimate: 15-20% of GLP-1-eligible patients decline injectable therapy specifically due to needle aversion
- If 110M obese US adults, ~30% clinically eligible (BMI ≥30 or ≥27 with comorbidity who have tried diet), that's ~33M eligible
- Current injectable uptake: ~6-7M for obesity (rest of 18-25M are diabetes patients)
- If 20% of the 26M non-adopters were deterred by injection → ~5.2M new patients enter with oral option
- At $349/month → $21.8B/year incremental revenue opportunity from needle aversion alone

### Calculation 2: Manufacturing Cost Differential
- Small molecule oral pills: typical COGS $0.50-5.00 per pill for generic-class molecules
- Peptide injectables: typical COGS $30-100/month (sterile fill/finish, cold chain, pen devices)
- Orforglipron is a non-peptide small molecule → standard tablet manufacturing
- No refrigeration → eliminates cold chain logistics cost ($0.50-2.00/unit)
- No pen device → eliminates device cost ($2-8/unit)
- Estimated Lilly gross margin on Foundayo: >85% (vs ~70-80% on injectable biologics)
- This means Lilly can price aggressively and still earn more per patient than competitors on injectables

### Calculation 3: Efficacy Per Dollar
- Foundayo: 12.4% weight loss at $349/month = 0.036% per dollar/month
- Wegovy injection: ~17% at $349/month (cash-pay) = 0.049% per dollar/month
- Zepbound injection: ~22.5% at $449/month = 0.050% per dollar/month
- Injectable Wegovy delivers 37% more weight loss for the same price
- But: Foundayo requires zero injection, no refrigeration, no food/water restrictions
- The "convenience premium" question: is 37% less weight loss acceptable for the convenience of a daily pill?

## Strongest Counterargument
The efficacy gap is real and clinically meaningful. For severely obese patients (BMI >40), the difference between 12.4% and 22.5% weight loss could mean the difference between resolving diabetes and not. Physicians who see the sickest patients will likely still prefer injectables. Orforglipron's discontinuation rate (24.4%) is also high — nearly 1 in 4 patients stopped the drug. And the FDA's post-approval requirements signal meaningful safety uncertainty (heart attack, stroke, liver injury). The $1.5B stockpile could become a writedown if safety signals emerge.

## Limitations
- ATTAIN-1 is 72 weeks; no long-term (5+ year) data on oral small-molecule GLP-1
- Needle aversion estimates come from general surveys, not specific to GLP-1 population
- Manufacturing cost comparisons use industry averages, not Lilly's actual COGS (proprietary)
- Early prescription data (1,390 in 2 capture days) may reflect pent-up demand, not steady-state
- Head-to-head trials of orforglipron vs tirzepatide (both Lilly products) may never happen (competitive self-cannibalization)
