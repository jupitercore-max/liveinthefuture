## 🔬 Research Rigor — 9.0/10

**Novel contribution:** YES. The energy economics breakdown — showing exactly why 150 fusions returns 0.528, that break-even requires 284, that net electricity requires 860, and that the alpha-sticking ceiling caps at 200 — is original analysis presented for a general audience. No other coverage of the Science Advances paper has connected these numbers in this way. The gap between 200 (physics ceiling) and 860 (economics floor) is the article's core insight.

**Limitations:** Explicit section covering: muon production energy estimates range 5-10 GeV (calculation uses optimistic 5 GeV), Acceleron has not disclosed fusions-per-muon counts, alpha-sticking rates range 0.5-1% (pessimistic end makes even cheaper muons insufficient), and the Tokyo paper is spectroscopy not engineering.

States "it is possible that no amount of money solves" alpha-sticking. Notes Acceleron's cost targets "require particle accelerator innovations that do not yet exist in prototype form." Does not dismiss.

**Verifiability:** All key claims hyperlinked to checkable sources.

**Methodology transparency:** Table shows exact inputs, assumptions, and step-by-step calculation with sources column.
