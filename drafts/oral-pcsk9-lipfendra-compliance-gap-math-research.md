# Research Notes: First Oral PCSK9 Inhibitor — The Injection Tax Math

## Topic
Merck's LIPFENDRA (enlicitide) — first and only oral PCSK9 inhibitor — approved by FDA on July 16, 2026. The story isn't just the drug; it's the 30 million statin-inadequate patients the injection barrier has kept out of PCSK9 therapy, and what an oral pill at $315/month could change.

## Primary Sources

### 1. Merck FDA Press Release (July 16, 2026)
- LIPFENDRA (enlicitide) tablets 20 mg, once-daily oral PCSK9 inhibitor
- Approved for adults with hypercholesterolemia, including HeFH
- **CORALreef Lipids**: 2,912 participants; LDL-C reduced 56% vs placebo at 24 weeks (60% post-hoc adjusted)
- **CORALreef HeFH**: LDL-C reduced 59% vs placebo at 24 weeks
- Also reduced non-HDL-C, ApoB
- Safety similar to placebo in CORALreef Lipids; in HeFH: diarrhea 7% vs 2%, dizziness 9% vs 4%
- Must take on empty stomach, no eating for 30 minutes after
- Mechanism: small peptide that mimics monoclonal antibody action, binds PCSK9's LDL receptor docking site
- IC50: 2.5 ±0.1 nM
- **List price: $315/month ($3,780/year)**
- CV outcomes trial ongoing, won't complete until 2029

### 2. CORALreef AddOn Trial (Merck press release, July 7, 2026)
- Head-to-head comparison at 8 weeks
- Enlicitide vs bempedoic acid vs ezetimibe vs bempedoic acid + ezetimibe (all added to statins)
- LDL-C reduction:
  - Enlicitide: dominant (exact % reduction from baseline not given in results, but showed statistically significant superiority over all comparators)
  - Non-HDL-C: enlicitide reduced 58.0% vs bempedoic acid 5.2%, ezetimibe 25.1%, bempedoic acid+ezetimibe 31.8%
  - Lp(a): enlicitide reduced 26.2% vs bempedoic acid +8.1%, ezetimibe 0%, bempedoic acid+ezetimibe +10.4%
- Goal attainment (≥50% LDL-C reduction + LDL-C <55 mg/dL):
  - Enlicitide: 78.2%
  - Bempedoic acid: 2.0%
  - Ezetimibe: 8.0%
  - Bempedoic acid + ezetimibe: 20.0%

### 3. FOURIER Trial (Sabatine et al., NEJM 2017)
- 27,564 patients with stable ASCVD + LDL ≥70 mg/dL on statins
- Evolocumab (Repatha) vs placebo; median 2.2 years follow-up
- Primary composite (CV death, MI, stroke, unstable angina hospitalization, revascularization):
  - Evolocumab: 9.8% vs Placebo: 11.3%
  - HR = 0.85 (95% CI: 0.79–0.92)
  - **Absolute risk reduction: 1.5 percentage points over 2.2 years**
- Key secondary (CV death, MI, stroke):
  - 5.9% vs 7.4% (HR = 0.80, 95% CI: 0.73–0.88)
- LDL reduced 59% from median 92 mg/dL to 30 mg/dL
- Effect grew over time: 12% relative risk reduction in year 1 → 19% beyond year 1
- **BUT: Total mortality and CV death were numerically INCREASED in evolocumab group** (BMJ Open reanalysis)

### 4. PCSK9 Adherence Studies (PMC, real-world data)
- US real-world (13,151 adults, 2016): 57.4% persistent, 48.9% adherent at 182 days (6 months)
- Norway (4,784 patients): 17% had treatment gap >180 days within 3 years
- Italy (AT-TARGET-IT, 798 patients): 95.2% high adherence, 98.1% persistence at 12 months (clinical trial setting)
- Swiss study: 89% self-reported full adherence at 12 months
- China (5,373 patients): Mean PDC was 0.09 at 12 months (very low)
- Key finding: Initial 6 months is the critical drop-off period for injectable PCSK9i

### 5. Phase I/II Clinical Trial Data (Wikipedia sourced to primary publications)
- Phase I (60 healthy males): doses 10-300 mg reduced free plasma PCSK9 by >93%
- Phase I (statin-treated): 10 mg and 20 mg daily reduced LDL by 58.2% and 60.5% at 14 days
- Phase IIB (hypercholesterolemia): 6, 12, 18, 30 mg doses → LDL reductions of 41.2%, 55.7%, 59.1%, 60.9%
- Full efficacy reached after 2 weeks
- AEs comparable to placebo at all doses

### 6. Market Data
- **Current PCSK9 market (~2025-2026):**
  - Repatha (Amgen/evolocumab): $900M in Q1 2026 (up 34% YoY) → ~$3.6B annualized; ~$3.47B in 2025 (~41% market share)
  - Praluent (Regeneron/Sanofi, alirocumab): $260M US in 2025
  - Leqvio (Novartis/inclisiran, siRNA): $400M in Q1 2026 (up 76% YoY) → growing fast
  - Total PCSK9 market: ~$8.3B combined globally (2025)
  - Repatha/Praluent list at $500-600/month
  - Leqvio: ~$6,500/year list
- **Lipfendra pricing: $315/month = $3,780/year** (35-73% cheaper than injectables)
- **Peak sales estimates: ~$5B/year** (analyst consensus)
- Merck hopes Lipfendra replaces Keytruda revenue ($32B/year, patent expiry 2028)

### 7. Patient Population & Treatment Gap
- 46 million US adults recommended for statins (AMA/CDC)
- 24 million eligible but NOT on statins
- 70% of patients on statins haven't reached recommended LDL levels (Barron's)
- 15 million Americans with prior CV events; only 68% treated (Johns Hopkins study)
- Statin-inadequate population: ~30+ million Americans with LDL not at goal
- Currently only ~3-4 million on any PCSK9 inhibitor in US

## Original Calculation: "The Injection Tax"

**Question**: How many cardiovascular events has the injection barrier cost, and what could an oral pill change?

### The Setup
- FOURIER showed evolocumab (injectable PCSK9i) reduces MACE by 1.5 absolute percentage points over 2.2 years in high-risk ASCVD patients
- Annualized: ~0.68 percentage points per year → NNT ≈ 147 per year
- But only ~3 million Americans use PCSK9 inhibitors, out of ~30 million statin-inadequate patients
- The 27 million gap = patients who COULD benefit but don't, largely due to:
  1. Injection aversion
  2. Cost ($5,850-6,500/year)
  3. Prior authorization burden

### The Calculation

**Step 1: Events prevented with current PCSK9i uptake**
- 3 million patients on PCSK9i
- Annual events prevented: 3,000,000 / 147 NNT = ~20,400 MACE events per year
- Adjusted for real-world adherence (50%): ~10,200 events prevented per year

**Step 2: Events prevented if oral PCSK9i reaches 5 million additional patients**
(Conservative: that's 17% of the 30M statin-inadequate population)
- 5,000,000 new patients × (1/147 NNT) = ~34,000 additional events prevented per year
- Adjusted for oral adherence (assume 70%): ~23,800 events prevented per year
- CAVEAT: Must risk-adjust. FOURIER patients were high-risk ASCVD. Broader statin-inadequate pool includes primary prevention patients with lower baseline risk. Using half the FOURIER ARR for this broader population: ~11,900 events prevented per year

**Step 3: The "injection tax" — events lost to format**
- If the 27M untreated patients had PCSK9i access with full adherence: 27M / 147 = 183,673 events per year (at FOURIER rates)
- Risk-adjusted (half the FOURIER rate for mixed primary/secondary prevention): ~91,837
- Minus current 10,200 actually prevented = ~81,637 events per year lost to the combination of injection barrier, cost, and access
- The injection barrier alone (injection-averse patients who would take a pill): estimated 30-50% of the gap = ~25,000-41,000 events per year

### Key Insight for Article
The injection format has imposed a hidden cardiovascular tax: tens of thousands of preventable heart attacks and strokes per year foregone because patients who won't take a needle could have taken a pill. Lipfendra is the first crack in that wall — but without CV outcomes data (due 2029), insurers may not cover it broadly enough to close the gap.

### The Keytruda Clock
- Keytruda patent expiry: 2028
- Keytruda 2025 revenue: ~$32B
- Lipfendra peak estimate: ~$5B
- Gap: Lipfendra fills only ~15.6% of the Keytruda revenue hole
- Merck's pipeline needs 5-6 more Lipfendras to replace Keytruda

## Strongest Counterargument
1. **No CV outcomes data yet.** Lipfendra has only shown LDL reduction, not actual heart attack/stroke prevention. The outcomes trial ends 2029. LDL is a surrogate marker. The FOURIER reanalysis showed numerically increased total mortality and CV death in the evolocumab arm. Until Lipfendra proves outcomes reduction, the entire "injection tax" calculation rests on inference from surrogate endpoints.
2. **Oral adherence isn't necessarily better.** Statin adherence at 12 months is only ~50%. The injection barrier is real, but pills have their own compliance problems (daily dosing fatigue, empty-stomach requirement, 30-minute fasting window).
3. **AstraZeneca is developing a competing oral PCSK9i** — Lipfendra's market advantage may be short-lived.

## Limitations for Article
- Original calculation extrapolates injectable PCSK9i outcomes data to an oral drug that hasn't demonstrated cardiovascular outcomes reduction
- Patient population modeling uses US-centric data; global dynamics differ
- Adherence comparisons between injectable and oral formats are estimated, not measured head-to-head
- The "injection tax" calculation assumes equivalent efficacy between injectable and oral PCSK9i, which hasn't been proven in outcomes trials
- Cost analysis uses list prices; net prices after rebates may differ significantly
