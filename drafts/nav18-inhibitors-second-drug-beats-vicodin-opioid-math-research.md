# Research: The Second Nav1.8 Inhibitor Just Beat Vicodin in a Clinical Trial. Here's the Math on What That Means.

## Core Source
- **Paper:** Phase 2b Trial of a NaV1.8 Inhibitor for Acute Pain — NEJM (published ~July 29, 2026)
- **Drug:** LTG-001 (Latigo Biotherapeutics, Thousand Oaks, CA)
- **Trial:** Double-blind, randomized, placebo- and comparator-controlled, 343 patients, abdominoplasty pain model
- **Lead Investigator:** Harold Minkowitz, M.D. (HD Research / ERG Research network; also UT MD Anderson)
- **CMO:** Neil Singla, M.D.
- **Context:** Only the SECOND original research article in NEJM reporting clinical results for a novel acute pain drug in 15 years

## Key Data Points

### Trial Design
- 343 participants with moderate-to-severe pain after abdominoplasty
- 4-arm randomization (1:1:1:1):
  - High-dose LTG-001: 450 mg loading → 300 mg q12h
  - Low-dose LTG-001: 300 mg loading → 150 mg q12h
  - Opioid comparator: Vicodin (5 mg hydrocodone / 325 mg APAP q6h)
  - Placebo
- Primary endpoint: SPID48 (Summed Pain Intensity Difference over 48 hours) vs placebo

### Results
- High-dose LTG-001 hit primary endpoint with high statistical significance
- ~50% greater analgesic effect than Vicodin (per Reuters Aug 5 coverage)
- 51% of high-dose group remained OPIOID-FREE for 48 hours (vs 22% placebo)
- Rapid onset of analgesia
- Favorable tolerability
- "Strongest effect ever reported in a study of non-opioid painkillers versus placebo for abdominal surgery"

### Mechanism: Nav1.8 Inhibition
- Nav1.8 = voltage-gated sodium channel found in peripheral pain-sensing neurons (nociceptors)
- Located in nerve tissue, NOT the brain → no addiction potential
- Selectively blocks pain signal transmission without CNS effects
- Part of a NEW CLASS of non-opioid analgesics

### Class Context
- **Journavx (suzetrigine):** First Nav1.8 inhibitor approved by FDA, January 2025 (Vertex Pharmaceuticals)
- **LTG-001:** Second Nav1.8 inhibitor with positive clinical data (Latigo Biotherapeutics)
- Both target same mechanism, validating Nav1.8 as a drug class, not a one-off

## Original Calculations

### 1. The Surgical Opioid Pipeline
- US surgical procedures/year: ~50 million (ACS/CDC estimates)
- Post-surgical opioid prescription rate: ~70% in US/Canada (vs 11% in Sweden)
  - Source: Cohort study cited in PMC/Br J Anaesth 2024 review
- That's ~35 million patients/year receiving surgical opioid prescriptions in the US alone

### 2. From Surgery to Addiction: The Conversion Rate
- New persistent opioid use after surgery: 5.9-6.5% (2017-2022 TRICARE data)
  - Down from 11% (2017-19) to 6% (2020-22) due to prescribing reforms
- Applied to 35M opioid-exposed surgical patients: ~2.1 million develop new persistent use per year
- Long-term (>180 days) use: ~3.1% of previously opioid-naive surgical patients

### 3. The Nav1.8 Impact Calculation
- LTG-001 kept 51% of patients opioid-free for 48 hours (vs 22% placebo)
- Net opioid avoidance: 29 percentage points
- If Nav1.8 inhibitors replace opioids as first-line post-surgical analgesia for 35M patients:
  - 29% additional patients never start opioids = ~10.15 million fewer opioid-exposed patients/year
  - At 6% new persistent use rate: ~609,000 fewer new long-term opioid users per year
  - Cost of opioid use disorder: ~$78,000/year per person (NIDA estimate)
  - Annual healthcare savings: ~$47.5 billion

### 4. The Mortality Calculation
- US opioid overdose deaths: ~81,000 in 2023 (CDC WONDER)
- Surgical prescribing estimated gateway for ~21% of opioid use disorder cases (JAMA Surgery 2017)
- 21% × 81,000 = ~17,000 deaths/year with surgical opioid origin
- If Nav1.8 inhibitors prevent 29% of surgical opioid exposure: ~4,930 lives saved per year

### 5. The Class Validation Math
- Journavx (Vertex, approved Jan 2025): First Nav1.8 approval
- LTG-001 (Latigo, NEJM Jul 2026): Second Nav1.8 with positive Phase 2b
- Two drugs, same mechanism, both work → reduces "one-off" risk to near-zero
- Historical precedent: When ACE inhibitors validated as a class (captopril 1981 → enalapril 1985), they replaced a $2B/year antihypertensive market within a decade

## Limitations
- Phase 2b, not Phase 3: 343 patients is convincing for a proof of concept but doesn't prove commercial viability
- Abdominoplasty is a specific pain model — may not generalize to all surgical pain
- 48-hour window: does the opioid-sparing effect persist beyond 48 hours?
- LTG-001 vs Journavx head-to-head: no data yet on which Nav1.8 inhibitor is better
- Cost of LTG-001 unknown — if priced like Journavx, accessibility concerns remain
- The 51% opioid-free number is encouraging but 49% still needed rescue opioids

## Strongest Counterargument
The strongest case against reading too much into this trial: abdominoplasty is a relatively mild pain model compared to thoracotomy or major orthopedic surgery where opioids are most commonly prescribed long-term. The 8.5% persistent opioid use rate after open thoracic surgery (BMJ 2014) vs ~3% after abdominal surgery suggests the hardest cases haven't been tested yet. Nav1.8 inhibitors may work well for moderate pain but fail where opioids are most entrenched — in the severe post-surgical pain that creates the most addictions.
