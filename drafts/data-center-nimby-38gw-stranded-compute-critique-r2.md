## Critic Scores

### 1. 🔍 General Editor — 8.5/10 ✅

**Strengths:**
- Exceptionally well-sourced with 8 primary sources (Bernstein, Reuters, JLL, Cleanview, CNBC, Gallup, Data Center Tracker, governor's office). Every major claim is hyperlinked and quantified.
- The Hochul Paradox section is genuinely compelling narrative journalism — juxtaposing the same governor's Empire AI announcement against her moratorium two years later crystallizes the national contradiction in a single character.
- The six-regime regulatory taxonomy, now properly formatted as a bulleted list with bold labels, is an original analytical framework that gives the piece lasting reference value beyond the news hook.

**Critiques:**
- The "What You Can Do" section packs three audiences into three enormous sentences (~50 words each). Each audience gets one breathless construction. Breaking into shorter sentences per audience would make the actionable advice more scannable.
- The NASA budget comparison ("exceeds NASA's entire cumulative budget from Apollo through the Artemis program") is ambiguous — NASA's total spending since 1958 is ~$1.3T adjusted, but the combined Apollo + Artemis program budgets are closer to $350-400B. The claim is defensible under one reading but misleading under another; a parenthetical "(roughly $350 billion in today's dollars)" would lock it down.

**Strengths:**
- Zero banned phrases. Zero "However" transitions. No delve/leverage/nuanced/pivotal/robust/harness/cornerstone/underscores. The piece consistently finds its own language.
- Rhythm check passes all three criteria cleanly: variance 214.3 (≥200), short% 10.5 (≤15), long% 48.8 (≥15). Good histogram spread across all bins with no empty buckets, including 3 sentences under 5 words and 22 sentences over 31 words. The piece breathes.

### 3. ⚖️ Ethics Reviewer — 8.5/10 ✅

**Strengths:**
- "The Strongest Case for the Ban" section is a genuine steelman backed by specific data: 30% rate increases (JLL), 2% local labor share, Pennsylvania land-deal asymmetry (96 households/$500M while neighbors get nothing), water consumption (20-30M gallons/yr = 45 Olympic pools), and the voluntary pledge with no enforcement. Concluding "They have a case" earns credibility the article would not otherwise have.
- The closing paragraph refuses to adjudicate: "Whether you view this as democratic self-defense or as a generational infrastructure failure depends on where you sit, but the math does not care about your position." This frames the dispute as a legitimate values conflict rather than declaring one side irrational.
- Polling data (Reuters/Ipsos 1-in-3 approval, Gallup 70% opposition) gives democratic legitimacy to the resistance without dismissing it.

**Critiques:**
- The headline uses "Compute NIMBYs" — a pejorative that frames community opposition as parochial selfishness before the reader encounters the body's more balanced treatment. The article's body largely earns its balanced credentials, but the headline front-loads a value judgment. (Editorially defensible as a widely-used policy term, but worth flagging.)
- Environmental case is present but thin relative to the economic analysis — water gets one sentence, while carbon emissions, noise, heat island effects, and habitat disruption are absent. For an article that later says "the industry internalizes its externalities," the environmental externality catalog is incomplete.

### 4. 📱 Social/Shareability — 9.0/10 ✅

**Strengths:**
- Headline is elite-tier viral: "38 GW frozen vs 15 GW operating" is an instant-share juxtaposition that works as a standalone tweet. The stat-bomb structure (big number + context number) compels engagement before the click.
- Multiple independently shareable data points: "$380 billion in frozen investment," "2.5× more compute than they currently run," "70% of Americans oppose," same governor/24 months apart. Different readers will latch onto different facts, which broadens the share surface.
- Two-sided framing doubles the audience: pro-development readers share to highlight NIMBY obstruction, anti-development readers share "The Strongest Case for the Ban" as validation. Both camps get ammunition.

**Critiques:**
- No shareable data visualization — the 38 GW vs 15 GW comparison is begging for a simple bar chart that could circulate independently on social media. The article relies entirely on prose.
- At 9 minutes, the piece is 2-3× the optimal length for social-driven reads. Most social referral traffic bounces within 2 minutes, so the strongest material needs to appear very early.
- Missing a human character — the Pennsylvania land-sale stat (96 households, $330K/acre) is begging for a one-sentence vignette with a name or quote that makes the stakes visceral.

### 5. ⚖️ Legal Accuracy — 8.5/10 ✅

**Strengths:**
- Correctly and consistently characterizes Hochul's action as an "executive order" imposing a "one-year moratorium" with a specific threshold (50 MW+). The Generic Environmental Impact Statement process is accurately described.
- Properly distinguishes between regulatory instruments: moratoriums (NY), vetoes (Maine), consumption taxes (Virginia), ballot bans (Monterey Park), and geographic restrictions (Texas). Each is correctly characterized with its specific mechanism.
- The March Ratepayer Protection Pledge is accurately described as "voluntary" with "no enforcement mechanism" — correctly distinguishing between voluntary industry commitments and binding regulatory instruments.

**Critiques:**
- The headline and deck use "ban" while the body correctly uses "moratorium." These are legally distinct instruments (temporary vs permanent); the headline conflates them for punch. Standard in journalism but a precision issue.
- The Amsterdam reference ("joining Amsterdam's 2022 ban") is a secondary point in a U.S.-focused piece. The Amsterdam moratorium has been extended multiple times but its legal character is different from U.S. executive orders — calling it a "ban" may overstate its permanence.

### 6. 🔬 Research Rigor — 8.5/10 ✅

**Strengths:**
- Novel analytical contribution: the 2.5× ratio of stranded-to-operating capacity, the $380B estimate, the $38-76B/yr foregone revenue range, and the 46% YoY growth calculation (26→38 GW) are original derivations from Bernstein source data that no other outlet has published. This is analysis, not aggregation.
- The Limitations section is exemplary for tech journalism: it flags the relocation-vs-permanent-block ambiguity in the 38 GW figure, the $5-15M/MW cost range, the scope mismatch between 15 GW operational and 50.7 GW pipeline figures, and explicitly notes the 2.5× comparison is "conservative by one measure and generous by another."
- Source diversity is strong: Bernstein (investment research), Reuters (news wire), JLL (commercial real estate), CNBC (financial media), Gallup/Ipsos (polling), state government press releases, and the Data Center Tracker database (grassroots opposition tracking). No single-source dependency.

**Critiques:**
- The "1,200 public actions" figure has no denominator. Out of how many proposed data center projects? A 10% opposition rate and a 90% opposition rate could both produce 1,200 actions depending on the project count. The raw number looks impressive but is uninterpretable without base rate context.
- The NASA budget comparison needs verification — the claim that $380B "exceeds NASA's entire cumulative budget from Apollo through the Artemis program" is ambiguous and potentially inaccurate depending on how "cumulative budget" and inflation adjustment are interpreted.

**Critiques:**

Other high-impact numbers (2.5×, $38-76B/yr, 70% opposition, 46% YoY growth) get no typographic emphasis, pull-quote treatment, or data-callout formatting. A data-dense 9-minute piece needs more scannable entry points.
- Historical baselines are now present for pipeline clearance (6-8→10→12 years) and transformer lead times (40-50→80-100 weeks), which is good. But the "1,200 public actions" still has no denominator, and the Pennsylvania stats (96 households, $500M, $330K/acre) and 2% local labor figure remain without source attribution in a piece that otherwise cites every claim.
