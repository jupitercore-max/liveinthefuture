# Research: Factorial Semi-Solid-State Battery Road Test — First North American EV Integration

## Story Angle
Solid-state batteries have been "5 years away" for 15 years. LITF's own broken-promises scorecard tracked the repeated missed deadlines. But this week, Stellantis put a Dodge Charger Daytona development vehicle on public roads with Factorial Energy's FEST cells inside — the first integration of solid-state (technically semi-solid-state) battery technology into a real EV in North America. The question: does "semi-solid" count, and what does the fine print say about the production timeline?

## Kill Test: PASS
- Real milestone: an actual car driving on actual roads with actual next-gen cells. Not a press release, not a lab demo, not a simulation.
- But the "semi-solid-state" distinction matters enormously: Factorial's FEST chemistry retains small amounts of liquid electrolyte. True all-solid-state (Toyota, QuantumScape) remains undriven in any production vehicle.
- Original contribution: compare Factorial's cell-level claims to pack-level reality; cost the Wh/kg gap between FEST and conventional NMC/LFP at pack level; timeline stress-test against LITF's broken-promises scorecard.

## 10-Star Test
- **Original calculation**: pack-level energy density discount (cell-level 375 Wh/kg → estimated pack-level ~250-275 Wh/kg after thermal management, BMS, structural overhead), compared to Tesla 4680 pack (~180-190 Wh/kg), CATL Qilin (~255 Wh/kg)
- **Comparison table**: 6 solid-state battery startups (Factorial, QuantumScape, Solid Power, Samsung SDI, Toyota/Panasonic, ProLogium) by cell chemistry, Wh/kg, cycle life, max charge rate, furthest integration milestone, and production date claim
- **Data point**: Factorial IPO via SPAC — FAC on Nasdaq at $13.80, implied valuation vs revenue (pre-revenue company, compare to QuantumScape's trajectory from $47B peak to ~$2B today)
- **Timeline stress test**: map each company's original production promise vs current status, using LITF broken-promises methodology
- **Counterpoint**: GM simultaneously pivoting from LFP to LMR (lithium manganese-rich) — suggests the "next chemistry" might not be solid-state at all but a better liquid-electrolyte variant

## Key Data Points

### Factorial FEST Cells
- Energy density: 375 Wh/kg (cell level)
- Charge speed: 15% to 90% in 18 minutes
- Cycle life: 600+ validated cycles (77 Ah cells, April 2025 validation)
- Temperature range: -22°F to 113°F (-30°C to 45°C)
- Discharge rate: up to 4C
- Chemistry: semi-solid-state (FEST = Factorial Electrolyte System Technology — small amounts of liquid electrolyte remain)
- IPO: SPAC merger with Cartesian Growth Corp III completed June 2026, trades as FAC on Nasdaq
- Partners: Stellantis, Hyundai, Kia, Mercedes-Benz, Karma
- Production timeline: batteries for Karma supercar in 2027-2028

### Stellantis Integration
- Vehicle: Dodge Charger Daytona development vehicle (STLA Large platform)
- Status: road testing and calibration, North America, June 2026
- Pack design: patented mechanical architecture to extract max performance
- Quote: "This milestone shows we are bringing solid-state batteries closer to our customers" — Ned Curic, Stellantis CTO

### GM LMR Pivot
- GM battery chief Kurt Kelty: "There is a possibility where LFP does not earn its way into our portfolio"
- LMR (lithium manganese-rich): ~same cost as LFP in US, higher energy density
- Tennessee factory starting LFP production June 2026 — but for energy storage, not EVs
- Ford also working on LMR for future EVs
- Challenge: LMR cells degrade faster with use (S&P Global assessment)

### NOVONIX/Panasonic
- First North American synthetic graphite anode C-sample delivered to Panasonic (June 11, 2026)
- Critical for US battery supply chain independence from China
- Mass production target: H2 2027

### Competitive Landscape
- QuantumScape: true solid-state (lithium-metal anode, ceramic separator), shipped B samples to VW/PowerCo, stock collapsed from $47B to ~$2B
- Solid Power: sulfide-based solid electrolyte, BMW/Ford partnerships, also pre-revenue
- Samsung SDI: targeting 2027 for "all-solid-state" pilot line
- Toyota: largest solid-state patent portfolio, targeting 2027-2028 production with Prime Planet Energy (joint venture with Panasonic)
- ProLogium: Taiwan-based, Gigafactory in France, shipping to Mercedes
- CATL: condensed battery (semi-solid) already shipping in Nio ET7 (150 kWh pack, 1,000 km range claim)
