# Research: Fervo Energy IPO — Fracking Tech Goes Clean

## Story Angle
Fervo Energy filed for a $6.5B IPO to build the first commercial enhanced geothermal plant in the U.S., using hydraulic fracturing technology from oil & gas. The original contribution: calculating what the IPO valuation actually implies about investor confidence — the $6.5B valuation is LESS than the company's $7.2B contracted revenue backlog, meaning the market isn't even pricing in the 42 GW of theoretical capacity. Plus: a per-MWh cost comparison between geothermal at 90% capacity factor and solar+storage at 25%.

- Enhanced Geothermal Systems (EGS): horizontal drilling + hydraulic fracturing
- Borrowed from shale oil/gas revolution
- 70% reduction in per-foot drilling costs (2022-2025) — S-1 filing

## Original Analysis

### Valuation vs Revenue Backlog
- IPO valuation: $6.5B
- Contracted revenue backlog (binding PPAs): $7.2B
- Ratio: 0.90× — investors paying LESS than 1× contracted revenue
- Interpretation: market prices in significant execution risk; giving zero credit for 42 GW theoretical

### Implied Valuation Per MW
- Per operating MW (3 MW): $2.17B/MW (meaningless, all pipeline)
- Per contracted MW (658 MW): $9,878/kW
- Per pipeline MW (3,650 MW): $1,781/kW
- Per theoretical MW (42,000 MW): $154/kW
- Compare: Fervo's own construction cost = $7,000/kW → $3,000/kW target

### Capacity Factor Advantage
- Geothermal: ~90% capacity factor (24/7 baseload)
- Solar: ~25% capacity factor
- Wind (onshore): ~35% capacity factor
- 1 GW geothermal = 3.6 GW solar on energy-equivalent basis
- Annual output from 3.65 GW pipeline at 90% CF: ~28.8 TWh
- At $50/MWh: $1.44B annual revenue potential
- $6.5B valuation / $1.44B = 4.5× revenue multiple (comparable to NextEra ~5×)

### Cost Per MWh Comparison (levelized, 30-year life)
- Geothermal at $7,000/kW, 90% CF, 30yr: ~$30-40/MWh (no fuel cost, minimal O&M)
- Solar + 4hr battery storage: ~$45-65/MWh (Lazard 2025)
- Nuclear (new build): ~$80-120/MWh
- Natural gas combined cycle: ~$50-70/MWh (plus fuel volatility)
- Geothermal at $3,000/kW target: ~$15-20/MWh — would be cheapest baseload ever
