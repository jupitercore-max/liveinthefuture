# Research: CRISPR-Edited "Invisible" Cells Survive 14 Months Without Immunosuppression

## Story Angle
One man with Type 1 diabetes received CRISPR-edited islet cells injected into his forearm — no immunosuppression drugs, no rejection, functioning insulin production for 14+ months. This is the first proof in a human that gene editing can make transplanted cells invisible to the immune system. The math on what this means for 103,000+ people on the organ transplant waitlist and 1.6 million Americans with T1D.

## Original Analysis (LITF Kill Test)
1. **Lifetime cost calculation:** T1D management costs $18,817/year per patient. Over 60 years (diagnosis ~age 10, life expectancy ~70), that's $1.13M per person. A one-time cell therapy priced at $500K saves ~$630K in NPV terms.
2. **The 7%-to-100% dose scaling math:** 79.6M cells = 7% of therapeutic dose. Full dose ≈ 1.14 billion cells. From cadaveric donors, you need approximately one donor pancreas per patient. With 1.6M T1D Americans and ~8,000 donor pancreases available per year, the cadaveric route takes 200 years. SC451 (stem cell-derived) is the only path to scale.
3. **Platform multiplication:** The same three-gene edit (B2M KO + CIITA KO + CD47 overexpression) that works for islets works for any cell type. Two CRISPR-edited organ transplant trials (liver NCT07053488, kidney NCT07053462) are now recruiting. If HIP works for whole organs: 103,223 people on the transplant waitlist, 17 dying per day.

### 5. Patient Data (from NEJM + Medscape)
- 42-year-old man, 37-year history of T1D
- HbA1c 10.9% at baseline, no measurable C-peptide
- CRISPR-Cas12b editing + lentiviral CD47 overexpression
- Gene edits: B2M knockout (no HLA class I), CIITA knockout (no HLA class II), CD47 overexpression ("don't eat me" signal)
- Product: UP421 — mix of fully edited hypoimmune islet cells + some partially edited + residual wild-type
- 79.6 million engineered islet cells implanted into forearm brachioradialis muscle via 17 microinjections
- NO immunosuppressive drugs given
- Result at 12 weeks: Wild-type cells rejected (strong T-cell response), HIP cells caused ZERO T-cell activation, ZERO NK/macrophage killing, ZERO antibody induction
- C-peptide: stable basal + glucose-responsive on mixed-meal tolerance tests at weeks 4, 8, 12 (zero at baseline)
- PET-MRI confirmed viable, endocrine-active islets at transplant site
- HbA1c decreased ~42% (driven mainly by exogenous insulin)
- Four mild adverse events, none serious or drug-related
- 14-month follow-up: cells still alive, still producing insulin, still immune-silent

## Counterargument (Strongest Case Against)
Single patient, 7% of therapeutic dose, cadaveric donor cells (not scalable), 14 months (not decades), no efficacy data (HbA1c improved but from exogenous insulin increase), Sana has $101M cash and burns $37M/quarter (mid-2027 runway), stock at $3.15 (down from $6.55 52-week high). SC451 (the stem cell version) hasn't entered humans yet — the hardest part (manufacturing a stable, tumor-free master cell bank from gene-edited iPSCs) is "still a science project" per Sana's own CEO. Genomic instability after gene editing can cause tumor-forming mutations.

## Limitations
- Single-patient case report, not a randomized trial
- 7% dose — proof of survival, not proof of cure
- Forearm implantation (not hepatic portal vein, the standard site) — chosen for safety/monitoring, may not be optimal for full-dose therapy
- Unknown long-term durability beyond 14 months
- Manufacturing scale-up from cadaveric to stem cell-derived is the critical bottleneck
- CD47 overexpression may interfere with cancer immunosurveillance (theoretical risk)
- Sana's financial position requires further funding to reach Phase 1 with SC451
