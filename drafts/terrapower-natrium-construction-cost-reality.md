# Research: TerraPower Natrium Construction Start — Cost Reality Check

## Thesis
Bill Gates' TerraPower started construction on the first commercial non-light-water reactor in the US in 40+ years on April 23, 2026. At $4 billion for 345 MW, it's $11,594/kW — cheaper per kilowatt than Vogtle's $16,115/kW. But the real question isn't whether the first unit pencils out (it won't — FOAK never does). The question is whether the nth-of-a-kind cost can hit the $3,600/kW target that makes Natrium competitive with combined-cycle gas.

## Original Contribution
A cost-per-megawatt decomposition comparing Natrium FOAK economics against Vogtle, NuScale (cancelled), and the stated nth-of-a-kind targets. Nobody has run the comparison with the actual $4B figure now public. Also: a HALEU fuel supply chain bottleneck analysis — Centrus has produced only 920 kg by mid-2025, Natrium's first core needs ~6 tonnes, and the Russian ban means no backup supply.

## Key Facts & Sources

### TerraPower Natrium
- **Construction permit:** NRC Part 50 authorization March 4, 2026 — first commercial reactor permit in nearly a decade, first non-LWR commercial permit in 40+ years
- **Construction start:** April 23, 2026, Kemmerer, Wyoming
- **EPC contractor:** Bechtel (also built Vogtle 3&4)
- **Reactor type:** 345 MW sodium-cooled fast reactor + molten salt thermal energy storage (can boost output to 500 MW for 5.5+ hours)
- **Cost:** Up to $4 billion (ENR reporting)
- **Federal support:** Up to $2 billion through DOE ARDP (50-50 cost share)
- **Schedule target:** 2030 operational
- **HALEU fuel required:** 5-20% enriched uranium; Centrus has produced only 920 kg total by mid-2025
- **Schedule delay already:** Late 2022 delay due to insufficient HALEU manufacturing capacity and loss of Russian supply
- **Site:** Near PacifiCorp's Naughton coal plant (being retired) — coal-to-nuclear transition
- **Labor:** Union labor agreement
- **Sources:** Power Magazine, Power Engineering, ENR, NRC, DOE

### Comparison: Vogtle 3&4
- **Cost:** $36 billion total for 2 units (2,234 MW combined) = $16,115/kW
- **Original estimate:** $14 billion — 2.6x overrun
- **Schedule:** 15 years total (original: ~7 years) — 2x overrun
- **Result:** Operational, but Westinghouse went bankrupt during construction
- **Source:** Power Magazine, Wikipedia, ANS

### Comparison: NuScale UAMPS
- **Status:** Cancelled November 2023
- **Reason:** Cost escalated from $5.3B to $9.3B; subscribers pulled out
- **Capacity:** 462 MW (6 x 77 MW modules)
- **Per-kW at cancellation:** ~$20,130/kW
- **Source:** Industry reporting

### HALEU Supply Chain
- **Only Russia and China** have infrastructure to produce HALEU at scale
- **Centrus Energy** (Piketon, Ohio): Pilot cascade operational Oct 2023; 920 kg delivered to DOE by mid-2025
- **Russian ban:** US enacted ban on Russian uranium imports May 2024
- **Urenco:** NRC authorization to enrich to 10% in 2025; building UK facility targeting 2031
- **Orano:** Developing Project IKE at Oak Ridge, Tennessee with DOE support
- **Gap:** Natrium first core needs ~6 tonnes HALEU; total US production as of mid-2025: <1 tonne
- **Source:** World Nuclear Association, IAEA, DOE

### Nth-of-a-Kind Targets
- TerraPower has stated NOAK target of ~$3,600/kW
- Learning rate assumption: ~20% cost reduction per doubling of cumulative capacity
- If FOAK = $11,594/kW, need ~62% reduction → roughly 3 doublings → 8 cumulative units
- Bechtel's pitch: "delivery approach designed for repeatability"
- **Source:** TerraPower presentations, Bechtel statements

### Context
- US electricity demand surging from data centers + AI
- Nuclear widely seen as essential for baseload + decarbonization
- X-energy (second ARDP awardee) building HALEU fuel fabrication facility, Xe-100 reactor later
- Kairos Power Hermes 2 also under development (LITF has covered this)
- Political support: bipartisan (ADVANCE Act 2024, NEIMA 2019)
