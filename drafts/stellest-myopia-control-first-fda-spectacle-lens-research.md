# Research: Stellest Myopia Control — First FDA-Cleared Spectacle Lens

## Core Technology
- **H.A.L.T.** = Highly Aspherical Lenslet Target technology
- 1,021 invisible aspherical micro-lenslets arranged in 11 concentric rings
- Clear 9mm diameter center zone for sharp central vision
- Lenslets create peripheral light defocus → volume of myopic defocus signal in front of retina
- Signals eye to slow axial elongation (the mechanism of myopia progression)
- FDA De Novo authorization: **September 25, 2025** — first eyeglass lens ever cleared by FDA for myopia control
- Breakthrough Device designation: April 30, 2021
- Ages 6-12 at initiation of treatment
- Made by EssilorLuxottica (Essilor of America Inc.)

## Clinical Data

### 2-Year RCT (Wenzhou Medical University, China, Published JAMA Ophthalmology)
- **71% reduction** in myopia progression (SER) at 24 months
- **53% reduction** in eye elongation (axial length) at 24 months
- Full-time wearers (≥12h/day): 0.48D progression vs 0.93D part-time wearers
- Full-time axial elongation: 0.28mm vs 0.43mm for part-time
- 0.64D less progression than single vision control
- No serious adverse events; some blur and halos reported

### 6-Year Data (n=42, ages 8-13 at start → 14-19 at end)
- HAL group: -1.50D mean change vs -3.45D modelled SVL control
- **57% (1.95D) reduction** in myopia progression over 6 years
- Axial: 0.76mm vs 1.57mm → **52% (0.81mm) reduction**
- 90% of full-time wearers showed eye growth similar to non-myopic children

### Stellest 2.0 (HALT Max, next-gen)
- Doubled lenslet power and asphericity
- 0.107mm additional reduction in axial elongation over 12 months vs standard HALT
- 67% slowing claimed in Singapore trial

### Pre-Myopia Data
- 1-year RCT: HAL lenses reduced axial elongation in pre-myopic children (hyperopic reserve +0.00 to +2.00D)
- Delaying myopia onset by 1 year → reduces final myopia by ~0.75D

### No-Rebound Data (Vietnam, n=119)
- Children switching from HAL to conventional lenses showed no rebound effect
- Benefits preserved after cessation

## Myopia Epidemic Data
- **US prevalence:** ~40% of population (doubled in recent decades)
- **Global 2050 projection:** 4.758 billion myopic (49.8%), 938 million high myopia (9.8%)
- **Children 2050:** 740 million affected globally
- **East/Southeast Asia:** 80-90% of children myopic
- **Complications of high myopia:** retinal detachment, myopic maculopathy, glaucoma, cataracts
- **Global productivity cost** of uncorrected myopia: ~$244 billion/year (Holden et al. 2016)
- **Myopia control lens market:** $1.25B (2025) → $3.55B by 2032 (16.07% CAGR)

## Cost Analysis (Original Calculation)
- Stellest lenses: estimated ~$300-500/pair at retail (specialty myopia control lens pricing, no public MSRP)
- Children need new lenses every 6-12 months as prescription changes during growth years
- Treatment window: ages 6-12 → ~6 years of treatment
- Estimated 6-12 pairs of lenses → $1,800-$6,000 total cost
- MiSight contacts: ~$500-1,000/year → $2,500-$6,000 over 5 years
- Orthokeratology: ~$1,000-2,000/year including fitting + lenses
- High myopia complication costs: retinal detachment surgery $5,000-$20,000; glaucoma treatment $1,000-$3,000/year ongoing; myopic maculopathy treatment (anti-VEGF injections) $2,000+/injection
