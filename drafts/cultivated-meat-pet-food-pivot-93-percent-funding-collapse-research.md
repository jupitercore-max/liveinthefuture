# Research: Cultivated Meat Investment Fell 93% in Four Years. Every Company Still Raising Money Sells to Dogs.

## Journalist: Priya Desai (Consumer Tech / Biotech)
## Category: 🍖 Food
## Article Number: 322

## Core Thesis
Cultivated meat investment peaked at $989M in 2021 and collapsed to ~$65M by 2025 — a 93% decline. The companies that targeted human food (Believer Meats, Meatable, SCiFi Foods, CellRev, Upstream Foods) are dead. Every cultivated meat company that successfully raised money in 2025–2026 has converged on the same market entry strategy: pet food. The technology is identical. The investor base overlaps. The variable that determines survival is the entry market — specifically, the regulatory pathway.

## Key Data Points

### The Death Toll (Human Food Companies)
- **Believer Meats** (2018–2025): $225M in debt at bankruptcy. Had raised $390M (investors included Tyson Foods). Built $154M facility in North Carolina capable of 12,000 tonnes/year. First foreign company to get US regulatory approval for cultivated chicken. Dead Dec 2025.
- **Meatable** (2018–2025): Raised ~$105M. Dutch cultivated pork. Dissolved Dec 2025. Shareholder Agronomics confirmed unable to secure funding.
- **SCiFi Foods**: Closed 2024. Pivoted to public use of cell lines.
- **CellRev** (2018–2025): Newcastle University spin-out. Couldn't deliver commercial milestones for Series A. Dead Aug 2025.
- **Upstream Foods** (2022–2025): Dutch cultivated fish fat. Ceased operations mid-2025 after 3 years.
- **UPSIDE Foods**: Layoffs, mothballed Glenview Illinois plant. Still operating but struggling.
- **Aleph Farms**: Cut workforce. Partnering with Mitsubishi for Japan market.
- **Eat Just**: Paused human-food scale-up.
- **Meati Foods**: $450M valuation, facing collapse after lender unexpectedly withdrew funding. 150 jobs at risk.
- **Beyond Meat**: Refuting bankruptcy claims (plant-based, not cultivated, but same alt-protein space).

### The Funding Collapse
- 2021 peak: $989M invested in cultivated meat
- 2025: ~$65M (93% decline in 4 years)
- Alternative protein total 2025: $881M ($19.4B cumulative since 2016)
- Plant-based rose 39% to $450M in 2025; cultivated and fermentation dropped
- 15% decline in cultivated meat companies in 2025 (down to ~140 companies)
- Former Meatable CEO Jeff Tripician: "Cultivated meat is broken"

### The Pet Food Survivors (All Still Raising)
- **Meatly** (UK): £10.4M Series A, May 2026. Building 20,000L bioreactor in London (Europe's largest cultivated meat facility). First cultivated meat product approved in any European country (July 17, 2024). Pet food only.
- **BioCraft Pet Nutrition**: Cleared EU feed pathway March 2025. Raising capital.
- **Bene Meat Technologies** (Czech): Registered as EU feed material Nov 2023. Targeting 2026 pet food launch.
- **Bond Pet Foods**: Partnership with Hill's (Colgate-Palmolive subsidiary).
- **Magic Valley** (Australia): Moving through voluntary framework.
- **Friends & Family**: Singapore approval 2025.

### Meatly Unit Economics (The Key Breakthrough)
- Traditional cell culture media: ~£700/litre (albumin, transferrin, insulin, growth factors)
- Meatly's protein-free formulation: £0.22/litre today → target £0.015/litre at industrial scale
- That's a 3,182x reduction from traditional media to Meatly's industrial target
- Biopharma-grade stainless-steel bioreactors (300L): £200K–£250K
- Meatly's patented design (320L): ~£12,500 (95% reduction)
- Achieved by removing pharmaceutical sterility specs that pet/food doesn't need

### The 4% Inclusion Hack
- First commercial product: "Chick Bites by The Pack" — 50g pouch at £3.49
- Sold at Pets at Home Brentford, launched Feb 7, 2025
- Cultivated chicken = ~4% of formulation
- At 4% inclusion, even expensive cultivated chicken ($100/kg) adds only ~£0.07 to a 50g pouch
- Dilution makes unit economics work NOW, not after some future cost breakthrough

### Regulatory Arbitrage
- UK pet food: regulated by Defra and Animal & Plant Health Agency (animal feed pathway)
- Meatly authorized July 17, 2024 — took ~2 years
- UK human food: Food Standards Agency novel-foods process
- FSA Cell-Cultivated Products Regulatory Sandbox launched March 2025
- No cultivated product approved for human consumption in UK as of mid-2026
- Human food applications target completion ~Feb 2027
- US: UPSIDE Foods and Eat Just got FDA/USDA clearance but still can't scale profitably
- US states banning cultivated meat for human consumption (Florida, Alabama, others)

### Market Size Context
- Global pet food ingredients market: $56.77B (2024) → $92.74B by 2032 (6.44% CAGR)
- Pet food processing market: $6.08B → $9.91B by 2034
- Mars Petcare + Big Idea Ventures launched 2026 Global Pet Food Innovation Program
- Partners: AAK, Bühler, Givaudan, Ingredion — all focused on alternative protein ingredients for pets

### Growth Factor Cost Reduction
- Orf Genetics (Iceland): barley-based growth factors, raised €5M, targeting 14x capacity by 2027 and 10,000x by 2032
- BioBetter (Israel): $10M funding, cell-cultured meat via tobacco plants
- Growth factors represent 55–95% of media cost in traditional formulations

## Strongest Counterargument
Pet food is a tiny fraction of the meat market. Global pet food ingredients: $57B. Global meat market: $1.8 trillion. If cultivated meat can only serve 3% of the protein market (pets), it hasn't solved its core promise. The pet food pivot may prove these companies can manufacture cells at scale — but the leap from premium pet treats at 4% inclusion to price-competitive human chicken breast at 100% inclusion remains 100x in cost reduction.

## Limitations
- No audited cost-per-kilogram data for any cultivated meat company (all figures are self-reported or estimates)
- Meatly's £0.22/litre and £0.015/litre media costs are company claims, not independently verified
- The 93% funding decline captures industry-wide sentiment but some capital may be flowing through unreported channels
- State-level bans on cultivated meat (Florida, Alabama) affect the human food pathway specifically; their impact on pet food is unclear
