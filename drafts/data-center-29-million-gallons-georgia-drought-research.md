# Research Notes: Data Center Drained 29 Million Gallons During Georgia Drought

## Story Angle
A Blackstone-owned data center in Fayetteville, Georgia secretly consumed 29 million gallons of water over 9-15 months without paying a dime. Residents discovered the drain only when their water pressure dropped. The company paid $147,474 retroactively — and faced zero fines, even though it violated peak-use restrictions during a state drought emergency. The county water director's explanation: "They're our largest customer and we have to be partners. It's called customer service."

This is a microcosm of a national collision: AI infrastructure is consuming water at industrial scale while communities bear the cost.

## Key Facts — The QTS Incident

- **Company:** Quality Technology Services (QTS), owned by Blackstone
- **Location:** Fayetteville, GA, ~20 miles south of Atlanta
- **Campus:** 6.2 million sq ft, 615 acres, one of America's largest
- **Water consumed:** 29 million gallons (44 Olympic pools) over 9-15 months
- **Bill:** $147,474 — paid retroactively, zero fines
- **Discovery:** Annelise Park subdivision residents noticed low water pressure
- **Disclosed by:** James Clifton (attorney, county commission candidate) via public records request, posted May 2026 on Facebook
- **Two industrial hookups:** one installed without utility knowledge, other not linked to any account
- **Water Director Vanessa Tigert:** blamed cloud-based metering transition, staff shortages ("I've got one person that's doing inspections and plan review")
- **Gregory Pierce (UCLA Water Resources Group):** "I don't know exactly what's happening here, but they probably don't want to upset one of their new and largest customers"
- **Fayetteville city council:** subsequently banned new data centers entirely

## Novel Analysis (ORIGINAL CONTRIBUTION)

### 1. The "Too Big to Fine" Discount
- QTS paid $147,474 / 29,000,000 gallons = $0.00509 per gallon
- Average US residential water: ~$4-5 per 1,000 gallons = $0.004-0.005/gallon
- QTS paid roughly the RESIDENTIAL rate — with no penalties for drought-period peak violations
- Georgia drought surcharge penalties for residential customers: typically 2-4x base rate for excessive use
- If QTS had been fined at the same scale as a household violating drought restrictions, the bill would have been $295K-$590K+
- **The regulatory asymmetry:** residents who water their lawns get fined. A data center that drains 29M gallons gets "customer service."

### 2. Water-Per-Household Equivalence
- 29 million gallons = 265 average US households' annual water use (at 300 gal/day)
- QTS claims fully operational facility will use equivalent of 4 households/month — but construction phase used 265 households' worth
- Nationally: 150.4 billion gallons/year by 2030 = 4.6 million households

### 3. The Disclosure Gap
- Of the Big 4 (Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft), only Amazon and Google provide partial data
- Meta and Microsoft: "remain silent" on facility-level water data
- AGU paper confirms: no standardized facility-level water reporting exists
- QTS incident was discovered only through a FOIA request by a private citizen — not through any reporting mechanism
