# Research Notes: Warming Acceleration & Clean Air Paradox

## Topic
Global warming has statistically confirmed acceleration since ~2015, nearly doubling its rate from 0.2°C to 0.35°C per decade. The leading cause is a paradox: cleaning up air pollution (aerosols from fossil fuels) has unmasked warming that dirty air was hiding. The 1.5°C Paris threshold is now on track to be breached before 2030.

## Primary Sources

### Source 1: Foster & Rahmstorf (2026) — Geophysical Research Letters
- **Title:** "Global Warming has Accelerated Significantly"
- **Published:** March 9, 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1029/2025GL118804
- **URL:** https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2025GL118804
- **Key findings:**
  - Warming rate nearly doubled from ~0.2°C/decade to ~0.35°C/decade starting around 2015
  - First study to confirm acceleration with statistical significance
  - Tested across all 5 global temperature datasets (NASA GISS, NOAA, HadCRUT, Berkeley Earth, ERA5)
  - After filtering out El Niño, volcanic eruptions, and solar variation, acceleration persists
  - Even including 2023 and 2024 (two hottest years on record), the result holds
  - At current pace, 1.5°C Paris threshold exceeded before 2030
  - Leading theory: reduction in atmospheric aerosols (from cleaner air) unmasking warming
- **Authors:** Grant Foster (retired, Tempo Analytics), Stefan Rahmstorf (Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research)
- **Key quote:** "We can now demonstrate a strong and statistically significant acceleration of global warming since around 2015." — Grant Foster

### Source 2: Global Carbon Budget 2025 — Nature
- **Published:** November 2025
- **URL:** https://globalcarbonbudget.org/fossil-fuel-co2-emissions-hit-record-high-in-2025/
- **Key findings:**
  - Global fossil CO2 emissions: 38.1 billion tonnes in 2025 (record high, +1.1% YoY)
  - Land-use change emissions: 4.1 billion tonnes (declining — deforestation down)

### Source 3: IMO 2020 / NOAA Aerosol Study — Communications Earth & Environment (2024)
- **URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-024-01911-9
- **Key findings:**
  - IMO 2020 regulation: shipping fuel sulfur content cut from 3.5% to 0.5%
  - Sulfate aerosols from ships had been reflecting sunlight and brightening low-level marine clouds
  - Reducing sulfur pollution decreased cloud reflectivity → net warming effect
  - Radiative forcing from regulation: large but difficult to isolate from natural cloud variability
  - CIRES/NOAA/Imperial College London team
  - Unintended consequence: better air quality = more warming unmasked

### Source 4: IPCC AR6 — Aerosol Masking Estimates
- Aerosols estimated to mask ~0.0°C to -1.7°C of warming (best estimate ~-0.5°C to -1.0°C)
- As air pollution declines, this "cooling debt" comes due
- Total anthropogenic warming: ~1.1°C (measured) + ~0.5°C (masked) = ~1.6°C actual forcing

## Novel Contribution: The Timeline Math

### Old rate vs. new rate
- Current anomaly: ~1.35°C above preindustrial (2024-2025 average after removing El Niño)
- Remaining to 1.5°C threshold: ~0.15°C
- At OLD rate (0.2°C/decade = 0.02°C/year): 0.15/0.02 = ~7.5 years → ~2033
- At NEW rate (0.35°C/decade = 0.035°C/year): 0.15/0.035 = ~4.3 years → ~2029-2030
- Timeline acceleration: 1.5°C arrives 3-4 years earlier than projected

### Carbon budget countdown
- Remaining 1.5°C budget: 170 Gt CO2 (from Jan 2025)
- Annual fossil emissions: 38.1 Gt CO2 (2025)
- Annual land-use: 4.1 Gt CO2 (2025)
- Total annual: ~42.2 Gt CO2
- 170 / 42.2 = 4.03 years → budget exhausted ~early 2029
- This is consistent with the warming rate analysis

### The paradox quantified
- Reducing aerosols is unambiguously good for public health (7M premature deaths/year from air pollution per WHO)
- But it unmasks ~0.5°C of hidden warming
- The "net zero aerosol" state means we're closer to 2°C of actual forcing than 1.5°C
- Every country that cleans its air (China's Blue Sky policy, EU air quality directives, IMO 2020) accelerates visible warming
- This is NOT an argument against clean air — it's an argument that our emissions reduction targets were always too lenient

## Strongest Counterargument
The decade since 2015 could be an outlier driven by unusual ocean dynamics (Atlantic Multidecadal Oscillation, Hunga Tonga eruption 2022 injecting water vapor). If natural variability is larger than modeled, the acceleration might revert to the mean. Rahmstorf himself acknowledges: "This may not occur if the past decade turns out to be an outlier."

## Limitations
1. Only one decade of accelerated data — trend could be transient
2. Aerosol forcing is the least well-constrained factor in climate models (IPCC ranges from -0.0 to -1.7°C)
3. The study uses regression-based natural variability removal, which depends on model assumptions about El Niño/volcanic contributions
4. Hunga Tonga-Hunga Ha'apai eruption (Jan 2022) injected ~150 Tg of water vapor into the stratosphere — a novel forcing not fully accounted for in standard filtering
5. 1.5°C "crossing" may be temporary (annual vs. decadal average)

## Actionable Takeaways
1. For policymakers: National emissions targets (NDCs) set at the old rate are structurally inadequate. Every NDC should be revised assuming 0.35°C/decade.
2. For investors: Insurance, real estate, and infrastructure in coastal/heat-vulnerable zones need pricing models updated for accelerated timeline.
3. For individuals: The window for 1.5°C is essentially closed. Personal adaptation planning (property, career, health) should assume 2°C by ~2050-2060.
4. For climate scientists: The aerosol masking uncertainty is now the most consequential unknown in climate policy. Constraining this should be the #1 research priority.
