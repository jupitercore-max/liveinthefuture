### 6. 🔬 Research Rigor — 9.0/10
**Original contribution:** ✅ BLS cross-reference (218K fewer job openings) is a novel calculation nobody else published. Career premium calculation ($600K-$900K over 30 years) is a second original contribution. Upwork/Fiverr parallel analysis adds independent corroboration from a different data domain.
**Limitations:** ✅ Dedicated section "What the Data Cannot Tell Us" covering: temporal coverage (GPT-3→GPT-4o era, likely understating current), geographic bias (high-income Anglophone), postings ≠ employment, confounders (pandemic, monetary policy, tech correction), missing worker-level longitudinal data. Explicit: "we know that 218,000 fewer job openings exist, but we do not know what happened to the specific people who would have filled them."
**Counterargument:** ✅ Dedicated section with ATM/teller, spreadsheet/clerk, and solar cost-curve parallels. Full strength: "This argument has genuine force, and dismissing it would be dishonest." Structural weakness identified (10-20 year vs 18-month timeline).
**Verifiability:** All major claims linked. Math shown transparently (1,897,100 × ~50% × 23% = ~218K).
**Methodology transparency:** ✅ PRISMA methodology described. Funnel (1,847 → 94 → 42) clearly stated. Eligibility criteria explained.
**Verdict:** Passes comfortably.

### 7. 📊 Data Presentation — 9.0/10
**Format:** Inline numbers throughout. Appropriate for narrative journalism. Enumerated list for 4 findings works well.
**Denominators/base rates:** BLS total (1,897,100) given before applying 23%. "Entry-level and mid-level positions at roughly half" gives the denominator. Median salary ($133,080) given before wage premium calculation.
**Human-scale anchors:** 23% → 218,000 jobs. Premium → $600K-$900K career difference. Upwork layoffs → 24% of workforce.
**Comparison framing:** 14-41% range with median 23% (proper uncertainty). Low vs high end (218K vs 389K). Historical comparison (Frey & Osborne 47% vs actual 14-41%). Freelance spending trend (0.66% → 0.14%) contrasted with AI spending (0 → 3%).
**"So what?" test:** Every number has a human-scale anchor. The $600K-$900K figure makes the abstract premium personally relevant.
**Weaknesses:** Very number-dense. A summary box could help readers track key figures across sections. But this is a stylistic preference, not a deficiency.
**Verdict:** Passes comfortably.
