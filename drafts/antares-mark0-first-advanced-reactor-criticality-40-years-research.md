# Research: Antares Mark-0 First Advanced Reactor Criticality in 40+ Years

## Core Facts
- **What:** Antares Nuclear's Mark-0 microreactor achieved zero-power criticality at Idaho National Laboratory (INL)
- **When:** Announced June 4, 2026
- **Where:** INL's Reactor and Critical Experiment (RACE) facility, Building MFC-793, Materials and Fuels Complex
- **Significance:** First privately developed non-light-water reactor to reach criticality in the US in 40+ years; 53rd reactor built at INL since 1951; first novel design criticality at INL in 50+ years

## Antares Nuclear
- Founded: 2023
- HQ: Torrance, California
- CEO: Jordan Bramble
- Funding: $140M+ total, including $96M Series B (Dec 2025, led by Shine Capital, with Alt Capital, FiftyThree Stations, Industrious, Caffeinated). $71M equity + $25M debt.
- Federal contracts: $12M+ including Defense Innovation Unit

## Mark-0 Reactor Specs
- Type: Sodium heat-pipe-cooled microreactor
- Fuel: HALEU TRISO compacts (<120 kg total), fabricated by BWXT in Lynchburg, VA
- Core: Began machining graphite core Jan 12, 2026 at Antares Prime facility
- Purpose: Zero-power criticality testing only — no power conversion or heat removal systems
- Timeline: PDSA approved Jan 2026, DSA approved April 2026, criticality June 4, 2026
- Decommission: Cool 30-180 days, defuel, fuel retained for Mark-1

## R1 Commercial Product
- Power: 100 kWe to 1 MWe (also described as 200-300 kW / 500 kW in different sources)
- Fuel cycle: 6+ years between refueling, no grid connection needed
- Cooling: Passive sodium heat pipes
- Core: TRISO-fueled prismatic graphite
- Heat exchanger: Fin-and-tube primary
- Power conversion: Recuperated nitrogen-closed Brayton cycle (<300 psi)
- Transport: Ships in integrated transport cradle with shielding
- Optimized for: Reliability, uptime, manufacturability (not maximum power density)

## Roadmap

## DOE Reactor Pilot Program
- Established under EO 14301 (May 23, 2025)
- Goal: At least 3 advanced reactor criticalities by July 4, 2026
- 11 projects from 10 companies selected
- Antares is FIRST to hit criticality

- **Valar Atomics** — Ward 250 (high-temp gas reactor, 100 kWt/5 MWe), final DSA approved April 23. Had separate zero-power criticality at LANL in Nov 2025 (under NNSA, not DOE Pilot). Targeting POWER operations by July 4, not just criticality.
- **Aalo Atomics** — Critical Test Reactor at INL, DSA approved April 30. Sodium-cooled, UO2-fueled. 10 MWe fuel load. Post-criticality plans through H2 2026.

- **Oklo** — Groves isotope reactor in Lockhart, TX. Construction substantial completion in 229 days. PDSA in review, DSA submitted. July 4 criticality target stated.

## HALEU Supply Chain (KEY LIMITATION)
- No US commercial enricher currently produces HALEU at commercial scale
- Centrus Energy: Single 16-machine demo cascade in Piketon, Ohio — produced ~920 kg total
- Mark-0 requires <120 kg HALEU
- DOE/NNSA provided government-held scrap material for Mark-0
- Antares signed "world's first multi-year" commercial HALEU supply contract with Urenco (May 2026)
- Urenco's Advanced Fuels Facility at Capenhurst, UK — comes online 2031, initial capacity 27 MT/year (enough for ~30 advanced reactors)
- 5-year gap between demo success and commercial fuel availability

What changed: DOE authorization pathway bypasses NRC licensing entirely. No probabilistic risk assessment, no environmental impact statements, no public comment periods (in the traditional NRC sense). Speed is the tradeoff.

## Counterargument
Zero-power criticality ≠ electricity. INL Director Wagner was careful to distinguish. The chain reaction was self-sustaining at "essentially no measurable energy output." The system has no power conversion or heat removal. Mark-0 is a physics experiment, not a power plant. Going from "we can sustain a chain reaction" to "we can sell electricity to the grid" involves thermal management, power conversion, long-duration fuel performance, regulatory approval for power operations, and waste handling — none of which are validated.

Historical precedent: Many reactor concepts achieved criticality and never reached commercial operation. The EBR-II operated at INL from 1964-1994 but its commercial descendants took decades more. Fort St. Vrain (HTGR) went critical 1974, operated poorly, shut down 1989.
