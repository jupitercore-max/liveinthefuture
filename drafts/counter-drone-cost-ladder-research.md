# Research: The Counter-Drone Cost Ladder
## Farnborough 2026 reveals the full spectrum — and the math nobody has run

### Story Angle
Farnborough Airshow 2026 (opened July 21) is the first event where every tier of counter-drone technology is on display simultaneously — from $2,000 Ukrainian kamikaze drones to $4M Patriot missiles, with microwave weapons, low-cost European interceptors, and jet-powered variants in between. For the first time, we can build a complete cost exchange ratio table and run the annual defense budget math.

### ORIGINAL CALCULATION: The Cost Exchange Ratio Ladder
Nobody has built a comprehensive cost-per-kill table across ALL tiers and calculated what a year of sustained defense costs at each tier.

### Source Data — Attack Drones

**Shahed-136 (Geran-2) — propeller-powered**
- Production cost: $20,000-$50,000 per unit (CSIS estimate: $35,000)
- Russian domestic production at Yelabuga: designed for 6,000/year (~500/month)
- Iran HESA production: 150-250/month across variants
- Speed: ~185 kph
- Source: Wikipedia (leaked documents), CSIS, Quwa, Reuters

**Shahed-238 — jet-powered variant**
- Production cost: $70,000-$100,000 per unit (Quwa estimate)
- 15-20% of Russian Shaheds now jet-powered (Ukraine Air Force commander Yuriy Cherevashenko, Reuters April 2026)
- Speed: up to 500 kph
- Source: Reuters, Ukraine Air Force

**Russian production capacity:**
- Yelabuga: 500/month target (6,000/year)
- Reports say Russia launches "thousands per month" — Reuters says Russia "makes thousands a month in its own factories"
- Conservative estimate for calculation: 1,500 Shaheds/month total directed at Ukraine

### Source Data — Interceptor Systems (Tier 1: Sub-$5,000)

**Ukrainian FPV/Interceptor Drones (The Sting, Bullet by General Cherry)**
- Cost: $1,500-$2,000 per unit
- One-use (kamikaze)
- Bullet has destroyed 1,000+ Shaheds since Nov 2025
- Speed: ~310 kph (existing Bullet)
- Source: Reuters, Global Taiwan Institute

**SkyFall P1-SUN**
- Cost: estimated $3,000-$5,000 (not publicly stated, but Ukrainian interceptors are "several thousand dollars")
- One-use
- 5,500 Shaheds hit since Nov 2025 (SkyFall claims)
- Capacity: 50,000 units/month production
- Speed: 310 kph (original), 370 kph (new high-speed variant)
- Source: Reuters (Farnborough July 21)

### Source Data — Interceptor Systems (Tier 2: $20,000-$30,000)

**APKWS (Advanced Precision Kill Weapon System)**
- Cost: ~$27,000 per round (BAE Systems/Northrop Grumman/General Dynamics)
- Guided 70mm Hydra rocket with laser guidance + proximity fuze
- Used by US forces
- Source: DefenseScoop, Yuma Proving Ground tests

### Source Data — Interceptor Systems (Tier 3: Novel Technologies)

**Lockheed Martin MORFIUS X-Rotor** — Farnborough 2026 reveal
- Reusable airborne high-power microwave (HPM) system
- 50+ drone kills PER FLIGHT
- Weighs <30 lbs, fits in 6-inch launch tube
- ~1 gigawatt microwave burst
- Field reusable — recovers after mission
- "No dependence on fire control radars"
- Cost: not publicly disclosed, but amortized cost per kill is extremely low
- Source: Lockheed Martin PR, Farnborough, Breaking Defense

**MBDA Counter Mass Interceptor** — Farnborough 2026 reveal
- First sovereign European counter-mass-attack system
- Initial version available 2028
- Cost: not disclosed
- Led by British teams, French iteration planned
- Source: Reuters, MBDA, Farnborough

**Greenjets** — UK startup, £34M funding
- Electric jet-performance propulsion
- "Jet-power performance at electric prices"
- No US-made components (sovereign supply chain)
- LEAP programme (UK, France, Germany, Italy, Poland)
- Demo trials later 2026
- Source: The Times

### Source Data — Interceptor Systems (Tier 4: Missiles, $1M+)

**Lockheed PAC-3 ACE (Adapted Capability Effector)** — Farnborough 2026 reveal
- Cost: less than half of PAC-3 MSE = <$2M
- Targets: aircraft, cruise missiles, short-range ballistic missiles
- NOT designed for drones specifically
- Initial production: 2028
- Source: Reuters, Lockheed at Farnborough

**PAC-3 MSE**
- Cost: ~$4M per missile (US Army budget docs)
- Hit-to-kill technology
- The "gold standard" interceptor
- Source: US Army budget documents, Reuters
