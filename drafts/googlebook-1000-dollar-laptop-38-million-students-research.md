## The Angle

Google announced the Googlebook at I/O 2026 — a $1,000 AI-native laptop with mandatory premium hardware standards. It explicitly replaces the Chromebook as Google's laptop vision. But Chromebooks' entire empire was built on being the anti-MacBook: $300 devices that put a screen in front of every student. 38 million students worldwide rely on them. 93% of US school districts buy them. The school district in Fauquier County, Virginia chose Chromebooks specifically because they were $300 instead of $800 laptops — "for every one laptop, I could buy two Chromebooks."

Nobody at I/O mentioned what happens to the 38 million students on the cheap side of this transition. This article runs the math.

## Strongest Counterargument (at full strength)

Google explicitly said Chromebooks will continue to exist and get 10-year support. This is a *dual strategy*, not a replacement. The Googlebook is a premium tier competing with MacBook, not an education product. Acer, ASUS, Dell, HP, and Lenovo will keep making $300 Chromebooks. ChromeOS isn't dying — it's getting a sibling. Education-specific pricing hasn't been announced yet; Google may offer discounted Googlebooks for schools, or schools may simply keep buying Chromebooks until the heat death of the universe.

The $1.8B PIRG savings number assumed the same $300 Chromebook paradigm. If Chromebooks persist, the education math doesn't change at all.

**Why the counterargument doesn't kill the story:**
- Google called Googlebook "a new category" replacing the Chromebook *vision* — premium hardware is mandatory
- The same 5 OEMs making Chromebooks are making Googlebooks. R&D focus follows margin. When Lenovo can sell a $1,000 Googlebook or a $300 Chromebook, which gets the engineering attention?
- Google is investing in Gemini Intelligence for Googlebook, not ChromeOS. ChromeOS becomes maintenance-mode.
- The PIRG report already showed 4-year lifespan even WITH support. Hardware deterioration + software stagnation = zombie platform
- No education pricing has been announced for Googlebook. The silence speaks.

---

## Limitations ⚠️

1. **Google has not said Chromebooks are dying.** The company explicitly says they'll continue. Our thesis is about *incentive structures* and *innovation gravity*, not a stated policy.
2. **The $1,000 price is from early reports (BigGo Finance), not an official Google MSRP.** Actual education pricing could differ significantly.
3. **We model 4-year refresh cycles based on PIRG data.** Some districts stretch to 5-6 years. Others replace at 3.
4. **The teacher-salary equivalency assumes 100% of the cost difference is discretionary.** In reality, technology and personnel budgets are often separately line-itemed.
5. **Education-specific Googlebook pricing could emerge.** Google may announce education SKUs at lower price points. We can't evaluate a program that doesn't exist yet.
6. **The 38 million figure is global.** US-specific installed base is estimated at ~20 million via North America's 52.4% share, not directly reported.
