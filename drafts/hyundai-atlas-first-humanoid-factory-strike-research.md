# Research: Hyundai Atlas — First Factory Strike Over Humanoid Robots

## Story Angle (💼 Labor & AI)
The world's first factory shutdown over humanoid robots happened this week at Hyundai in Ulsan, South Korea. 40,000 unionized workers went on partial strike (4 hours/day, July 20-22) over Atlas deployment. The union demands — hourly→salary conversion, retirement age extension to 65, 30% of net income as bonuses — are unprecedented. But nobody has done the math: the union's demands cost MORE per year than the entire 25,000-robot fleet. The strike itself is the business case for Atlas.

## Original Calculation — The $134 Million Preview
The 4-hour daily partial strike costs ~5,000 vehicles and KRW 200B (~$134M) in Hyundai revenue over 3 days. That's ~$45M/day of production lost. Meanwhile, 25,000 Atlas robots working 24/7 never strike. The financial math reveals the union is inadvertently pricing itself above the robot alternative.

### Union Demands vs. Robot Fleet Cost

**Costing the Union's Demands (annual):**
- 30% of net income for bonuses: 30% × KRW 10.36T (FY2025 net income) = KRW 3.11T (~$2.2B)
- Retirement age extension 60→65 (amortized): 40,000 workers × avg comp ~KRW 80M/year ÷ transition period = ~KRW 2.67T/year (~$1.9B)
- TOTAL ANNUAL DEMAND: ~$4.1B/year

**Costing 25,000 Atlas Robots (annual):**
- Upfront investment at $140K/unit: $3.5B (amortized over 7-year depreciation = $500M/year)
- At scale ($30K/unit at 50K+ production): capital drops to $750M total ($107M/year amortized)
- Annual maintenance + electricity: ~$10-15K/robot × 25,000 = $250-375M
- TOTAL ANNUAL ROBOT COST: $750M-$875M/year (initial), dropping to $357-482M/year at scale

**The Gap:** Union demands ($4.1B/yr) vs. robot fleet ($750M-$875M/yr) = 4.7-5.5× more expensive to meet the union's demands than to deploy the robots.

### Labor Hours Comparison
- 1 Atlas robot: ~8,000 productive hours/year (24/7 minus battery swaps/downtime, ~22 hrs/day)
- 1 Korean auto worker: ~2,000 hours/year (standard shifts + leave)
- 1 Atlas ≈ 4 worker-hour-equivalents
- 25,000 Atlas = 100,000 worker-hour-equivalents
- Hyundai's Korean union: ~40,000 workers
- The robots provide 2.5× the labor capacity of the entire Korean union workforce

### The Strike Insurance Calculation
- Korean auto industry has averaged ~10-15 strike days per decade
- Average strike cost at Hyundai scale: $45M/day
- If Atlas fleet eliminates strike vulnerability at US plants: $450M-$675M in avoided risk per decade
- This alone approaches the at-scale capital cost of the entire fleet ($750M)

## Strongest Counterargument
Atlas hasn't worked a single shift in a real factory. No production-model footage exists — only 3D renders and mockups. The production model will inevitably sacrifice performance for affordability (62 DoF prototype vs. mass-producible version). GM's 1980s Hamtramck experiment — "Lights-Out Factory" — was a $600M disaster that proved pure automation fails without decades of incremental learning. The Korean union's 40,000 workers hold institutional knowledge about car quality that isn't easily digitized. And at $130K-$140K per unit with competitors at $20-30K, Atlas may be the wrong humanoid anyway.

## Limitations
- Union's 30% bonus demand is their opening negotiation position, not the likely settlement
- $30K/unit at 50K+ volume is a projection, not demonstrated
- Atlas has no verifiable factory performance data — all claims based on demos and renders
- Korean auto worker compensation data is approximate (using industry averages, not Hyundai-specific disclosed figures)
- Strike costs are industry estimates, not Hyundai-confirmed
- The 4-worker-equivalent calculation assumes Atlas runs at full productivity, which is unproven
