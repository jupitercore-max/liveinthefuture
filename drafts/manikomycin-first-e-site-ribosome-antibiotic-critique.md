## 1. 🔍 General Editor — Score: 8.5/10

**Strengths:**
- Opening with the raw resistance frequency number (3.7 x 10^-10) is immediately arresting. Grabs attention by forcing the reader to reckon with scale.
- Strong narrative arc: discovery, mechanism, efficacy, pharmacokinetic problem, implications. Each section earns its place.
- Headline is genuinely provocative: combining a specific number with "no drug has ever touched" creates both curiosity and credibility.
- The "How Many Hidden Antibiotics Are Still Buried?" section delivers the original LITF contribution: the 0.4% hit-rate extrapolation.
- Deck is tight and information-dense. Reader knows exactly what they are getting.

**Weaknesses:**
- The comparison table positions manikomycin against colistin, ciprofloxacin, and meropenem, but the resistance frequency ranges for those antibiotics are approximate (drawn from different studies, different conditions). Should note the comparison is illustrative, not controlled.
- "The value may not be the molecule. It may be the map." is a strong line but borders on the kind of writerly flourish that could read as AI-polished. Use sparingly.
- Could use one more concrete named institution or company working on E-site drug development to add forward-looking specificity.

**Verdict:** Structurally sound, engaging, data-heavy. Minor calibration needed on comparison table sourcing.

## 3. ⚖️ Ethics Reviewer — Score: 9.0/10

**Strengths:**
- No self-congratulation. Does not frame manikomycin as a "breakthrough" or "miracle."
- Honest about the pharmacokinetic failure in mice. Does not bury the lede.
- Includes Derek Lowe's blunt assessment ("not going to be a wonder drug") at full strength without dismissing it.
- The narrow spectrum limitation is stated clearly, not hedged.
- AMR death toll figure (1.27 million direct, 4.95 million associated) is sourced to The Lancet.

**Weaknesses:**
- The article does not address who funded this research or potential conflicts of interest. McMaster and UIC are academic institutions, but the "analogue efforts underway" line could imply commercial development without naming who is doing it.
- No mention of the WHO GLASS (Global Antimicrobial Resistance and Use Surveillance System) or GARDP pipeline, which would contextualize where manikomycin sits relative to global AMR response efforts.

**Verdict:** Ethically clean. No hype inflation. Minor gap on funding disclosure.
