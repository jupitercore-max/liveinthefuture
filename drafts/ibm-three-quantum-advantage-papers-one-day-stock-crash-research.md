# Research: IBM's Three-Paper Quantum Advantage Day

## Story Angle
IBM dropped three quantum advantage papers on July 30, 2026 — just 16 days after its stock suffered the worst single-day crash in its 115-year history (down 25.2%, erasing ~$68.8B in market value). The science is real. The timing is corporate.

## Original Analysis: The Three-Paper Day Math

### The Papers (all July 30, 2026)
1. **IBM + Qedma** — Floquet Ising model dynamics on up to 74 qubits. Classical methods (including Fugaku supercomputer) couldn't match. QESEM error mitigation software. arXiv preprint.
2. **IBM + UChicago** — 70 logical qubits encoded, 2,415 logical two-qubit operations, 468 logical T gates. Quantum computation took ~15 minutes; classical methods faced "prohibitive runtimes." Structured alternative to random circuit sampling.
3. **IBM + Algorithmiq** — Heterogeneous quantum material simulation. 8 months on Quantum Advantage Tracker, no classical method matched. New trust framework for unverifiable results.

- But WSJ headline: "IBM Claims New Era of 'Quantum Advantage'"

## Strongest Counterargument
The science IS genuinely important. Cross-hardware validation is a first. The Floquet Ising model has real implications for optoelectronics and superconductors. The 70-logical-qubit experiment is among the largest error correction demos ever. Releasing data to the Quantum Advantage Tracker is the right scientific move. IBM's roadmap has been consistent since 2020. The papers were likely submitted months before the stock crash.

The timing may be coincidental — research papers follow submission-review cycles, not corporate calendars. But the coordinated press release across three papers and six partners on one day is an orchestrated media event, regardless of when the science was completed.

## Limitations
- IBM doesn't disclose quantum-specific revenue
- HSBC's $35-51B valuation is one analyst's model, not market consensus
- R&D allocation to quantum is estimated, not disclosed
- Paper submission timelines are not public — we can't prove the release date was moved
- Cross-hardware validation (Quantinuum) doesn't prove absolute correctness, only consistency
