## Core Thesis
The Air Force just fired an AIM-120 AMRAAM from a jet-powered combat drone for the first time. This isn't a tech demo — it's the birth of the "missile truck" concept that inverts 70 years of air combat economics. The math: a CCA delivers missiles to contested airspace at 38% the cost-per-slot of an F-35, absorbs attrition at 1/3.3 the financial cost, and adds zero pilot risk. When you do the force multiplication arithmetic, 1,000 CCAs triple the Air Force's air-to-air missile magazine without tripling the pilot pipeline.

## Original Calculation: Cost Per Missile-in-the-Sky

### F-35 missile delivery economics:
- F-35A flyaway cost: ~$82M (recent lots)
- Operating cost: ~$42,000/flight hour (GAO 2022 figure); ~$33,000-36,000/hr by FY2024 DoD estimates
- Internal AMRAAM capacity: 4 AIM-120s
- AIM-120C-8 domestic unit cost: ~$1.37M (FY2024 DoD procurement: 831 missiles for $1.14B)
- AIM-120D3 export unit cost: ~$3.325M (Poland deal: 400 missiles for $1.33B)
- Pilot training cost: ~$11M over career (USAF estimates)
- F-35 maintenance hours per flight hour: ~13:1 (vs F-16 at ~5:1)
- **Total platform cost to put 4 AMRAAMs in the sky: ~$90M+ ($82M aircraft + $5.48M missiles + pilot)**
- **Cost per AMRAAM slot: ~$22.5M**

### CCA missile delivery economics:
- CCA per-unit cost target: under $30M (Air Force says "beating" this — Col. Helfrich, Defense One)
- Estimated realistic unit cost: $20-25M (based on "doing much better" than 1/3 F-35 target)
- Operating cost: No pilot life support, ejection system, oxygen, G-suit systems; autonomous ops via ruggedized laptop; small maintainer crew
- Estimated CPFH: $8,000-15,000/hr (no pilot systems, simpler sensor suite for "follower" role)
- AMRAAM capacity: 2 (estimated, air superiority config for Increment 1)
- No pilot training cost ($0 vs $11M)
- **Total platform cost to put 2 AMRAAMs in the sky: ~$25-28M**
- **Cost per AMRAAM slot: ~$12.5-14M**
- **That's 38% cheaper per missile delivered than F-35**

## Original Calculation: The Attrition Ratio

### Loss of an F-35 + pilot:
- Aircraft: $82M
- Trained pilot (10+ years training): ~$11M sunk cost
- Human life: irreplaceable
- Political cost: enormous (Gold Star family, congressional hearings, media coverage)
- Fleet replacement time: 2-3 years for new airframe
- **Total financial loss: $93M+, plus irreplaceable human capital**

### Loss of a CCA:
- Aircraft: $25M
- No pilot loss
- No political cost
- Replacement: months (Arsenal-1 designed for high-rate production)
- **Total financial loss: $25M. Period.**
- **You can lose 3.7 CCAs for the cost of one F-35**
- **Critical insight: CCAs can fly into SAM-defended airspace that's currently denied to manned aircraft. The math changes the tactics.**

## Original Calculation: Force Multiplication Math

### Current Air Force air-to-air capacity:
- ~300 F-35As operational (out of 900+ total delivered, all variants, worldwide)
- Each carries 4 internal AMRAAMs in stealth config
- = ~1,200 stealth air-to-air missiles at any given time

### With CCA fleet added:
- Planned: 500 NGAD fighters × 2 CCAs each = 1,000 CCA drones
- FY2027 procurement: ~$1B = ~33-40 CCAs at $25-30M each
- 150+ CCAs by end of decade (Col. Helfrich)
- If each CCA carries 2 AMRAAMs: 1,000 × 2 = 2,000 additional missiles
- **Total: 1,200 (F-35s) + 2,000 (CCAs) = 3,200 stealth missiles**
- **That's a 2.67× increase in air-to-air missile capacity**
- **For the same $1B, you get 33 CCAs (66 missile slots) vs 12 F-35s (48 missile slots)**

## Strongest Counterargument
The CCA's autonomy is completely untested in real combat. Lab tests and simulated targets are not dogfights. In a contested electromagnetic environment — with GPS jamming, communications denial, and enemy electronic warfare — a CCA that loses its data link becomes a $25M paperweight flying blindly. The Air Force's assumption that these drones will execute "beyond-line-of-sight strikes" requires communications reliability that no military has achieved in a near-peer conflict. China's PLA has specifically developed anti-satellite and electronic warfare capabilities designed to sever exactly the kind of data links CCAs depend on.

## Limitations
- CCA unit costs are targets, not final procurement prices. Defense programs routinely exceed cost targets by 30-60%.
- The "under $30M" figure is the Air Force's claim — actual contract values haven't been disclosed
- CPFH estimates for CCAs are projected, not measured (no operational CCA has logged enough hours)
- AMRAAM load-out for CCAs is estimated from the air superiority mission profile; actual internal carriage TBD
- This analysis assumes Increment 1 (air superiority role). Increment 2 may have different costs/capabilities
- Arsenal-1 hasn't delivered production aircraft yet — high-rate manufacturing claims are aspirational
