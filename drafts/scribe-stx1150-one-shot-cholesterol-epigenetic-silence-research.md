# Research: Scribe STX-1150 Epigenetic Cholesterol Silencing

## Topic
First-in-human trial of CRISPR-based epigenetic silencing (NOT permanent gene editing) for cardiovascular disease. Scribe Therapeutics' STX-1150 silences PCSK9 gene to reduce LDL-C. Single dose. Reversible.

## Original Calculation: The Compliance Tax

### Inputs
- 40M Americans prescribed statins (AHA/CDC)
- 75% non-persistent at year 5 = 30M non-adherent
- Statin efficacy: ~22% CV event risk reduction per 1 mmol/L LDL-C reduction (CTT meta-analysis)
- Baseline 10-year MACE rate for statin-eligible population: ~7.5% (conservative, ACC/AHA threshold)
- Average acute MACE cost: ~$28,000 (AHA estimates)

### Calculation
Preventable events lost to non-adherence per decade:
30,000,000 × 0.075 × 0.22 = 495,000 events

Annual avoidable hospital cost:
495,000 / 10 × $28,000 = ~$1.39 billion/year

### Break-even for one-shot therapy
If STX-1150 could be administered to 30M non-adherent patients:
- At $100/dose: $3B total, recovered in ~2.2 years from avoided hospitalizations
- At $1,000/dose: $30B, recovered in ~21.6 years
- At $5,000/dose: $150B, not system-cost-saving from hospitalizations alone
- But: if priced like inclisiran (~$6,500/year), a single dose that lasts 5 years = $32,500 value equivalent

### Key insight
The cheapest drug is the one the patient takes. A $48 pill that 75% of patients abandon delivers less population-level cardiovascular protection than a $10,000 one-shot that works for years.

## Strongest Counterargument
STX-1150 is Phase 1 in 64 patients. NHP data does not always translate. The 18-month durability in primates could be much shorter in humans. Statins have 30+ years of outcomes data in hundreds of thousands of patients across dozens of randomized trials. No epigenetic silencer has ever demonstrated cardiovascular outcomes benefit. The compliance argument, while economically sound, assumes STX-1150 works at all in humans — which is the very question this trial exists to answer.
