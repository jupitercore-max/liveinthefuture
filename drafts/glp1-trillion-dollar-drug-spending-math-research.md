# Research: GLP-1 Drugs and the $1 Trillion Drug Spending Threshold

## Angle
U.S. prescription drug spending will cross $1 trillion in 2026 for the first time. A single drug class — GLP-1 receptor agonists — accounts for 14% of all spending and one-third of all growth. 40 million Americans now take these drugs. We ran the math on whether the drugs pay for themselves in avoided medical costs.

## Kill Test: Original Calculation
Cross-reference per-patient GLP-1 costs with real-world per-patient medical cost savings data to calculate the population-level net cost. Nobody has done this at the 40M-user scale with 2026 data.

## Original Calculations

### Calculation 1: Population-Level Cost-Benefit
- 40M GLP-1 users in the US
- Average annual GLP-1 drug cost: $132B / 40M = $3,300/user/year
- Per-patient annual medical savings: $3,342 (Komodo/PubMed study)
- At first glance: $3,300 cost ≈ $3,342 savings → roughly break-even

### Calculation 2: The Comorbidity Problem
- NOT all 40M users have obesity-related complications (the study population)
- 68% use brand-name, 19% compounded, 12% don't know
- Many users are BMI 25-30 "overweight" using for cosmetic weight loss
- Estimate: 60-70% of users (24-28M) have ≥1 obesity-related complication
- Those generating maximum medical savings: ~25M
- 25M × $3,342 = $83.6B in realized medical savings
- vs $132B total drug spending
- Net annual cost to the system: ~$48B

### Calculation 3: The Obesity Reduction Dividend
- Obesity rate: 39.9% (2022) → 36.4% (2026) = 3.5 percentage point drop
- US adult population: ~264M
- 3.5% × 264M = ~9.2M fewer obese adults
- During this period: cumulative GLP-1 spending ~$250B+ (2023-2025)
- Cost per person moved out of obesity: ~$27,000
- Annual obesity-attributable medical cost per obese adult: $2,505 (CDC estimate)
- Payback period: ~11 years per person (if they stay out of obesity)

### Calculation 4: Budget Comparisons
- GLP-1 spending ($132B) vs:
  - NASA ($25.4B) + EPA ($9.6B) + NSF ($10.1B) = $45.1B → GLP-1 = 2.9× these combined
  - Entire US National Institutes of Health budget (~$48B) → GLP-1 = 2.75× NIH
  - US military aid to Ukraine 2022-2025 (~$56B total) → GLP-1 annual > total Ukraine military aid
  - Total US agricultural subsidies (~$30B/year) → GLP-1 = 4.4× farm subsidies

### Calculation 5: The "California Problem"
- 40M Americans on GLP-1 = population of California
- If GLP-1 users were a country, their drug spending alone ($132B) would rank as the 57th largest GDP
- Per-capita GLP-1 spending: $132B / 330M US population = $400/person/year (whether you take them or not)

## Strongest Counterargument
The $3,342/year medical savings figure comes from a 12-month observational study of patients with ≥1 obesity-related complication. This likely overstates savings for several reasons:
1. Selection bias: patients who start GLP-1s may be more health-conscious
2. The savings may not persist — 40% of patients discontinue within a year
3. Weight regain after discontinuation erases medical benefits
4. Many users don't have comorbidities, so they generate minimal medical savings
The true population-average savings is probably $1,500-2,500/patient, making GLP-1s a net cost to the healthcare system.

## Limitations
- The $132B figure includes both diabetes and obesity indications; we can't cleanly separate
- Adherence data varies: 35-60% annual discontinuation rates across studies
- Medical cost savings are from 12-month follow-up; long-term effects unknown
- The obesity rate decline may not be entirely attributable to GLP-1s (pandemic reversal, other factors)
- Compounded semaglutide (19% of users) is cheaper and excluded from some spending data
