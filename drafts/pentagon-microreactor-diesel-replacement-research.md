# Research: Pentagon Microreactor vs. Diesel — The Fleet Replacement Math

## Core Angle
Two nuclear microreactors went critical in June 2026, 14 days apart — the first new reactor designs tested at INL in 50+ years. 10 companies are racing under Trump's DOE Reactor Pilot Program to hit a July 4, 2026 deadline for 3 criticalities. This article runs the original fleet-replacement math: what it would cost to swap DOD's diesel generators for microreactors at every installation, vs. what DOD currently pays for fuel.

## Key Facts & Sources

### Criticality Milestones
- **Antares Nuclear Mark-0**: Zero-power criticality at INL on June 4, 2026. First under DOE Reactor Pilot Program. 53rd reactor built at INL since 1951, first new design in 50+ years. (Source: powermag.com, army.mil, businesswire.com)
- **Valar Atomics Ward 250**: Zero-power criticality at Utah San Rafael Energy Lab on June 18, 2026. Second under RPP. First DOE-authorized reactor built and operated OUTSIDE national lab system. Gen IV TRISO-fueled helium-cooled HTGR. (Source: powermag.com, world-nuclear-news.org)
- **July 4 deadline**: EO 14301 mandated ≥3 reactors. 2 of 3 confirmed by June 18.
- **10 companies selected**: Aalo Atomics, Antares Nuclear, Atomic Alchemy (Oklo sub), Deep Fission, Last Energy, Natura Resources, Oklo (2 projects), Radiant Industries, Terrestrial Energy, Valar Atomics

### Antares R1 Commercial Design
- 100 kWe to 1 MWe modular, transportable
- TRISO fuel (HALEU, 19.75% enriched), prismatic graphite core
- Sodium heat pipes — passive, no pumps, no moving parts
- Nitrogen-closed Brayton cycle, <300 psi
- 6+ years between refueling
- Ships in integrated transport cradle with shielding
- Selected for Joint Base San Antonio by 2028
- Timeline: criticality 2026, electricity 2027, power to warfighter 2028
- CEO Jordan Bramble: "We said criticality in 2026, electricity production in 2027, and power to the warfighter in 2028. Today is the first of those commitments delivered on the schedule we set."

### Valar Ward 250 / Ward Reactor
- 5 MWe capacity (Ward reactor, as demonstrated in C-17 transport Feb 2026)
- TRISO-fueled, helium-cooled, graphite-moderated HTGR
- Plans "gigasites" with clusters of thousands of reactors
- First DOE-authorized reactor outside national labs
- Began non-commercial power ascension after criticality

### BWXT Project Pele
- $300M prototype contract (includes design + manufacturing + testing)
- 1-5 MWe transportable
- Assembled in 3 days, disassembled in 7
- Delivered to INL for testing
- TRISO fuel manufactured at BWXT Lynchburg, VA

### Janus Program (Army/ANPI)
- 8 companies for Advanced Nuclear Power for Installations
- Antares, BWXT, General Atomics, Kairos Power, Oklo, Radiant, Westinghouse, X-energy
- Build-own-operate model (like NASA COTS)
- 9 installation sites identified (Fort Liberty, Fort Cavazos, Fort Drum)
- 1-20 MWe range, shipping container transportable

### DOD Energy Consumption Data
- **$20B+/year** total DOD energy spending (fuel + electricity)
- **30 TWh/year** electricity at 500+ installations
- **85 million barrels** fuel/year
- **$4B/year** installation energy bill (Pew/Noblis study)
- **$3.8B requested** for installation energy FY2025 (Congressional testimony)
- Army generators: **357 million gallons/year** during wartime vs 26 million peacetime (2008 DSB Task Force)
- Generators are **single largest battlefield fuel consumer** — powering AC, lighting, refrigeration, comms

### Fully Burdened Cost of Fuel (FBCF)
- DESC wholesale price: **$2.19/gallon**
- Forward operating base (Afghanistan): **$6.39/gallon** (MEAT estimate)
- Tactical edge (outside the wire): **$11.70/gallon**
- Air/helicopter delivery to remote sites: **$20-$200/gallon**
- Extreme contested/remote: up to **$400/gallon** (DSB 2009, cited by Gen. Conway)
- Some analysts estimate up to **$1,000/gallon** in worst cases
- **1 casualty per 24 fuel convoys** (2009 Army EPI)
- **70% of convoys** are "liquid logistics" (fuel + water)
- June 2008: 44 trucks, 220,000 gallons fuel lost in single month at Bagram alone

### Microreactor Economics
- BWXT Project Pele prototype: ~$300M (first-of-a-kind, includes everything)
- Oklo Aurora 15 MWe: ~$70M, LCOE $80-130/MWh
- Michigan NERS study: microreactor LCOE $48-78/MWh with PTC, $50-67/MWh optimized
- SMR LCOE first-of-a-kind: $89-102/MWh
- PMC mass-production study: 20 MWe factory-built ~$70M ($3,465/kW)
- Expert median for 45 MWe SMR: $5,800/kW

## ORIGINAL CALCULATION: Diesel vs. Microreactor LCOE at Military Bases

### Diesel Generator Cost Per MWh
- Military diesel generator efficiency: ~35% (standard spec)
- 1 gallon diesel = ~38.3 kWh thermal energy
- At 35% efficiency: 1 gallon → 13.4 kWh electrical
- Therefore: 74.6 gallons per MWh

**LCOE of diesel at various FBCF levels:**
| FBCF ($/gallon) | Cost per MWh | Context |
|---|---|---|
| $2.19 | $163 | Wholesale (base price) |
| $6.39 | $477 | FOB Afghanistan |
| $11.70 | $873 | Tactical edge |
| $100 | $7,460 | Air delivery remote |
| $400 | $29,840 | Extreme contested |

**Microreactor LCOE (mass production estimates):**
| Technology | LCOE per MWh |
|---|---|
| Optimized microreactor with PTC | $48-78 |
| Oklo Aurora target | $80-130 |
| First-of-a-kind SMR | $89-102 |

**Ratio: At an FOB ($477/MWh diesel), a $60/MWh microreactor is 8x cheaper. At the tactical edge ($873/MWh), it's 15x cheaper. At air-delivery prices ($7,460/MWh), it's 124x cheaper.**

### Fleet Replacement Math
- 500+ DOD installations consuming 30 TWh/year
- Average per installation: 60 GWh/year = 6.85 MW average load
- At Antares R1 scale (1 MWe): ~3,425 units to power every base
- At Oklo scale (15 MWe): ~229 units
- At a mid-range estimate of ~7 MW avg, and using $3,465/kW mass-production estimate:
  - Total capital: $3,465/kW × 3,425,000 kW = **$11.9B**
- Current annual installation energy bill: **$4B/year**
- **Simple payback: ~3 years** (before counting convoy casualty savings, grid resilience value, reduced logistics chain)
- Even at $5,800/kW (expert median): $19.9B → 5-year payback

### What This Doesn't Account For (Limitations)
- Zero-power criticality ≠ electricity production (Antares targets electricity 2027)
- HALEU fuel supply is constrained — currently dependent on downblending Russian HEU or limited US production (Centrus/BWXT)
- LCOE estimates are for nth-of-a-kind mass production; first units will be 3-5x more expensive
- NRC commercial licensing still required after DOE pilot demonstrations
- Spent fuel disposal — no established pathway for TRISO/HALEU spent fuel
- Nuclear proliferation concerns with transportable HALEU reactors
- Not all 500+ installations need standalone power; many are on reliable grids

### Strongest Counterargument
Nuclear has a terrible track record on cost and schedule:
- NuScale Idaho project: escalated $5.3B → $9.3B, then cancelled
- Vogtle Units 3+4: $35B (double projection), 7 years late
- Every prior "nuclear renaissance" has failed to deliver on cost
- The entire business case rests on mass production achieving learning curves that have never been demonstrated for nuclear
- Centre for Net Zero found renewables + gas costs 43% less than SMRs for a 120 MW facility
