## Core Thesis

The 2026 El Niño is forming on a baseline ~1.3°C warmer than pre-industrial, meaning its absolute temperature peak will shatter any prior El Niño in the instrumental record. It's simultaneously converging with the Iran war's disruption to global fertilizer supply chains through the Strait of Hormuz. The original analysis calculates the "caloric deficit at risk" — how many calories for how many people could be lost when drought hits rice production in countries that produce 55% of global supply, compounded by a fertilizer shortage that forces farmers to apply less.

## Kill Test: Original Calculation

**Nobody has run this specific calculation:** normalizing the per-degree economic damage curve from the Dartmouth Science study against the 2026 event's hotter baseline, then mapping the rice production concentration risk against the FAO's new high-resolution drought probability maps.

### Calculation 1: Per-Degree Economic Damage (Nonlinear)

Historical data:
- 1982-83 El Niño: Peak ONI ~+2.1°C → $4.1T damage over 5 years (Callahan & Mankin, Science 2023)
- 1997-98 El Niño: Peak ONI ~+2.3°C → $5.7T damage over 5 years
- 2015-16 El Niño: Peak ONI ~+2.3°C (tied record) → estimated similar

The relationship is nonlinear. A 0.2°C increase in peak ONI (1982 to 1997) produced a 39% increase in economic damage ($4.1T → $5.7T).

**2026 projections:**
- Current Niño3.4: +1.24°C (as of week ending June 28)
- Rate of warming: +0.3°C in last 2 weeks
- Australian BoM: "could peak at levels among the highest observed since 1950"
- CSU Phil Klotzbach: "This will be a 1997 event"
- NOAA: 63% probability of "very strong" by end of year

If peak ONI reaches +2.5°C (conservative for "strongest since 1950"), using the nonlinear damage relationship:
- Damage = $5.7T × (2.5/2.3)^1.8 ≈ $6.8T (my estimate using power-law fit)

But this ignores the **baseline shift**: 2026 global temps are already ~1.3°C above pre-industrial. In 1997, the baseline was ~0.4°C above pre-industrial. Crops respond to absolute temperature, not anomalies.

**Citigroup estimate (independent confirmation):** $3-5T base case, up to $7T for super El Niño (6.4% of global GDP)

### Calculation 2: Rice Caloric Deficit at Risk

**Global rice production 2025/26:** 541 million metric tons (milled), per USDA
**Top producers under El Niño drought risk:**
- India: 152 MT (28% of global) — **5th driest June since 1901, 39.8% below average rainfall**
- Indonesia: 34.4 MT (6.3%) — **47% of zones have below-normal rainfall, BMKG warning**
- Thailand: 19.5 MT (3.6%) — farmers cutting fertilizer by 50% due to cost
- Philippines: 12.2 MT (2.3%) — could lose up to 6 MT (out of 19-20 MT total supply incl imports), per Integrated Rural Development Foundation

**Historical El Niño rice production drops:**
- 1997-98: Indonesia -4.3%, India -2%, Philippines -6.5%, Australia -90%+
- 2015-16: India ~-2%, Indonesia ~-3%

**My calculation:**
If 2026 repeats the 1997-98 pattern on rice production (conservative):
- India: 152 MT × 2% loss = 3.04 MT lost
- Indonesia: 34.4 MT × 4.3% = 1.48 MT lost  
- Thailand: 19.5 MT × 3% (estimated from fertilizer cuts) = 0.59 MT lost
- Philippines: 12.2 MT × 6.5% = 0.79 MT lost

**Total at risk: ~5.9 million metric tons of milled rice**

Rice provides ~1,527 kcal per kg (milled). 
5.9 MT = 5.9 billion kg × 1,527 kcal = **9.0 trillion kcal at risk**

Daily caloric need per person: ~2,000 kcal
**That's 12.3 million person-years of calories.** Or enough to feed the population of Portugal for a year.

But this is conservative. The 2026 event has two compounding factors the 1997-98 event didn't:
1. **Hotter baseline:** Crops face absolute heat, not anomalies. The +1.3°C higher baseline means peak temperatures during drought will exceed anything rice paddies in India have experienced.
2. **Fertilizer shortage:** Iran war → Strait of Hormuz disruption → fertilizer prices up. Thai farmers cutting fertilizer by 50%. Philippines farmers considering not planting at all.

### Calculation 3: The Fertilizer Compounding Effect

Strait of Hormuz handles ~20% of global LNG trade and significant fertilizer raw material shipments.
Rice prices already up 15% in past month at SE Asian export hubs.
Wheat prices up 20% since start of 2026.
Thai fertilizer: up from 850 baht/bag to 1,000-1,200 baht/bag (~30-40% increase).

If fertilizer cuts reduce yields by an additional 5-10% on top of drought losses (conservative — cutting fertilizer in half typically costs 15-25% of yield), the total rice production deficit could reach 8-10 MT.

**That's 15-18 million person-years of calories at risk.**

## Strongest Counterargument

China (27% of global rice) has historically been **less affected** by El Niño drought than India and SE Asia. If China has a normal or bumper crop, global supplies might hold. Also, global rice stocks are near record highs (~187 MT of ending stocks in 2024/25 per USDA), providing a buffer. The 2023-24 El Niño didn't cause the feared rice crisis — though that was a moderate event, not a super one.

## Limitations

1. The nonlinear damage curve is fit from only two major data points (1982-83 and 1997-98). The 2015-16 event, while tied for strongest ONI, had different geographic patterns (more central Pacific) and different economic outcomes.
2. India's monsoon forecasts are notoriously volatile — a delayed monsoon in June can still produce near-normal July-September rains.
3. The fertilizer compounding effect is estimated, not empirically measured for simultaneous drought+fertilizer shortage.
4. My "person-years of calories" calculation assumes all lost rice is lost calories, when in practice substitution effects (wheat, maize) partially buffer.
