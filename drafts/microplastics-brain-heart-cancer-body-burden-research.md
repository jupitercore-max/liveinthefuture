## Novel Contribution
1. **Brain bioaccumulation rate calculation:** Brain MNP concentration went from ~4,800 µg/g (pre-2016 samples) to ~12,000+ µg/g (2024 samples) — a 2.5× increase over ~8 years = ~12% compound annual growth. At that rate, brain MNP concentration doubles every ~6 years.
2. **Population-attributable risk:** If 100% of blood samples contain MNPs (ACC.25 data) and MNPs in arterial plaques carry a 4.53× hazard ratio for MI/stroke/death (NEJM), what fraction of cardiovascular events are potentially MNP-attributable? PAF = (prevalence × (HR-1)) / (prevalence × (HR-1) + 1) = (1.0 × 3.53) / (1.0 × 3.53 + 1) = 3.53/4.53 = 77.9%. CAVEAT: This assumes the NEJM hazard ratio is causal and applies at all exposure levels. The real PAF is certainly lower — the NEJM study compared detectable-in-plaque vs. not-detectable-in-plaque, not exposed vs. unexposed. But the direction is striking.
3. **Production-exposure gap:** Global plastic production is ~400 million metric tons/year and projected to triple by 2060. Humans already ingest ~5g/week (WWF/Newcastle estimate). Production is accelerating while accumulation is compounding.

## Primary Sources

### Source 1: Nature Medicine (Feb 2025) — Brain Bioaccumulation
- **Paper:** Nihart AJ, Garcia MA, et al. "Bioaccumulation of microplastics in decedent human brains." Nature Medicine 31(4):1114-1119.
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41591-024-03453-1
- **Institution:** University of New Mexico Health Sciences
- **Key findings:**
  - MNP concentration in brain tissue significantly higher than liver and kidney (7-30×)
  - Brain MNP concentration increased markedly from pre-2016 to 2024 autopsy samples
  - Primarily polyethylene (PE), with lesser polypropylene, polystyrene
  - Confirmed via pyrolysis GC-MS, ATR-FTIR, and electron microscopy with EDS
  - Blood-brain barrier is NOT preventing accumulation
  - Younger decedents (who grew up in higher-plastic-production era) tended to have higher concentrations

### Source 2: NEJM (March 2024) — Cardiovascular Events
- **Paper:** Marfella R, et al. "Microplastics and Nanoplastics in Atheromas and Cardiovascular Events." N Engl J Med 390(10):900-910.
- **DOI:** 10.1056/NEJMoa2309822
- **Institution:** University of Campania Luigi Vanvitelli
- **Key findings:**
  - Patients with MNPs detected in carotid artery atheromas (plaques): hazard ratio 4.53 (95% CI: 2.00-10.27, P<0.001) for composite of MI, stroke, or death over 34 months
  - 150 patients undergoing carotid endarterectomy; 58.4% had detectable polyethylene in plaques, 12.1% had PVC
  - MNP-positive plaques had higher inflammatory markers (IL-18, IL-1β, TNF-α) and more macrophage infiltration
  - First prospective evidence directly linking MNPs in human tissue to clinical cardiovascular outcomes

### Source 3: ACC.25 Presentations (March 2025) — Blood & Population Data
- **Study 1 (Nachnani et al.):**
  - Microplastics detected in 100% of analyzed blood samples
  - 58-100% of patients had MNPs within arterial plaques
  - Patients with acute coronary syndromes (especially acute MI) had higher MNP concentrations
  - Strong correlation between plaque MNPs and increased risk of MI, stroke, death

- **Study 2 (Ponnana et al.):**
  - 555 U.S. coastal census tracts analyzed
  - Microplastic concentration ranked in top 10 among 154 socioeconomic/environmental factors for predicting NCDs
  - Dose-response correlations: hypertension (r=0.24), diabetes (r=0.30), stroke (r=0.26)
  - Machine learning model: microplastic concentration was significant predictor of stroke prevalence, comparable to minority race and lack of insurance

### Source 4: Molecular Cancer (April 2026) — Carcinogenicity Pathways
- **Paper:** Abass D, Marcos R, Hernández A. "Emerging evidence on micro- and nanoplastics carcinogenicity: mechanisms, models, and signaling networks." Molecular Cancer.
- **DOI:** 10.1186/s12943-026-02657-y
- **Key findings:**
  - Higher concentrations of microplastics in cancerous tissues vs. adjacent healthy tissues (barrier organs: lungs, intestines, reproductive system)
  - Specific signaling pathways identified: NF-κB (inflammation/survival), JNK-MAPK, ERK1/2-MAPK, JAK-STAT (inflammation, DNA damage), PI3K-AKT-mTOR (proliferation)
  - Smaller nanoplastics cross biological barriers and accumulate in cells; larger microplastics cause physical stress, tight junction disruption, microbiota perturbation
  - MNPLs create "tumor-permissive environment" via metabolic reprogramming, malignant transformation

### Source 5: Nature Reviews Gastro & Hepatology (April 2026) — Liver Disease
- **Paper:** "Microplastics, nanoplastics and liver disease: an emerging health concern?"
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41575-026-01188-7
- **Key finding:** MNPs linked to liver fibrosis via cGAS/STING pathway activation

### Source 6: WWF/University of Newcastle (2019) — Human Ingestion Estimate
- Humans ingest approximately 5 grams of plastic per week (equivalent to credit card weight)
- ~100,000 microplastic particles per year
- ~250 grams total per year
- Sources: drinking water (largest), shellfish, beer, salt

### Source 7: UN Global Plastics Treaty Failure (August 2025)
- INC-5.2 negotiations in Geneva collapsed without agreement
- Saudi Arabia, Iran, Russia, China blocked production limits
- Petrochemical industry lobbied against binding targets
- No global regulatory framework for microplastics exists

### Source 8: Global Plastic Production
- ~400 million metric tons produced annually (2023)
- Projected to triple to ~1.2 billion metric tons by 2060 (OECD)
- Less than 9% of all plastic ever produced has been recycled (UNEP)
- The remaining 91% is in landfills, oceans, or the environment

## Strongest Counterargument
The NEJM study is observational, not randomized. Patients with MNPs in their plaques may differ in ways that independently increase cardiovascular risk (confounding by socioeconomic status, occupational exposure, diet, smoking). The hazard ratio of 4.53 survived adjustment for traditional risk factors, but residual confounding cannot be ruled out. The brain study shows correlation (concentration increase over time) but cannot establish that MNPs *cause* neurological harm — the brain may simply be accumulating inert material. And the WWF's "credit card per week" estimate has been challenged as an upper bound that overweights the drinking water pathway.

## Limitations
- Brain bioaccumulation study used autopsy samples (selection bias — decedents may not represent general population)
- NEJM cardiovascular study was single-center in Italy (may not generalize)
- No randomized controlled trial data exists or is feasible — you can't randomly assign humans to microplastic exposure
- Cancer pathway data is from in vitro and animal models — not yet confirmed in human epidemiological studies
- Exact dose-response relationship between exposure and disease in humans remains unknown
- The bioaccumulation rate calculation assumes linear extrapolation from two time points, which may not hold
