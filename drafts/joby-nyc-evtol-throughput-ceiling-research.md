# Research: Joby's NYC Air Taxi Demo — The Throughput Ceiling Nobody's Discussing

## Story Summary
Joby Aviation flew its first point-to-point eVTOL demonstration flights in NYC on April 27, 2026: JFK to Manhattan heliports in under 10 minutes. This is the most concrete milestone in eVTOL history — a real aircraft, real routes, real airspace, with FAA cooperation. But nobody is asking the infrastructure capacity question: can 3 Manhattan heliports handle enough flights to make this more than a luxury niche?

## Novel Contribution: The Throughput Ceiling Calculation

### Manhattan Heliport Capacity
- 3 heliports serve commercial traffic: Downtown Skyport, West 30th St, East 34th St
- NYC heliport operating hours: typically 8am-8pm due to noise restrictions (12 hours)
- Minimum spacing between eVTOL operations: ~3 minutes (landing, offload, reload, takeoff)
- Max operations per heliport per hour: ~12-15 (FAA separation + turnaround)
- Theoretical daily capacity: 3 heliports × 15 ops/hr × 12 hrs = 540 flights/day
- At 4 passengers/flight = 2,160 passengers/day
- Compare to JFK's ~73,000 daily passengers = 2.9% of JFK traffic at maximum theoretical capacity
- Compare to Blade's 90,000/year ÷ 365 = ~247/day (already using significant heliport capacity)

### Cost-per-mile comparison
- JFK to Midtown Manhattan: ~15 miles as the crow flies
- eVTOL at $200/seat: $13.33/mile
- Helicopter (Blade era): ~$200/seat: $13.33/mile
- Rideshare: $150-200 for ~18 miles (ground route): $8.33-11.11/mile
- Taxi: $100/ride ÷ 18 miles = $5.55/mile (splits to $1.39/mile with 4 passengers)
- Subway + AirTrain: $11.40/~18 miles = $0.63/mile

### Time-value analysis
- Rideshare/taxi JFK to Midtown: 45-90 min (avg 60 min)
- eVTOL: ~7-10 min flight + ~15-20 min getting to/from heliports = ~30 min door-to-door
- Time saved: ~30 minutes per trip
- NYC median hourly wage: ~$40/hr
- Value of 30 min: $20
- Premium paid over subway ($11.40 → $200): $188.60 for 30 min saved = $377/hr implied valuation of time
- Premium over taxi ($100 → $200): $100 for 30 min = $200/hr implied valuation
- This makes economic sense only for people whose time is worth $200+/hr ($400K+/year salary)

### The Real Question
Blade already ran this exact business model (helicopter NYC airport transfers) and the passenger business was SOLD to Joby. It served 90,000 passengers per year — a niche luxury product. The promise of eVTOL was supposed to be lower costs enabling mass-market adoption. But at $200/seat, it's the same price as Blade's helicopters. The only difference is lower noise and zero emissions.

To reach $200M+ revenue at $200/seat, Joby needs 1M+ passengers/year on the NYC corridor alone. That's 2,740/day. Current heliport infrastructure can barely handle that even at 100% utilization.
