# Research: UCLA/Ewha ATT Process — Mixed Plastics to Hydrogen

## Core Story
UCLA Samueli + Ewha Womans University (South Korea) published in PNAS (July 6, 2026) a process called Alkaline Thermal Treatment (ATT) that converts mixed PET/PE/PP plastic waste into high-purity hydrogen without sorting — at temperatures 300-400°C below conventional gasification — while capturing >75% of carbon as solid mineral (sodium carbonate).

## Kill Test: Original Calculation
**Nobody has run the global-scale hydrogen economics math.** I will calculate:
1. Hydrogen yield per tonne of mixed plastic at global waste volumes
2. What fraction of global H2 demand this could theoretically supply
3. CO2 avoided vs. incineration at scale
4. Economic value of ATT hydrogen vs. green/gray H2 prices

## Primary Sources

### 1. PNAS Paper (Primary)
- **Title:** "Selective and direct hydrogen generation from mixed plastic waste via alkaline thermal treatment with inherent carbon storage"
- **DOI:** 10.1073/pnas.2537552123
- **Published:** July 6, 2026
- **Authors:** Jieun Park et al. (Ewha Womans U + UCLA)
- **Key data:**
  - H2 yields: PET = 43.7 mmol/g, PE = 51.9 mmol/g, PP = 30.2 mmol/g
  - Temperature: 300-400°C below conventional steam gasification (~700-1000°C)
  - Purity: >90% H2
  - Carbon fate: >75% captured as solid carbonate or liquid organics, <13% gaseous
  - Operates at atmospheric pressure
  - NaOH-assisted alkaline thermal treatment
  - Thermal oxidation pretreatment activates PE/PP (oxygen functional groups lower reaction barriers)
  - Works on individual AND mixed waste without separation

- Co-PI: Ah-Hyung "Alissa" Park, Dean of UCLA Samueli

## Supplementary Data for Calculations

### Global Plastic Waste
- 2025: 225 million tonnes generated globally (Earth Action / letsrecycle.com Sep 2025)
- Only 9% recycled, 79% landfilled, 12% incinerated (OECD/PNAS paper)
- PET + PE + PP = ~65% of plastic waste stream (industry standard)
- Available feedstock (non-recycled PET/PE/PP): ~133 million tonnes/year

### Hydrogen Economics
- Gray H2 (SMR): $1.11-2.35/kg (BNEF 2026)
- Green H2 (electrolysis): $3.74-11.70/kg current, projected $1.60-5.09/kg by 2050 (BNEF)
- Global H2 demand: ~100 million tonnes/year (IEA)
- SMR produces 11 kg CO2 per kg H2 (ACS C&EN)

### Carbon Intensity
- Plastic incineration: ~2.3-3.1 tonnes CO2/tonne plastic (varies by type)
- Average plastic: ~60% carbon by weight → 2,200 kg CO2 potential per tonne
- ATT captures >75% → saves ~1,650 kg CO2/tonne vs. incineration

## Original Calculations

### 1. H2 Yield Per Tonne of Mixed Plastic
- Weighted average yield (equal mix PET/PE/PP): (43.7 + 51.9 + 30.2) / 3 = 41.9 mmol/g
- Molecular weight H2 = 2.016 g/mol
- Yield = 41.9 × 10⁻³ × 2.016 = 0.0845 g H2 per g plastic = **84.5 kg H2 per tonne of plastic**
- Note: H2 yield exceeds theoretical H content of plastic because water/NaOH contribute hydrogen

### 2. Global Scale Potential
- Available feedstock: ~133 million tonnes PET/PE/PP waste/year
- At 84.5 kg H2/tonne: 133M × 0.0845 = **11.2 million tonnes H2/year**
- Global H2 demand: ~100 million tonnes/year
- **ATT plastic-to-H2 could supply ~11% of global hydrogen demand**

### 3. CO2 Comparison
- Incineration CO2: ~2.7 tonnes CO2 per tonne plastic (midpoint)
- ATT captures >75% of carbon → releases <0.68 tonnes CO2/tonne
- Net CO2 savings: ~2.0 tonnes CO2/tonne plastic vs. incineration
- At 133M tonnes: **~266 million tonnes CO2 avoided/year** (0.7% of global emissions)
- For context: that's roughly the annual CO2 output of France

### 4. Economic Value
- Gray H2 midpoint: $1.73/kg → value per tonne plastic: 84.5 × $1.73 = **$146/tonne**
- Green H2 midpoint: $5/kg → value per tonne plastic: 84.5 × $5 = **$423/tonne**
- Current recycled plastic value: $100-300/tonne (sorted, clean)
- MRF sorting cost: $50-200/tonne (eliminated by ATT)
- Key insight: ATT H2 value ($146-423) competes with or exceeds recycled plastic value ($100-300) while ALSO avoiding sorting costs

## Strongest Counterargument
- Lab-scale results only — no pilot plant, no OPEX data, no NaOH cost accounting
- NaOH is energy-intensive to produce (chlor-alkali process, ~2.5 MWh per tonne)
- Na2CO3 byproduct must find market or becomes waste itself
- Actual mixed-stream plastics contain contaminants (dyes, adhesives, food residue)
- Scale: even 11% of H2 demand requires processing ALL non-recycled PET/PE/PP globally

## Limitations
- Yield numbers are lab-scale; industrial losses would reduce efficiency
- Economic analysis excludes NaOH cost, reactor CAPEX, energy input for pretreatment
- The 300-400°C temperature advantage does not mean ATT is low-energy; NaOH production and thermal oxidation pretreatment add upstream energy costs
- Mixed plastic waste composition varies geographically; yield calculations assume equal mix
- Only 3 of 7 common plastic types tested (misses PS, PVC, LDPE, nylon)
