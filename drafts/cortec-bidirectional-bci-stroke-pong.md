# Research: CorTec Brain Interchange — First Bidirectional Fully Implanted Wireless BCI

## Story Angle
The first BCI that both heals your brain AND reads your mind — using the same hardware. While Neuralink (21 patients) and Synchron race to build the best brain reader, a German startup demonstrated something nobody else has: a single fully implanted wireless device that does therapy (brain stimulation for stroke recovery) AND augmentation (computer control via thought). No modification to the implant required. Same hardware, dual function.

## Original Calculation: Therapeutic vs. Augmentative Market

**Augmentation market (current BCI focus):**
- Paralysis/ALS/locked-in: primary candidates
- ~5.4M Americans with paralysis (Christopher & Dana Reeve Foundation)
- But severe paralysis (quadriplegia/complete) who would be BCI candidates: ~300,000-500,000
- At ~$50K-100K/device → $15-50B total addressable market

**Therapeutic market (CorTec's breakthrough):**
- Chronic stroke survivors with motor impairment: ~3.9M in US alone
- New strokes adding ~400,000 chronic cases/year
- Epilepsy (drug-resistant): ~1M Americans
- Treatment-resistant depression: ~2.8M Americans
- Total addressable: 7-8M patients in US alone
- At ~$50K-100K/device → $350-800B total addressable market
- **The therapy market is 10-16× larger than the augmentation market**

This is the calculation nobody has run: by demonstrating that a single BCI can do BOTH, CorTec isn't competing with Neuralink for the same 500K patients. It's opening a market 10× larger.

## Strongest Counterargument
CorTec has exactly 3 patients. Neuralink has 21. The "Pong in 2 hours" demo, while remarkable, is N=1 for the BCI functionality — the trial was designed for stroke rehab, not computer control. The BCI capability was an opportunistic demonstration, not a pre-registered primary endpoint. And CorTec is pre-revenue, backed by German VC. The gap between "one patient played Pong" and "millions of stroke survivors get bidirectional BCIs" is a valley that has swallowed countless medical device companies. The electrode count likely means lower signal resolution than Neuralink's 1,024 penetrating electrodes — surface recordings are inherently noisier.

## Limitations
- Exact electrode count for AirRay® not publicly disclosed
- Signal quality comparison between surface vs. penetrating electrodes not available from CorTec's data
- Cost per device not disclosed (early clinical stage)
- Long-term therapeutic efficacy for stroke rehab not yet published (trial ongoing)
- Market size calculations use midpoint estimates for device cost ($50K-100K range), which is speculative at this stage
- Only first participant demonstrated BCI capability — the two subsequent patients may or may not have attempted BCI sessions
