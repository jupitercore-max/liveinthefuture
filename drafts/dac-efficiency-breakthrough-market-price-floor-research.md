## Core Thesis
Sustaera achieved 90%+ energy efficiency in direct air capture, making it 3-4x more efficient than thermal incumbents (Climeworks, Carbon Engineering). At 3-5x lower capital costs, this puts DAC on a credible path to sub-$100/ton — the threshold where carbon removal becomes cheaper than carbon market compliance costs. We built the first cost-curve-vs-market-price crossover timeline.

## Original Analysis

### Calculation 1: DAC Cost Learning Curve vs Carbon Market Prices
Plot historical DAC costs:
- 2011 (APS estimate): $600/ton
- 2018 (Carbon Engineering): $94-232/ton (theoretical)
- 2024 (Climeworks Mammoth, actual): ~$600-1,000/ton
- 2026 (Sustaera projected): sub-$200/ton (based on 3x cheaper claim vs $600)
- Target: sub-$100/ton

Cross-reference with carbon market prices:
- EU ETS: ~€65-70/ton
- California: ~$35-40/ton  
- Voluntary market (high-quality): $50-200/ton
- Frontier buyers (Microsoft, Stripe): paying $200-600/ton currently for permanence

**Key insight**: At sub-$100/ton, DAC becomes cheaper than EU ETS compliance for the first time — meaning companies could buy DAC credits instead of paying the carbon tax. That's the inflection point.

### Calculation 2: Scale Gap
- Global CO2 emissions: ~37 Gt/year
- All existing DAC capacity worldwide: <50,000 tons/year
- To remove just 1% of emissions (370 Mt): need 10,000x current capacity
- At $100/ton and 370 Mt: $37 billion/year
- Compare to: global fossil fuel subsidies (~$7 trillion/year, IMF)
- DAC at scale = 0.5% of what we spend subsidizing fossil fuels

### Calculation 3: Efficiency-to-Cost Translation
- Thermal DAC (40% efficiency): requires ~2,000 kWh per ton CO2
- Sustaera (90%+ efficiency): requires ~900 kWh per ton CO2
- At $0.03/kWh (solar in best locations): energy cost alone = $27/ton vs $60/ton
- Energy is 40-60% of total DAC cost → total cost difference is dramatic

## Limitations to Acknowledge
- Sustaera's 90% efficiency is self-reported, not independently verified
- The company is pre-revenue and its cost projections have not been proven at commercial scale
- Efficiency at lab/pilot scale may not translate to commercial deployment
- "Sub-$100/ton" is a target, not an achieved cost
- Capital costs for Sustaera's system are claimed 3-5x lower but not independently benchmarked
- DAC capacity worldwide is still negligibly small — scaling from 50,000 tons to 370 million tons is a 10,000x gap that no technology has achieved in under 20 years

## Strongest Counterargument
The strongest case against DAC reaching market relevance is not the technology but the math of scale. Even at $100/ton, removing 1% of global emissions would cost $37 billion per year — roughly the entire global carbon removal market today. The learning curve that took solar from $76/watt (1977) to $0.20/watt (2024) took 47 years. DAC has been commercially attempted for less than 10 years, and the largest facility (Climeworks Mammoth) removes 36,000 tons per year — equivalent to the emissions of 7,800 Americans. The question is not whether DAC technology can reach $100/ton, but whether it can reach $100/ton AND scale to meaningful volume within the timeframe that matters for climate (before 2050). Solar had a massive tailwind: each panel generates revenue from day one. DAC facilities generate revenue only from credits — and if carbon market prices stay below DAC costs for another decade, the industry could die before it matures.
