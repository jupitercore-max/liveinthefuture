# Research: The Fed's AI Inflation Tax

## Thesis
The Federal Reserve has elevated AI infrastructure to a core inflation driver — the same tier as tariffs and the Middle East war. Original calculation: the AI buildout imposes a three-channel cost of $280–$650/year on the average American household through electricity, chipflation, and interest rates.

## Original Calculation: The Three-Channel AI Inflation Tax

### Channel 1: Electricity
- Average monthly bill: $163 (national average, June 2026)
- ICF projects 15–40% residential rate increase by 2030
- Data centers/AI responsible for majority of near-term demand growth
- Grid operators attribute ~60% of new load growth to data centers
- AI share of rate increase: 60% × 15–40% = 9–24%
- On $163/month: $14.67–$39.12/month
- **Annual: $176–$469/year**

### Channel 2: Chipflation (Consumer Electronics)
- Apple raised prices 17–29% (LITF's own article, July 9)
- DRAM spot up 40% in one quarter
- Average US household electronics spending: ~$1,000–1,200/year
- Weighted average price increase: ~5–8% (not all electronics are memory-intensive)
- **Annual: $55–$96/year** → round to $60–$100

### Channel 3: Interest Rates
- 59% probability of 25bps rate hike (Polymarket)
- Impact on variable-rate debt:
  - Credit cards ($1.1T): +$2.75B/year
  - HELOCs ($391B): +$978M/year  
  - Adjustable mortgages (~$1.3T): +$3.25B/year
  - Total: ~$7B/year → ~$54/household
- Plus new mortgage/auto origination impact: ~$10-15/household
- **Annual: $40–$70/year per household** (if Fed hikes)

### Total
| Channel | Low | High |
|---------|-----|------|
| Electricity | $176 | $469 |
| Electronics | $60 | $100 |
| Interest rates | $40 | $70 |
| **Total** | **$276** | **$639** |
| **Central estimate** | **~$460** |

Nationally: ~$460 × 130M households = **~$60 billion/year**

Context: Big Tech is spending $168B/quarter ($672B annualized) on AI infrastructure. American households are effectively subsidizing ~9% of the AI buildout through higher prices.

## Historical Precedent: Railroad Build-Out
- 1860s-1870s: massive railroad construction ($64M in tax incentives + 100M acres)
- By 1870s: overbuilding, Panic of 1873 (65-month contraction, longest in US history)
- Productivity gains didn't fully materialize until ~1900 (30 years after peak build-out)
- "The track stayed in the ground... all that infrastructure, built with European money, now wiped out, was available at fire-sale prices" (Fabricated Knowledge)
- Parallel: AI infrastructure is physical, capital-intensive, and front-loaded — just like railroads

## Strongest Counterargument
- AI productivity gains may arrive faster than railroads (software scales faster than track)
- Warsh created productivity task force — takes the deflationary case seriously
- If productivity gains materialize by 2028-2029, the inflationary phase is short
- BUT: "this effect would likely take time to materialize" (FOMC minutes)
- Warsh himself "has lately acknowledged the timing is uncertain" (Reuters)
