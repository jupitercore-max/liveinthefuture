# Research: Seven Robotaxi Companies, Seven Architecture Bets, One 2027 Deadline

## News Hook (June 16-18, 2026)
- **Mobileye** (June 16): Announced own robotaxi fleet — 100 vehicles in unnamed US city in 2027, scaling to 17,000 in 5 years. First time ADAS supplier has launched competing service. Combines Mobileye Drive + Moovit platform. Won't manufacture vehicles — partners with external platforms. $7.4B market cap (MBLY).
- **Uber/Nuro/Lucid** (June 17-18): Named Houston as second robotaxi market, mid-2027 launch. SF Bay Area launching later this year. 100 test vehicles across CA/TX, 24/7. Uber secured 50K sq ft Houston depot. Minimum 35,000 vehicles globally across dozens of markets. Lucid Gravity SUV + Nuro Driver L4 platform.
- **Aurora** (reported same week): Went back to safety driver behind wheel after starting driver-out commercial operations in April. PACCAR (OEM partner) forced reinstatement. Questions about partnership strength.

## The Seven Competing Architectures

### 1. Waymo (Alphabet) — Full Vertical Integration
- Fleet: ~3,000 vehicles across 10+ US cities
- Rides: 10M+ paid rides, aiming for 1M rides/week by end 2026
- Vehicle: Ojai (custom, built with Zeekr/Geely), Jaguar I-PACE
- Valuation: $126B (raised $16B in latest round)
- Coverage: 1,400+ sq miles, 50M+ population coverage
- Architecture: Own sensors, own software, own fleet ops, own customer app
- Expanding to 20+ cities

### 2. Tesla — Manufacturing-First
- Vehicle: Cybercab (purpose-built, no steering wheel/pedals)
- Software: FSD (vision-only, no lidar)
- Austin launch underway
- Advantage: Lowest-cost vehicle manufacturing, billions of FSD miles trained
- Architecture: Vision-only (cameras), end-to-end neural net
- Also has owner-operated "Tesla Network" model

### 3. Mobileye (Intel) — Supplier-Turned-Operator
- Vehicle: Partner-sourced (external platforms + fleet managers)
- Software: Mobileye Drive (camera + lidar + radar)
- App: Moovit (existing transit app with global user base)
- Fleet target: 100 vehicles (2027) → 17,000 (2032)
- Market cap: ~$7.4B; parent Intel: INTC
- Key tension: Now competing with own customers (VW MOIA uses Mobileye Drive)
- CEO Amnon Shashua: "Operating our own service allows us to accelerate adoption"

### 4. Uber/Nuro/Lucid — Horizontal Consortium
- Vehicle: Lucid Gravity SUV + future Midsize (Lucid manufactures)
- Software: Nuro Driver (L4 universal autonomy platform, lidar+camera+radar)
- Platform: Uber network (fleet ops, rider experience, depot management)
- Fleet target: 35,000+ vehicles globally
- Markets: SF Bay Area (2026), Houston (mid-2027), dozens more planned
- Architecture: Each company handles its specialty — unbundled model

### 5. Zoox (Amazon) — Purpose-Built Bidirectional
- Vehicle: Custom bidirectional pod, no front/back
- Software: Own stack (camera + lidar + radar)
- Still in testing phase
- Amazon backing provides deep capital
- Target: ride-hailing service, not personal vehicles

### 6. Aurora — Trucking-First (Stumbling)
- Focus: Autonomous trucking (Class 8)
- Partner: PACCAR
- Was first to go driver-out commercial (April 2026)
- PACCAR forced safety driver reinstatement
- Also exploring passenger vehicles
- Stock under pressure from execution questions

### 7. May Mobility / Smaller Players
- May Mobility: shuttle/transit focus, Toyota backing
- Various Chinese players (BYD, Pony.ai, WeRide)
- Cruise: restructured after GM pulled out, now exploring partnerships

## Novel Contribution: The Capital Math

### Combined Fleet Commitments
- Waymo: ~3,000 current + expansion to 20+ cities = est. 10,000-15,000 by 2028
- Mobileye: 17,000 by 2032
- Uber/Nuro/Lucid: 35,000 globally
- Tesla: Unknown but Terafab suggests massive scale
- Zoox: Unknown but Amazon capital implies thousands
- **Conservative total: 75,000-100,000+ committed vehicles by early 2030s**

### US Rideshare Fleet Size
- Uber + Lyft combined: ~1.4M active drivers in US
- Total US taxi/for-hire fleet: ~200,000-300,000 vehicles
- Robotaxis replacing ~10-15% of current rideshare would require ~100,000-200,000 vehicles

### Per-City Infrastructure Cost
- Waymo reportedly spends ~$500M-$1B per new city launch (mapping, depot, ops center, regulatory)
- At 20+ target cities × 7 entrants = potential $70B-$140B in combined infrastructure investment
- But most cities can't support 7 competing robotaxi services

### The Economics Gap
- Waymo: estimated $6-8 per mile all-in (vs Uber ~$2-3 per mile)
- No robotaxi operator has publicly demonstrated profitable per-ride economics
- Infrastructure costs (mapping, depots, teleops, maintenance) create massive fixed cost base
- Scaling to profitability requires density that fragmentation prevents

## Strongest Counterargument
The market may be larger than anyone thinks. If robotaxis are cheaper and more accessible than human-driven rideshare, total addressable market could expand 5-10x (people who currently drive themselves). McKinsey estimates robo-mobility could be a $1.5T market by 2030. In that scenario, multiple competitors can thrive.

## Limitations
- Per-mile cost estimates rely on analyst projections, not disclosed financials
- Tesla and Zoox fleet targets are speculative
- Chinese robotaxi market (larger in absolute terms) not deeply analyzed
- Infrastructure cost per city varies enormously by geography and regulation
- Timeline slippage is endemic in AV industry — 2027 targets may shift
