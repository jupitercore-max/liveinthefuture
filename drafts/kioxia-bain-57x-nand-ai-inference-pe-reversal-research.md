# Research Notes: Kioxia — From PE Write-Off to $250B+ / 57× in 18 Months

## Article #534 | Journalist: Marcus Chen (Hardware & Industry Economics)
## Category: 💻 Semiconductors / Tech Economics

---

## Core Thesis
Kioxia's stock has risen 57× from its IPO price in 18 months — the fastest large-cap wealth creation in Japanese market history. The Bain Capital consortium that bought Toshiba's memory unit for ¥2T ($18B) in 2018 nearly wrote the deal off (IPO valued the company at ¥750B, half the acquisition price). Then AI inference — not training — flipped the NAND market. The deal that looked like PE's biggest Japan mistake became the greatest carve-out return in PE history.

## Original Calculation (Kill Test)
1. **Bain's PE reversal math:**
   - 2018: Consortium pays ¥2T ($18B) for Toshiba Memory
   - Oct 2024: Bain tries ¥1.5T IPO. Investors say ¥800B. Bain scraps it.
   - Dec 18, 2024: IPO at ¥1,455/share, market cap ¥750B (~$4.9B). The entire company is worth 37.5% of what the consortium paid. On paper: 62.5% value destruction.
   - Jul 3, 2026: Stock at ¥83,300. Market cap ¥41.64T (~$270B). That's 20.8× the original ¥2T acquisition price.
   - Bain's remaining 18.16% stake alone = ¥7.56T (~$49B) — 3.78× the entire original acquisition price, from just 18% of the company.

2. **NAND supply squeeze math:**
   - Samsung + SK hynix shifted wafer capacity to HBM for AI training → NAND supply contracted
   - TrendForce: NAND prices surged 85-90% QoQ in Q1 2026
   - Kioxia is the ONLY company shipping next-gen (332-layer) NAND in volume for FY2026
   - Samsung V10 (430-layer) delayed from 2025 target, mass production no earlier than H1 2026 (likely later)
   - SK hynix targeting 10th-gen NAND in early 2027
   - Kioxia has a 12-18 month monopoly window on next-gen NAND

3. **The AI inference flip:**
   - AI training = DRAM/HBM-intensive (SK Hynix was the early winner)
   - AI inference = NAND/SSD-intensive (Kioxia is the late winner)
   - As AI shifts from training to inference (which happens at scale), NAND demand explodes
   - "They prioritised DRAM so much that they put NAND investment and development on the back burner. They haven't been able to respond to the current NAND boom at all. That is why demand is now concentrated on Kioxia alone." — Satoru Oyama, consultant, ex-Tokyo Electron
