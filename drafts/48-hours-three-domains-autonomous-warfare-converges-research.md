# Research: 48 Hours, Three Domains — Autonomous Warfare Converges

## Topic
Between July 13 and July 15, 2026, autonomous weapons systems achieved combat or test "firsts" in all three domains of conventional warfare — air, sea, and land — within the same 48-hour window. This article examines the convergence, prices out the combined multi-domain unmanned force package against its crewed equivalents, and asks what it means when machines can project force across every surface simultaneously for a fraction of the cost.

## Primary Sources

### 1. AIR — Anduril Fury CCA Fires AIM-120 AMRAAM (July 15, 2026)
- **Source:** Wall Street Journal, "Air Force Test Fires Long-Range Missile From Combat Jet Drone for the First Time," July 15, 2026
- **Source:** U.S. Air Force official statement via Gen. Kenneth Wilsbach, Chief of Staff
- **Source:** Anduril statement from Mark Shushnar, VP Autonomous Air Power
- **Key facts:**
  - YFQ-44A Fury fired AIM-120 AMRAAM at Edwards Air Force Base, California
  - "Beyond-line-of-sight strike against a simulated target" (not a live aircraft)
  - First air-to-air missile ever fired from a CCA-class autonomous combat drone
  - Anduril Fury: ~4,000 lbf thrust (Williams FJ44-4M), Mach 0.95, 50,000 ft ceiling, 9g
  - Human retains weapons release authority
  - Air Force awarded production contracts to Anduril and General Atomics in June 2026
  - Fleet target: ~1,000 CCAs by end of decade; ~100 in first increment
  - CCA program: FY2027 budget $996.5M procurement + $1.431B development
  - Quote: "Usually we shoot the missiles at the drones. The fact you have a drone shooting a missile—that's a whole new ballgame." — Houston Cantwell, retired USAF brigadier general
- **Prior milestone:** Inert captive carry tests began Feb 2026
- **Timeline:** First flight Oct 31, 2025 → Inert carry Feb 2026 → Live fire Jul 2026 = 8.5 months maiden flight to weapon employment

### 2. SEA — Saronic Corsair USVs Strike Bandar Abbas (July 13, 2026)
- **Source:** U.S. Central Command statement
- **Source:** Wall Street Journal, "America's Latest Weapon Against Iran: Sea Drones," July 13, 2026
- **Source:** Washington Examiner, "US uses one-way sea drones for first time in combat," July 13, 2026
- **Key facts:**
  - 3 Corsair unmanned surface vessels hit Bandar Abbas Naval Base (Iran's navy headquarters)
  - Struck submarine and ship maintenance facility
  - "First time American forces have employed sea drones in combat operations" — CENTCOM
  - Saronic Corsair specs: 24 ft, >35 knots, 1,000 NM range, 1,000 lb payload
  - Cost: <$1 million per unit (WSJ, Saronic spokesperson)
  - Arrived in Middle East late March 2026
  - $392M Navy production contract awarded Dec 2025
  - Made in Texas by Saronic (co-founded by former Navy SEAL Dino Mavrookas)
  - Prior milestone: First combat rescue of aircrew by USV, June 8, 2026 (Strait of Hormuz)
  - Navy inventory went from 4 USVs to 500+ in ~2 years
  - Navy goal: half the surface fleet unmanned
  - Quote: "Those days of using high value defenses to shoot down cheap targets are behind us." — Adm. Brad Cooper, CENTCOM Commander
- **Context:** Used as one-way attack (kamikaze) — all 3 expended = $3M total

## Strongest Counterargument (must address at full strength)
These were asymmetric strikes against targets that couldn't effectively shoot back. The Corsairs hit a port, not an adversary's fleet at sea. The Fury fired at a digital target, not through integrated air defenses. The UGV rolled onto a spit held by a demoralized garrison, not through a contested beachhead. Against a peer adversary — one with advanced electronic warfare, networked air defense, and cyber capabilities — these systems face entirely different survival calculus. Autonomous weapons that lose communications, get spoofed, or face directed energy may be more expensive paperweights than force multipliers. The Pentagon's own wargames show that in a Taiwan scenario, even crewed aircraft face 20-30% attrition in the first three weeks; autonomous systems with simpler avionics and less hardened electronics could fare worse. The 48-hour convergence proves the technology works in permissive environments. Whether it works when the enemy fights back at scale is the question these tests cannot yet answer.

## Limitations
- Fury test was against a simulated (digital) target, not in combat; results are test environment only
- Corsair combat use was against Iran, not a peer adversary with advanced naval defenses
- Ukraine's UGV operation was against a relatively static defensive position
- Exact Ukrainian UGV/USV costs are not publicly disclosed; estimates used
- CCA unit cost is budgetary target, not confirmed production price
- None of these systems have been tested in heavily contested electronic warfare environments
- Human was still in weapons release loop for all three
