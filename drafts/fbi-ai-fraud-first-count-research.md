## Novel Analysis: The Undercount Multiplier

The FBI's $893M in AI-attributed losses is almost certainly a massive undercount for several compounding reasons:

1. **Reporting gap**: FBI relies on voluntary complaints to IC3. Most fraud victims never report. FTC estimates only ~5% of fraud victims file reports.
2. **Attribution gap**: Victims may not know AI was used. If you fall for a phishing email written by AI, you report "phishing" not "AI fraud." The $3B in BEC losses likely has significant AI involvement that isn't captured in the AI section.
3. **Geographic gap**: FBI counts US reports only. UNODC says US losses to Southeast Asian operations alone were $10B in 2024 — but most of that doesn't flow through IC3.
4. **Business gap**: Companies rarely report AI fraud publicly due to liability/reputation concerns.
5. **Indirect costs**: The 500 unwanted calls/year that every American receives represent a massive opportunity cost — time, trust erosion, reduced phone utility — that no dollar figure captures.

**Conservative estimate**: If only 5-10% of AI fraud is reported AND correctly attributed to AI, the real figure is $9-18 billion. That would make AI fraud responsible for a significant portion of the $21B total, not the ~4% that the FBI's dedicated section implies.
