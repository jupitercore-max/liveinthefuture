## Kill Test
Would people notice if this wasn't published? YES — this represents a paradigm shift in how materials science is conducted. The autonomous robot scientist is no longer a prototype; it's producing world-class results at superhuman speed across multiple fields simultaneously.

## 10-Star Test
What would make this a 10-star article? Showing that the traditional lab bench — fundamentally unchanged since the 1800s — is being automated out of existence, and quantifying exactly how much faster/cheaper robot scientists are.

## Novel Contribution
1. **Speed calculation**: 50,764 experiments ÷ ~8/day (human pace for complex perovskite fabrication involving spin-coating, annealing, electrode deposition, characterization) = 6,346 person-days = 24.4 person-years at 260 working days/year
2. **Cost comparison**: At ~$70K/year loaded postdoc cost, 24.4 person-years = $1.7M in labor alone. Ginkgo data shows traditional labs spend 95% on overhead vs 5% reagents; autonomous labs flip to ~10% overhead → 10x more data per dollar
3. **Efficiency parity**: Robot achieved 27.0% PCE (26.5% certified) — competitive with best human-fabricated perovskite cells (world record 27.17% just set by Chinese researchers, May 2026)
4. **Combined experiment count**: Hong Kong PolyU (50,764) + Ginkgo/OpenAI (36,000) = 86,764+ autonomous experiments in early 2026 across completely different fields (energy + biology)

## Strongest Counterargument
The strongest case against autonomous labs: they're expensive to build (Hong Kong system has 101 modules, 1,500+ components), they can only optimize within known parameter spaces (they don't make conceptual leaps like new cell architectures), and the best human-fabricated cells still hold the overall records (27.17% for single-junction vs the robot's 27.0%). Robots are superb optimizers within a defined search space, but they haven't yet invented a new category of solar cell. The paradigm shift is in execution speed, not in scientific creativity.

## Limitations
- We don't know the exact time the Hong Kong system took to run 50,764 experiments (weeks? months?)
- The cost of building/operating the robotic system itself isn't publicly disclosed
- The 24-year calculation assumes ~8 devices/day which is an estimate for skilled postdoc; some labs might do more with batch processing
- Ginkgo is financially struggling ($19M revenue, pivoting strategy) — autonomous labs aren't yet a proven business
