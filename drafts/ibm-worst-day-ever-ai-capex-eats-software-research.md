# Research: IBM's Worst Day Ever — AI Infrastructure Spending Cannibalizes Software

## Story Thesis
IBM just suffered its worst single-day stock decline in its 115-year history — down 25%, erasing $66.5 billion in market value — not because of a market crash, but because enterprise clients are redirecting their software budgets to buy AI infrastructure hardware. This is the first concrete, earnings-level proof that AI capex isn't additive — it's cannibalistic. The money flowing into servers, memory, and GPUs is being ripped directly from software and services budgets.

## Limitations
- IBM's miss may be partially idiosyncratic (mainframe cycle, z17 launch anniversary)
- The "capex reprioritization" could be a one-quarter inventory buildup, not a permanent shift
- We're extrapolating from one company's miss to a sector-wide thesis
- We don't have granular data on which clients shifted budgets or by how much
- Supply constraint concerns may ease if chip production catches up

## Strongest Counterargument
IBM's mainframe business was already in a cyclical downturn (z17 anniversary comparisons). The company was warning of infrastructure revenue declines before the AI capex narrative. The real story might be simpler: IBM missed because its mainframe refresh cycle peaked, and the "capex reprioritization" framing is CEO spin to make a cyclical miss sound like an industry trend. The 7% infrastructure decline vs. expected 2.7% could be mostly z17 fatigue, not AI cannibalizing software. If so, the 95:1 market reaction is an overreaction to a narrative, not a signal.
