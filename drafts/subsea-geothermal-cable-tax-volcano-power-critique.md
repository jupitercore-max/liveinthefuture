## Critic 1: 🔍 General Editor

**Score: 9.0 / 10**

**Strengths:**
- The lede ("Three hundred and eighty-six degrees Celsius.") is superb — concrete, surprising, immediately grounding.
- The "cable tax" framing is a genuinely original editorial concept that gives the reader a single variable to watch. Excellent structural choice.
- Three-scenario breakdown (Pacific Island / US Coast / Remote Mid-Ocean) is perfectly calibrated — each scenario answers a different reader question, and none feels redundant.
- The "From 100 Kilowatts to a Gigawatt" section is appropriately skeptical without being dismissive. The SpaceX scale-up comparison (10,000× vs. Falcon 1-to-9's scale-up over 7 years) is sharp.
- Closing line ("SpaceX taught its alumni to think in terawatts. The ocean will teach them to think in dollars per kilometer.") is strong — memorable and earned by the preceding analysis.

**Issues:**
- **Word count at ~1,882 vs. 800–1,200 guideline.** The data density justifies the length, but it could tighten by ~200 words. The second paragraph (investor list + team background) is the weakest candidate for trimming — standard funding-announcement boilerplate.
- **Missing "Related articles" section.** The STORY_GUIDE requires 2–3 links to other stories at the end. Two internal links are already in the body (Fervo bankability, Fervo IPO), but there is no dedicated Related section.
- **No comparison table.** The three cable tax scenarios beg for a summary table: Distance | Depth | Cable Cost | Cable Tax/MWh | Total LCOE | Competitor Price | Verdict. The guide says "Include comparison tables where relevant" — this is clearly relevant.

## Critic 6: 🔬 Research Rigor

**Score: 9.0 / 10**

**Original Contribution:**
✅ **PASSES the kill test.** The cable tax LCOE penalty analysis across three distance/depth scenarios is a genuine original calculation. Nobody in public reporting has run this math. The Carnot efficiency comparison (58.3% vs 37%) between subsea and land geothermal, while straightforward thermodynamics, has not been applied to this specific company's claims. The market segmentation conclusion (islands yes, grid no, data centers maybe) is a novel analytical finding.

**Limitations Section:**
✅ **Strong.** Names five specific uncertainties: (1) no discounting, (2) optimistic generation cost assumption, (3) unknown subsea operational costs, (4) straight-line cable assumption with 15–30% real routing penalty, (5) sensitivity analysis ("if generation costs reach only $100/MWh, the technology works in Pacific islands but fails everywhere else").

**Strongest Counterargument:**
✅ **Well-constructed.** Names Fervo specifically with bankable data (9-bank project finance, 400 MW Cape Station, 3 GW Google PPA), gives the experience curve cost trajectory ($100–120/MWh → $65–75/MWh), and delivers the devastating final line without immediate rebuttal: "The seafloor might be thermodynamically ideal and economically redundant for every customer except remote island nations."

**Methodology Transparency:**
✅ **Good.** Shows inputs for all three scenarios: distance, depth, cable cost per km, plant capacity, capacity factor, plant life, total generation, and resulting cable tax per MWh. Reader can reproduce every number.

**Issues:**
- The 90% capacity factor assumption is stated but not sourced. Conventional geothermal runs 85–95% — 90% is reasonable but should cite the DOE or NREL ATB data for conventional geothermal capacity factors.
- The "practical turbine efficiency ~40%" and "~25%" claims for subsea and conventional geothermal respectively are stated without citation. These are reasonable engineering estimates but should either cite a source or explicitly note they are author estimates.
- The 386°C measurement was at the seafloor, but it's not clear this is the temperature at the wellhead of a drilled system — natural vent temperatures can differ from what a drilled well produces. A sentence noting this distinction would strengthen rigor.

## Critic 7: 📊 Data Presentation

**Score: 8.0 / 10**

**Strengths:**
- Cable tax per MWh is bold-formatted for visual hierarchy — the most important number in each scenario pops.
- Each scenario follows a consistent format: distance → depth → cable cost → total generation → cable tax. Reader can compare mentally.
- The "so what?" test is passed at every data point: the $12.70/MWh cable tax is immediately contextualized against $350/MWh diesel; the $25.40 against $50/MWh wholesale.
- Carnot efficiency numbers are presented with enough input values (temperature in °C) that a reader with a calculator can verify them.

**Issues:**
- **Missing summary table.** This is the biggest data presentation gap. The three scenarios cry out for a single comparison table:

  | Scenario | Distance | Cable Cost | Cable Tax/MWh | Total LCOE | Competitor | Verdict |
  |----------|----------|------------|---------------|------------|------------|---------|
  | Tonga | 50 km | $125M | $12.70 | $73–93 | $350 diesel | ✅ 74–79% savings |
  | US Coast | 200 km | $1B | $25.40 | $85–105 | $50 grid | ❌ 70–110% premium |
  | Mid-Ocean | 500 km | $3B | $30.40 | $90–110 | $50 grid | ❌ Uncompetitive |

  Without this table, readers must reconstruct the comparison from prose across three separate sections. A table after Scenario 3 summarizing all three would dramatically improve scanability.
- The Carnot comparison (58.3% vs 37%) would benefit from a simple two-row table showing hot source temp, cold sink temp, Carnot %, and practical efficiency side by side.
- The 100 kW → 1 GW scaling gap is described as "10,000×" — this is powerful but the human-scale anchor could be stronger. Consider: "If Endurance's demo were a single light bulb, their commercial target would power a city of 700,000 homes."

**This is the only critic below 8.5. The missing summary table is the primary issue.**
