# Research: West Siberia Retrogressive Thaw Slumps — 23-Fold Increase, Winter Warming, Infrastructure Risk

## Source Paper
- **Authors:** Nesterova et al.
- **Title:** "Rapid increase in West Siberia's retrogressive thaw slumps since 1964 associated with Arctic winter warming"
- **Journal:** *Scientific Reports* (2026)
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41598-026-56146-9
- **Published:** June 11, 2026

## Core Findings

### RTS Inventory
- 6,168 retrogressive thaw slumps (RTSs) mapped across Yamal and Gydan peninsulas
- **23-fold increase** in RTS numbers from 1964 to 2024
- Initiation rates rose **26-fold** over the same period
- Headwall retreat rates relatively stable: median 1.3 → 1.6 m/yr

### Drivers
- **Summer max precipitation** is strongest SHORT-TERM trigger: 29.76 percentage-point increase in initiation probability per 1 standard deviation
- **Winter warming is dominant LONG-TERM driver:** +4.82K → 29 pp increase in annual initiation probability
- This is counterintuitive — most prior work focused on summer temps
- Winter air temps warmed ~0.674°C/decade vs summer ~0.285°C/decade (winter warming 2.4x faster)

### Projections
- Winter warming of 2.48–11.67°C by 2100
- Corresponding 15–72 pp increase in RTS initiation probability
- Range depends on emissions scenario (SSP1-2.6 to SSP5-8.5)

## Infrastructure at Risk

### Yamal Peninsula Linear Infrastructure (~1,056 km total)
- 718.7 km of Bovanenkovo-Ukhta pipeline
- 73.5 km of Obskaya-Bovanenkovo-Karskaya railway
- 211 km local roads/bridges in Bovanenkovo settlement
- 51.8 km electrical lines

### Bovanenkovo Gas Field Context
- Largest gas field on Yamal Peninsula
- **4.9 tcm reserves** (trillion cubic meters)
- Production capacity: **115 bcm/year** (99 bcm produced in 2020)
- Operated by Gazprom, production started 2012
- Bovanenkovo-Ukhta pipeline: ~1,200 km each line, two parallel lines, 120 atm working pressure

## Original Contribution (LITF Value-Add)

### Economic Exposure Calculation (nobody has done this)
- Russia's wholesale gas price has varied widely; export price even more
- At European gas prices (~$300/1000 m³ average in recent years), 99 bcm = ~$29.7B/year
- At long-term contract prices (~$250/1000 m³), 99 bcm = ~$24.75B/year
- The 718.7 km pipeline segment crosses permafrost terrain with accelerating RTS activity
- A single pipeline rupture at 120 atm working pressure in permafrost terrain = months of repair in Arctic conditions
- Even a 30-day outage at 99 bcm/yr = ~$8.1B lost throughput at $300/1000 m³

### Broader Context
- Yamal contributes ~12% of Russia's total gas production
- Russia's federal budget depends heavily on gas revenue (estimated 15-20% of total budget)
- Sanctions have redirected exports toward Asia but Bovanenkovo-Ukhta feeds European-facing infrastructure
- Insurance implications: permafrost infrastructure is increasingly uninsurable

## Study Methods
- Multi-temporal satellite imagery (Landsat, Sentinel-2, Corona KH-4B declassified spy photos)
- 60-year time series (1964-2024)
- Machine learning classification + manual verification
- Generalized additive models (GAMs) for climate driver attribution
- ERA5 reanalysis for climate variables

## Why This Matters Beyond Russia
- 23% of Northern Hemisphere land surface is underlain by permafrost
- Permafrost contains ~1,500 Gt of organic carbon (roughly 2x atmospheric CO₂)
- RTS are among the most dramatic forms of permafrost degradation
- Winter warming trend is circumpolar, not limited to Yamal
- Infrastructure risk applies to Alaska (Trans-Alaska Pipeline), Canada (highways, mining), Nordic countries
