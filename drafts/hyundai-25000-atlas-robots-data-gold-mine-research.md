## Original Calculations

### A. Total Deployment Investment
25,000 robots × $130,000 = **$3.25 billion** upfront hardware investment

### B. Per-Robot ROI (US Georgia plant)
- US worker fully loaded cost: ~$58,105/year (MotorTrend)
- Benefits + overhead typically add 30-40%: ~$75,500/year fully loaded
- Atlas operates ~20 hrs/day (3 shifts × ~6.7 hrs accounting for battery swap + maintenance)
- At 3× efficiency claim: 1 Atlas replaces ~2.5 human shifts of output
- Annual labor savings per robot: 2.5 × $75,500 = **~$188,750/year**
- Payback: $130,000 / $188,750 = **~8.3 months** (not 2 years)
- Note: 2-year payback figure may account for maintenance, downtime, integration costs

### C. Data Value Per Robot
- $2.7B annual data profit / 25,000 robots = **$108,000 per robot per year**
- This nearly equals the robot's purchase price annually
- Total per-robot annual value: $188,750 (labor) + $108,000 (data) = **$296,750/year**
- Including data: payback drops to **~5.3 months**

### D. Korean Worker Displacement
- Korean avg compensation: 92M won ($78,000/year)
- 25,000 robots at 3× efficiency = ~75,000 worker-equivalents displaced
- Hyundai Motor + Kia have ~120,000 production workers
- Robot equivalents = **62.5% of current production workforce**

### E. Boston Dynamics Revenue Check
- 25,000 robots × $130K = $3.25B hardware revenue
- But Hyundai Mobis captures 60% ($78K/robot) = $1.95B to Mobis
- BD hardware margin: ~$52K/robot × 25K = **$1.3B** to BD
- Cumulative BD losses: ~$1.4B → hardware alone barely covers the hole
- The data business ($2.7B/year) is where profitability actually lives
