# Research: Epicrispr EPI-321 — First Epigenetic Editing Efficacy Data in FSHD

## Primary Sources

### Epicrispr 6-Month Data (Jun 26, 2026)
- Source: BusinessWire press release
- URL: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260626528684/en/Epicrispr-Reports-First-Clinical-Evidence-of-Increased-Lean-Muscle-Volume-in-Patients-with-FSHD-Following-Treatment-with-EPI-321
- Data cutoff: May 12, 2026
- 9 patients treated across 2 dose cohorts
  - Cohort 1: 6 patients at 2×10^13 vg/kg (target dose)
  - Cohort 2: 3 patients at 4×10^13 vg/kg
- First 3 evaluable patients (Cohort 1) at 6 months:
  - ALL demonstrated gains in lean muscle volume vs baseline
  - Average: ~370 mL (~0.8 pounds)
  - Individual range: 0.5 to 1.3 pounds
  - Some muscles gained 15% lean volume
- No serious adverse events
- cfDNA biomarker reductions consistent with DUX4 pathway suppression
- MRI by Springbok Analytics: AI-powered, up to 140 individual muscles
- NCT06907875
- Next data: World Muscle Society Congress, September 2026
- Primary study completion: mid-2027
- CEO: Amber Salzman, Ph.D.
- PI: Russell Butterfield, M.D. (University of Utah)

- J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference presentation

## Comparator: Fulcrum Losmapimod Phase 3 REACH Trial (Failed Sept 12, 2024)
- Source: Reuters, FSHD Society, BioSpace
- 260 patients, randomized, placebo-controlled, 48 weeks
- Primary endpoint (RWS): 0.013 vs 0.010 improvement (p=0.75) — NOT significant
- MFI: 0.42% vs 0.576% increase (p=0.16) — NOT significant
- All secondary endpoints failed
- Stock dropped ~70%
- Program suspended
- Data transferred to FSHD Society (Feb 2025)
- Small molecule (oral) targeting DUX4 expression reduction
- Phase 2 had shown some signal

## Epigenetic Editing Landscape (3 companies in human trials)

### 1. Epicrispr — EPI-321 for FSHD
- Phase 1/2, AAV delivery, dead Cas12F
- First efficacy data reported June 2026

### 2. nChroma Bio — CRMA-1001 for chronic Hepatitis B
- Co-founded by Jonathan Weissman (MIT)
- CTA approved Hong Kong Dec 2025, NZ + UK additional approvals
- First patient dosed Jan 2026
- Phase 1/2
- LNP delivery, proprietary methyltransferase

- Co-founded by Fyodor Urnov (UC Berkeley)

## FSHD Disease Background
- ~870,000 patients worldwide (1 in 8,000)
- Progressive genetic neuromuscular disease
- Caused by aberrant expression of DUX4 (normally silenced)
- Muscle weakness starts face/shoulders/arms, spreads
- No approved disease-modifying therapies
- 12+ active drug programs targeting DUX4 (FSHD Society)

## Key Science: Epigenetic Editing vs Gene Editing (CRISPR)
- Source: Nature article (d41586-026-01976-w)
- Gene editing (CRISPR-Cas9): cuts DNA → risk of off-target mutations, DNA rearrangement
- Epigenetic editing: modifies chemical markers on DNA → no DNA cuts, no off-target mutations
- Epigenetic modifications potentially reversible
- Dead Cas = Cas protein with cutting activity disabled, still finds target
- Different delivery: AAV (Epicrispr) vs LNP (nChroma, Tune)
- LNP = potentially redosable; AAV = single dose but durable, pre-existing immunity concerns

## Original Analysis Ideas
1. Direction-of-travel reversal: Every prior FSHD trial measured how much muscle patients LOST. Epicrispr's patients GAINED muscle. First sign reversal.
2. Platform comparison: Epigenetic vs traditional approaches — Fulcrum's small molecule failed at 260 patients, Epicrispr's epigenetic approach showing signal at 3 patients
3. Timeline math: CRISPR gene editing went from first patient (2019) to first approved therapy (2023) in ~4 years. Epigenetic editing dosed first patients in 2025-2026. If similar timeline, first approvals ~2029-2030.
4. The three companies have 3 different targets, 2 different delivery systems, and 2 different dead Cas variants. This isn't one company's bet — it's a platform validation moment.
