# Research: DAC Opportunity Cost — Nature Study Shows Renewables Beat DAC in 21 of 22 US Grid Regions

## Primary Source
Kashtan, Y., Pendleton, J., Sousa, B., Willis, M.D., Michanowicz, D.R., Shonkoff, S.B.C., & Buonocore, J.J. (2026). "Direct air capture has substantial health and climate opportunity costs." *Communications Sustainability*, vol. 1, article 67.
- URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s44458-026-00068-0
- Affiliations: PSE Healthy Energy, Boston University School of Public Health, Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health
- Published: ~May 2026

## Core Methodology
- Cost-equivalent comparison: $100M/year (2024 USD) spent on DAC vs. utility-scale wind or solar
- 22 US electrical grid regions (EIA regions)
- Timeline: 2020-2050
- 8 hypothetical grid scenarios from EIA Annual Energy Outlook (AEO)
- Tool: CoBE Projection (health + climate cost-benefit modeling)
- Compared grid-connected DAC, grid-islanded DAC, and counterfactual renewable deployment

## Four DAC Technology Scenarios
| Scenario | Energy (kWh/ton) | Cost ($/ton) | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stagnation | 5,500 | $1,000 | **NET NEGATIVE** — worsens emissions |
| Efficiency Improvement | 2,500 | $750 | Essentially zero benefit |
| Advanced Efficiency | 1,500 | $500 | Modest benefit, still less than renewables |
| Breakthrough | 800 | $100 | Only scenario DAC slightly outperforms renewables |

## Key Findings
1. Renewable energy yields greater combined climate + health benefits than DAC across nearly ALL scenarios and regions
2. Grid-connected DAC at Stagnation costs produces more GHGs and health harms than it prevents — it's worse than doing nothing
3. Health benefits are the differentiator: renewables displace fossil generation, reducing PM2.5, NOx, SO2; DAC has zero air quality benefit
4. DAC only approaches cost-effectiveness under "Breakthrough" assumptions ($100/ton, 800 kWh/ton) — no commercial system is close
5. Grid-islanded DAC (paired with dedicated renewables) avoids the grid pollution penalty but still underperforms building those renewables as grid-connected generation

## Current Commercial Reality
- Climeworks: ~$600-1,000/ton currently, Gen 3 aims for $300-500/ton
- Heirloom: limestone-based, aims for $100/ton by 2035
- 1PointFive (Occidental/Carbon Engineering): STRATOS hub in Texas, 500,000 ton/year target

## Novel Contribution: DOE Hub Opportunity Cost Calculation
Cross-referencing the $3.5B DOE investment with the study's framework:

**$3.5B on DAC (at current ~$800/ton mid-range):**
- Capacity: ~4.375M tons over plant lifetime
- On fossil-heavy grids: some of this is net-negative (grid electricity emissions)
- Grid-islanded versions perform better but require dedicated renewables (doubling infrastructure)

**$3.5B on utility-scale solar instead:**
- At ~$1.00/W installed cost (2025 US utility solar): ~3.5 GW capacity
- At 25% capacity factor: ~7.67 TWh/year
- At US grid average ~0.39 tCO2/MWh displacement: ~2.99M tons CO2/year
- Over 25-year lifespan: ~74.8M tons CO2 displaced
- Plus: avoided particulate, NOx, SO2 emissions → $100-400M/year in health benefits (EPA VSL methodology)

**Ratio: Solar displaces ~17x more CO2 per dollar over 25 years than DAC at current costs**

This calculation hasn't been published. The Nature study provides the theoretical framework; applying it to actual DOE allocations is our original contribution.

## Strongest Counterargument
DAC serves a fundamentally different purpose than renewables. Renewables prevent NEW emissions; DAC removes EXISTING atmospheric CO2. After net-zero, DAC is the only tool that can draw down legacy concentrations. The study explicitly acknowledges this: DAC's role is "atmospheric restoration" post-net-zero. Investing in DAC now builds the industrial base for that future need. You wouldn't compare R&D spending to deployment spending and declare R&D wasteful.

## Limitations
- Study compares only against wind/solar, not against hard-to-abate sector decarbonization (cement, steel, aviation)
- Uses EIA AEO grid scenarios that may not capture rapid grid decarbonization
- Health benefits valued using social cost methodology (politically contested)
- Does not model learning-by-doing cost reductions from early DAC deployment
