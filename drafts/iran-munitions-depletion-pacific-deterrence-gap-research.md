## Core Thesis

The 38-day bombing campaign against Iran (Operation Epic Fury, Feb-Apr 2026) burned through a significant fraction of the Pentagon's key munitions — the same weapons earmarked for a potential Pacific contingency with China. Current production rates can't close the gap until 2029-2031. Nobody has run the precise production-rate-to-depletion math for all seven key systems simultaneously, or calculated how long the "window of vulnerability" actually lasts.

## Original Contribution (Kill Test)

**The calculation nobody ran:** A system-by-system "deterrence recovery calendar" showing exactly when each of the Pentagon's 7 key depleted weapons systems returns to pre-war levels at current production rates, and when all 7 overlap to restore full Pacific deterrence capability. The CSIS report said "window of vulnerability" but didn't calculate the aggregate closure date. I will.

**Second original calculation:** The "Tomahawk paradox" — 850-1,000+ fired in 38 days vs. 57 budgeted for purchase in FY2026. At the decade-average procurement rate of 86/year, replacing 1,000 Tomahawks takes 11.6 years. Even at maximum surge production, CSIS estimates 2030-2031. That means the Tomahawk inventory won't return to pre-Iran-war levels until roughly the time China's military modernization timeline peaks (2027-2035 window per DoD China Military Power Report).

**Third original calculation:** The cost asymmetry that caused the depletion. Iran's Shahed-136 drones cost $10,000-$50,000 domestically; the Patriot interceptors used to shoot them down cost $4M+; the SM-6 missiles cost $4.7M. At these exchange rates, Iran can force the US to spend $100 in defense for every $1 in offense. Quantify how this inverted the budget across the campaign.

## Strongest Counterargument

The Pentagon maintains it has "sufficient munitions for any plausible Iran scenario" and military commanders publicly state readiness in the Indo-Pacific has not been degraded. Production lines are ramping — JASSM and PrSM could be restored within a year. The real-world deterrent effect depends not just on inventory counts but on demonstrated willingness to use force, which the Iran campaign abundantly showed. China's calculus may actually be MORE cautious after seeing the US willing to expend 12,000+ strikes in 38 days.

## Limitations

- Exact inventory numbers are classified; analysis relies on CSIS estimates confirmed by anonymous DoD sources
- Surge production rates are also classified; restoration timelines are estimates
- The "window of vulnerability" calculation assumes China's own munitions inventory is static (it isn't — China is also building rapidly)
- This analysis doesn't account for potential new munitions technologies (directed energy, hypersonics) that could change the Pacific calculus
- US repositioning of assets from Indo-Pacific to Gulf is confirmed but the exact force reallocation is classified

## Actionable Insights

- For defense investors: missile production companies (Raytheon/RTX, Lockheed Martin) face a multi-year production surge that's funded but bottlenecked by facilities and workforce
- For policymakers: the FY2026 Tomahawk procurement of 57 units is indefensible when 850+ were expended in one campaign
- For general readers: the "cheap drone vs. expensive missile" cost inversion isn't theoretical — it just consumed half the Pentagon's air defense reserves in 38 days
- For China watchers: the 2029-2031 window (when US stockpiles finally recover) overlaps with China's 2027-2035 military modernization window
