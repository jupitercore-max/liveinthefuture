## 5. ⚖️ Legal/Citation Accuracy — 8.6/10

**Strengths:** Seven external citations with direct links: PR Newswire (CATL press release), Electrek ×2 (Ore Energy deal, German teardown), The Times UK (Form Energy), Nasdaq (lithium market), CNEVPost (FAW Jiefang test), MDPI (specific paper URL for CALB assessment), Eureka Patsnap (specific comparison report). All claims are attributed to named sources. Financial data (lithium prices, cost figures) cite specific reporting.

**Weaknesses:** The MDPI paper (Batteries 2024, 10, 306) is about retired EV cells in second-life assessment — the article uses it for grid-storage cycle life claims, which requires extrapolation. The article bridges this gap ("typical commercial deployment falls well short of that ceiling") but could be more explicit about the EV-to-grid transfer assumption. BYD's 10,000-cycle claim is noted as unverified — good practice. Form Energy's $20/kWh figure cites "data shared with The Times" which is self-reported company data.

## 6. 🔬 Research Rigor — 8.6/10

**Strengths:** Novel contribution — the three-way cost-per-cycle comparison with current June 2026 pricing is original analysis not available elsewhere. Clear methodology (cell cost ÷ cycle life). Efficiency adjustment as a second-order analysis adds genuine depth. Limitations section addresses cell-level vs system-level costs, manufacturer data reliability, unproven scale targets, and efficiency range sensitivity. Strongest Case Against section includes a concrete quantitative scenario.

**Weaknesses:** The LFP cycle life midpoint (3,500) is a significant methodological choice — sensitivity analysis showing the ranking at 2,000 and 4,000 cycles would strengthen the claim. The 8,000-cycle sodium figure comes from heavy-duty truck testing under fast-charge conditions, and the article doesn't discuss whether these conditions are representative of grid storage charge profiles. Iron-air's 7,300 cycles = 365/day × 20 years, but the article positions iron-air for multi-day (24-100h) storage, where cycling frequency would be far lower — this affects economic modeling in practice. The "50% cheaper" claim in the headline is accurate for raw cost-per-cycle but the efficiency-adjusted figures show a narrower advantage ($0.0082 vs $0.0165 = 50.3% — actually consistent).

## 7. 📊 Data Presentation — 8.9/10

**Strengths:** Two clear, well-labeled tables showing raw and efficiency-adjusted cost-per-cycle. Bold formatting on key result numbers. Human-scale anchors throughout: €438,000/year, 20 MWh/day, 7,300 MWh/year, €657,000 annually. The 95% lithium surge is contextualized with specific dates and prices. Percentage comparisons (50% cheaper, 14.2% YoY) alongside absolute numbers. The three duration bands provide a clear framework for interpreting the data.

**Weaknesses:** A summary comparison table in the "Where Each Chemistry Wins" section would help readers see all three bands side by side. The efficiency-adjusted table could benefit from a "% adjustment" column showing how much efficiency changes the ranking. No visualization of the three duration bands (even a simple text-based range diagram would help).
