# Research: Pentagon DAWG $55B Drone Budget vs US Industrial Base

## Core Thesis
The Pentagon just requested $54.6 billion for drone/autonomy development under DAWG — a 24,070% increase over FY26. Meanwhile, ~500 American drone manufacturers collectively build fewer than 100,000 drones per year. DJI alone produces 500,000+. The industrial math reveals a massive production gap that no amount of money can close overnight.

## Original Contribution: The Production Gap Math

### The Pentagon's Implicit Demand
- $54.6B for DAWG (R&D, not all procurement — but signals scale of ambition)
- At Replicator's $5,000/unit target: $54.6B / $5,000 = 10.9 million drone-equivalents
- Even if only 10% is procurement: that's 1.09M drones
- Current US output: ~100,000/year total across all companies

### The Scale Problem
- PDW (newest, largest): 100,000 drones/year capacity (announced Apr 30)
- Skydio SkyForge: ~5x current (current output undisclosed, likely 20-40K → 100-200K)
- Best case with expansions: maybe 300-400K drones/year by 2028-2029
- DJI alone: 500,000+/year RIGHT NOW
- Gap: even with $3.5B+ in investment, US won't match a single Chinese company's output for years

### Cost Per Unit Comparison
- US military drone (Replicator target): $5,000-50,000 per unit
- DJI commercial equivalent: $500-2,000
- Ukraine's FPV drones: $300-500
- US premium: 3-100x Chinese/Ukrainian equivalents

### The Human Capital Bottleneck
- Defense manufacturing workforce aging out
- Drone assembly requires different skills than traditional defense
- 5,000 Skydio jobs + PDW Huntsville = significant but insufficient
- Pentagon's 24,070% budget increase meets maybe a 20% capacity increase
