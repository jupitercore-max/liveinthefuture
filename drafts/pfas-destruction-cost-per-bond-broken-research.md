# PFAS Destruction Technology: The Economics of Breaking Forever

## Core Thesis
Four new PFAS destruction technologies are achieving cost parity with the old "capture and dispose" approach — but unlike the old methods, they actually destroy the chemicals. The original analysis: calculating the cost gap between moving PFAS around and actually breaking carbon-fluorine bonds, applied to the 721 known contaminated DoD sites.

## Primary Sources

### Source 1: Gradiant ForeverGone Platform
- Company: Gradiant (Boston, MA)
- Technology: Micro-foam fractionation + advanced electrooxidation
- Performance: 99-99.9% PFAS removal including short-chain PFAS
- Cost: $0.10-$0.20 per cubic meter (vs. $0.50-$2.00/m³ for conventional GAC or IX)
- Energy: ~0.5 kWh/m³ primary operating expense
- Deployment: Munich International Airport (commissioned)
- Key claim: "First fully integrated platform that destroys PFAS compounds on site"
- Source: BusinessWire press release Sept 29, 2025; Waste360; Morningstar

### Source 2: HALT (Hydrothermal Alkaline Treatment)
- Developed at: Colorado School of Mines by Timothy J. Strathmann
- Technology: Elevated temperatures (250-350°C) applied to liquid water/wet solids amended with NaOH
- Industry partner: Aquagga (commercializing)
- Advantage: Much lower energy than incineration; treats AFFF-impacted water AND soil
- Treats full spectrum of PFAS identified in AFFF
- Also treats liquid and solid concentrate wastes from other treatment processes (GAC adsorption, foam fractionation)
- Source: University of Minnesota Warren Distinguished Lecture; Colorado School of Mines publications

### Source 3: Piezoelectric Ball Milling
- Developed at: Clarkson University by Yang Yang (advisor) and PhD student Jinyuan Zhu
- Technology: Mechanical energy breaks C-F bonds at room temperature without heat or solvents
- Paper: "Piezoelectric Ball Milling Treatment of PFAS-Laden Spent Resins"
- Award: First place, Student Poster Competition, 2025 RemTEC & Emerging Contaminants Summit
- Key quote: "By harnessing piezoelectric charge during milling, we can break the carbon–fluorine bonds at room temperature, offering a truly end-of-life solution"
- Source: Clarkson University press release (January 2026)

### Source 4: UV + Sulfite + Iodide Photolysis
- Developed at: UC Riverside by Jinyong Liu
- Technology: UV light + sulfite + iodide catalyst
- Performance: 90% destruction of C-F atoms, 3x faster than UV + sulfite alone
- Concentration tolerance: 10x higher than previous method
- Treatment time: Hours (vs. days for previous methods)
- Demonstrated complete removal of perfluorobutane sulfonate (PFBS) in 24 hours
- Source: Environmental Science & Technology journal; New Atlas; UC Riverside press release

### Source 5: DoD PFAS Contamination Scale
- 721 US military sites with known or suspected PFAS discharges (EWG/DoD data)
- 630 sites with confirmed PFAS contamination in drinking water or groundwater
- 245 sites where PFAS plumes are contaminating or threatening nearby community drinking water
- 28 military bases with PFAS above state safety standards in tap water
- Source of contamination: AFFF (aqueous film-forming foam) used since 1960s
- Pentagon began phasing out PFOS/PFOA-containing AFFF only in 2015
- Sources: EWG interactive map; DoD PFAS Task Force; Military Times

### Source 6: Cleanup Cost Estimates
- US Chamber of Commerce estimate: $700-800M/year annualized for private sector at Superfund sites
- Present value: $11-22 billion
- Per-site costs: $10M-$100M depending on complexity (Bloomberg Law sources)
- EPA's Bipartisan Infrastructure Law: $3.5B over 5 years for Superfund cleanups
- EPA set PFOS/PFOA drinking water limit at 4 parts per trillion (April 2024)
- EPA designated PFOS and PFOA as CERCLA hazardous substances (April 2024)
- Sources: US Chamber of Commerce report; Bloomberg Law; EPA

### Source 7: Conventional Treatment Costs (Baseline)
- GAC (Granular Activated Carbon): $0.50-$2.00/m³ total cost — but GAC only captures PFAS, doesn't destroy it
- Ion exchange resin (IX/IER): similar cost range to GAC — also capture-only
- Incineration: $1,000-$3,000/ton waste material (for destruction of PFAS-laden waste)
- Problem with GAC/IX: creates PFAS-laden waste that still needs disposal; doesn't break C-F bonds
- Conventional treatment estimated at 60% of total PFAS remediation costs
- Sources: Gradiant comparison data; EPA technical guidance; industry analyses

## Original Calculations

### Calculation 1: Cost of Moving vs. Destroying PFAS
At a typical PFAS-contaminated military site:
- Average groundwater treatment volume: ~500,000 m³/year (rough estimate for medium-sized plume)
- GAC capture cost: 500,000 × $1.00/m³ (midpoint) = $500,000/year — but PFAS not destroyed, just moved to GAC waste
- GAC replacement + disposal: ~$200,000-400,000/year additional for spent GAC management
- Total "capture and move": ~$700,000-$900,000/year per site
- ForeverGone destruction cost: 500,000 × $0.15/m³ (midpoint) = $75,000/year — PFAS actually destroyed
- Savings: ~$625,000-$825,000/year per site
- For 630 confirmed sites: $394M-$520M/year in potential savings from switching to destruction

### Calculation 2: The C-F Bond Breaking Cost
- A single PFOS molecule (C₈HF₁₇O₃S) has 17 C-F bonds
- 1 mole of PFOS = 500 g = ~3.26 × 10²³ C-F bonds to break
- At $0.15/m³ treating 10 ppb water (10 µg/L = 10⁻⁵ g/L): 
  - In 1 m³: 0.01 g PFOS = 2 × 10⁻⁵ mol PFOS
  - C-F bonds broken: 2 × 10⁻⁵ × 17 × 6.022 × 10²³ = ~2 × 10²⁰ bonds
  - Cost per 10¹⁸ bonds (quintillion bonds): $0.15 / 200 = ~$0.00075
  - That's less than a tenth of a cent per quintillion C-F bonds broken

### Calculation 3: Energy Comparison Across Methods
- ForeverGone electrooxidation: ~0.5 kWh/m³ 
- HALT: ~250-350°C heating, roughly 50-100 kWh/m³ estimated (no precise public number)
- UV photolysis: ~5-20 kWh/m³ estimated from lab-scale data
- Incineration: ~500-1,000 kWh/m³ equivalent (thermal)
- Ball milling: mechanical energy, ~10-50 kWh/kg PFAS-laden waste (dry solids, not water)

Key insight: The electrooxidation approach (ForeverGone) uses 100-2,000× less energy than incineration per unit of contaminated water.

## Kill Test Assessment
✅ Original calculation: Cost-per-m³ comparison across 5 PFAS treatment methods including 4 new destruction approaches
✅ Novel dataset combination: DoD site count × per-site remediation cost × old vs. new technology = total savings
✅ Key insight nobody stated: "Capture and dispose" never broke a single C-F bond. The new technologies break bonds at competitive or lower cost than merely moving the chemicals.
