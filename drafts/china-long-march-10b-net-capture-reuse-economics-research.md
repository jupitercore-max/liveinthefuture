## Event
- **What:** China's Long March 10B maiden flight, first successful orbital-class booster recovery using net-capture system
- **When:** July 10, 2026, 12:15 PM local time (0415 GMT)
- **Where:** Wenchang, Hainan Island → offshore platform in South China Sea
- **Recovery:** Wire/net-capture system caught first stage ~6 min after separation

## Primary Sources

### 1. Reuters

- Booster recovered ~6 min after separation on offshore platform using net
- 16 metric tons to LEO

- China plans to reuse this first stage for another launch by end of 2026
- CALT expert Chen Muye quote: "Net-based recovery helps simplify the rocket's onboard structure, reduces vehicle mass and increases payload capacity. It is also highly adaptable to landing-point deviations."

## Original Calculations

### 1. Payload Penalty Comparison
- Falcon 9 expendable: 22,800 kg LEO
- Falcon 9 reusable: ~15,600 kg LEO
- Penalty: 7,200 kg or 31.6%
- Sources of penalty: landing legs (~2,000 kg structural) + reserved propellant for landing burns
- Net capture eliminates landing leg mass (1,500–2,500 kg savings)
- Still requires some retro-propulsion but less than propulsive hoverslam
- LM-10B achieves 16,000 kg reusable on maiden flight vs Falcon 9's 15,600 kg

### 2. Cost Per Kilogram Projection
- Estimated total vehicle cost: $40M (comparable Falcon 9 class)
- Booster cost at 75%: $30M
- After 10 reflights: $30M ÷ 10 = $3M/flight
- Upper stage + operations: ~$10M/flight
- Total per-flight cost: ~$13M
- Per-kg cost: $13M ÷ 16,000 kg = **$813/kg**
- Comparison: SpaceX internal ~$1,795/kg, list $2,720/kg

### 3. Reuse Timeline Gap
- SpaceX: First landing Dec 21, 2015 → First reflight March 30, 2017 = 15.3 months
- China: First catch July 10, 2026 → Target reuse by end of 2026 = ~5 months
- 3x faster, but SpaceX was pioneering with no roadmap to follow

### 4. Launch Cadence Gap
- SpaceX 2025: ~150 Falcon 9 launches/year (Starlink-driven demand)
- China 2025: 67 total orbital launches (all types)
- If LM-10B gets 30% of manifest: ~20 flights/year
- Each booster would fly ~5x/year vs SpaceX's ~10x/year
- Half the cadence = half the reuse savings

### 5. Guowang Deployment Math (Core Original Calculation)
- Guowang: ~13,000 satellites planned
- LM-10B capacity to 900 km SSO: 11,000 kg (SpaceNews)
- Estimated satellite mass: ~300 kg (comparable to Starlink v1 at 260 kg)
- Satellites per launch: 11,000 ÷ 300 ≈ 36
- Total launches needed: 13,000 ÷ 36 ≈ **361 launches**
- At 20 launches/year: 18 years
- At 50 launches/year: 7.2 years
- At 100 launches/year: 3.6 years
- SpaceX comparison: deployed ~10,800 Starlink in ~6 years at 150+ launches/year
- Key insight: China needs to triple its current total launch rate just for Guowang

### 6. Satellite Gap
- China: 1,353 satellites (end 2025)
- SpaceX: 10,800+ (Starlink alone)
- Gap: ~9,447 satellites
- Even with reusability, closing this gap is a decade-long project

## Strongest Counterargument
Net capture may solve the wrong constraint. China's bottleneck is not landing tech but launch cadence and satellite manufacturing throughput. SpaceX produces 40+ Starlink satellites/week. SpaceX's advantage is having a captive customer (Starlink) generating 150 launches/year of internal demand, amortizing reuse faster than government contracts. Guowang lacks paying customers, completed regulatory framework, or proven satellite bus.

## Limitations
- Landing leg mass savings estimated by Falcon 9 analogy (China doesn't disclose)
- LM-10B costs not publicly available
- Guowang satellite mass estimated from comparable architectures
- Cost projections assume China matches SpaceX's economic structures (took SpaceX a decade)
- SpaceX is already moving to Starship/Super Heavy (mechanical catch), potentially <$500/kg
- Comparing to Falcon 9 may be wrong benchmark by time Guowang deploys
