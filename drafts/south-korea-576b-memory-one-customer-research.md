# Research: South Korea's $576B Memory Bet — One Customer Wants 60% of Global Output

## Thesis
South Korea just committed $576 billion to semiconductor manufacturing — the largest single-country chip investment in history. The math behind it reveals something extraordinary: OpenAI alone wants 900,000 DRAM wafers per month by 2029, roughly 60% of today's entire global DRAM output. One customer. And at least five more hyperscalers are in line. Even after doubling capacity, Samsung and SK Hynix would still struggle to serve the demand.

## Original Calculations

### Calculation 1: OpenAI Demand Absorption Rate
- Samsung current DRAM: ~675K wafers/month (midpoint of 650-700K)
- SK Hynix DRAM (estimated): ~400-450K wafers/month (Samsung has ~40% share, SK Hynix ~29%)
  - Combined Samsung+SK Hynix: ~70% of global DRAM = ~1.075-1.15M wafers/month
  - Implies global total: ~1.54-1.64M wafers/month
- OpenAI Stargate: 900K wafers/month by 2029
- OpenAI as % of Samsung+SK Hynix current output: 900K / 1.1M = **~82%**
- OpenAI as % of global DRAM: 900K / 1.6M = **~56%**
- After doubling (South Korea's 5-year goal): Samsung+SK Hynix at ~2.2M wafers/month
- OpenAI as % of doubled output: 900K / 2.2M = **~41%**
- Remaining for all other customers: **59%** of doubled capacity = ~1.3M wafers/month
- But that's LESS than today's total global output (~1.6M), split among billions of devices

### Calculation 2: Investment Per Dollar Comparison
- South Korea: $576B government+corporate announcement
- US CHIPS Act: $52.7B in subsidies
- Ratio: 576/52.7 = **10.9×** South Korea's investment vs US subsidies
- Even including all US private sector pledges (~$400B from TSMC, Intel, Samsung, Micron): $452.7B total
- South Korea corporate-only (800 trillion won = $518B) still exceeds US total pledges

### Calculation 3: Samsung's Long-Term Commitment in Context
- Samsung 2026-2040: 2,450 trillion won = $1.59T
- South Korea GDP 2025: ~$1.8T
- Samsung's 15-year chip commitment: **88% of South Korea's annual GDP**
- Annual run rate: $1.59T / 15 years = $106B/year in domestic semiconductor investment
- For context: entire US defense budget is ~$886B/year

### Calculation 4: The Hyperscaler Queue Problem
- If OpenAI alone absorbs ~41% of doubled Samsung+SK Hynix capacity...
- Google, Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, xAI each building multi-GW data center campuses
- Conservative estimate: top 5 other hyperscalers collectively need at least 1× OpenAI's demand
- That's another 900K wafers/month
- OpenAI + top 5 others: ~1.8M wafers/month
- As % of doubled Samsung+SK Hynix capacity: 1.8M / 2.2M = **82%**
- Leaving just 18% for smartphones, PCs, automotive, IoT, gaming, enterprise servers
- Today smartphones alone consume ~30% of DRAM output

## Limitations
- The 900K wafer figure is from an LOI, not a binding purchase agreement. Actual orders may differ.
- "Doubling DRAM capacity in 5 years" is a government target, not guaranteed buildout. SK Hynix's Chey noted that Yongin took 9 years.
- Wafer-to-HBM conversion isn't 1:1 — HBM requires stacking (8-12 DRAM dies per stack) + advanced packaging. Not all wafers become HBM.
- CXMT and other Chinese fabs could grow faster than projected, altering supply dynamics.
- Demand projections assume AI scaling continues at current rates. A slowdown would change everything.

## Strongest Counterargument
Professor Lee Jong-ho (Seoul National University) captured it directly: "It would be ideal if demand remained strong for the next 20 or 30 years, but no one can know that with certainty...If demand were to decline, the consequences would be severe." South Korea is concentrating a historically large bet on a single demand driver (AI), in a region (Gwangju/South Jeolla) that currently lacks the supplier ecosystem, talent pool, and infrastructure that Seoul-area clusters built over decades. The 2000s LCD oversupply crisis, where Samsung and LG massively overbuilt panel capacity only to face 50%+ price crashes, is the closest historical analogy from South Korea's own industrial history.
