## Core Thesis
The EU taxes electricity at 2x the rate of natural gas — then wonders why electrification has been stuck at 23% for a decade while China races past 29%. The Iran war has now added €50B to the EU's fossil fuel import bill in just 5 months, and the EU's response — the Electrification Action Plan due July 17 — is the most consequential energy policy shift since REPowerEU. But the math exposes a structural paradox: Europe generates nearly half its electricity from renewables (47.3% in 2025) yet can't get anyone to use it.

## Original Calculation: The Electrification Tax Inversion

**The hidden cost of the EU's tax structure:**
- EU electricity taxes + levies are ~2x higher than gas taxes (Strategic Perspectives analysis)
- EU electrification rate: 23% (stagnant for ~decade)
- EU's 2025 fossil fuel import bill: €337 billion (Eurostat)
- Iran war premium since Feb 2026: €50 billion (EU data, Reuters Jul 9)
- €50B ÷ ~5 months = ~€10B/month = ~€333M/day in extra fossil fuel costs
- At ~€10,000/heat pump installation: €50B = 5 million heat pumps
- EU has roughly 100-110M households, ~70% use gas/oil heating
- 5M heat pumps = ~7% of fossil-heated homes could have switched

**The generation-consumption gap:**
- 47.3% of EU electricity from renewables in 2025 (record, Eurostat)
- Solar was #1 electricity source in June 2025 (22%) for first time ever
- Yet electricity is only 23% of final energy consumption
- Goal: 32% by 2030, ~50% by 2040-2050
- This means EU must nearly double electricity's share in ~14 years
- IEA projects EU electricity demand needs to increase 60% by 2030

**The import savings math:**
- Higher electrification could replace 2/3 of gas consumption (EC estimate)
- Could halve oil consumption (EC estimate)
- Total import bill savings: ~€200B (OilPrice.com/Bloomberg, provisional EC estimates)
- 2022 Russia/Ukraine crisis cost EU ~€1 trillion in extra imports over 2022-2024 (Ember)
- If current Iran war persists at €10B/month: another €120B/year added to fossil fuel bill
