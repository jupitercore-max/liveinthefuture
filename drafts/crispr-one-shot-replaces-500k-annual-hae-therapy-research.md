# Research Notes: In Vivo CRISPR One-Shot HAE Therapy

## Story Angle
First Phase 3 in vivo CRISPR gene-editing trial succeeds. Intellia's lonvoguran ziclumeran (lonvo-z / NTLA-2002) achieved 62% complete attack freedom and 87% attack reduction with a single IV infusion in hereditary angioedema patients. If approved, it becomes the first in vivo CRISPR therapy for any condition. BLA rolling submission initiated April 2026; completion expected H2 2026. Planned US launch 1H 2027.

## Primary Sources

### 1. NEJM Phase 3 HAELO Trial (June 2026)
- **Citation:** Cohn DM et al. "Lonvoguran Ziclumeran — In Vivo CRISPR Gene Editing in Hereditary Angioedema." NEJM (2026). DOI: 10.1056/nejmoa2600931
- **Design:** Phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled
- **Patients:** 80 randomized, ages 16+, HAE with C1 inhibitor deficiency
- **Intervention:** Single IV infusion, 50 mg fixed dose
- **Primary endpoint:** Monthly attack rate, weeks 5-28
- **Key results:**
  - 87% reduction in monthly attack rate vs placebo
  - 62% of lonvo-z patients became completely attack-free
  - Cross-trial comparison: 62% attack-free vs 0% for berotralstat, 0% for C1 inhibitor, 31-44% for lanadelumab
- **Safety:** Well-tolerated. Mild infusion reactions, headache, fatigue, back pain — all resolved quickly. No serious adverse events in treatment group.
- **Durability:** Phase 1/2 data (37 participants) shows sustained efficacy and safety at 4 years post-infusion

### 2. Intellia SEC Filings (8-K, April 27, 2026)
- **Source:** sec.gov, 8-K filing
- Rolling BLA submission to FDA initiated April 2026
- RMAT (Regenerative Medicine Advanced Therapy) designation — enables priority review
- BLA completion expected H2 2026
- Planned US launch 1H 2027, pending FDA approval
- Lonvo-z targets KLKB1 gene in liver → reduces kallikrein levels → eliminates HAE attacks
- Mechanism: LNP-delivered CRISPR-Cas9 knocks out KLKB1 gene in hepatocytes

### 3. AAFP (American Academy of Family Physicians) — HAE Cost/Prevalence (March 2026)
- **Citation:** White L. "Hereditary Angioedema: Recognition and Treatment." AAFP, March 9, 2026.
- Prevalence: 1 in 50,000 to 1 in 100,000 in the US
- Prophylactic treatment costs: estimated $500,000/year per patient
- 90% of HAE is type 1 or 2 (C1 inhibitor deficiency — the target population for lonvo-z)

### 4. US Claims-Based Prevalence Study (PubMed)
- **Citation:** IQVIA PharMetrics Plus claims database analysis
- 2020 prevalence: 6,595-8,694 diagnosed HAE patients in US
- Adjusted estimate: ~2.43 per 100,000

### 5. Real-World HAE Treatment Costs (Multiple Sources)
- **Lanadelumab total HAE costs:** $283,241-$377,326/year (MarketScan claims data)
- **Berotralstat total HAE costs:** $373,010-$757,137/year (models and claims)
- **On-demand only treatment:** $363,795/year direct + $52,576 indirect = $416,371/year (Castaldo et al.)
- **Dosing burden comparison (from Intellia SEC filing/investor deck):**
  - lonvo-z: 1 infusion, lifetime
  - berotralstat: 365 daily oral tablets/year
  - C1 esterase inhibitor (LTP): 104 injections/year
  - donidalorsen: 7-13 injections/year
  - garadacimab: 13 injections/year
  - lanadelumab: 13-26 injections/year

### 6. In Vivo vs Ex Vivo CRISPR Context
- Casgevy (exagamglogene autotemcel): First CRISPR therapy approved (Dec 2023), for sickle cell disease and beta-thalassemia. EX VIVO — requires stem cell extraction, myeloablative conditioning (chemotherapy), cell editing in lab, re-infusion. Hospitalization ~30 days. Price: $2.2M.
- lonvo-z: IN VIVO — single IV infusion in outpatient setting. No cell extraction, no chemo. Dramatically simpler.
- Key difference: In vivo opens CRISPR to diseases where cells can't be extracted/returned (liver, eye, brain). Ex vivo limited to blood diseases.

## Original Calculations

### A. Lifetime Cost Comparison (Novel)
- Median HAE onset: age 10-15 (symptoms manifest between 4-11, diagnosis 10-22)
- Assumed diagnosis age: 15 (conservative)
- US life expectancy: 77.5 years (CDC 2023)
- Remaining treatment years: 62.5
- Annual prophylaxis cost (AAFP mid-estimate): $500,000
- Lifetime prophylaxis cost: 62.5 × $500,000 = **$31.25 million**
- If lonvo-z priced at Casgevy level ($2.2M): Break-even in **4.4 years**
- Net lifetime savings: $31.25M - $2.2M = **$29.05 million per patient (93% reduction)**
- Even at aggressive pricing ($5M): Break-even in 10 years. Net savings: $26.25M.

### B. Dosing Burden Over a Lifetime (Novel)
- 62.5 remaining years from diagnosis:
  - lonvo-z: **1 dose, total, forever**
  - lanadelumab (26 injections/yr): 1,625 injections
  - C1 inhibitor LTP (104/yr): 6,500 injections
  - berotralstat (daily pill): 22,813 pills
  - lonvo-z eliminates 99.99% of lifetime pharmaceutical interactions

### C. Total US Market Transformation
- ~7,000 diagnosed US patients (conservative estimate from claims data)
- Current annual market: 7,000 × $500,000 = $3.5 billion/year recurring
- One-time CRISPR market (at $2.2M): 7,000 × $2.2M = $15.4 billion one-time
- Pharma business model inversion: $3.5B/yr annuity → $15.4B one-shot with zero recurring revenue
- This is the gene therapy pricing paradox: cures destroy the revenue streams that fund them

## Strongest Counterargument
The strongest case against treating this as a fait accompli: Intellia's OTHER in vivo CRISPR program — nex-z (NTLA-2001) for ATTR amyloidosis — is currently on FDA clinical hold. The FDA flagged safety concerns serious enough to pause a Phase 3 trial. If regulators have worries about one Intellia in vivo CRISPR program, those concerns could cast a shadow over lonvo-z's BLA review, even though they're different programs targeting different genes. The irreversibility of gene editing means the safety bar is legitimately higher than for reversible therapies. With only 80 patients in the Phase 3 and 28 weeks of controlled follow-up, the dataset is thin for a permanent intervention. Four years of Phase 1/2 data helps but doesn't eliminate tail risk.

## Limitations
- 80-patient Phase 3 trial — small N inherent to rare disease
- 28-week primary endpoint; long-term safety extrapolated from 37 Phase 1/2 patients at 4 years
- No head-to-head trials vs newer agents (garadacimab also showed 62% attack-free rates in its own trial)
- Cross-trial comparisons are illustrative, not definitive — different patient populations, trial designs
- Pricing unknown — all break-even calculations use assumed price points
- Global access: LNP-CRISPR requires cold chain, specialized infusion centers — may not reach patients in lower-income countries
- KLKB1 knockout permanence: theoretical long-term liver effects of permanent kallikrein suppression unknown
