# Research Notes: Kairos Hermes 2 — First Gen IV Reactor vs. US Nuclear Construction Track Record

## News Hook (April 17, 2026)
Kairos Power broke ground on the Hermes 2 Demonstration Plant in Oak Ridge, Tennessee — the company's first commercial-scale reactor and the first-ever power-producing Gen IV reactor to receive a construction permit from the NRC.

- **50 MW** of clean electricity to TVA grid
- First deployment under **Google's 500 MW fleet deal** (up to 7 reactors by 2035)
- Target: first power by **2030** (4 years from groundbreaking)
- Technology: fluoride-salt-cooled high-temperature reactor (KP-FHR) using TRISO fuel + Flibe molten salt
- Factory-built modular approach — equipment fabricated in Albuquerque, NM, shipped for assembly
- Google and Kairos bear financial risk, NOT ratepayers (via TVA PPA)

### Gap Calculation
- Total announced Big Tech nuclear capacity: ~10 GW
- At 90% capacity factor: 10 GW × 0.90 × 8,760 hours = **78.8 TWh/yr**
- Projected 2027 AI demand: 500 TWh/yr
- Nuclear covers: **78.8 / 500 = 15.8%** of projected AI data center demand
- **Even if every announced reactor is built on time, nuclear covers less than 1/6 of AI power needs**

## What's Actually Different About Kairos
1. **Factory-built:** Equipment fabricated in Albuquerque, shipped for assembly. Not site-built like Vogtle.
2. **Small scale:** 50 MW vs. 1,100 MW per Vogtle unit. Less complexity, fewer failure modes.
3. **Iterative:** Hermes 1 already under nuclear construction (started May 2025). Lessons feed directly to Hermes 2.
4. **Risk allocation:** Google and Kairos bear the financial risk, not ratepayers. No incentive to gold-plate.
5. **Different technology:** KP-FHR uses passive safety (no high-pressure systems), unlike AP1000 (Vogtle/Summer).
6. **Startup DNA:** Founded 2016, 10 years of focused development. Not a utility trying to build for the first time in 30 years.

## Strongest Counterargument
Comparing Kairos to Vogtle may be fundamentally unfair. Vogtle used first-of-a-kind AP1000 technology built by utilities with no recent nuclear construction experience, under a cost-plus regulatory framework that incentivized gold-plating. Kairos is a startup using an inherently simpler design (no pressurized water), with factory fabrication reducing site labor (the #1 cost/schedule driver at Vogtle), and iterative development from Hermes 1. The NuScale comparison is more apt — and NuScale's UAMPS project was cancelled in 2023 after costs escalated from $5.3B to $9.3B for 462 MW. Even "new nuclear" has a 0/1 track record in the US.
