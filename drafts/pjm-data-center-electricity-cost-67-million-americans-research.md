# Research: AI Data Centers and Residential Electricity Costs

## Slug: pjm-data-center-electricity-cost-67-million-americans
## Journalist: Zara Osman (Energy/Climate beat)
## Category: ⚡ Energy

## Thesis
Data centers have already added a quantifiable dollar amount to household electricity bills across PJM's 13-state footprint — and the grid's own watchdog says the price impact is "not reversible." Original calculation: data center demand accounted for ~$340/year per household in added costs, or ~18-21% of the average PJM residential bill.

## Original Calculation

### Per-Household Cost of Data Centers (PJM)
Using Monitoring Analytics' own attribution:
- Jan-May 2026: $3.8B attributable to data centers (23% of $16.25B increase)
- Annualized: $3.8B × (12/5) = $9.12B/year
- PJM households: 67M people ÷ 2.53 people/household (Census avg) = 26.5M households
- Per-household cost from data centers: $9.12B ÷ 26.5M = ~$344/year

### Cross-Check with Capacity Auction Data
- $23.1B from three capacity auctions (covering ~3 delivery years)
- Per year: ~$7.7B
- Per household: $7.7B ÷ 26.5M = ~$291/year from capacity costs alone

### Percentage of Average Bill
- EIA average residential electricity bill (2025): ~$1,840/year nationally, ~$1,660/year in PJM states
- Data center premium: $344/year = ~20.7% of average PJM household bill
- OR: $291/year (capacity only) = ~17.5% of average bill

### Supply-Demand Gap Widening
- Reserve margin shortfall: 210 MW (2026/2027) → 6,520 MW (2027/2028) = 31× increase
- At $344/household/year growing, the trajectory points to $500+/household by 2028

## Counterargument
Data centers bring tax revenue, jobs, and economic development. Northern Virginia's data center corridor generates billions in property taxes, which have helped reduce residential property tax rates. A full accounting must include both the electricity cost increase and the fiscal benefit.

## Limitations
- Monitoring Analytics' $3.8B data center attribution uses their internal methodology; the actual pass-through to retail bills varies by utility and rate structure
- PJM is one (albeit the largest) of several US grid operators; other regions may see different dynamics
- Not all wholesale cost increases pass through to retail bills immediately — regulatory lag means some costs are spread or delayed
- The 23% data center share of cost increases may include load growth from electrification and EVs that coincides with data center expansion
