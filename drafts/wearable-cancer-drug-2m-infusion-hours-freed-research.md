# Research: The IV-to-SC Cancer Drug Migration and Infusion Chair-Hour Liberation Math

## News Peg
Two cancer drug approvals in the same week (July 9-10, 2026) mark the culmination of the IV-to-subcutaneous conversion wave:

1. **Sarclisa Escena** (isatuximab-irfc SC via on-body injector) — FDA approved July 9, 2026
   - First cancer drug approved for delivery via on-body injector (OBI)
   - Enable Injections' CirCLIQ OBI (enFuse platform): automated, hands-free, retractable 30g needle
   - 13 minutes vs. up to 3 hours IV
   - Infusion reactions: 25% (IV) → 1.5% (SC) — 16.7× reduction
   - 74.5% patient preference for OBI (IZALCO Phase 2 study)
   - 70,000+ patients treated worldwide
   - Sales: €588M ($671M) in 2025, expected €710M in 2026
   - Source: FDA approval notice, Sanofi press release (GlobeNewsWire), Reuters (July 11, 2026)

2. **Keytruda QLEX** (pembrolizumab + berahyaluronidase alfa-pmph SC) — FDA approved 2025, available late September 2026
   - First and only subcutaneous immune checkpoint inhibitor
   - 1 minute (Q3W) or 2 minutes (Q6W) vs. 30 min IV infusion
   - 38 solid tumor indications
   - Berahyaluronidase alfa from Alteogen (NOT Halozyme ENHANZE)
   - Keytruda = world's biggest cancer drug: $31.7B in 2025 worldwide sales
   - US: ~$20.6B (65% of sales)
   - Peak expected: $35B by 2028 (before LOE)
   - Source: Merck press release (merck.com, ~July 6, 2026), DailyMed, Zacks

## ORIGINAL CALCULATION: Infusion Chair-Hours Freed

### Methodology
For each drug, I estimate: (a) US patients/year, (b) annual IV infusion visits, (c) drug-specific infusion time per visit, (d) SC administration time, (e) net time freed per patient per year.

**I count only the drug-specific infusion time eliminated** — not the entire chair booking, because combo patients still need the chair for other IV drugs. For monotherapy patients, the entire facility visit is eliminated, but I conservatively count only infusion duration.

### Drug 1: Keytruda → Keytruda QLEX

**US patients:** $20.6B US revenue ÷ ~$170K net price/patient/year ≈ 121,000 patients
(List price $208K, net after rebates/discounts ~$170K per ICIJ investigation data)

**IV infusion time:** 30 minutes per infusion
**Visits/year:** Blended Q3W and Q6W dosing → ~13 visits/year
**SC time:** 1-2 minutes injection (no significant chair time needed beyond observation)

**Drug-specific infusion time eliminated:**
121,000 patients × 13 visits × 30 min = 47.19M minutes = **786,500 hours/year**

**For monotherapy patients (~25-30% of Keytruda users):**
~33,000 patients who no longer need any infusion center visit for Keytruda
33,000 × 13 visits × 45 min (full chair booking including pre/post) = 289,575 hours of total facility time eliminated

### Drug 2: Sarclisa → Sarclisa Escena (OBI)

**US patients:** ~35,000 (estimated from 70K worldwide, US ~50% of revenue)

**IV infusion time:** ~3 hours per infusion (per prescribing info)
**Dosing schedule:** Q1W (cycle 1, 4 infusions) → Q2W (cycles 2-4, ~12 infusions) → Q4W (cycle 5+, ~6-13 infusions/year)
**Blended visits/year:** ~18 visits for year 1 patients, ~13 for maintenance patients
**Conservative average:** ~15 visits/year across patient mix

**OBI time:** 13 minutes per administration

**Drug-specific infusion time eliminated:**
35,000 patients × 15 visits × (180 min - 13 min) = 35,000 × 15 × 167 min = 87.68M min = **1,461,250 hours/year**

### Drug 3: Darzalex → Darzalex FASPRO (historical, already completed)

**US patients:** ~100,000 (from $8.27B US revenue, ~$85K net/patient/year)

**IV infusion time (before FASPRO):** First dose 7 hrs, second 4.3 hrs, subsequent ~3.4 hrs
**FASPRO time:** 3-5 minutes
**Blended annual visits:** ~15 (weighted across dosing phases)
**Average IV time saved per visit:** ~3.2 hours (mostly in maintenance phase)

**Historical infusion time eliminated when transition happened:**
100,000 patients × 15 visits × 3.2 hrs = **4,800,000 hours/year** (already freed since 2020)

### What 2.25M Chair-Hours Means

**In nurse labor:**
- 2.25M hours × $42/hr median nurse wage = **$94.5M/year** in freed nursing capacity
- 2.25M hours ÷ 2,080 hrs/yr (FTE) = **1,082 nurse-FTEs** freed
- Against a deficit of 78,000 infusion nurses, this is ~1.4% of the gap

**In physical infrastructure:**
- Average US infusion center chair: ~10 hrs/day × 260 days = 2,600 productive chair-hours/year
- 2.25M ÷ 2,600 = **865 infusion chairs** freed
- At ~20 chairs per center = **~43 infusion centers' worth of capacity**
- Cost to build a new ambulatory infusion center: $5-15M
- Equivalent construction cost avoided: **$215M - $645M**

**In revenue capacity:**
- At $660/chair-hour (CancerNetwork data): **$1.48B/year** in freed revenue-generating capacity
- This capacity can be redirected to growing patient volume (2M+ diagnoses/year)

## Strongest Counterargument

The SC conversion doesn't eliminate all infusion center visits. Most cancer patients are on multi-drug regimens where other drugs (chemotherapy agents) still require IV administration. Converting one drug to SC while keeping others IV means the patient still occupies a chair — you've shortened the visit by 30 minutes, not eliminated it.

**For Keytruda specifically:** ~70-75% of patients are on combination regimens. For these patients, the chair-hour savings are real but modest (30 min per visit, not the full visit).

**For Sarclisa:** All patients are on combination (always combined with pomalidomide+dex or carfilzomib+dex). They still need oral drugs, but not necessarily IV infusion for the other agents. So chair-time savings may be larger.

**Counter-counter:** Even modest per-visit savings compound. 30 minutes freed per visit × 13 visits × 121,000 patients = 787K hours. And for the ~25-30% on monotherapy, the entire visit is eliminated. Furthermore, SC/OBI delivery enables home administration for many patients, which is a fundamentally different care model.

## Limitations

1. Patient counts estimated from revenue/price, not actual patient registries. Real net prices vary by payer.
2. Transition timing is uncertain — uptake curves for new SC formulations take 2-4 years to reach steady state (Darzalex FASPRO took ~3 years for majority conversion).
3. Chair-hour calculations assume average dosing schedules; actual schedules vary by indication, disease stage, and treatment response.
4. Infusion center costs vary enormously by geography and setting (hospital OPD vs. freestanding vs. physician office).
5. The analysis doesn't account for monitoring requirements that may keep patients in the facility even after SC injection.
6. Keytruda QLEX won't be available until late September 2026 — current savings are projected, not realized.
