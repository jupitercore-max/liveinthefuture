# Research: NASA X-59 Quiet Supersonic — Mach 1.4, 53-Year Ban

## News Hook
- NASA's X-59 QueSST hit Mach 1.4 (924 mph) at 55,000 ft on June 12, 2026
- First supersonic flight was June 5, 2026 at Mach 1.1
- Part of Quesst mission to build data case for lifting 53-year FAA overland supersonic ban

## X-59 Aircraft Specs
- Builder: Lockheed Martin Skunk Works, Palmdale CA
- Engine: GE F414-GE-100 (same as F/A-18E/F Super Hornet)
- Design speed: Mach 1.4 (~924 mph at altitude)
- Cruise altitude: 55,000-60,000 ft
- Max gross weight: 24,300 lbs; Empty: 15,000 lbs; Fuel: 8,000 lbs
- Length: ~99.7 ft (30.4 m)
- Key innovation: shaped to produce 75 PLdB sonic thump vs ~110 PLdB for conventional supersonic
- No forward cockpit windows — uses eXternal Vision System (XVS): HD cameras + AR display
- Test pilot: Jim "Clue" Less (NASA/Lockheed)
- First flight: October 28, 2025 (subsonic)
- First supersonic: June 5, 2026 (Mach 1.1)
- Second supersonic: June 12, 2026 (Mach 1.4 — design speed)
- Program cost: ~$247 million
- NASA administrator: Jared Isaacman

## Quesst Mission Phases
1. Phase 1 (current): Envelope expansion at Edwards AFB — proving the aircraft can fly safely at design conditions
2. Phase 2: Acoustic validation flights over desert — ground microphone arrays to measure actual perceived noise
3. Phase 3: Community overflights of selected US cities — measure public response to the "thump"
4. Goal: provide noise data to FAA and ICAO for new noise-based supersonic certification standards

## Regulatory Context
- FAA banned overland civil supersonic flight March 1973 (14 CFR §91.817)
- Reason: public complaints about Concorde-era sonic booms (measured ~110 PLdB)
- 75 PLdB target = ~90% perceived noise reduction (log scale: 35 dB difference ≈ roughly 1/100th the acoustic energy reaching the ground)
- Trump executive order (June 6, 2025): lifted ban, set timeline for noise-based certification framework
- H.R. 3410 (Supersonic Aviation Modernization Act): passed House unanimously; requires FAA to issue final noise-based certification rule by April 1, 2027
- International: ICAO has no supersonic noise standard yet; X-59 data intended to inform one

## Concorde Comparison
- Concorde: Mach 2.0, 100-144 seats, retired 2003
- Fuel consumption: ~22 tons/hour; 15.8 passenger-miles/gallon vs 747's 46.4
- Ticket price: ~$12,000 round-trip London-NYC (2003 dollars)
- Rarely more than half full
- Only 14 used commercially (7 BA, 7 Air France)
- Development cost: ~£4.26 billion (~$37.5 billion in 2017 dollars)
- Only profitable routes: NYC-London, NYC-Paris (transoceanic only, because overland ban)
- Total Concordes built: 20 (including prototypes)

## Competitors / Industry Context
- Boom Supersonic (Denver): Overture targeting Mach 1.7, 64-80 passengers, ~$5,000 tickets
  - Demonstrated "boomless cruise" via Mach cutoff in January 2025
  - 130+ pre-orders from American Airlines, United Airlines, JAL
  - No afterburner design (SAF-compatible)
- ICCT study: lifting overland ban could increase global supersonic jet demand from ~12 to ~240 aircraft
- Spike Aerospace: S-512 quiet supersonic business jet concept
- Exosonic: USAF contract for quiet supersonic Air Force One replacement

## Original Contribution: The Regulatory Tax Calculation
- Concorde was economically limited to transoceanic routes by the overland ban
- Only ~20 viable city pairs (ocean-crossing with sufficient demand): NYC-London, NYC-Paris, etc.
- With overland supersonic allowed, addressable city pairs explode:
  - US domestic alone: NYC-LA, NYC-Chicago, NYC-Dallas, SF-NYC, LA-NYC, Miami-NYC, etc.
  - OAG data shows ~150+ US domestic routes with daily nonstop demand over 500 passengers
  - European domestic: London-Madrid, Paris-Rome, etc.
  - Asian: Tokyo-Singapore, Beijing-Bangkok, etc.
- Conservative estimate: overland routes outnumber transoceanic viable routes by ~10:1
- The 1973 ban effectively locked out ~90% of the addressable supersonic market for 53 years
- X-59 cost ($247M) vs Concorde development ($37.5B adj.) = 0.66%
- For 0.66% of Concorde's development cost, NASA is buying the data that could unlock 10x the addressable market
- ICCT's 12→240 aircraft estimate implies a 20x demand multiplier from regulation change alone
- At Boom's estimated $200M per aircraft: 240 × $200M = $48 billion addressable market from one rule change
