# Research: Starship V3 Orbital Refueling Gap

## Thesis
SpaceX just completed a full-duration static fire of Starship V3 (April 14, 2026), targeting a May launch for Flight 12. The rocket is bigger, more powerful, and carries 100+ tons to LEO. But the critical path for Artemis III isn't the rocket — it's orbital refueling. SpaceX needs to demonstrate 14+ tanker flights to fill a propellant depot, transfer fuel to the Human Landing System, and fly it to the Moon. They have never transferred a single liter of propellant in orbit. The flight cadence math for a 2028 Artemis III is brutal.

## Novel Contribution: Flight Cadence Analysis

**The math nobody has published:**

Assumptions:
- Artemis III target: mid-2028 (July 2028 = ~26 months from now)
- SpaceX must complete before Artemis III: (a) V3 maiden flight, (b) orbital refueling demo, (c) depot test, (d) 14+ operational tanker flights, (e) HLS transfer and lunar demo
- Current average cadence: 1 flight every ~2.7 months (11 flights in 30 months)
- V3 improvement: SpaceX claims infrastructure for much faster cadence

**Timeline back-calculation:**
- If Artemis III is July 2028, HLS must be ready 3-6 months earlier for integration (Jan-Apr 2028)
- The 14 tanker flights alone, at 2.7 months/flight, would take 37.8 months — that's past the deadline before even starting
- To complete 14 tanker flights in 12 months (Jan 2027-Jan 2028), SpaceX needs ~1.2 flights/month = one every 25 days
- Adding 5-6 development/demo flights beforehand (V3 maiden, refueling demo, depot test, etc.) in 8 months (May 2026-Jan 2027) = ~0.75 flights/month
- Total flights needed: ~20 in ~26 months = ~0.77 flights/month as a floor, but that assumes zero failures
- Historical Starship success rate: 55% overall, improving to ~80% for recent Block 2 flights
- At 80% success, you need ~17-18 tanker attempts for 14 successes
- This pushes required cadence to ~1 flight/month minimum, sustained over 18+ months

**Comparison to historical programs:**
- Falcon 9 in 2024: 92 launches = 7.7/month (but mature vehicle, 12+ years of flight)
- Space Shuttle peak: 9 flights in 1985 = 0.75/month
- Saturn V: 13 launches in 4 years = 0.27/month
- No rocket in history has achieved 1/month cadence in its second year of a new variant

**Cost calculation:**
- Starship launch cost: ~$90M per Musk's estimates, likely higher for early V3 flights
- 14 tanker flights at $90M = $1.26B in launch costs alone for one Artemis III fueling campaign
- Compare: SLS costs $4.1B per launch, but flies once
- The economics only work if Starship hits rapid reuse — otherwise the 14-flight refueling model is more expensive than expected
