# Hyundai Plans 25,000 Atlas Robots for US Factories — Research

## Core Facts

### Deployment Plan
- Hyundai Motor Group plans to deploy ~25,000 Atlas humanoid robots across Hyundai Motor and Kia manufacturing facilities (Source: KED Global, 2026-05-19, citing JPMorgan Chase-hosted investor relations session)
- This accounts for >80% of Hyundai's targeted annual humanoid production capacity in 2028
- Annual production capacity target: 30,000 Atlas robots by 2028 (includes Atlas, Spot, and Stretch)
- First deployment: Hyundai Motor Group Metaplant America (HMGMA) in Georgia, starting 2028
- Kia Georgia plant to follow in 2029
- CEO Kia Song Ho-sung confirmed the timeline

### Pricing
- Boston Dynamics will price Atlas below the cost of employing two US manufacturing workers for two years — approximately $320,000 (Source: KED Global, 2026-01-20)
- Atlas maintenance cost estimated at ~$9,500/year (Source: Interesting Engineering, citing industry estimates)
- Leveraging Hyundai Motor Group's automotive supply chain to reduce costs

### Union Opposition
- Hyundai Motor's Korean Metal Workers' Union issued statement: "Keep in mind that not a single robot can enter the workplace without labor-management agreement. If you want to destroy labor-management relations, we will show you how it ends." (Source: Seoul Economic Daily)
- Union called deployment "unwelcome from workers' perspective"
- Union specifically cited the sub-$320K price as a threat: cheaper than 2 years' wages
- "This is a good excuse for capitalists who seek to maximize profits from a long-term viewpoint"

### Supply Chain
- Korea's humanoid supply chain added $68 billion in market value as auto and IT parts makers pivot to robotics (Source: KED Global, 2026-05-28)
- Companies pivoting: Hyundai Mobis (shedding bumper business for robotics), HL Mando (actuators for Spot, targeting Tesla Optimus), LG Innotek (vision sensors for Atlas), Samsung/Rainbow Robotics partnership
- LG Energy Solution developing robot-dedicated batteries for Chinese and US humanoid makers
- Korea positioned as alternative to China in global robot supply chains

### Data Revenue Angle
- Hyundai aims to turn robot factory into "data gold mine" with $2.7 billion annual profit potential (Source: KED Global)
- Robots as data-gathering platforms, not just labor substitutes

### Broader Investment
- Hyundai Motor Group pledged $86.7 billion (125.2 trillion won) investment in Korea through 2030 for global robotics hub
- In US: $26 billion investment through 2028, creating 25,000 new direct jobs
- Currently supports 570,000 jobs in US (per CEO José Muñoz at 2026 NY Auto Show)
- Two US plants: Montgomery, Alabama (4,200 employees, since 2005) and HMGMA Georgia (1,400+ employees, opened Oct 2024)
- HMGMA capacity: 300,000 vehicles/year
- Milan Kovac (ex-Tesla VP robotics) hired as Boston Dynamics director

### BLS Manufacturing Wage Data (May 2025)
- Goods-producing, production workers: ~$32.28/hour average
- Manufacturing average weekly earnings: ~$1,317 (production workers)
- Annual: ~$32.28 × 2,080 hours = ~$67,142
- Total compensation (including benefits): ~$48.97/hour for goods-producing = ~$101,858/year
- Two-year total comp: ~$203,716

### Competitor Context
- Figure AI: F03 robot, deployed at Catalyst Brands, 200 hours, 250K packages, zero failures
- EngineAI: 10,000/year production target
- 1X NEO: Sold 10,000 robots in 5 days (teleoperated)
- BMW: 2 robots, 30,000 cars, $8.67 each — small-scale experiment
- Tesla Optimus: production timeline unclear, Korean battery suppliers
- Boston Dynamics-Toyota humanoid partnership announced

### Hyundai Financials
- Revenue (TTM Q1 2026): $131.7 billion
- Market cap: ~$109 billion
- Employees (Hyundai Motor Co): 72,759

## Original Contribution Angle
**Robot-per-worker ratio calculation**: 25,000 robots vs. current US manufacturing workforce. HMGMA has ~1,400 employees for 300,000 vehicles/year. If 25,000 robots deploy across global Hyundai/Kia factories (not just US), what's the per-plant density? What does "augmentation" vs "replacement" look like at these numbers?

**Cost comparison math**: Atlas at <$320K vs. total compensation $102K/year per manufacturing worker. Two-year payback if the robot replaces one worker. But robots run 24/7 (3 shifts) = equivalent of 3 workers = payback in ~8 months if utilization is maximized. Union's fear is economically rational.

**Scale gap**: Boston Dynamics currently makes ~4 robots/month (per our existing article). 30,000/year = 2,500/month = 625× current production. That's the bridge they need to cross in <2 years.
