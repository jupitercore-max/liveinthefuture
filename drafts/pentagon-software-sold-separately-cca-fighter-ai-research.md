# Research: The Pentagon Just Separated the Brain from the Body of a Fighter Jet

## Core Thesis
On June 17, 2026, the US Air Force awarded production contracts for Collaborative Combat Aircraft (CCA) — AI-piloted drone wingmen. But the real news isn't the robots. It's the procurement model. For the first time in 78 years of Air Force history, the Pentagon is buying the airframe and the autonomy software from *different vendors*. Two companies build the body (General Atomics FQ-42, Anduril FQ-44). Six companies compete to build the brain (Anduril, General Atomics, Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, RTX/Collins Aerospace, Shield AI). The Air Force calls it "software sold separately."

This is the original analysis angle: what does decoupling software from hardware mean economically for the defense industrial base?

## Original Calculation: The Force Multiplication Math

### Traditional Squadron vs. CCA-Augmented Squadron

**Traditional Squadron (24 F-35s):**
- Aircraft acquisition: 24 × $110.3M = $2.647B
- Pilot training: 24 × $13.1M = $314M
- Annual operating: 24 × $6.6M = $158M/yr
- 5-year operating: $792M
- **5-year total: ~$3.75B for 24 combat platforms**

**CCA-Augmented Squadron (12 F-35s + 48 CCAs, 1:4 ratio):**
- F-35 acquisition: 12 × $110.3M = $1.324B
- CCA acquisition: 48 × $30M = $1.44B
- Pilot training: 12 × $13.1M = $157M
- F-35 annual operating: 12 × $6.6M = $79M/yr
- CCA annual operating: 48 × ~$2M (estimate, lower maintenance, no pilot life support) = $96M/yr
- 5-year operating: $876M
- **5-year total: ~$3.80B for 60 combat platforms**

**Result:** Approximately the same 5-year cost. 2.5× the platforms. Zero additional pilots at risk.

### The Software Marketplace Calculation

If CCA autonomy software = 15-25% of unit cost:
- At $30M per CCA: software = $4.5M-$7.5M per airframe
- For 1,000 CCAs: $4.5B-$7.5B total software market
- 6 competing vendors vs. traditional model where one prime captures everything
- Historical precedent: when DoD separated engines (Great Engine War, 1984 — GE vs P&W for F-16), engine unit costs dropped ~20% over 10 years

### What This Means for the Industrial Base
- Airframe vendors can be swapped (both FQ-42 and FQ-44 in production)
- Software vendors compete continuously
- No single vendor lock-in (contrast with F-35/Lockheed monopoly)
- DoD gains negotiating leverage it hasn't had since the Great Engine War
