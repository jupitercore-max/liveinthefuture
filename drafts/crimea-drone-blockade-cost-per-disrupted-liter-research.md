# Research Notes: Crimea Drone Blockade — Cost Per Disrupted Liter

## Article #437 | 🛡️ Defense | Elena Vasquez
## Slug: crimea-drone-blockade-cost-per-disrupted-liter

## Thesis
Ukraine has achieved a de facto blockade of Crimea — a peninsula of 2.4 million people with zero refining capacity — using $50,000 mid-range drones to systematically close all three land supply corridors. The original calculation: Ukraine is disrupting approximately 1 million liters of daily fuel supply to Crimea at a cost of roughly $0.38 per denied liter. This is history's cheapest blockade and a proof-of-concept that drones can accomplish what previously required a navy.

## Key Data Points

### Crimea Fuel Crisis (June 2026)
- **June 21, 2026:** ALL civilian fuel sales halted across Crimea — cash, card, and coupons (Reuters, Aksyonov statement)
- **May 31, 2026:** Fuel rationing first imposed — coupons only, 20L/vehicle/week (Reuters)
- **June 4, 2026:** Cash sales suspended, no new coupons issued (Reuters)
- **3km queues** reported at Sevastopol gas stations (Reuters)
- **Black market prices:** double market rate (NPR/AP)
- Gas siphoned from parked cars (social media reports)
- Public transport stopped in Simferopol on June 6 (social media)
- Tourist hotline launched for stranded visitors
- "Worst energy crisis since 2014 annexation" (AP)

### Three Supply Corridors — All Degraded
1. **Chonhar Bridge (R-280):** Freight traffic declined **71% in two weeks** (CNN, Robert Brovdi, commander of Ukraine's drone forces). Bridge suffered "critical damage" from drone strikes June 6-7 and June 12-13.
2. **Northern bridges (Henichesk-Arabat Spit, Armiansk):** Multiple bridges hit. Satellite imagery shows "critical damage" to North Crimean Canal bridge near Armiansk. Russia building pontoon bridges and earthen bypass crossings. (RBC-Ukraine, CyberBoroshno)
3. **Kerch Bridge/Strait:** Closed 9+ hours June 21 after drone strikes on both sides. Ferry traffic suspended. Oil terminal at Chushka set ablaze. Russia already restricted hazardous cargo (fuel) on bridge. (Reuters, The Times)

### Drone Systems Used
- **FP-1/FP-2 (Fire Point):** 300/day production (up from 30/month in 2023), ~€50,000-55,000 each, 1,600km range (FP-1), responsible for 60% of all Ukrainian drone strikes. (Irish Times, Militarnyi, AP)
- **Behemoth (Culver Aerospace/GLEFA):** 300km range, 75kg payload, tandem warhead (EFP + thermobaric), Starlink comms, low-altitude autonomous flight. New mid-range system. (sUAS News)
- **Hornet-type drones:** Mid-range logistics strike drones used on "Road of Death" supply convoys (The Sun, CEPA)
- **FPV drones:** ~$500-1000 each, used in Spiderweb and logistics attacks

### Broader Russia Refining Impact
- **IEA (June 17):** Russian crude production dropped to 8.7M bpd, 10% below May target. IEA cut Russia forecast by 200,000 bpd. "Due to continued strikes on oil infrastructure." (Reuters)
- **>20% of Russian refining capacity offline** (WSJ, citing outside analysts)
- **IEA:** "This level of disruption is unprecedented in the history of the Russia-Ukraine conflict" (WSJ)
- **Moscow refinery struck:** supplies 40% of Moscow's fuel. Combined with Taneco refinery damage = ~600,000 bpd offline (OilPrice)
- **Russia rationing gasoline in Moscow** — purchase limits at Rosneft, Lukoil, Tatneft stations (OilPrice)
- Russia banned gasoline exports through end of July
- Russia reportedly importing gasoline from Asia (OilPrice)

### Operation Spiderweb Precedent (June 1, 2025)
- 117 FPV drones, smuggled into Russia by truck, struck 5 airfields simultaneously
- 41 aircraft hit (including Tu-95, Tu-22M3, Tu-160, A-50)
- NATO confirmed 34% of Russian strategic aviation fleet destroyed
- Estimated equipment value destroyed: **$7 billion** (Ukrainian SBU)
- Cost of 117 FPV drones: ~$117,000 (at ~$1,000/drone)
- Asymmetric ratio: **~60,000:1**
- (Source: Finabel, Militarnyi, CEPA, Army Recognition)

### Production Scale
- Ukraine drone production surging in 2026:
  - Reconnaissance drones: +441% vs all of 2025
  - Mid-strike drones: +312%
  - Deep-strike drones: +53%
  - Fiber-optic FPV: +179%
  - (Source: Frontier14, Ukrainian defense ministry)
- Fire Point: Full autonomy strategy — no Chinese or US components

## Original Calculation: Cost Per Disrupted Liter

### Crimea Fuel Demand Estimate
- Population: ~2.4 million (Russian census claim; includes Sevastopol)
- No refining capacity on peninsula (Ukrainian Week expert confirmation)
- Russia per-capita motor gasoline consumption: ~36M tonnes/year for 146M population = ~246 kg/person/year = 0.67 kg/day = ~0.93 liters/person/day (using gasoline density 0.72 kg/L). Wait — let me recalculate.
- Actually: 36M tonnes/year ÷ 146M people = 246.6 kg/person/year ÷ 365 = 0.675 kg/day ÷ 0.72 kg/L = 0.94 liters/person/day. But this is all motor gasoline including commercial/industrial.
- Per-capita civilian consumption more like 0.5-0.6 liters/day accounting for commercial.
- Crimea civilian demand: 2.4M × 0.55 L/day = **~1.32M liters/day**
- Military garrison: estimated 20,000-40,000 troops + associated vehicle fleet. Military consumption hard to estimate but significant. Conservatively 200,000-400,000 liters/day.
- **Total estimated Crimea fuel demand: ~1.5-1.7 million liters/day**

### Supply Disruption Estimate
Pre-crisis supply: ~1.7M liters/day via three corridors
- Corridor 1 (Chonhar/R-280): ~40% = 680,000 L/day → 71% decline = 197,000 L/day remaining. Disrupted: 483,000 L/day
- Corridor 2 (Northern bridges): ~25% = 425,000 L/day → ~80% decline (critical damage, pontoon alternatives) = 85,000 L/day remaining. Disrupted: 340,000 L/day
- Corridor 3 (Kerch): ~35% = 595,000 L/day → ~50% decline (intermittent closures, hazmat restricted) = 297,500 L/day remaining. Disrupted: 297,500 L/day
- **Total remaining supply: ~579,500 L/day (34% of pre-crisis)**
- **Total disrupted: ~1,120,500 L/day**
- Cross-check: rationing at 20L/person/week = 2.86L/person/day × 2.4M = 6.8M L/day. That's way more than supply. But coupons "snapped up immediately" and many couldn't get fuel, so effective supply was well below even the ration allocation. Full halt June 21 confirms supply essentially collapsed.
- Better: If fuel stations halted all sales, disruption = 100% of civilian supply = ~1.32M liters/day as of June 21.

### Drone Campaign Cost Estimate
The Crimea logistics campaign uses a mix of drone types:
- Infrastructure strikes (bridges, depots): FP-2 type, ~$55,000 each, 3-5/day = $165,000-$275,000
- Convoy strikes (fuel trucks, military vehicles): Hornet/mid-range FPV, ~$5,000-15,000 each, 10-20/day = $50,000-$300,000
- **Estimated daily expenditure on Crimea-specific logistics strikes: $250,000-$500,000**
- **Midpoint: ~$375,000/day**

### The Math
- Disrupted fuel: ~1,000,000 liters/day (conservative, using 1.12M as ceiling)
- Daily drone cost: ~$375,000
- **Cost per disrupted liter: $375,000 / 1,000,000 = $0.375 ≈ $0.38/liter**

### Context
- Russian retail gasoline price: ~60 rubles/liter ≈ $0.65/liter
- Crimea black market: ~$1.30/liter (double market)
- Ukraine is disrupting fuel at **58% of its retail value**
- For every $1 spent on drones, ~$1.73 in retail fuel value is denied

### Historical Comparison: Blockade Costs
- **US submarine campaign vs Japan (1942-45):** ~$1.2B spent (1945 dollars, ~$22B in 2026). Denied ~7M tons of imports/year by 1945. Rough cost: ~$3,143/ton or ~$2.36/liter equivalent. *Ukraine's drone blockade is ~6× cheaper per unit disrupted.*
- **US naval blockade of Cuba (1962):** 183 ships, 30,000 personnel, 13 days. Cost ~$200M (2026 dollars). No cargo was actually interdicted (ships turned back). Cost per actual interdicted unit: effectively infinite.
- **Israeli naval blockade of Gaza (2007-present):** ~$500M/year operating cost for 1.8M people. ~$278/person/year.
- **Ukraine drone blockade of Crimea: ~$375K/day = ~$137M/year for 2.4M people = ~$57/person/year.** That's **5× cheaper than Israel's Gaza blockade** on a per-capita basis, using drones instead of a navy.
