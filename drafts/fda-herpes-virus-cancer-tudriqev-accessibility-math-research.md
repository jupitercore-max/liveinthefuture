# Research: FDA Approves Genetically Modified Herpes Virus for Cancer — Tudriqev Accessibility Math

## Story Summary
On August 7, 2026, the FDA approved Replimune's Tudriqev (RP1 / vusolimogene oderparepvec) — a genetically modified herpes simplex virus engineered to infect and kill cancer cells while alerting the immune system to attack the cancer elsewhere. It's approved in combination with Bristol Myers Squibb's Opdivo (nivolumab) for advanced melanoma in patients who progressed on anti-PD-1 therapy.

This approval came on the THIRD attempt after two FDA rejections (July 2025, April 2026). The company pushed the White House to re-examine the drug. An advisory panel voted 10-to-3 in favor in late July 2026.

## Kill Test — Original Calculation
**Treatment accessibility gap between Tudriqev and Amtagvi (its only competitor).**

Amtagvi (Iovance's TIL cell therapy, approved Feb 2024) requires:
- Major surgery to harvest tumor tissue
- Ex vivo manufacturing (weeks of cell expansion)
- Lymphodepletion chemotherapy
- Infusion + ICU monitoring
- Only ~30 certified treatment centers in the US can administer it
- Price: ~$515,000 per treatment course
- 2025 sales: ~$220M

Tudriqev requires:
- Intratumoral injection (in-office procedure)
- No surgery, no manufacturing delay, no lymphodepletion
- Can be administered at any oncology center with injection capability (~1,500+ centers)
- Price: TBD (not announced yet as of Aug 9, 2026)

**Nobody has calculated the throughput ceiling.** At 30 Amtagvi centers processing ~50-70 patients/year each (manufacturing + scheduling constraints), max throughput is ~1,500-2,100 patients/year. The addressable population is ~14,000+/year. Amtagvi structurally cannot reach most eligible patients.

## Key Data Points

### Efficacy
- **Tudriqev + Opdivo (IGNYTE trial, FDA label):** 24.2% ORR, 14.1 months median duration of response
- **Tudriqev + Opdivo (earlier IGNYTE data cut, April 2026):** 34% ORR, 24.8 months median DOR (per-protocol/evaluable population)
- **Amtagvi (lifileucel, C-144-01 trial):** 31.4% ORR, 75% of responses lasting 12+ months
- **Favorable safety profile** for Tudriqev — 2 patients discontinued for side effects (nausea, injection site)

### Epidemiology
- 112,000 new melanoma cases in US per year (2026 estimate)
- ~8,500 melanoma deaths annually
- Melanoma is 5th most common cancer in the US
- ~50% of advanced melanoma patients progress on PD-1 immunotherapy
- Addressable population for post-PD-1 therapies: ~14,000-20,000 patients/year

### Regulatory Timeline
1. **July 2025:** FDA issues first Complete Response Letter (CRL) — rejects BLA, cites single-arm IGNYTE study lacking control group
2. **October 2025:** Replimune resubmits BLA (Class II resubmission), PDUFA date April 10, 2026
3. **April 10, 2026:** FDA issues SECOND CRL — rejects again. CEO: "Not because the medicine failed. Because the system did." Company threatens to "eliminate jobs" and "scale back U.S. manufacturing."
4. **June 26, 2026:** FDA accepts THIRD resubmission (Class 1 response), expedited review, goal date August 2, 2026
5. **Late July 2026:** FDA advisory panel votes 10-to-3 that trial data is clinically meaningful
6. **August 7, 2026:** FDA grants accelerated approval. Confirmatory Phase 3 study ongoing.

### Science
- RP1 is engineered HSV-1 (herpes simplex virus type 1)
- Armed with fusogenic protein (GALV-GP R-) and GM-CSF
- Mechanism: injected directly into tumors → virus replicates selectively in cancer cells → kills cells → releases tumor antigens → GALV-GP R- causes cell fusion (spreads killing to neighboring cancer cells) → GM-CSF recruits immune cells → systemic anti-tumor immune response activated
- Dual mechanism: LOCAL (direct viral killing) + SYSTEMIC (immune activation against distant metastases)
- This is the second approved oncolytic virus therapy (after Amgen's Imlygic/T-VEC, also HSV-based, approved 2015 but commercially failed)
