# Research Notes: NASA Moon Base CLPS Delivery Gap

## Core Thesis
NASA's $30B Moon Base plan requires 77 commercial lunar missions in 10 years. The Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) program has completed 4 launches in 7 years, with only 1 fully successful. That's a 13× acceleration needed — and it needs to happen at 30% lower cost per mission.

## Key Data Points

### NASA Moon Base Plan (announced March 24, 2026; contracts May 26, 2026)
- Total budget: ~$30B over next decade
- Lunar Gateway CANCELED — redirected to surface-first approach
- Location: Shackleton Connecting Ridge, lunar south pole
- Three phases:
  - Phase 1 (2026-2029): Robotic scouts, 25 missions, 21 landings
  - Phase 2 (2029-2032): Early infrastructure, 60 tons across 24 landings, JAXA pressurized rover
  - Phase 3 (2032-2036): Permanent base, fission reactors, water extraction, 8,000 kg cargo every 4 weeks

### May 26 Contract Awards (~$1B total)
- Astrolab: $219M for CLV-1 (Crewed Lunar Vehicle)
- Lunar Outpost: $220M for Pegasus rover (with GM/Goodyear)
- Blue Origin: Blue Moon Mark 1 Endurance (Moon Base 1, fall 2026)
- Firefly: Elytra Dark orbiter for reconnaissance
- Both rover companies' vehicles must survive 10 years on lunar surface

### Moon Base Missions This Year (2026)
1. Moon Base 1: Blue Origin Blue Moon Mark 1, fall 2026 — BUT New Glenn pad exploded May 28
2. Moon Base 2: Astrobotic Griffin + Astrolab FLIP rover, SpaceX Falcon Heavy
3. Moon Base 3: Intuitive Machines Nova-C Trinity + Lunar Vertex science package

### CLPS Track Record (2018-2026)
- 14 missions awarded to 5 vendors
- 4 missions launched
- Average delay: 14 months per mission (NASA OIG 2024 report)
- 1 vendor (Masten Space Systems) went bankrupt
- Results:
  - Peregrine (Astrobotic, Jan 2024): Propellant failure, never reached Moon
  - Odysseus/IM-1 (Intuitive Machines, Feb 2024): Landed but tipped over, partial success
  - Blue Ghost (Firefly, 2025): Only fully successful mission
  - Griffin (Astrobotic): Not yet flown
- Success rate: 1 fully successful / 4 launched = 25%

### CLPS 2.0 Requirements
- 77 missions over next decade (7.7/year needed)
- ~$91M per mission (down from $129M average under CLPS 1.0 = 30% cost reduction)
- vs CLPS 1.0: 4 launches in 7 years = 0.57/year
- Acceleration needed: 7.7 / 0.57 = 13.5×

### ISS Assembly Comparison
- ISS assembly: 37 flights over 13 years (1998-2011) = 2.85 flights/year
- ISS total cost: ~$150B (construction + 25 years operations)
- ISS habitable volume: ~388 cubic meters pressurized
- Cost per cubic meter: ~$387M
- Used proven launch vehicles (Shuttle, Proton, Soyuz)

### Apollo Program Comparison
- JFK speech 1961 → landing 1969 = 8 years
- 11 crewed Apollo flights, many unmanned tests
- Peak NASA budget: 4.4% of federal budget (1966)
- Current NASA budget: ~0.5% of federal budget

### Moon Base at $30B
- $30B / 10 years = $3B/year
- NASA's 2026 budget request: ~$25.4B total
- Moon Base = ~12% of total NASA budget
- vs Apollo peak spending: 4.4% of entire federal budget

## Original Calculation: CLPS Required Success Rate

If NASA needs 77 successful deliveries, and history shows:
- CLPS 1.0 full success rate: 25% (1/4)
- At 25% success rate → need 308 launch attempts for 77 successes
- At 50% success rate → need 154 attempts
- At 75% success rate → need 103 attempts
- At 90% success rate → need 86 attempts

For 77 missions in 10 years at different success rates:
- At 90%: 8.6 attempts/year (currently 0.57/year = 15× current rate)
- At 75%: 10.3 attempts/year (18× current rate)

## Strongest Counterargument
- CLPS 1.0 was the learning phase — first-flight failures are expected
- Blue Ghost's success shows the industry IS maturing
- SpaceX Falcon Heavy is proven (unlike New Glenn)
- Commercial incentives now aligned: $7B+ in CLPS 2.0 contracts
- Isaacman brings commercial accountability as administrator
- Multiple competing vendors creates redundancy
- Past programs (Apollo, ISS) also had early failures but scaled successfully

## Limitations
- We don't have CLPS 2.0 contract details beyond the May 26 awards
- Habitable volume targets for Moon Base not disclosed
- Don't know how many of the 77 missions are cargo vs crewed
- Phase 3 technology (fission reactors, ISRU) is largely undemonstrated
- Budget figures may shift with future congressional appropriations
- China's timeline for its own lunar base creates political pressure that could force spending regardless of delivery readiness
