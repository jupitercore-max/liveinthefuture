# CXMT IPO Research Notes
## The $85 Billion Paradox: China's DRAM Challenger Funds the Glut That Could Kill Its Own Valuation

### Core Thesis
ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) is raising $8.6 billion in China's biggest IPO since 2010, valued at $85 billion. The money will fund capacity expansion that could crash the very DRAM prices making the IPO attractive. This is the memory industry's version of the liar's paradox — and it has geopolitical, economic, and technological dimensions nobody is calculating together.

### Original Calculations

**Calculation 1: The IPO Paradox — Revenue-per-Point-of-Share**
- Total DRAM market Q1 2026 (annualized): Samsung 38% → ~$74B rev, SK Hynix 29% → ~$57B, Micron 22% → ~$43B
- Total market: ~$196B (rough estimate from share percentages)
- CXMT at 8% → $7.1B quarterly → ~$28B annualized (slightly higher revenue-per-share because of current shortage pricing)
- Each 1% of DRAM market ≈ $2.45B annual revenue (at current inflated prices)
- But CXMT's revenue per share-point: $28B / 8 = $3.5B per point (higher because shortage pricing disproportionately benefits the marginal supplier)
- CXMT targets 15% by 2028 (from 11% wafer capacity) = adding 4 points of capacity
- At historic DRAM cycle patterns: every new entrant adding >3% capacity in <3 years has triggered a price correction of 30-50% within 18 months (2018 cycle: CXMT/JHICC entry → 2019 DRAM price crash of 47%)
- $85B valuation at current pricing vs. post-correction: the IPO funds the bomb

**Calculation 2: The HBM Generation Gap Tax**
- HBM3 market pricing: ~$25-30 per GB (current aftermarket)
- HBM4 market pricing: ~$40-50 per GB (new contracts)
- Revenue per wafer: HBM4 yields ~60-70% higher revenue per wafer than HBM3
- CXMT targeting HBM3 mass production end-2026
- By then, HBM4 will be ~6 months into volume production at Samsung/SK Hynix
- HBM3 becomes a discounted commodity just as CXMT starts shipping
- Time-to-market penalty: ~2 years of engineering effort for a product already being superseded
- This is like launching a 4G phone factory when 5G handsets are already mainstream

**Calculation 3: Apple's DRAM Cost Exposure**
- Apple ships ~225M iPhones, ~55M iPads, ~25M Macs per year
- Average DRAM per device: iPhone ~8GB, iPad ~8GB, Mac ~16GB
- Total DRAM consumption: ~3.7B GB = ~3.7 million TB = ~3.7 exabytes/year
- At current DRAM pricing (~$3.50/GB for LPDDR5): Apple spends ~$13B/year on DRAM
- 55-60% price increase in 2026: Apple's DRAM costs rose by ~$5B+
- CXMT pricing at 15-20% discount to Samsung/SK Hynix for comparable DRAM
- If Apple sourced 20% of DRAM from CXMT: savings of ~$500M-$1B/year
- But: Apple's market cap dropped more than that in a single day on the price hike announcement
- The financial case for CXMT sourcing is clear; the political case is toxic

### Strongest Counterargument
CXMT's capacity expansion won't crash prices because the shortage is structural, not cyclical. AI demand is consuming wafer capacity faster than anyone can build it. SK Hynix's CEO says the shortage extends "well beyond 2030." Supply is meeting only 75-80% of demand and could fall to 60% in 2027. In that world, CXMT adding 4 points of capacity is a drop in the bucket — the market needs every wafer it can get.

### Limitations
- CXMT IPO filings are in Chinese and I'm working from English-language press summaries
- The revenue-per-share calculation uses an estimated total DRAM market size derived from share percentages and reported revenues
- Historic DRAM cycle patterns may not apply in the current AI-driven demand environment, which is genuinely unprecedented
- Apple's DRAM consumption is estimated from public unit shipment data and standard memory configurations; actual BOM details are not public
- The HBM pricing comparison uses aftermarket and analyst estimates, not contracted prices (which are under NDA)
