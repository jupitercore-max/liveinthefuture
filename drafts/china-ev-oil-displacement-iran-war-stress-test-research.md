## Core Thesis
China's EV fleet just survived its first real oil shock. The country cut crude imports 41% in June 2026 without rationing — travel demand hit all-time highs. The original calculation: China's 40M+ EV fleet has a theoretical oil displacement capacity of ~2.3 million bpd, but measured displacement is ~150K bpd (JP Morgan) = ~7% utilization. Even at 7%, it absorbed the worst supply shock since 1973.

## Original Calculation: Displacement Utilization Rate

### Per-vehicle displacement capacity:
- Average Chinese passenger car: 18,000 km/yr, 7.82 L/100 km = 1,407.6 L gasoline/yr
- 1 barrel crude → ~42% gasoline yield → ~66.8 L gasoline per barrel
- Each ICE car requires: 1,407.6 / 66.8 = 21.1 barrels crude/yr
- Per day: 21.1 / 365 = 0.058 bpd per vehicle

### Fleet-level theoretical displacement:
- 40 million EVs × 0.058 bpd = 2,320,000 bpd = 2.3 million bpd
- For context: Kuwait produces 2.4 million bpd

### Measured displacement:
- JP Morgan: 150,000 bpd gasoline demand decline in 2026
- Displacement utilization: 150,000 / 2,320,000 = 6.5% → ~7%

### Why utilization is low:
1. Not all 40M EVs replaced an ICE 1:1 (some are additional vehicles)
2. Many are PHEVs that still use some gasoline
3. Not all private EVs are driven at the 18,000 km/yr average
4. The fleet is still growing — newest EVs haven't accumulated full-year mileage

### Why this matters:
- Even at 7% utilization, China absorbed the worst oil shock since 1973
- As taxi electrification hits 90% (Greenpeace 2035), utilization rises dramatically
- The displacement capacity grows by ~16.5M new EVs per year at current sales rate
- By 2028 (fleet ~73M), theoretical displacement = 4.2M bpd (≈ Iraq's entire output)

### Strongest counterargument:
Most of the 41% import decline isn't from EVs — it's from stockpile drawdowns (1.2-1.3B barrels of pre-war reserves), refinery run rate cuts, and export curbs. The EV fleet's contribution is the 150K bpd structural demand reduction, not the 4.9M bpd import drop. China still needs massive oil volumes.

### Limitations:
- The 7.82 L/100km fuel consumption figure is from a 2018 study; newer ICE cars are more efficient
- "40 million EVs" includes PHEVs which still consume some gasoline
- JP Morgan's 150K bpd figure is a projection, not a directly measured displacement
- Stockpile drawdowns are the largest factor in the June import decline, not EVs
- The 42% gasoline yield per barrel is an industry average; Chinese refinery configurations may differ
