## Novel Contribution: The Three-Body Problem of iPhone Manufacturing
Nobody has run this specific calculation: What does it actually cost Apple per-iPhone to manufacture in each scenario?

1. **China + tariffs:** BOM ~$570 (iPhone 16 Pro Max est.), assembly ~$15-20, tariff at 10% = ~$57-59. Total per-unit tariff burden: ~$57.
2. **India manufacturing:** Assembly costs reportedly 10-15% higher than China due to lower labor productivity and supply chain immaturity. Assembly ~$17-23. But no tariff if India rate stays paused/negotiated to 0%.
3. **US manufacturing:** Tim Cook has said US assembly would add $100+ per device. Completely uneconomical.

The calculation: At 55 million units/year from India, saving ~$57/unit in tariffs = ~$3.1B annual savings. Vs. $2.4B investment in Indian factories. The India shift pays for itself in under a year at current tariff rates.

But the catch: India tariff of 26% (if un-paused) would cost MORE than China's 10% blanket. Apple is betting on India getting a favorable trade deal. If that bet fails, they've built $2.4B in factories in the wrong country.

## Strongest Counterargument
Apple's India bet might be brilliant even if tariffs change, because it eliminates single-country dependency on China. Supply chain resilience has value beyond tariff arithmetic. COVID-era shutdowns in Zhengzhou cost Apple $4-8B in delayed shipments. Diversification is insurance.

## Limitations
- Assembly is final-stage only; ~70% of iPhone component value still comes from suppliers in China, Japan, South Korea
- India's component ecosystem is nascent; pre-assembled component sets are imported from China
- Labor productivity gap data is proprietary; estimates based on industry analysts
- Supreme Court Section 122 challenge could change everything
