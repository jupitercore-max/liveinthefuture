## Key Data Points

### Rentosertib (ISM001-055) — Insilico Medicine
- **First drug where AI discovered BOTH target (TNIK) AND molecule** — no human hypothesis guided either step
- Target ID platform: PandaOmics (multi-omics, deep feature synthesis, causality inference)
- Molecule design: Chemistry42 generated 78,000 virtual TNIK inhibitors, filtered to 60 for synthesis
- **Hit rate: 16.7%** vs **0.1%** for traditional high-throughput screening (167× improvement)
- **Timeline: 30 months** from novel target to Phase I vs **6-8 years** traditional
- Phase 2a trial: 71 patients (GENESIS-IPF trial)
- **60mg dose: +98.4 mL FVC improvement** vs **-62.3 mL decline in placebo** (>160 mL difference)
- Results published in **Nature Medicine, June 2025**
- Disease: Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF) — ~5 million people worldwide, kills within 2-5 years
- IND clearance for inhaled version (13th AI-driven candidate from Insilico pipeline)
- Phase III regulatory discussions underway as of early 2026
- Source: Nature Medicine (June 2025), AJMC, Managed Healthcare Executive, humAI.blog

### Zasocitinib (TAK-279) — Schrödinger/Nimbus/Takeda
- Already in Phase III for psoriasis and inflammatory conditions
- Different approach: quantum mechanical simulations + ML (physics-based, not generative)
- Schrödinger's role: predicting precise molecular binding interactions
- Acquired by Takeda
- Phase III readouts expected 2026
- Source: humAI.blog

## Novel Contribution (Original Calculation)
**Pipeline probability analysis:** If 173 AI compounds are in trials with 80-90% Phase I rates, what's the probability at least one gets approved? Traditional approval rate from Phase I to market is ~10%. If AI improves this to even 15%, the expected number of approvals from 173 candidates is ~26. The probability of ZERO approvals from 173 candidates at 15% per-phase-adjusted success: essentially zero. The question isn't IF an AI drug gets approved — it's HOW MANY and HOW FAST.

**Cost comparison:** Traditional drug: $2B over 10-15 years. Insilico's rentosertib: 30 months to Phase I, hit rate 167× higher. If we assume the per-compound cost of screening drops proportionally to the hit rate improvement (0.1% → 16.7%), the preclinical screening cost drops from ~$500M to ~$3M. Even if clinical trials cost the same (~$1-1.5B), the total is half or less.

**The real breakthrough metric:** Not just speed (30 months vs 6-8 years) but the ability to find targets humans never hypothesized. TNIK wasn't suspected of being involved in fibrosis. This means AI can explore biological hypothesis space that no human research program would have reached — opening entirely new therapeutic categories.

## Kill Test
✅ Passes — This is a genuine inflection point. We've moved from "AI can find drug candidates faster" to "AI-discovered drugs actually work in humans, and we now have statistical power (173 compounds) to evaluate whether the early advantages translate."

## 10-Star Test
✅ "173 AI-designed drugs are in human trials right now, and the first one where AI picked both the disease target and the molecule showed lung function IMPROVEMENT in a disease that's always been one-way decline. 15 more hit Phase III this year."

## Strongest Counterargument
Phase III is where 50%+ of all drug candidates fail, regardless of how they were discovered. AI's advantages in speed and hit rate may not translate to Phase III success because the hard part isn't finding candidates — it's navigating the complexity of human biology over long treatment periods. The 80-90% Phase I rate may simply mean AI is better at finding safe molecules, not effective ones. Until a Phase III readout arrives, the "AI advantage" thesis is extrapolation from early-stage data.

## Limitations
- Phase 2a sample size is small (71 patients)
- IPF is one disease — generalizability unclear
- Most "AI-discovered" compounds used AI for only part of the process (not end-to-end like rentosertib)
- The 173 figure includes drugs where AI was used only for optimization, not discovery
- Cost data for AI drug development is mostly self-reported by AI companies
- No independent verification of hit rate claims
