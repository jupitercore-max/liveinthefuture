# Research: Tesla Killed Its Oldest Car Line to Build Robots. It Has 6,567x to Go.

## Topic
Tesla ended Model S and Model X production on May 10, 2026 after 14 and 11 years respectively, converting the Fremont factory lines to manufacture Optimus Gen 3 humanoid robots. Target: 1 million units/year. Last year, Tesla shipped 150 robots. The entire United States shipped ~450. China shipped ~18,000. This is the story of the largest manufacturing bet in robotics history, and the production gap it needs to close.

## 10-Star Test
This fundamentally reframes what Tesla is: not a car company pivoting to robots, but a company betting that a robot factory is worth more than the car line that proved EVs were viable. The Model S was the iPhone moment for EVs. Now its factory makes robots. That's a 10-star story.

## Novel Contribution
1. **Production ramp math:** Tesla shipped 150 Optimus in 2025. Target is 1M/year. That's 6,667x. Tesla went from 0 cars (2012) to 1M cars (~2020) = 8 years. At that same ramp rate, 1M robots wouldn't arrive until ~2034. Musk says 2026-2027.
2. **Revenue substitution calculation:** Model S/X lines produced roughly 15-20K vehicles/year at ~$80K average = $1.2-1.6B revenue. If Optimus at $25K × 1M = $25B, that's a 16-20x revenue upgrade from the same factory floor. But margin structure is unknown.
3. **US-China production gap:** US shipped ~450 humanoid robots in 2025. China shipped ~18,000. That's a 40:1 ratio. In EVs, the ratio was never this lopsided — Tesla was the global leader from 2018-2022. In humanoid robots, the US starts behind.
4. **Figure AI comparison:** Figure AI went from 1 robot/day to 1 robot/hour in 120 days (24x). At that exponential rate, 1M/year requires roughly 4 more 24x doublings. That's optimistic but not impossible.

## Counterargument (Strongest)
The strongest case against Tesla's robot bet: Tesla promised 10,000 Optimus units in 2025 and delivered 150. That's a 98.5% miss. Musk's production timelines for everything from the Semi to the Cybertruck to Full Self-Driving have been years late. Converting a car line to a robot line is not the same as scaling a car line — robots require fundamentally different supply chains (actuators, sensors, AI compute) than cars (batteries, motors, frames). The 1M target may be the new "Full Self-Driving by end of year" — perpetually 18 months away. Meanwhile, China isn't waiting.

## Limitations
- Tesla doesn't break out Model S/X production separately from Cybertruck in "Other Models" category
- Exact Optimus unit costs not disclosed; $20-30K is from analyst estimates and Musk statements
- China shipment numbers vary by source (13,000-18,000 for 2025)
- "Shipped" vs "deployed in production" vs "sold" — definitions vary across companies
- Figure AI production rate is self-reported; independent verification limited
- Factory conversion timeline (when Optimus lines actually start producing) not confirmed
