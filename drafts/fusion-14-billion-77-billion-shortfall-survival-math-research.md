# Research Notes: Fusion Industry $77B Funding Gap Survival Math

## Topic
The fusion industry just hit a record $4.48B annual investment (FIA 2026 report, July 2026). Total: $14.2B since tracking began in 2021. 56 private companies surveyed. But the median company still needs $700M more to reach pilot plant — total industry need: ~$77B. That's 5.4x what's been raised. A Nature Energy paper (Tang et al., April 2026) says fusion is "unlikely to become competitive" anyway.

Original calculation: Capital concentration + consolidation timeline math.

### 3. Tang et al., Nature Energy (April 2026) — "Fusion power unlikely to become competitive"
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-026-02022-9
- Authors: Lingxi Tang, Bessie Noll, Anurag Panda, Tobias S. Schmidt (ETH Zurich)
- Methodology: 28 semi-structured expert interviews covering magnetic and laser inertial fusion
- Key findings:
  - FPP experience rates (ERs): 2-8% (based on technologies with similar characteristics: large unit size, extreme design complexity, moderate-to-high customization)
  - FOAK cost estimates: $1,400 to $43,000 per kW (wide range)
  - At 5% ER projected from IQR of FOAK costs, fusion "likely to remain uncompetitive relative to other low-carbon electricity supply technologies"
  - Recommends policymakers re-evaluate public funding in fusion
  - Suggests directing funding to designs with more promising tech characteristics (i.e., higher potential ERs)

## ORIGINAL CALCULATION: Capital Concentration & Consolidation Timeline

### The Survival Math
56 companies need a total of ~$77B more (median: $700M each).
Industry raises ~$4.5B/year (2026 record).

**Capital concentration (from F4E data):**
Top 4 unicorns cumulative funding: €2.6B + €1.9B + €1.9B + €1.3B = €7.7B (~$8.5B USD)
Total industry cumulative: ~$14.2B
Top 4 = ~60% of cumulative capital

**Funding runway at current rates:**
- $77B needed ÷ $4.5B/year = 17.1 years to fully fund all 56 companies
- But capital concentrates: if top ~8 companies absorb 75% of annual inflows ($3.375B/year), the remaining 48 companies share $1.125B/year → $23.4M each/year
- At median $700M need and $23.4M/year: 30 years to fund each mid-tier company
- Most companies have 2-3 years of runway before needing new capital

**Predicted consolidation:** 
If non-leaders burn $20-50M/year (small team + modest hardware) and have $50-100M in the bank:
- Companies with <$100M raised have 2-4 years of runway
- At least 30-35 of the 56 companies will be acquired, merged, or shut down by 2031
- The industry will converge to 12-15 players, with 5-7 having funded a pilot reactor
- The rest become IP portfolios acquired by the survivors

### The PPA Implied Price (Speculative)
CFS ARC: 400MW, early 2030s
Eni deal: "$1B+ worth of power" (duration/capacity undisclosed)
If we assume 200MW share (half the plant, the other half is Google's) over 20 years at 80% CF:
- 200,000 kW × 8,760 h × 0.8 = 1,401,600 MWh/year
- Over 20 years: 28,032,000 MWh
- $1B / 28,032,000 = $35.68/MWh

That would be cheaper than any baseload source today. But the "$1B+" is likely a headline number and actual terms are more nuanced. Still, the fact that Eni and Google are signing PPAs means sophisticated energy buyers believe the economics will work.

### The ITER vs. Private Sector Speed Comparison
- ITER: Started 2006, will achieve first fusion reactions 2039 = 33 years. Cost: €25B+. Result: No electricity.
- CFS SPARC: Started construction ~2021, first plasma 2026 = ~5 years. Cost: ~$2.8B total raised. ARC commercial 2030s.
- Per-year cost: ITER ~€760M/year for 33 years. CFS ~$560M/year for 5 years of SPARC construction.
- ITER designed for Q=10 but won't generate electricity. CFS SPARC designed for Q>1, ARC for grid connection.

## Strongest Counterargument
Tang et al. (2026) in Nature Energy: Fusion's experience rates (2-8%) mean it will never catch up to renewables' cost curves. Even at the optimistic end of FOAK cost estimates, fusion needs an impossible number of cumulative doublings to reach competitive LCOE.

## Limitations
- FIA survey is self-reported by companies (selection bias)
- PPA pricing is speculative — neither CFS nor Eni disclosed terms
- China's $4.4B EUR is mostly state-funded (different dynamics than market competition)
- Nature Energy paper interviewed only 28 experts — fusion is highly heterogeneous
- Consolidation prediction assumes current funding velocity holds — if AI data center demand accelerates, the $4.5B/year figure could grow significantly
