# Research: The Fusion Investment–Experience Rate Gap

## Thesis
The private fusion industry has raised ~$9.77B as of July 2025 (FIA), with total global private sector funding reaching €13B (~$15.2B) by September 2025 (F4E Fusion Observatory). Helion alone is now valued at $15.5B after a $465M Series G in June 2026. Meanwhile, a peer-reviewed Nature Energy paper (Tang et al., April 2026) shows that fusion's experience rate — the % cost reduction per doubling of capacity — is likely 2-8%, with a justified median of 5%. Solar's experience rate is 23%. Battery storage has declined 93% since 2010. The math shows fusion can never catch up. This is the story nobody has run the numbers on.

## Original Calculations (Novel Contribution)

### 1. Doublings to parity
Nature Energy FOAK cost range: $1,400–$43,000/kW (interquartile). Geometric midpoint ~$7,800/kW.
Solar+storage firm LCOE (IRENA 2025): $54–82/MWh, implied capital cost ~$1,500–2,000/kW.

At 5% ER: doublings to reach $2,000/kW = ln(2000/7800) / ln(0.95) = 26.6 ≈ 27 doublings
At 8% ER (optimistic): doublings = ln(2000/7800) / ln(0.92) = 16.3 ≈ 16 doublings

Starting from one 400MW plant (CFS ARC-class):
- 27 doublings → 400MW × 2^27 = 53,687 GW needed (6× world's entire installed capacity of ~9,000 GW)
- 16 doublings → 400MW × 2^16 = 26,214 GW (3× world capacity)

This is physically absurd. Fusion can't build enough capacity to learn its way to cost parity.

### 2. The moving target problem
Solar doubles capacity every ~3 years. In the 30 years fusion might take to do 27 doublings:
- Solar does ~10 more doublings at 23% ER: $39/MWh × 0.77^10 = $2.85/MWh
- Even at a slower 5-year doubling: $39 × 0.77^6 = $8.15/MWh
- Solar+storage firm LCOE projected below $50/MWh by 2035 (IRENA)

The target is accelerating away from fusion faster than fusion can close the gap.

### 3. What ER would fusion need?
To be competitive with solar+storage's projected 2035 cost (~$50/MWh, ~$1,200/kW firm), starting from $7,800/kW, with 10 doublings (aggressive 20-year buildout):
$1,200 = $7,800 × (1-ER)^10
ER = 16.2%

Fusion would need a 16% experience rate — higher than wind (12%), approaching solar (23%). The Nature Energy justified estimate: 5%. Investors are implicitly betting on 3× the peer-reviewed evidence.

### 4. The $77B funding gap
FIA survey: 53 fusion companies need $77B total to reach pilot stage. $9.77B raised. At current funding rate ($2.5B/year), need ~27 more years of fundraising. Most of this produces demonstrations, not deployable plants.

### 5. Helion valuation sanity check
$15.5B valuation. $1.5B total raised. Zero revenue.
Microsoft contract: 50MW by 2028. At $100/MWh (generous premium): 50MW × 8,760h × 0.9 CF × $100 = $39.4M/year revenue.
Implied P/Revenue on first plant: ~393×.
For comparison: NextEra Energy (largest US utility) trades at ~6× revenue. Duke Energy: ~3×.
Implied assumption: Helion builds 60-100+ plants. At fusion's ER, those plants don't get meaningfully cheaper.

### 6. Implicit experience rate from investor behavior
Total private fusion capital: ~$15B. If investors model a 15-20% ER (venture capital standard for "tech"), fusion reaches $2,000/kW in 8-12 doublings = 3,200-1,638,400 GW. Still enormous but closer to VC growth assumptions. The disconnect: fusion plants aren't software. They're more like nuclear fission reactors (2% ER) than like solar panels (23% ER).
