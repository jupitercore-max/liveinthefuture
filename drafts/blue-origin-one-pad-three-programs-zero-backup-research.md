# Blue Origin New Glenn Pad Explosion — Research

## Event Details
- **Date:** May 28, 2026, shortly after 9 PM ET
- **Location:** Launch Complex 36 (LC-36), Cape Canaveral Space Force Station
- **Event:** Explosion during static fire (hot fire) test
- **Booster destroyed:** "No, It's Necessary" (had never flown before, named after Interstellar line)
- **Configuration:** Both first stage and second stage stacked on pad; payload (48 Amazon Leo satellites) NOT yet integrated
- **Injuries:** None reported
- **Pad damage:** "Practically destroyed" per industry source (Reuters). Strongback destroyed, one of two lightning conductor towers destroyed. Site burned for hours.
- **LC-36 cost:** $1 billion+ to build, took 8+ years
- **Debris warning:** Cape Canaveral Space Force Base warned debris could wash ashore; hazardous material, do not touch

## New Glenn Flight History
1. **NG-1 (Jan 16, 2025):** Debut flight. Success — reached orbit. Booster landing failed (engines didn't properly reignite)
2. **NG-2 (Nov 2025):** Success. Booster landed safely. Carried NASA Escapade mission to Mars
3. **NG-3 (Apr 19, 2026):** Mixed. Booster "Never Tell Me the Odds" landed successfully (2nd consecutive landing, reused booster). Upper stage BE-3U engine didn't reach full thrust → AST SpaceMobile BlueBird 7 satellite lost. FAA mishap investigation launched, cleared May 22, 2026.
4. **NG-4 (DESTROYED):** Was to carry 48 Amazon Leo satellites. Target launch ~June 4. Destroyed during static fire test May 28.

## Pad Situation — Single Point of Failure
- LC-36 is Blue Origin's ONLY New Glenn launch pad
- Vandenberg SLC-14 is planned future pad but NOT built yet
- $600M Florida expansion announced recently but focused on manufacturing, not pads
- Recovery timeline estimates: 6+ months (Reuters source), 12-15 months (experts), 1-2 years (The Times)
- NY Post/experts: 15 months "best case"

## SpaceX AMOS-6 Precedent
- **Date:** Sept 1, 2016, during static fire test at LC-40
- **Cause:** Breach in cryogenic helium system of second stage LOX tank
- **Recovery:** SpaceX resumed launches from LC-39A (backup pad) on Jan 14, 2017 (~4.5 months)
- **LC-40 repair:** Dec 15, 2017 (~15 months)
- **Key difference:** SpaceX had LC-39A as a second pad. Blue Origin has NO backup pad for New Glenn.

## Amazon Leo (Project Kuiper) Impact
- Amazon has ~300 satellites deployed out of 3,232 planned
- FCC original deadline: half (1,616 satellites) by July 30, 2026
- Amazon requested 24-month extension on Jan 30, 2026 → July 30, 2028
- Amazon booked 12 New Glenn launches + options for 15 more
- Also has 9 ULA Vulcan launches, Arianespace launches, 10+ Falcon 9 launches
- New Glenn carries ~48 Amazon Leo satellites per launch
- Falcon 9 carries ~24 (roughly half)
- Amazon manufacturing 30 satellites/week but launch capacity is bottleneck
- Amazon expects only ~700 satellites deployed by July 2026

## NASA/Artemis Impact
- Blue Moon Mark 1 uncrewed lunar lander scheduled for fall 2026 via New Glenn
- $468 million contract for two commercial rovers (pre-Artemis 4, targeting 2028)
- NASA announced moon base plans May 26, just 2 days before explosion
- NASA Admin Isaacman: will "assess near-term mission impacts"
- Lunar payloads are designed around specific launch vehicles; switching rockets is complicated

## BE-4 Engine Supply Chain Risk (Original)
- New Glenn first stage uses 7× BE-4 engines
- ULA's Vulcan rocket also uses BE-4 engines (supplied by Blue Origin)
- If explosion root cause traces to BE-4 engine → Vulcan could face stand-down too
- Barron's flagged: "ULA uses the same engines for its Vulcan rocket. That rocket might face delays"
- Amazon also has 9 Vulcan launches booked → double jeopardy

## Government Response
- U.S. Space Force and NRO affirmed commitment to Blue Origin on May 29
- Standing by newly awarded national security launch contract
- FAA noted static fire test was "not within scope of FAA licensed activities"

## Original Analysis: The Replacement Launch Math
- 12 booked New Glenn missions × 48 satellites = 576 satellites
- Replacing via Falcon 9 (24 sats each) = 24 missions needed
- SpaceX launches ~120 Falcon 9 per year; 24 additional = 20% manifest increase
- SpaceX manifest already crowded with Starlink + government
- ULA Vulcan also at risk if BE-4 is root cause
- Amazon's "largest launch procurement in history" bet on three rockets that all face problems:
  - New Glenn: grounded (pad destroyed)
  - Vulcan: shares BE-4 engine (potential supply chain risk)
  - Ariane 6: had its own delays
