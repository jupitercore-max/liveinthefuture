# Research: Casgevy CRISPR Gene Therapy — 69 Patients Cured, 100,000 Left

## Core Story
On July 1, 2026, the FDA expanded Casgevy (exagamglogene autotemcel) approval to children as young as age 2 for sickle cell disease (SCD) and transfusion-dependent beta thalassemia (TDT). This is the first CRISPR-based gene therapy approved for toddlers. Vertex claims ~5,500 additional children are now eligible. But the throughput math tells a darker story: at current treatment rates, reaching every eligible patient in the US would take centuries.

## Primary Sources

### 1. FDA Approval Documents
- FDA approval letter: July 1, 2026 (STN 125787)
- Prior approvals: Dec 8, 2023 (SCD, 12+), Jan 16, 2024 (TDT, 12+), Mar 18, 2026 (interim expansion)
- Approved in 53 days via Commissioner's National Priority Voucher (CNPV) pilot program
- Source: fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/casgevy

### 2. Vertex Pharmaceuticals Financial Disclosures
- **2024 full year:** 5 patients treated, $10.0M revenue
- **2025 full year:** 64 patients treated, $115.8M revenue
  - Q1: $14.2M, Q2: $30.4M, Q3: $16.9M, Q4: $54.3M (30 patients in Q4)
- **2026 guidance:** $500M+ combined from non-CF products (Casgevy + Journavx)
- **Treatment centers:** 40-50 authorized in US, targeting 75 globally
- **Patient funnel (Q4 2025 earnings call):** ~300 patients initiated, ~150 cell collections
- Source: Vertex Q4 2025 earnings call, Feb 2026; Q1 2026 earnings

### 3. CDC Epidemiological Data
- ~100,000 people with SCD in the United States
- >90% are Black/African American; 3-9% Hispanic/Latino
- 1 in 365 Black/African American births; 1 in 16,300 Hispanic births
- Life expectancy 20+ years shorter than average
- Source: cdc.gov/sickle-cell/data

### 4. Vertex Press Release (July 1, 2026)
- ~5,500 additional children in the US now eligible (ages 2-11)
- ~16,000 patients originally eligible (12+ with severe SCD)
- Total US eligible: ~21,500 (SCD with recurrent VOCs)
- Source: Vertex business wire, July 1, 2026

### 5. NEJM Clinical Trial Data (Published June 29, 2026)
- Pediatric trial (ages 5-11):
  - SCD: 8/8 evaluable patients VOC-free for 12+ months
  - TDT: 8/9 evaluable patients achieved transfusion independence
  - Median TDT transfusion independence: 20.1 months
- Age extrapolation granted for 2-4 year olds (no separate trial)
- Source: NEJM, June 29, 2026; HCA Healthcare press release

### 6. Health Economics Data
- Casgevy list price: $2.2M per treatment
- Lyfgenia (bluebird bio/Genetix) list price: $3.1M per treatment
- Cost-effectiveness threshold: $1.35M-$2.05M depending on perspective
  - At $100K-$150K WTP per QALY (standard health economics threshold)
  - Casgevy at $2.2M exceeds the upper bound of its own cost-effectiveness range
- Lifetime SCD healthcare costs: estimated ~$1.6M-$2.4M per patient
- Source: Cytotherapy 2025; PMC economics literature

### 7. Competitor Status
- Lyfgenia (Genetix/bluebird bio): $3.1M, approved Dec 2023
  - bluebird bio renamed to Genetix Biotherapeutics, faced financial distress
  - Safety concern: black box warning risk (myelodysplastic syndrome)
- No other CRISPR gene therapies approved for SCD
- Source: Reuters, BioSpace reporting

## Original Calculations

### Throughput Bottleneck Analysis
- **Total US eligible patients (post-expansion):** ~21,500 (16,000 12+ SCD + 5,500 2-11 SCD)
  - Note: TDT adds another ~1,000 eligible patients
- **2025 treatment rate:** 64 patients/year
- **At 2025 rate to treat all eligible US SCD patients:** 21,500 / 64 = 336 years
- **Even at 10x scaling (640/year):** 21,500 / 640 = 33.6 years
- **Vertex "line of sight" (~300 patients initiating/year):** 21,500 / 300 = 71.7 years

### Treatment Center Throughput
- 50 US authorized centers / 64 patients in 2025 = 1.28 patients per center per year
- Revenue per center: 1.28 × $2.2M = $2.8M/year
- For context: the conditioning chemotherapy, stem cell harvest, gene editing, and infusion cycle takes 4-6 months per patient
- Each center needs dedicated apheresis capacity, cell processing coordination, ICU beds for engraftment

### Revenue vs. Total Addressable Market
- Total US TAM: 21,500 × $2.2M = $47.3 billion
- 2025 capture: $115.8M / $47.3B = 0.24% of US TAM
- At 2025 pace to exhaust TAM: $47.3B / $115.8M = 408 years
- Vertex 2026 guidance (~$500M combined with Journavx) — if Casgevy is ~$300M of that: $300M / $47.3B = 0.63%

### The Age Economics
- A 2-year-old with SCD has ~70 years of disease burden ahead
- At $25,000-$35,000 annual SCD management costs, that's $1.75M-$2.45M lifetime
- $2.2M one-time cure vs. $1.75M-$2.45M lifetime management
- For a 2-year-old: the cure is roughly cost-neutral to cost-saving
- For a 30-year-old (40 years remaining): $1.0M-$1.4M lifetime cost — cure is cost-INEFFECTIVE
- Age at treatment dramatically shifts the cost-effectiveness calculation

### Demographic Equity Gap
- 90% of SCD patients are Black/African American
- Myeloablative conditioning requires: extended hospitalization, fertility preservation, months of recovery
- Average Black household income: ~$52,860 (Census 2024) vs. US median $80,610
- The patients who need this cure most face the greatest structural barriers to accessing it
- 40-50 treatment centers clustered in major metros — geographic access is limited

## Kill Test
**Original calculation:** The throughput-to-population math. Nobody is publicly reporting that at current rates, treating every eligible US patient would take 336 years, or that Vertex's $47 billion total addressable market will take over 400 years to exhaust at current revenue pace. The age-dependent cost-effectiveness inversion is also novel: the age expansion to 2+ is justified precisely because younger patients have longer benefit horizons, but the math shows Casgevy is cost-effective for toddlers and cost-ineffective for adults — creating a paradox where the populations with the most disease burden (older patients) have the weakest economic case for treatment.
