# Research Notes: Valar Atomics Ward 250

## Core Event
- **Valar Atomics Ward 250** achieved zero-power fueled criticality on **June 18, 2026** at ~4:30 PM MDT
- Location: Utah San Rafael Energy Lab, Emery County, Utah
- **Second** advanced reactor to reach criticality under Executive Order 14301
- **First** DOE-authorized reactor built and operated **outside** a national laboratory
- Reactor type: Gen IV TRISO-fueled modular high-temperature gas reactor (HTGR), helium-cooled, graphite-moderated
- Power: 100 kWt test reactor; commercial Ward 250 generates 5 MWe
- "Nine months ago, this was an empty site" — Isaiah Taylor, Founder & CEO

## Timeline
- **Sept 2025:** Groundbreaking at USREL site
- **Dec 2025:** NOVA Core (Ward 250 subset) achieves cold criticality at LANL/NCERC
- **Feb 2026:** DOE approved Valar's preliminary design safety analysis; C-17 airlift (convoy of 3 Globemaster IIIs, 8 modules from March AFB, CA to Hill AFB, UT)
- **Late April 2026:** Documented safety analysis approved
- **June 18, 2026:** Ward 250 achieves zero-power criticality at USREL
- Now: Non-commercial power ascension underway

## Executive Order 14301 Race
- Trump EO (May 23, 2025): 3 advanced reactors critical by July 4, 2026
- **Reactor #1:** Antares Nuclear Mark-0, June 4, 2026, Idaho National Lab
- **Reactor #2:** Valar Atomics Ward 250, June 18, 2026, Utah (OUTSIDE national lab)
- **Reactor #3:** Likely Aalo Atomics Aalo-X at INL — 10 MWe sodium-cooled, unveiled CTR facility March 2026, expects criticality "well before" July 4
- 10 companies in DOE Reactor Pilot Program: Aalo, Antares, Atomic Alchemy, Deep Fission, Last Energy, Oklo, Natura Resources, Radiant Energy, Terrestrial Energy, Valar Atomics
- Status: Oklo, Valar, Aalo started construction by Oct 2025; Deep Fission broke ground Dec 2025; Last Energy building in Texas; remaining 4 (Radiant, Natura, Terrestrial Energy, Atomic Alchemy) still in permitting phases

## Comparative Timelines (ORIGINAL ANALYSIS)
**DOE Pathway (Reactor Pilot Program):**
- Valar: 9 months from empty lot to criticality
- Antares: Founded 2023, critical June 2026 (~3 years from founding, <12 months from construction start)
- DOE bypasses NRC licensing entirely — DOE authorization process

**NRC Pathway:**
- NuScale Power: 23 years of development, NRC design certification 2023, flagship Idaho project collapsed when costs hit $9.3B
- Nano Nuclear KRONOS: CPA submitted March 2026, NRC accepted April 2026, expects ~12-month technical review, construction permit H2 2027, prototype construction at UIUC — total to criticality: ~3-4 years from CPA submission
- TerraPower: Founded 2008, Natrium groundbreaking 2024, operations ~2030 (22+ years)
- X-energy: Founded 2009, still in NRC design review

**Historical comparison:**
- Vogtle 3&4 (traditional LWR): Construction started ~2013, Unit 3 commercial July 2023, Unit 4 April 2024 = ~12 years, ~$35B (vs $14B original estimate), 2.2 GW total
- At Valar's 9-month pace, the US could have built and tested **16 different reactor designs** in the time Vogtle took to finish ONE
- At $35B / 12 years, Vogtle cost ~$15.9M per MW. A 5 MWe Ward 250 commercial unit, if costs scale roughly to the DOE pathway, would need to come in under $80M to match on a $/MW basis — unclear if achievable at commercial scale

## Original Calculation: The Build-Test-Iterate Gap
Under the DOE pathway, a company can go from empty lot to critical reactor in 9 months. Under the NRC pathway, just the CPA *review* takes 12 months before construction can even begin. 

If the US needs 300 additional GW by 2050 (Trump's 400 GW goal minus ~100 GW existing):
- At Ward 250's 5 MWe and 9-month build time: 60,000 reactors needed (absurd at this scale, but demonstrates iteration speed)
- At NuScale's 924 MWe per plant and ~10-year build time: ~325 plants (realistic but slow)
- The sweet spot: use the DOE fast pathway to iterate through designs at 9-month cycles, then feed proven designs into NRC for commercial licensing

In the time it took Vogtle 3&4 to come online (12 years), the DOE pathway could theoretically cycle through **16 reactor design iterations**. That's the real comparison — not megawatts per reactor, but design cycles per decade. Silicon Valley ships products every sprint. The nuclear industry ships products every generation.

## Limitations
- Ward 250 is a 100 kWt test reactor — not a power plant. Zero-power criticality means self-sustaining chain reaction at essentially no measurable energy output
- All EO 14301 reactors are demonstration/test units, not commercial generators
- DOE pathway's speed comes partly from bypassing NRC — which has legitimate safety oversight functions
- HALEU fuel supply still constrained (~920 kg total US production, per Antares article)
- Microreactors at 5 MWe won't individually dent the 300 GW gap; the value is in proving designs fast
- None of these companies have generated commercial electricity yet
