# Research Notes: TSMC's $265B Arizona Bet — The Insurance Math

## Thesis
TSMC just posted its best quarter ever ($22B profit) and committed $265 billion to Arizona — the largest foreign direct investment in US history. But run the math on what that buys: 30% of advanced-node capacity moves to US soil. The other 70% stays on one earthquake-prone, geopolitically exposed island 100 miles from Chinese missile batteries. The world is paying a $265B insurance premium that covers less than a third of the risk.

## Original Calculations

### 1. The Insurance Premium Math
- $265B to move 30% of advanced capacity to US
- TSMC underpins ~$5-6 trillion of annual global tech value chain (smartphones, AI servers, PCs, automotive)
- 30% of that ≈ $1.5-1.8 trillion of annual supply chain value
- $265B / $1.65T = 16% of one year's value as "insurance premium"
- Amortized over 20-year fab lifetime: $13.25B/year to protect $1.65T = 0.8% annual premium
- Compare: actual insurance industry premiums for catastrophic risk typically run 1-3% of insured value
- TSMC's Arizona bet is priced like insurance, but leaves 70% uninsured

### 2. Capex Intensity vs. History
- TSMC at 37% capex/revenue is 61% above the 40-year semiconductor average of 23%
- Only two prior periods exceeded 30%: 1985 peak (28% 5yr avg → 17% market crash) and 2000 peak (29% → 32% market crash)
- BUT: TSMC IS 73% of the market. Previous peaks were diversified across many companies.
- Counter: TSMC's customers (Nvidia, Apple, AMD, Broadcom) are all placing multi-year orders. This isn't speculative capacity — it's prepaid.

### 3. Cost Per Wafer of Transferred Capacity
- $265B / 100,000 wafers per month = $2.65M per monthly wafer-start of capacity
- Each wafer at 3nm produces ~100 large AI chips (GPU/accelerator-class)
- Annual output at full build: ~1.2M wafers × 100 chips = 120M chips
- Infrastructure cost per chip: $265B / (120M × 20 years) = $110 per chip
- Compare: AI accelerators sell for $10,000-$40,000 each
- The Arizona premium adds ~0.3-1.1% to chip cost — negligible

### 4. The 70% Exposure Number
- Even after $265B, 70% of the world's most advanced chips still come from Taiwan
- Taiwan Strait is 100 miles wide; China has ~2,000 ballistic missiles aimed at Taiwan (DoD 2025 report)
- A single week of TSMC Taiwan shutdown costs: ~60,000 advanced wafers × $20-30K each = $1.2-1.8B in wafer value
- But downstream impact is 100x larger: each wafer enables $500K-2M in end-product revenue
- One month of shutdown: $30-120B in global GDP impact (depending on downstream multiplier)

## Kill Test (Original Contribution)
✅ The "insurance premium" calculation comparing Arizona investment cost to insured supply chain value — nobody has framed the $265B this way
✅ The capex intensity comparison showing TSMC at 61% above historical norms, with the crucial caveat that TSMC IS the market (making historical comparisons misleading)
✅ The "70% exposure" number — even the largest FDI in US history only hedges 30% of the risk
