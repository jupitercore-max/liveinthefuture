# Research Notes: Seaweed Cattle Methane Reduction — The 77% Solution and Its 22,000× Scaling Gap

## News Hook
Adelaide University researchers (April 2026, Frontiers in Animal Science) demonstrated that bromoform extract oil from Asparagopsis seaweed reduces methane emissions by 49-77% in 80 grazing Angus cows over 8 weeks — the first rigorous trial in extensive grazing systems (not feedlots). No harm to calves.

A second study (PMID: 41463909, Animals journal) found 73.6% reduction (53.7 g/d vs 203.2 g/d) with once-daily dosing, with persistent suppression 3-4 days after supplement removal.

## Primary Sources
1. Caetano et al. (2026) "Bromoform extract oil from Asparagopsis reduces methane in grazing Angus cows" — Frontiers in Animal Science. 80 cows, 8-week trial, 49-77% reduction. Adelaide University.
2. PubMed PMID 41463909 — "Evaluation of Seaweed-Based Feed Additive on Enteric Methane Emissions of Grazing Heifers" — Animals 15(24):3625. 73.6% reduction. GreenFeed monitoring system.
3. FAO (2025) — Livestock sector: 6.2 Gt CO2-e/year. Cattle = 62-75% of total livestock emissions. Enteric fermentation = 46% of livestock emissions. Cattle responsible for 75% of global enteric methane.
4. CH4 Global (Jan 2025, BusinessWire) — World's first commercial-scale Asparagopsis facility, EcoPark in Louth Bay, South Australia. 10 ponds = 80 metric tons Asparagopsis/year. Planned expansion: 100 ponds = 45,000 cattle/day. Production cost 1/10th of tank-based methods.
5. FAO livestock and enteric methane page: ~600 Mt methane/year globally, agriculture = ~40% of anthropogenic methane, livestock = 32%. Enteric methane from cattle = 30% of global methane emissions.

## Original Calculations

### The Global Enteric Methane Budget from Cattle
- Total livestock emissions: 6.2 Gt CO2-e/year (FAO 2025)
- Enteric fermentation: 46% of livestock = 2.85 Gt CO2-e
- Cattle's share: 75% of enteric = 2.14 Gt CO2-e from cattle burps alone
- In raw methane terms: ~77 Mt CH4/year (using GWP-100 of 27.9)

### Current Supply: CH4 Global's EcoPark
- Largest Asparagopsis producer globally
- At 100-pond full capacity: serves 45,000 cattle per day
- Global cattle: ~1 billion head
- Current coverage at full capacity: 45,000 / 1,000,000,000 = 0.0045%
- To serve just 1% of global cattle (10M head): need 222× current planned capacity
- To serve 10% (100M head): need 2,222× — call it a 22,000× gap to reach global scale

### The Methane Reduction Math
If 10% of cattle received Asparagopsis (optimistic near-term target):
- 2.14 Gt CO2-e × 10% × 63% average reduction = 0.135 Gt CO2-e avoided per year
- That's 135 million tons CO2-e/year
- For comparison: All direct air capture plants globally removed ~0.001 Gt CO2 in 2025
- Seaweed supplementing 10% of cattle = ~135× more climate impact than all DAC combined

### Cost Comparison
- DAC: $400-1,000 per ton CO2
- Voluntary carbon market: ~$5-15/ton
- Compliance carbon market (EU ETS): ~$50-80/ton
- If Asparagopsis costs converge to industry target of ~$0.50/cow/day:
  - 100M cattle × $0.50/day × 365 = $18.25 billion/year
  - Abating 135 Mt CO2-e = ~$135/ton CO2-e
  - Competitive with DAC, but unlike DAC, the infrastructure is ponds, not turbines
  - Plus co-benefits: cattle eat slightly less feed with supplement → feed cost savings

### The Grazing System Problem
- ~70% of world's cattle are in extensive grazing systems
- Previous methane-reduction supplements (3-NOP/Bovaer) approved mainly for feedlots
- Adelaide trial is significant because it proves Asparagopsis works in grazing herds
- But delivery is harder: you can't mix it into a TMR (total mixed ration) like in feedlots
- Solutions being tested: lick blocks, water trough delivery, bolus capsules

## Kill Test
