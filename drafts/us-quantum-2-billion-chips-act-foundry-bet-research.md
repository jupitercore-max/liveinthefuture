# Research: The US Government Just Bought Equity in 9 Quantum Companies for $2 Billion. The Combined Revenue Is $169 Million.

## Journalist: Kai Nakamura
## Category: 💻 Quantum
## Date: July 1, 2026

## Core Thesis
The US Department of Commerce announced $2.013 billion in CHIPS and Science Act funding for 9 quantum computing companies, taking minority equity stakes in each. This is the first time the US government has taken equity stakes in quantum companies — structured like a sovereign wealth fund rather than a traditional grant. The original analysis reveals that the government's investment buys into an industry where combined market capitalizations exceed $55 billion but combined annual revenues total roughly $169 million — a price-to-sales ratio of 326x across the sector, roughly 47x higher than the NASDAQ's dot-com peak of ~7x P/S.

## Original Analysis: The $326x Problem

### Calculation 1: Sector Price-to-Sales Ratio
Combined market caps of publicly traded quantum pure-plays (as of Jul 1, 2026):
- D-Wave: $8.9B
- Rigetti: $6.4B
- IonQ: $19.9B
- Quantinuum: $21.3B
- **Total pure-play market cap: ~$56.5B**

Combined annual revenues:
- D-Wave: $24.6M (FY2025)
- Rigetti: $7.1M (FY2025)  
- IonQ: $130M (TTM)
- Quantinuum: estimated ~$7M (pre-IPO, from Honeywell filings suggesting minimal revenue)
- **Total: ~$169M**

**Sector P/S ratio: $56.5B / $169M ≈ 334x**

For comparison:
- NASDAQ P/S at dot-com peak (March 2000): ~7x
- Cisco at dot-com peak: ~40x  
- Current AI sector average P/S: ~25-30x
- Quantum sector: 334x = 48x the dot-com NASDAQ peak

### Calculation 2: Government's Equity Value Analysis
The government is getting equity stakes for its $2B investment. Known data point: GlobalFoundries stake is ~1% for $375M (implying ~$37.5B valuation; actual GFS market cap ~$30B — so modest premium for the government).

For pure-plays receiving $100M each:
- D-Wave ($8.9B market cap): $100M ≈ 1.1% stake
- Rigetti ($6.4B market cap): $100M ≈ 1.6% stake  
- Quantinuum ($21.3B market cap): $100M ≈ 0.5% stake

If quantum computing achieves IBM's projected $850B economic value by 2040, and these companies capture even 10% of that value ($85B), the government's combined equity could be worth $1-3B — a modest return on a $2B investment. But if quantum follows the historical pattern of DOE investments...

### Calculation 3: Historical Government Tech Bet ROI
- Human Genome Project: $3.8B invested (1990-2003) → $796B economic impact by 2013 = 209:1 ROI (Battelle/NHGRI study)
- DARPA ARPANET/Internet: ~$4.5B invested → several trillion in economic value = >1000:1 ROI
- Manhattan Project: ~$28B (inflation-adjusted) → nuclear energy industry worth ~$1T
- CHIPS Act semiconductor incentives: $52.7B allocated, returns TBD

$2B for quantum at 209:1 (HGP rate) = $418B return. At even 10:1 = $20B. The bet math works if quantum delivers, which is the entire question.

### Calculation 4: Revenue-Per-Dollar-of-Government-Investment
The 7 quantum companies (excluding foundries) get $638M total. Their combined revenue is roughly $169M. The government is paying $3.78 for every $1 of current revenue these companies generate.

### Calculation 5: The Announcement Day Amplifier
On May 21, D-Wave jumped 30%+, Rigetti 30%+, Infleqtion 24%. Three companies that received $300M combined added ~$5B in market cap in a single session. The market valued each $1 of government money at $16.70. SoftBank's Vision Fund was mocked for paying 5-10x premiums. The quantum market beats SoftBank at its own game.

## Key Tensions / Strongest Counterargument
- The counterargument: Quantum computing IS fundamentally different from dot-com stocks. Those companies were selling ads and pet food online with established business models. Quantum companies are building hardware that could break encryption, revolutionize drug discovery, and solve optimization problems no classical computer can touch. Comparing P/S ratios to dot-com is category error — it's like comparing Manhattan Project costs to Department of Agriculture spending. The government isn't buying revenue; it's buying optionality on a technology that could reshape national security.
- The Gil conflict is serious but limited — IBM's quantum supremacy was real and publicly demonstrated; they earned the selection on technical merits regardless of who was at DoE.
- The "5 modalities, 1 winner" problem: neutral-atom (2 companies), superconducting (3 including IBM), trapped-ion (2 including Quantinuum), photonic (1), silicon-spin (1). History says 1-2 modalities win. The government is hedging all of them.

## Limitations
- Exact equity stake percentages not disclosed for most companies

- Private companies (Atom Computing, PsiQuantum, Diraq) have no public financials
- China's quantum spending figures are estimates from BCG, not official disclosures
- Letters of intent are not finalized deals — awards contingent on milestones
