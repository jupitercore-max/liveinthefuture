# Research: APOE2 Longevity Gene — Neurons Resist Aging via DNA Repair

## Core Story
Buck Institute study (published May 11, 2026 in Aging Cell) reveals the mechanistic reason APOE2 carriers live longer and resist Alzheimer's: their neurons are better at repairing DNA damage and resisting cellular senescence. This shifts the APOE field away from cholesterol/amyloid-beta and toward DNA integrity and cellular aging — two of the most druggable hallmarks of aging.

## Key Data Points

### APOE Allele Frequencies (Global)
- APOE3 (ε3): ~77% of alleles — "neutral" baseline
- APOE4 (ε4): ~14% of alleles — risk factor. ~25% of people carry at least one copy
- APOE2 (ε2): ~8% of alleles — protective. ~11% of people carry at least one copy
- APOE4/4 homozygotes: ~2% of population, ~12x Alzheimer's risk
- APOE2/2 homozygotes: <1% of population, ~0.5x Alzheimer's risk (exceptionally low)

### Alzheimer's Disease Scale
- ~55 million people with dementia worldwide (WHO 2023)
- ~60-80% of AD cases involve at least one APOE4 allele
- ~6.9 million Americans living with AD (Alzheimer's Association 2024)
- AD costs $360 billion/year in the US alone

### Buck Study Key Findings
- Used isogenic iPSC-derived neurons (differ ONLY at APOE locus)
- APOE2 GABAergic neurons upregulate DNA repair pathways
- APOE4 neurons show Alzheimer's disease transcriptional signatures
- APOE2 neurons show less DNA strand breaks (direct measurement)
- Under stress (radiation, doxorubicin), APOE2 neurons show lower senescence markers (p16, CRYAB)
- **Transferable protection:** Adding recombinant APOE2 protein to APOE4 neurons REDUCED DNA damage
- Mouse hippocampal tissue confirms: APOE2 mice show smaller nucleoli, better Lamin A/C levels, preserved heterochromatin

### Senolytic Drug Convergence
- Senolytics (dasatinib + quercetin) target cellular senescence — the SAME pathway APOE2 naturally resists
- Mayo Clinic Phase 1 (NCT04063124): D+Q safely crossed BBB and cleared senescent cells in AD patients
- ALSENLITE Phase 2 (NCT04785300): Ongoing, testing D+Q in early AD with tau-PET biomarkers
- BUT: A study showed senolytics can damage oligodendrocytes and cause demyelination — carpet-bombing senescent cells is risky
- The Buck study suggests a MORE TARGETED approach: boost DNA repair in neurons rather than kill all senescent cells

## Novel Contribution / Original Analysis

**The Convergence Nobody Is Calculating:**
1. APOE2's mechanism IS anti-senescence (Buck study)
2. Senolytics target senescence in AD (Mayo Clinic trials)
3. APOE2 protein externally applied to APOE4 neurons reduces damage (transferable!)
4. This suggests an APOE2-mimetic drug could be more precise than senolytics — boosting DNA repair specifically in neurons vs. carpet-bombing all senescent cells

**Population math:**
- ~55M dementia patients worldwide × ~70% APOE4-involved = ~38.5M patients where APOE status matters
- ~8 billion people × 14% APOE4 allele frequency × 2 chromosomes × correction = roughly 1.12 billion APOE4 carriers alive today who could benefit from APOE2-mimetic intervention BEFORE symptoms
- The preventive market dwarfs the treatment market

**The centenarian enrichment:**
- APOE2 frequency in centenarians: enriched to ~12-17% vs 8% in general population
- APOE4 frequency in centenarians: depleted to ~5-7% vs 14% in general population
- This is one of the most replicated findings in human longevity genetics
