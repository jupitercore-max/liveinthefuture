### Student-Teacher Ratio & Class Size
- Student-teacher ratio: ~8:1 (estimated from school descriptions of small class sizes)
- Average class size: ~16–20 students
- *Note: Peninsula School does not prominently publish these figures; estimates based on PrivateSchoolReview and school descriptions*

### Math Curriculum/Approach
- Progressive/constructivist approach — learning through exploration, projects, and play
- No published data on Singapore Math, ability grouping, or math competition teams
- As a progressive school, Peninsula emphasizes hands-on, project-based learning rather than traditional acceleration or tracking
- **No known MATHCOUNTS or AMC participation**
- *Source: [peninsulaschool.org](https://www.peninsulaschool.org/)*

### High School Placement
- No published placement list found on school website
- Graduates typically attend local public high schools (Menlo-Atherton, Palo Alto High, Gunn) and some independent schools (Menlo School, Sacred Heart Prep, Mid-Peninsula)

### Acceptance Rate / Selectivity
- Not published. Reportedly selective given small class sizes
- Application deadline: January 9, 2026 (for 2026–27)
- *Source: [peninsulaschool.org/admissions](https://www.peninsulaschool.org/admissions)*

### Niche.com Grade
- Not clearly rated on Niche (school may not be prominently listed or rated under the generic "Peninsula School" name)

### GreatSchools Score
- Not rated (private schools are generally not scored by GreatSchools)

### Diversity Stats
- Not publicly published by the school

### Entry Points
- Main entry: Preschool (age 3), Kindergarten
- Limited spots in other grades when available
- Application deadline: January 9 (priority)
- No standardized testing requirement noted
- *Source: [peninsulaschool.org/admissions](https://www.peninsulaschool.org/admissions)*

### Unique Programs / Notable Features
- One of the oldest progressive schools on the West Coast (founded 1925)
- Emphasis on child-led, play-based, experiential learning
- Multi-age classrooms in some divisions
- Strong arts, woodworking, and outdoor programs
- Historic campus with a community feel — parents are deeply involved
- Specialist programs (art, music, PE, Spanish)

### Strongest Counterargument (Case Against)
- **Academic rigor concerns**: The progressive model de-emphasizes grades, standardized testing, and traditional metrics. Parents seeking strong math acceleration, competition preparation, or structured academic pathways may find the curriculum too loose. Some parents report that graduates need to "catch up" in certain subjects when transitioning to academically rigorous high schools. The lack of published placement data or standardized test scores makes it hard to assess outcomes objectively.

## 5. GERMAN INTERNATIONAL SCHOOL OF SILICON VALLEY (GISSV)
**Coed | Grades PreK–12 | Mountain View**

### Tuition (2025–26)
- **Preschool 5-Day AM: $25,700**
- **Preschool/KG Full-Day: $33,700**
- **Grades 1–12 Morning Program: ~$26,000–$30,000** (exact per-grade not shown; 2026–27 preschool = $26,900 AM, $35,250 full-day)
- Plus annual fees: $825 enrollment + $150 membership + $1,250 facilities = **$2,225 in fees**
- *Source: [gissv.org/tuition-fees/mountain-view-campus](https://www.gissv.org/gissv-home-english/admissions/tuition-fees/mountain-view-campus)*

### Grades & Enrollment
- Grades: Preschool (age 2) – Grade 12
- Two campuses: Mountain View (PreK–12) and San Francisco (PreK–8, adding 9th in 2026–27)
- Total enrollment: ~400–500 (estimated; not precisely published)
- *Source: [gissv.org/about-gissv](https://www.gissv.org/gissv-home-english/about-gissv)*

### Student-Teacher Ratio & Class Size
- Student-teacher ratio: Less than **20:1** school-wide, **10:1** in preschool
- Max class size: 20 in preschool groups (2 educators per group)
- Elementary/secondary classes are small by design
- *Source: [gissv.org/about-gissv](https://www.gissv.org/gissv-home-english/about-gissv)*

### Math Curriculum/Approach
- **German-based (Thuringian) math curriculum** — known for rigor, particularly in middle and upper grades
- Math and science taught primarily in German
- MINT (STEM) designated school
- Differentiated instruction based on German three-track secondary system (Gymnasium, Realschule, Hauptschule) — students are assessed and placed at end of grade 5
- **No MATHCOUNTS/AMC participation noted** (different competition ecosystem)
- "Even average students [can] understand and be successful at advanced topics" — per parent testimonial
- *Source: [gissv.org/about-gissv](https://www.gissv.org/gissv-home-english/about-gissv)*

### High School & College Placement
- GISSV goes through Grade 12
- Graduates earn **dual diplomas**: German International Abitur (DIA) + California High School Diploma
- DIA qualifies graduates for admission to German/European universities (often tuition-free)
- No published U.S. college matriculation list found
- *Source: [gissv.org/about-gissv](https://www.gissv.org/gissv-home-english/about-gissv)*

### Financial Aid
- Not prominently published; school is nonprofit

### Acceptance Rate / Selectivity
- Not published; school accepts students from all language backgrounds
- No prior German required (support offered for German/English as second language)

### Niche.com Grade
- Not clearly rated on Niche for the Mountain View campus

### GreatSchools Score
- Not rated (private/international)

### Diversity Stats
- Inherently diverse — multicultural, multiethnic student body (German, American, international families)
- Specific percentages not published
- *Source: [gissv.org/about-gissv](https://www.gissv.org/gissv-home-english/about-gissv)*

### Entry Points
- Main entry: Preschool (age 2–3), Kindergarten, Grade 1
- Spots available at other grades depending on space
- Open house tours available (see gissv.org/tours)
- Application fee: $75

### Unique Programs / Notable Features
- **Full German-English dual immersion** from preschool through 12th grade
- Only elementary and secondary school in Western U.S. meeting German government accreditation standards
- Third language (French or Spanish) from Grade 6
- German International Abitur opens doors to European universities at fraction of U.S. costs
- Homeroom teachers stay with classes through 4-year divisions
- Accredited by German government (ZfA), WASC, CAIS
- Designated MINT (STEM), Digital School, SMART School
- Saturday German school for community
- Afternoon club with homework supervision until 5:30 PM

### Strongest Counterargument (Case Against)
- **Language barrier and niche appeal**: If your child doesn't want to be (or you don't want them to be) fully bilingual in German, this school makes no sense. Core subjects are taught in German, which can be alienating for English-dominant children especially in later grades. The German three-track secondary system (Gymnasium/Realschule/Hauptschule) effectively sorts students at age 10–11, which can feel limiting. The school is in **Mountain View** (~20 min from Menlo Park), and the campus is a converted public school, not purpose-built. U.S. college placement data is not published, making it hard to evaluate outcomes for families not pursuing European university.
