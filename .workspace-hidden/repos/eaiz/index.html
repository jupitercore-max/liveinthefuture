Why Do Balloons Float?

Balloons go up! Some gas inside makes them fly.

Some balloons float because they have a special gas called helium inside.

Helium is lighter than the air around us, so a balloon filled with helium floats upward like a bubble in water.

Archimedes' principle explains buoyancy: the air displaced by the balloon weighs more than the helium inside it, creating net upward force.

The ideal gas law (PV=nRT) and molecular kinetic theory explain why helium's low molar mass creates density differences that produce buoyant force.

Helium's atomic mass of 4 u vs. air's average 29 u creates buoyant force. This piece covers gas density, Archimedes' principle, and the looming helium supply crisis.
