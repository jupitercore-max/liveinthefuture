1. The Deal

On March 26, 2026, the Los Angeles Dodgers unveiled "UNIQLO Field at Dodger Stadium" as their new field presenting partnership. Tadashi Yanai, founder of Fast Retailing (Uniqlo's parent), confirmed the deal covers five years at a total value of "more than" $125 million.1

The structure is notable: the Dodgers preserved "Dodger Stadium" as the venue name while selling the field naming rights. Every PA announcement, every broadcast graphic, every scoreboard display reads "UNIQLO Field at Dodger Stadium." The stadium name survives. The brand infiltrates every mention of the playing surface.

2. Impression Inventory

Naming rights generate impressions through five distinct channels. Each has different attention quality and recall characteristics.

We assume 85% are unique visitors over the season (~3.3M unique attendees). Each attendee is exposed to the UNIQLO Field branding on signage, PA, and scoreboards an estimated 15-20 times per game.

3. Incremental Awareness Model

Raw impressions don't equal awareness. We need to model how many people who didn't previously know Uniqlo will know it after exposure through UNIQLO Field.

3a. Uniqlo's Current US Brand Awareness

Assumption: Uniqlo's US aided brand awareness is approximately 35-40%. They have 77 stores (mostly coastal: NYC, LA, SF, Chicago), launched a Galleria Dallas store in October 2024, and are expanding aggressively. US market share rose from 4.8% to 7.3% in 2024.3 In contrast, aided awareness among the Dodgers fanbase (LA metro + national baseball fans) is likely higher, perhaps 45-50%, since Dodgers fans skew urban/coastal where Uniqlo has stores. We model 37% baseline US aided awareness and 47% among Dodgers-adjacent audience.

3b. From Impressions to Recall

Not every impression creates awareness. Research on stadium naming rights shows frustratingly low recall rates:

VenueSponsor RecallSource
Crypto.com Arena (Staples Center)27% aware of name changeConsumer survey, 20224
MetLife Stadium29% unaided recall; 71% unaware of changeFan survey, post-deal4
SoFi Stadium371M impressions in week 1, but brand recall plateauedSoFi media analysis2
Key assumption: We model a 3% impression-to-recall conversion rate for naming rights. This is deliberately conservative. Academic research (Cornwell et al., 2005; Walraven et al., 2012) shows stadium sponsorship recall ranges from 2-8% depending on fan engagement level and exposure frequency. We use 3% because: (a) "UNIQLO Field at Dodger Stadium" preserves the original name, diluting the Uniqlo signal; (b) many impressions are passive (background on TV, not focused attention); (c) the Crypto.com data suggests even aggressive rebranding achieves only 27% awareness after years.

3c. From Recall to Incremental Awareness

Incremental Aware = Total Impressions × Recall Rate × (1 - Baseline Awareness)

Of the people who recall seeing "UNIQLO Field," some already knew Uniqlo. We subtract the baseline:

6. The Ohtani Multiplier

Every model above understates one variable: Shohei Ohtani.

Ohtani's arrival in 2024 fundamentally changed the Dodgers' media footprint. He's not just the highest-paid player in MLB history ($700M/10yr), he's a cultural bridge between the US and Japan's 125M population. Japanese broadcaster NHK shows every Ohtani plate appearance. NHK's Dodgers viewership in Japan reportedly exceeds 5M viewers per game.5

The Japan factor: Uniqlo has 794 stores in Japan (vs. 77 in the US). Japanese awareness is already ~95%+. For Uniqlo, the Japan viewership doesn't drive awareness; it drives brand reinforcement and US market positioning. But for US awareness modeling, the Ohtani effect shows up in: (a) dramatically elevated national broadcast appearances (Fox/ESPN prioritize Ohtani), (b) social media virality (Ohtani highlights shared globally), and (c) earned media (every sportswriter mentions the venue).

Conservative estimate: the Ohtani effect inflates our impression numbers by 20-40% above what a non-Ohtani Dodgers team would generate. For Uniqlo specifically, there's a brand alignment premium: a Japanese company sponsoring the field where the most famous Japanese athlete plays. That narrative writes itself and generates earned media that pure impression math can't capture.

If we apply a 30% Ohtani multiplier to impressions, Year 1 base case improves from $1.28 to approximately $0.98 per incremental aware.

8. The Verdict

This is a good deal for Uniqlo.

Here's why:

1. The math works. At $1.28 per incremental aware consumer (base case, Year 1), it's cheaper than most sustained awareness channels and comparable to targeted social media campaigns, but with dramatically higher impression quality (emotional sports context vs. scrolling past a banner ad).

2. The strategic context is perfect. Uniqlo has 77 US stores against an ambition of 200 by 2027 and $20B in US revenue (currently ~$2B). They need to build mass awareness before the stores arrive. Stadium naming rights are one of the few channels that deliver both mass reach and prestige simultaneously.

3. The Ohtani alignment is unreplicable. No other stadium deal in any sport has this kind of cultural bridge between the sponsor's home market and the team's star player. This isn't just impressions. This is narrative. Japanese media covers this deal as a cultural event, not an ad buy.

4. The field-only structure limits downside. If fans revolt against "UNIQLO Field" the way they did with "Crypto.com Arena," the damage is capped. "Dodger Stadium" survives. The brand is additive, not a replacement. This is psychologically important: fans who say "I'm going to Dodger Stadium" aren't lying, they're just omitting the field name. The sponsor gets mentioned on broadcasts without triggering the visceral "they sold our stadium" reaction.

What could go wrong:

1. Fan resistance. L.A. Times columnist Bill Plaschke called it "awful" hearing the PA announcer say "UNIQLO Field at Dodger Stadium" on opening day. He wrote the stadium "appears to have been transformed into something straight from NASCAR."1 If fan sentiment turns toxic, the brand association becomes negative.

2. Ohtani injury/decline. The Japanese media multiplier depends on Ohtani playing. A season-ending injury cuts the global impression floor significantly.

3. Dodgers decline. A last-place team doesn't get 25 national broadcasts or postseason games. The impression model assumes competitive excellence, which the current roster supports but 5 years is a long time.

4. US-Japan trade tensions. Tariffs and geopolitical friction could make "Japanese company buys American stadium naming" a political flashpoint, though Uniqlo's "LifeWear" positioning as apolitical basics mitigates this.
