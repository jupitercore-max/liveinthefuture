Overview: What You Need to Legally Resell in California

To legally resell goods in California, you need three things at minimum:

  1. California Seller's Permit (from CDTFA) — free, required before your first sale
  2. Resale Certificate (Form CDTFA-230) — lets you buy inventory without paying sales tax
  3. Local Business License — varies by city/county; $50-$200+ annually

Total startup cost for the paperwork: $50-$200 depending on your jurisdiction. The seller's permit itself is free. Most of this can be done in a single afternoon online.

Step 1: California Seller's Permit (CDTFA)

What It Is

A Seller's Permit (also called a "resale license" or "sales tax permit") is issued by the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration (CDTFA). It authorizes you to collect sales tax on taxable sales and to issue resale certificates when purchasing inventory for resale.

Key Facts

  • Cost: Free. No fee to apply or maintain.
  • Timeline: Permit number issued instantly online. Physical copy mailed in 7-10 business days.
  • Required: Before your first taxable sale in California.
  • Applies to: Any individual or business selling tangible personal property in CA, including online sales.
  • Security deposit: May be required if you have a history of unpaid taxes. Typically waived for new, small-scale sellers.

How to Apply

1 Go to CDTFA Online Services and create an account.

2 Select "Register a New Business" → "Sales and Use Tax".

3 Provide required information:

  • SSN or ITIN + California driver's license / state ID
  • EIN (if you have one; sole proprietors can use SSN)
  • Business name, structure (sole prop, LLC, corp), and start date
  • Business address and NAICS code (e.g., 454110 for e-commerce)
  • Estimated monthly sales volume
  • Banking information for electronic payments

4 Submit. Your permit number appears immediately on screen.

5 Print or save it. Physical copy arrives by mail in 7-10 days.

Source: CDTFA Online Services. See also Publication 73 (Your California Seller's Permit) for complete requirements.

⚠️ Temporary sellers: If you're only selling at a craft fair, flea market, or event lasting fewer than 90 days, you still need a permit — but you can apply for a temporary seller's permit instead. Separate permits may be required for different event locations.

Step 2: Resale Certificate (Form CDTFA-230)

What It Is

The Resale Certificate (Form CDTFA-230) is a document you give to your suppliers to buy inventory without paying sales tax at the point of purchase. You're not exempt from tax — you're deferring it. You collect sales tax from the end buyer when you resell the item.

How to Use It

  • Fill out Form CDTFA-230 with your seller's permit number, business name, and a description of the property you're purchasing for resale.
  • Present it to any California supplier (wholesale, retail, liquidation) when buying inventory intended for resale.
  • The supplier keeps the certificate on file. You don't pay sales tax on that purchase.
  • You then collect and remit sales tax when you sell the item to the end consumer.

Important Rules

  • Good faith requirement: The certificate is only valid for items genuinely purchased for resale. Using it for personal purchases is tax fraud under CA R&T Code §6094.5 — penalties of 10% of the tax, plus interest.
  • No expiration: Unlike some states, California resale certificates do not expire. A timely and valid certificate remains in effect until revoked.
  • Partial resale: If you buy 10 items but use 2 personally, you owe use tax on those 2.
  • Online purchases: You can provide CDTFA-230 to online wholesalers too. Many B2B platforms (Faire, Alibaba, wholesale suppliers) accept them electronically.

Source: CDTFA Publication 103 (Sales for Resale). Form: CDTFA-230.

Step 3: Local Business License

Menlo Park / Unincorporated San Mateo County

The Menlo Park area near Colby Ave and Menlo Oaks Dr is unincorporated San Mateo County, not the City of Menlo Park. This matters because your business license comes from the county, not the city.

Home-Based Reselling

If you're reselling from home (most resellers are), you may need a Home Occupation Permit in addition to the business license. Unincorporated San Mateo County requires Planning & Building approval. Key restrictions:

  • No customer visits to the home for pickups/sales
  • No exterior signage
  • No employees working at the home location
  • No excessive deliveries or traffic

Contact: San Mateo County Planning & Building at (650) 363-4161. Business Licenses: smcgov.org/tax/business-licenses or call (866) 220-0308.

Filing Frequency

CDTFA assigns your filing frequency based on estimated tax liability:

  • Annual: Less than ~$100/month in tax liability (under ~$1,200/yr in tax, or ~$13K/yr in sales)
  • Quarterly: Most small to mid-sized businesses. Returns due by the last day of the month following the quarter end (Jan 31, Apr 30, Jul 31, Oct 31).
  • Monthly (with prepayments): If your tax liability exceeds $10,000/quarter. Prepayments due by the 24th of the current month.

Use Tax

Use tax applies when you buy inventory from out-of-state sellers who don't charge California sales tax. The rate is the same as sales tax. If you buy 50 phone cases from a Shenzhen supplier on Alibaba and don't pay CA tax, you owe use tax on your purchase price — unless you resell all of them (in which case you collect sales tax from buyers).

Items you pull from inventory for personal use also trigger use tax.

Zero Returns

Even if you made no sales in a reporting period, you must file a return showing $0. Failing to file — even a zero return — triggers penalties and interest. CDTFA does not assume silence means no sales.

Source: CDTFA Publication 73.

Marketplace Facilitator Rules

This is the single most important thing for online resellers to understand. Under California AB 147 (effective October 2019), marketplace facilitators — platforms like eBay, Amazon, Poshmark, Mercari, StockX, and GOAT — are required to collect and remit sales tax on your behalf for sales made through their platform.

What This Means for You

If you sell exclusively through major platforms, you do not need to collect or remit sales tax yourself for those sales. The platform handles it. However:

  • You still need a seller's permit — it's legally required regardless of who collects the tax.
  • You still must file returns — your platform sales appear as "marketplace facilitator" sales. File the return showing these amounts; the tax due will be $0 for those transactions.
  • If you sell off-platform (your own website, local sales, in-person) — you collect and remit tax yourself.
  • You still need a resale certificate to buy inventory tax-free from suppliers.

Platform-Specific Requirements

eBay

  • Seller account: Free to create. Listing fees: up to 250 free listings/month, then $0.35/listing.
  • Final value fee: 13.25% for most categories (electronics: 14.95%; sneakers: 12.9%; watches: 15%). Includes payment processing.
  • Tax reporting: eBay issues Form 1099-K if you exceed $600 in gross payments (federal threshold as of 2024+).
  • Best for: Broad categories, electronics, collectibles, auto parts, used goods.

Amazon (FBA & FBM)

  • Individual plan: $0.99/item sold (no monthly fee). Professional plan: $39.99/month (unlimited listings).
  • Referral fee: 8-15% depending on category. FBA fees: Additional fulfillment + storage fees based on size/weight.
  • Approval required: Some categories are gated (watches, beauty, groceries). Requires invoices from authorized distributors.
  • Best for: New-in-box retail/online arbitrage, wholesale, private label.

Poshmark

  • Commission: Flat 20% on sales over $15; $2.95 on sales under $15.
  • Shipping: Prepaid USPS Priority Mail label included (buyer pays $7.67).
  • No listing fees. Free to list unlimited items.
  • Best for: Clothing, shoes, accessories, handbags.

Mercari

  • Selling fee: 10% of sale price.
  • Payment processing: 2.9% + $0.50 per transaction.
  • Shipping: Prepaid labels available; seller or buyer pays.
  • Best for: General merchandise, electronics, toys, home goods.

StockX / GOAT

  • StockX: Transaction fee 8-10% + payment processing 3%. Minimum payout threshold.
  • GOAT: Commission varies by seller level (up to 20% for new sellers, as low as 9.5% for high-volume).
  • Authentication: Both platforms authenticate items before shipping to buyer.
  • Best for: Sneakers, streetwear, watches, handbags.

The Bottom Line on Tax Software

If you only sell on marketplace platforms (eBay, Amazon, Poshmark, Mercari) — you technically don't need TaxJar or Avalara because the platforms collect tax for you. You need bookkeeping (My Reseller Genie or QuickBooks) to track COGS and profit for your income tax return (Schedule C).

If you sell on your own website or in-person — you need TaxJar or Avalara to handle sales tax collection and filing.

Common Mistakes & Pitfalls

1. Using Your Resale Certificate for Personal Purchases

This is tax fraud. CA R&T Code §6094.5 imposes a 10% penalty on the tax owed plus interest. The CDTFA audits resale certificate usage. Don't buy a TV "for resale" and put it in your living room.

2. Not Filing Zero Returns

Had a slow quarter? Still file. CDTFA charges penalties for unfiled returns even when no tax is due. Set a calendar reminder for your filing deadlines.

3. Ignoring Use Tax on Personal Consumption

If you pull inventory for personal use or buy supplies from out-of-state, you owe use tax. The CDTFA will find out — they cross-reference your seller's permit with your filed returns and look for purchase/sale mismatches.

4. Not Tracking Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)

Your income tax (federal + CA state) is on profit, not revenue. If you sell $50K on eBay but spent $30K on inventory, you owe tax on $20K. But only if you tracked COGS. Without records, the IRS assumes your COGS is zero and taxes the full $50K.

5. Thinking "It's Just a Hobby"

The IRS and CDTFA don't care what you call it. If you're buying items with the intent to resell for profit — repeatedly — it's a business. The $600 1099-K threshold means platforms report your sales to the IRS. You need to report them too.

6. Not Getting a Business License

Fines for operating without a license vary by jurisdiction. San Mateo County can impose penalties retroactive to when you started. It's $50 — just get it.

7. Mixing Personal and Business Finances

Open a separate checking account. It doesn't have to be a "business" account — a free personal checking account that you use only for reselling works fine. This makes bookkeeping, audits, and tax prep dramatically easier.

8. Ignoring the 1099-K Threshold Change

As of 2024, platforms report sales exceeding $600 (down from the previous $20,000 / 200 transactions). This means almost every active reseller gets a 1099-K. The IRS sees it. Report it.

Quick-Start Checklist

Before Your First Sale

  1. ☐ Apply for California Seller's Permit at onlineservices.cdtfa.ca.gov (free, instant)
  2. ☐ Download and fill out Form CDTFA-230 (Resale Certificate) — PDF here
  3. ☐ Apply for San Mateo County Business License at smcgov.org ($50)
  4. ☐ Open a separate bank account for reselling transactions
  5. ☐ Set up seller accounts on your chosen platforms (eBay, Poshmark, Mercari, etc.)
  6. ☐ Install bookkeeping software (My Reseller Genie or QuickBooks) and connect your bank/platforms
  7. ☐ Set calendar reminders for sales tax filing deadlines

Ongoing

  1. ☐ Track COGS for every item (purchase price, shipping, supplies)
  2. ☐ File sales tax returns on time, even if $0
  3. ☐ Track mileage to thrift stores, post office, sourcing trips (58.5¢/mile deduction)
  4. ☐ Save receipts for business expenses (shipping supplies, packaging, software subscriptions, phone/internet %)
  5. ☐ Pay estimated quarterly taxes (federal + CA) if you expect to owe $1,000+ for the year
  6. ☐ Renew business license annually
