# Creator × Brand Exclusivity Analysis: Watches → Wearables

## The Watch Industry as a Natural Experiment

The watch creator ecosystem provides a near-perfect A/B test for brand exclusivity strategies because creators span the full spectrum from brand-owned to fully independent. Here's the data:

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Multi-Brand Creators Grow Faster and More Sustainably

**Teddy Baldassarre** went from 0 to 1.4M subscribers while covering 50+ brands. His growth accelerated as he added more authorized brands — each new brand = new audience segment discovering him. The channel IS the top-of-funnel; the store IS the conversion.

**Watchfinder**, despite being owned by Richemont (Cartier, IWC, Panerai, Vacheron Constantin), maintains broad coverage because they understood that audience trust requires perceived independence. They review Rolex, Omega, and non-Richemont brands regularly. ~1.8M subs, but growth has plateaued since the acquisition.

### 2. Exclusivity Killed Hodinkee's Commerce Business

**Hodinkee is the cautionary tale.** Timeline:
- 2012-2020: Independent editorial → massive trust, grew to dominant watch media
- 2021: Raised $40M (investors included LVMH), launched Hodinkee Shop as authorized dealer for ~30 brands
- 2022: ~150 employees, aggressive commerce push
- 2023: Layoffs in January, 20% cut in August, 50% cut in November
- 2024: COO, CTO, VIP program head all left. Down to 30-75 employees
- 2025: Pulled back to editorial. Shop significantly reduced.

**What went wrong:** When Hodinkee started selling watches, readers questioned every editorial recommendation. "Is this review genuine, or are they pushing inventory?" The LVMH investment made it worse — readers assumed (often correctly) that LVMH brands got preferential coverage. Trust eroded, commerce never reached profitability (unprofitable for 3+ years per founder's own admission).

**The lesson:** Editorial credibility and commerce create tension. Teddy Baldassarre solves this by being openly a dealer — no pretense of independence. Hodinkee tried to be both independent media AND a store, and the audience rejected the contradiction.

### 3. Brand-Owned Channels Hit a Ceiling

**Watchfinder** has ~1.8M subscribers but their per-video engagement has declined since Richemont acquisition. The production quality is exceptional, but savvy viewers know it's corporate media. They can't credibly trash a Cartier or IWC, and the audience senses it.

**Compare to Nico Leonard** (~900K subs) who regularly calls out overpriced watches, criticizes brands by name, and isn't afraid to say "this is a terrible value." His engagement rate per video is significantly higher than Watchfinder's despite having half the subscribers.

### 4. The Collab Model Outperforms Both

**The Urban Gentry** (TGV) built ~700K subscribers independently, then launched **Islander watches** — his own brand manufactured in collaboration with factories. This is neither exclusive ambassador nor multi-brand dealer. It's a creator-owned brand that:
- Doesn't compromise editorial independence (he still reviews everything)
- Creates direct revenue that doesn't depend on any brand's goodwill
- Builds equity in something he owns

**Teddy Baldassarre + Brew** collab watch follows the same pattern — use the audience to launch owned products while maintaining the multi-brand dealer business.

## Applying This to Wearables

### The Wearables Creator Landscape Today

| Creator Type | Examples | Model |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-brand tech reviewers | MKBHD, MrWhoseTheBoss, Dave2D | Cover Apple, Samsung, Google, Garmin, etc. |
| Fitness-specific | DC Rainmaker, The Quantified Scientist | Cover Garmin, Apple, Whoop, Coros, Polar |
| Brand-exclusive | Apple events creators, Samsung partners | Paid access/early review in exchange for favorable framing |
| Brand-owned | Apple Newsroom, Samsung Mobile | Corporate channels |

**DC Rainmaker** is the Teddy Baldassarre of wearables — trusted multi-brand reviewer with affiliate links. His Garmin/Apple Watch/Whoop reviews drive significant purchase decisions. He's never gone exclusive because his credibility IS his business.

**MKBHD** covers everything but has done "produced in partnership with" content for Google (Pixel Watch) and Samsung. Each sponsored video gets called out in comments, and his engagement dips on clearly sponsored content.

### Recommendation: Should Wearable Brands Push for Exclusivity?

**No. Multi-brand affiliate/AD is almost always better than exclusivity. Here's why:**

#### For the Brand:

1. **Exclusive creators lose credibility, which reduces conversion.** An Apple Watch ambassador who only covers Apple is less persuasive than DC Rainmaker saying "I've tested all of them, and here's why the Apple Watch Ultra is the best for [X]." The comparative context IS the value.

2. **Multi-brand creators have larger, more diverse audiences.** Teddy Baldassarre at 1.4M reaches Tudor buyers AND Tissot buyers AND Grand Seiko buyers. A Tudor-exclusive creator would only reach Tudor-interested viewers — a fraction of the addressable market.

3. **Exclusivity concentrates risk.** If your exclusive ambassador has a controversy (and they will eventually), your brand is disproportionately exposed. Multi-brand creators spread that risk.

4. **The Hodinkee lesson applies directly.** If a wearables media company (say, The Verge) took Samsung money and became Samsung-exclusive, they'd lose trust overnight. The same dynamic applies to individual creators.

#### For the Creator:

1. **Multi-brand coverage compounds.** Every new brand covered = new SEO surface area, new audience segment, new comparison video. "Apple Watch Ultra vs Garmin Fenix" serves both audiences. Single-brand content only serves one.

2. **Independence is monetizable.** DC Rainmaker's affiliate revenue from honest multi-brand reviews almost certainly exceeds what any single brand would pay for exclusivity.

3. **Own-brand products are the endgame.** Like Urban Gentry → Islander and Teddy → Brew, the smartest creators build their own products. You can't do this if you're contractually exclusive to another brand.

#### The Exception: Early-Stage Brands

The ONE case where exclusivity makes sense is for **unknown brands trying to break through.** If you're a new wearable startup (like Whoop was in 2018), getting one influential creator to go deep — wearing it daily, building content around it over months — can establish legitimacy faster than spreading thin across many creators. Whoop's early strategy with CrossFit athletes and fitness creators was effectively this.

But once you're established (Apple, Samsung, Garmin), exclusivity is value-destructive.

## The Framework

| Brand Stage | Recommended Strategy | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-launch / Unknown** | 2-3 exclusive deep partnerships | Build credibility through association |
| **Early growth** | Transition to multi-creator, non-exclusive | Broaden reach, build comparative context |
| **Established** | Multi-brand AD/affiliate, never exclusive | Trust comes from comparative coverage |
| **Dominant** | Let creators come to you | Apple doesn't need exclusivity — everyone covers Apple |

| Creator Stage | Recommended Strategy | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **<100K subs** | Take brand deals, even semi-exclusive | Need revenue to survive |
| **100K-500K** | Multi-brand affiliate, build AD relationships | Independence = growth fuel |
| **500K+** | Multi-brand AD + own product line | Teddy/TGV model: dealer + owned brand |
| **1M+** | Platform play: become the marketplace | Teddy Baldassarre IS the platform now |

---

## Bottom Line

**Exclusivity is a tax on credibility.** Every watch/wearables creator who went brand-exclusive either plateaued (Watchfinder), collapsed (Hodinkee commerce), or was already a corporate entity (brand-owned channels). Every creator who stayed multi-brand grew faster and built more durable businesses.

For wearable brands at Meta's scale: **invest in being the product that multi-brand creators genuinely recommend after honest comparison.** That's worth more than any exclusive deal.
