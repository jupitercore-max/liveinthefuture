# Daydream Article Seeds

Seeds must pass the same kill test as regular articles: Is there a novel contribution? Would a knowledgeable reader learn something new?

---

## Seed 1: AI Overviews Are Killing Independent Publishing (LITF)
**Pitch:** 500+ articles across 8 sites, all built assuming organic search traffic. Google AI Overviews now appear on 25% of searches with 93% zero-click rates. Use Ray's own sites as the case study — actual traffic analytics before/after AIO expansion, cross-referenced with industry data. The self-aware angle (we built an AI content machine, a bigger AI content machine is eating us) is exactly LITF's brand.
**Novel contribution:** First-person data from an AI-assisted publishing operation measuring its own obsolescence.
**Kill test risk:** Could feel navel-gazing. Needs hard traffic data to transcend opinion.

## Seed 2: 7 Companies Racing to Put AI on Your Face (LITF)
**Pitch:** Meta, Apple, Google/Warby Parker, Samsung, Nothing, Snap, Lenovo — all shipping or announcing AI smart glasses in 2026. Compare technical approaches (Apple's S-series silicon vs. Meta's Qualcomm, Google's software-first vs. Meta's hardware-first). The real story: the neural band is Meta's only defensible moat, and nobody's talking about it.
**Novel contribution:** Technical comparison of silicon strategies and what they mean for battery life (the actual #1 user complaint). The neural interface angle as competitive moat.
**Kill test risk:** Lots of "roundup" articles exist. Need deeper technical analysis to stand out.

## Seed 3: The Economics of "The Last" (Efficient Design)
**Pitch:** Why end-of-line performance cars always appreciate, with data from Bring a Trailer auction results. 993 Porsche, last Viper ACR, E46 M3, and now the CT5-V Blackwing. Plot the appreciation curves. The underlying economics: known finite supply + emotional attachment + functional excellence = reliable appreciation.
**Novel contribution:** Data-driven analysis from actual auction results, not vibes. Marcus Thorne's cars + watches crossover.
**Kill test risk:** Need to pull actual BaT data. Risk of being too "investment advice."
