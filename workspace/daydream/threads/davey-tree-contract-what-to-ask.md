# The Davey Contract: What to Ask Before Signing

**Created:** 2026-04-05 (Iteration 1)
**Status:** Actionable — time-sensitive (spring treatment window)
**Urgency:** Medium-High (caterpillars won't wait)

## Context
Ray needs to sign a Davey Tree Service contract for tussock moth treatment on 6 live oaks, $600. He noted: "ask what product they're spraying." This is the right instinct, and here's why it matters more than he might think.

## The Products They Might Use

### Best case: Btk (Bacillus thuringiensis var. kurstaki)
- A naturally occurring soil bacterium that produces proteins toxic only to caterpillar larvae
- **Safe for kids, pets, bees, birds, fish.** Essentially organic.
- Must be applied when caterpillars are actively feeding (early instar stages = NOW in spring)
- Breaks down in UV light within days — no residual contamination
- Brand names: DiPel, Thuricide, Monterey Btk
- **This is what you want.** If they're using Btk, sign the contract.

### Acceptable: Spinosad
- Derived from soil bacterium *Saccharopolyspora spinosa*
- Effective against caterpillars, some risk to bees during wet application (safe once dry)
- Brand names: Captain Jack's, Entrust
- Wait until spray dries before kids play outside

### Caution: Synthetic pyrethroids (bifenthrin, permethrin, lambda-cyhalothrin)
- Broad-spectrum insecticides. Kill caterpillars but ALSO kill bees, beneficial insects, aquatic organisms
- Can irritate skin and eyes. Children should stay off treated areas for 24+ hours
- Persist in environment for weeks
- Common in commercial tree services because they're cheap and effective
- **Ask specifically if they use these. If so, request Btk instead.**

### Avoid: Carbaryl (Sevin)
- Neurotoxic to insects, toxic to bees, moderately toxic to mammals
- Older product, less commonly used by professional services now
- No good reason to use this on residential oaks in 2026

## Timing Matters
Tussock moth caterpillars in the Bay Area typically emerge March-May. Treatment is most effective on early-instar larvae (small caterpillars). By late May, they're too large and pupating — treatment window closes. **If Ray delays past mid-April, the $600 might be wasted.**

## The Question to Ask Davey
> "What specific product and active ingredient are you planning to use? My preference is Btk (Bacillus thuringiensis). I have kids who play in the yard and I want to understand the re-entry interval."

This is a reasonable homeowner question. Professional arborists will respect it.

## What Ray Probably Doesn't Know
Live oaks in the Bay Area are facing a multi-year tussock moth cycle. If he skips treatment this year, the caterpillars will defoliate again, and weakened trees become vulnerable to secondary infections (sudden oak death, *Phytophthora ramorum*, is present in San Mateo County). The $600 isn't just about this year's leaves — it's about preventing a $6,000+ tree removal in 3-5 years.
