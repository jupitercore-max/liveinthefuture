# Tariffs Are About to Hit Ray's Day Job — And Nobody's Talking About It

**Created:** 2026-04-06 (Iteration 2)
**Status:** Active — HIGH urgency, directly impacts Meta Wearables
**Urgency:** High (structural, imminent)

## The Situation

The US tariff landscape as of April 2026:
- **Section 122 baseline:** 10% on ALL imports (effective Feb 24, 2026), threatened increase to 15% by July 24
- **China specifically:** 250% tariff rate (de facto embargo on Chinese-origin goods)
- **IEEPA tariffs invalidated** by Supreme Court (Feb 20, 2026), but Section 122 tariffs remain
- **Optical industry in alarm:** The Vision Council held emergency webinar on "significant implications for optical industry's international supply chains." Optometry Times reports eye care providers citing tariffs as major concern.

## Why This Matters for Meta Wearables

Ray-Ban Meta glasses are manufactured through the EssilorLuxottica partnership. EssilorLuxottica:
- **Headquarters:** Paris, France (EU tariffs apply)
- **Manufacturing:** Italy (frames), China (components, some assembly), Southeast Asia
- **Supply chain:** Lenses, camera modules, audio components, batteries — heavily sourced from China/SE Asia

The $499 Blayzer/Scriber launch (April 14) was priced BEFORE the full tariff regime. Here's the math:
- Frame components from Italy: +10% (Section 122)
- Electronic components from China: +250% (effectively sourced elsewhere or margin-destroyed)
- Lens manufacturing with Chinese inputs: exposure varies

**The question Ray's team is probably dealing with right now:** Can the $499 price hold? If component costs jump 10-25%, do they eat the margin or pass it through? Apple, Samsung, and Google face the same squeeze — but Apple manufactures at such volume they can absorb it. Meta's 9.6M unit smart glasses market is smaller.

## Second-Order Effects

1. **EssilorLuxottica may renegotiate terms** — they're a $100B+ company, they won't eat tariff costs
2. **Competitors may delay launch** — Samsung/Google glasses programs could slow if component costs spike
3. **Prescription glasses buyers are price-sensitive** — the whole pitch is "$499 replaces your next pair of frames." At $599, that math changes
4. **Supply chain diversification** = slower iteration cycles. Every new component source needs qualification

## What Ray Should Be Thinking About

- Does the Wearables team have a tariff impact model? If not, this is exactly the kind of operational risk that catches product teams off-guard
- The Privacy lawsuit + tariff pressure = double squeeze on the glasses P&L
- This could actually HELP Meta if competitors with thinner margins (Samsung, Google partnerships) blink first
