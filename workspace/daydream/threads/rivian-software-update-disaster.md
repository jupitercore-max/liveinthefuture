# Rivian 2026.07 Software Update: Bricking Keys on Gen 1 Vehicles

**Created:** 2026-04-13 (Iteration 8)
**Status:** CRITICAL — Ray has 2 Rivian R1S. Immediate family safety/convenience concern.
**Action:** Verify whether Ray's vehicles received the update. Ensure backup key cards are accessible in both vehicles.

## The Situation

Rivian halted rollout of its **2026.07 software update** over the weekend of April 12-13 after it caused critical failures on Gen 1 vehicles:

1. **Phone app access disabled** — can't unlock or start car via phone
2. **Key fobs bricked** — physical keys stopped working
3. **Backup key cards** are the ONLY way to access affected vehicles
4. **Data wiped** — saved radio stations, elevation numbers, owner contact information all reset
5. Stock dropped ~2% on the news (Stocktwits, April 13)

Source: eletric-vehicles.com, Stocktwits business coverage

## Why This Matters for Ray

- **Ray has 2 Rivian R1S** — both potentially affected
- If one or both received the 2026.07 update before the halt, he could be locked out
- **Family safety angle:** If Ray's partner drives the R1S without knowing about the key issue, she could be stranded
- **Kids in car concern:** A bricked key fob with kids in/near the vehicle is a safety scenario

## Immediate Actions

1. **Check both R1S vehicles** — did either receive the 2026.07 update?
2. **Locate backup key cards** for both vehicles — keep one in wallet, one with partner
3. **Don't manually trigger any pending software update** until Rivian confirms the fix
4. **Check Rivian app** for any notification about the halted update

## Also: Shadow Braking Reports

Separately from the software update, there are reports of Rivian R1S **shadow braking** — the ADAS system misinterpreting shadows (e.g., overpass shadows) as solid objects and triggering hard braking. This is a computer vision limitation affecting the 2026 R1S. No formal NHTSA recall yet, but worth monitoring.

## Vehicle Safety Article Angle

This is a strong VS article candidate: "Your EV's Software Update Just Locked You Out: When OTA Goes Wrong." The Rivian 2026.07 incident is a concrete case study of the risks of over-the-air updates that control physical access to vehicles.
