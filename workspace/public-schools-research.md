### Bond Measures & Parcel Tax History
- **First bonds approved:** 1995
- **First parcel tax:** 2000
- **Measure B (November 2021):** Renewal of existing parcel tax with $192.50 increase. Supports class sizes, teaching staff, and academics. Required 2/3 supermajority.
- MPCSD is **community-funded**: ~90% of funding from local sources (property tax, parcel taxes, philanthropic giving)
- **AAA credit rating** for general obligation bonds
- The Board cut over **$3 million since 2017** as part of balanced financial planning
- 89% of budget goes to salaries and benefits
- *Source: [MPCSD OpenGov](https://stories.opengov.com/mpcsdca/published/VWOSfVHzG)*

### Math Curriculum & Acceleration Policy
**Elementary (K-5):** **Math Expressions** by Houghton Mifflin Harcourt
- Supplemented by **DreamBox** (adaptive online platform, K-5, English & Spanish)
- Member of **SVMI** (Silicon Valley Mathematics Initiative)

**Middle School (6-8):** **Big Ideas** by Ron Larson & Laurie Boswell

**Acceleration/Compaction Pathways:**
- **5th Grade:** Two options — standard 5th Grade CC Math OR **5/6A Compacted CC Math** (covers all 5th grade + 1/3 of 6th grade standards at accelerated pace)
- Multiple data points used for placement: end-of-year assessment, SBAC scores, teacher observations
- **Hillview Middle Math Pathways:** District developed a compaction model supporting **single and double acceleration**
  - **Math 7/8 Compacted:** Compresses Math 7 and Math 8 into one year for 7th graders, preparing them for **Algebra 1 in 8th grade**
  - Multiple entry points throughout middle school for acceleration
  - *"Regardless of math placement in 5th grade, MPCSD has designed multiple entry points for students at Hillview to either accelerate their learning or continue with the Common Core Mathematics pathway"*
- **No formal GATE program** — MPCSD does not have a designated GATE (Gifted and Talented Education) program; instead relies on differentiation, compaction, and DreamBox personalization

*Sources: [MPCSD Adopted Book List](https://district.mpcsd.org/departments/educational-services/curriculum-and-instruction/adopted-book-list), CAASPP search results*

### Community Sentiment & Controversies
- **Measure V (2022):** Menlo Park housing development ballot measure — the MPCSD school board initially took a position then flip-flopped under community pressure
- **California Math Framework Debate:** Like many high-performing Bay Area districts, MPCSD has navigated the statewide controversy over math acceleration. The CA framework recommends all students take the same math through 10th grade before tracking — MPCSD maintains its acceleration pathways despite this
- **COVID Response:** MPCSD was the **first Bay Area public school district to reopen in Fall 2020** (in-person), widely praised
- **Demographic shifts:** The district's White population has declined from ~80% in the 1990s to ~46% today, while Asian enrollment has risen from ~9% to ~19% and Hispanic from ~8% to ~21%
- **Enrollment pressure:** The district notes "20 years of increased enrollment and much more housing development on the way" as a funding challenge

## KEY TAKEAWAYS FOR MENLO OAKS AREA

1. **Which school zone?** Colby Ave & Menlo Oaks Dr is in the **MPCSD** attendance area. The specific elementary school assignment depends on exact address — check [MPCSD enrollment](https://district.mpcsd.org) for boundary maps. Likely **Encinal Elementary** given the Atherton/Menlo Oaks geography, but could be Laurel.

2. **All three MPCSD elementaries are excellent** — all rated 10/10 (Top 5% in CA). Oak Knoll has the highest math scores, Laurel the highest ELA, and Encinal is the most diverse.

3. **Hillview Middle is the only option** in MPCSD and is very strong (80% ELA, 76% Math meeting standards). It offers Algebra 1 in 8th grade via compaction.

4. **La Entrada (Las Lomitas)** outperforms Hillview academically but is in a different district — only accessible if you live in the Las Lomitas attendance area.

5. **M-A High is a good comprehensive high school** but has a wide achievement gap due to its very diverse catchment. Well-resourced families do well (41% of AP test-takers score 5), but the school-wide math proficiency of 54% masks a bimodal distribution.

6. **Per-pupil spending is very high** (~$26,000-$27,000) thanks to property taxes, parcel taxes, and philanthropy. This is ~30-40% above the state average.

7. **Math acceleration is available** at all levels — compaction starts in 5th grade, Algebra 1 is accessible in 8th, and M-A offers AP Calculus AB/BC.

8. **No formal GATE program** — MPCSD uses differentiation, compaction, and adaptive tools (DreamBox) instead of a separate gifted track.
